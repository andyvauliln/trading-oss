_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: A (A official programme pages, own seven-day on-chain payout census and live fee-flag census; B Data API wallet-level fee and rebate fields; D for promo-conversion practice, which rests on affiliate pages) · Last verified: 2026-09-28_

### Taker rebates, fee tiers and promo extraction
- **One-line summary**: Treat the venue's fee configuration as a strategy input rather than a constant: consolidate all taker flow into one attributable account so it climbs the 30-day weighted-volume tiers (3-50% of taker fees back, paid daily in pUSD), route each taker agent by the live per-market `feeSchedule` (zero-fee geopolitics, game-day NFL, legacy 0.03 sports futures), and, for a US-resident team member, harvest the bounded Polymarket US deposit credits and prior-month taker-rebate tiers. It re-prices every other taker file in this survey; it earns nothing on its own.
- **Category**: Incentive farming [cost overlay: a shared fee-and-rebate module for files 07, 08, 12, 13, 17, 18, 19, 24, not a standalone strategy]
- **Maturity**: Established as a programme, not as a strategy. The Taker Rebate Program has run since 2026-05-28 [1] and is the largest subsidy line on the venue: pUSD transfers from its distributor `0x520BF77D9d34C34a6A9723f50E1Dcb887eD238C5` [10] over the seven daily runs of 22-28 September 2026 (paying 21-27 September) totalled $1,220,944 to 7,000 distinct wallets, $139.7k-$206.9k per run [9], against $2.04M over the eight daily runs of 13-20 September counted for pack E2 [E2]. Nobody earns from the tier itself: the twelve largest weekly recipients ($17.5k-$75.0k each) are crypto and sports takers whose lifetime rebate equals 8-33% of lifetime fees for eleven of them (one shows 55%, above the programme's 50% cap, so the field is not clean), and five of the twelve are net losers after fees despite the rebate (realised PnL -$0.17M to -$10.0M) [12]. Wash-climbing tiers is negative-sum before enforcement (arithmetic below), so the overlay's value is exactly the fee it removes from strategies that are already positive.
- **Requires AI?**: no. Everything is arithmetic on public fields: `feeSchedule` per market [6][7], the wV formula [1], and the Data API's `fees`, `taker_rebate` and `maker_rebate` fields per wallet [12]. Optional: a rules-reader that flags fee-category mislabels (a "Culture" tweet-count ladder at 0.05 sits beside "Mentions" at 0.04 [7]).
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Read the fee of every market from Gamma's `feeSchedule` object (`rate`, `exponent`, `takerOnly`, `rebateRate`) and `feeType`, as the docs have required since 2026-03-31 [5][6]. The taker fee is `C x rate x p x (1-p)` per share; makers pay nothing; the maker rebate pool is `rebateRate` of the fees collected in that market [2][3]. Do not read the CLOB market endpoint for this: it still returns the legacy `maker_base_fee` / `taker_base_fee: 1000` on every fee market, including the zero-fee NFL game, and `0` on fee-free geopolitics, and carries no schedule [8].
  2. Convert each agent's expected taker notional into Weighted Volume: `wV = trade size x (1 - entry price) x category weight x bonus multiplier` (the multiplier is 1 unless Polymarket runs an event bonus), weights Sports 1.0, Politics / Finance / Mentions / Tech 1.3, Economics / Culture / Weather / Other 1.7, Crypto 2.3, Geopolitics 0 [1]. Because the fee is `size x rate x (1-p)` in notional terms, wV per fee dollar is simply `weight / rate`: 20 in sports, 32.5 in politics, 32.9 in crypto, 34 in economics, 33.3 on the legacy 0.03 sports futures (derived from [1][2][7]).
  3. Sum the wV across every taker agent for a trailing 30 days and read the tier: Bronze $2k (3%), Silver $20k (8%), Gold $200k (18%), Platinum $1M (32%), Diamond $4M (44%), Obsidian $10M+ (50%); tiers recompute at midnight UTC, apply going forward only, and decay after a short grace period; one-off level-up bonuses of $10 / $50 / $250 / $1,500 / $7,500 / $25,000 [1]. In fee dollars per 30 days that is Gold at $10,000 (sports) or ~$5.9k-$6.2k (crypto, politics, economics), Platinum at $50k / ~$29-31k, Obsidian at $500k / ~$294-308k (derived).
  4. Consolidate: one account earns the tier for all agents; split accounts each sit lower. Omnibus wallets of third-party integrations are ineligible, and Polymarket may strip rebates and tier status for wash trading, self-matching or "inauthentic trading" [1].
  5. Route: send taker-style agents to fee-free inventory (all geopolitics, `feesEnabled: false` [7]; NFL game markets and player props on the day the game is played, `feeType: zero_fees` [7]; a few explicitly zero-fee geopolitics markets) and maker-style agents to fee-enabled inventory where the rebate pool exists; put tier-building flow in the categories with the best wV per fee dollar and keep it out of geopolitics, which earns no wV [1].
  6. Re-cost every taker strategy at its realistic tier: break-even edge per share falls from `rate x p(1-p)` to `(1 - rebate) x rate x p(1-p)`; at 50c a crypto taker needs 1.75c of edge at tier 0, 1.44c at Gold, 1.19c at Platinum, 0.875c at Obsidian; sports 1.25c / 1.03c / 0.85c / 0.625c; politics 1.00c / 0.82c / 0.68c / 0.50c (derived from [1][2]).
  7. US leg (US person or entity only): Polymarket US charges takers `0.0695 x C x p(1-p)` and credits makers `0.0125 x C x p(1-p)` at the fill; prior-calendar-month taker volume of $250k / $1M / $10M earns 10% / 25% / 50% of taker fees back, paid weekly, and "Accelerated Tier Placement" accepts proof of volume on another prediction market [14]. Deposit credits ($50 for a $10 deposit, once), refer-a-friend ($25 to each side, 50 referrals) and cohort-based deposit-and-trading campaigns ($10-$500, capped at $1,000 per campaign and $2,000 per participant) are non-withdrawable trading credits that can be converted by hedging the credited position elsewhere [16].
  8. Reconcile daily: the Data API v2 `user-stats` object carries `fees`, `fees_paid`, `taker_rebate`, `maker_rebate`, `reward_income` and `volume_usdc` per wallet [12]; on-chain, rebates arrive as pUSD from the distributor in the 00:00-00:45 UTC run [9]; on the US venue they appear as `ACTIVITY_TYPE_TAKER_FEE_REBATE` in the portfolio activities API [18].
- **What creates the edge**: A venue subsidy with a non-linear schedule. Polymarket rebates fees to concentrate flow: the marginal fee for the same trade differs by up to 2x between an Obsidian account and a tier-0 account, so the same signal is profitable in one and not the other. The formula rewards buying low-priced shares in high-weight categories (a $50 crypto trade at 5c earns $109.25 wV, at 50c $57.50 [1]), and rewards consolidation because thresholds are per account. Fee geometry adds a second edge: the fee is proportional to `p(1-p)`, so as a share of notional it is `rate x (1-p)`: 3.5% of notional for crypto at 50c but 0.7% at 90c and 0.21% at 97c; tail buyers pay the most per dollar of upside (6.65% at 5c) [2]. Routing edge comes from configuration that moves without notice: sports futures created before the 2026-07-10 fee rise still carry `sports_fees_v2` at 0.03 / 25% (143 of the 2,047 unique markets among the 2,100 most-traded rows, 9.2% of 24-hour volume, e.g. Ballon d'Or created 2025-09-22, MLS Cup 2026-02-06, Champions League 2026-07-02), while game markets show `sports_fees_v3` at 0.05 / 15% [7][5]. The counterparty is not a trader: it is Polymarket's marketing budget, which returned ~5.6% of the $21.6M of gross fees DefiLlama's adapter counted for 21-27 September [11][9]; the losers are the operators who run five small bots from five wallets and sit at tier 0.
- **Capital required**: none for the overlay; the capital is whatever the underlying taker agents use. Reaching Gold requires ~$170-400k of fee-paying taker notional at mid prices per 30 days (about $6-10k of fees), i.e. $10k of capital turning over 17-40x a month; Platinum ~$0.9-2M of notional; Obsidian ~$8-20M (derived from [1][2]). Rebates settle daily in pUSD, so lock-up is under a day [1]. Promo credits: $10-$250 of deposits per campaign, once per KYC identity [16].
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: Gamma `/markets` poller for `feeType` / `feeSchedule` / `feesEnabled` (paged at 100; `offset` above 2,000 is rejected with "use /markets/keyset for deeper pagination" [7]), changelog and docs watcher (the taker-rebate launch never appeared in the changelog; the July sports change did [5]), Data API v2 `user-stats` and `user-pnl` per wallet [12][13]. (2) Logic: a fee table per market and per hour (game-day flags are set hours before kickoff: the Eagles-Bears game with a 00:15 UTC start was `zero_fees` at 20:06 UTC and still at 20:58 UTC [7][8]), a wV ledger per account, a tier forecaster (rolling 30-day sum, daily update), a re-costing function every taker agent calls before sizing, and a router that sends orders to the account that will hold the highest tier. (3) Execution: one signing key per venue and per legal identity; never cross your own resting orders (self-match strips rebates [1] and breaches the "no change in beneficial ownership" clause [E9]). (4) Risk: alert when a market's `feeType` changes, when the rebate distributor pays less than the ledger predicts, and when tier decay is imminent. (5) Test: replay last quarter's fills through the fee table. Build time 1-2 weeks solo on an existing execution stack; the US leg adds KYC onboarding and a separate API client (no code sharing with the international CLOB [14][18]).
- **Opportunity size**: For the venue: $1.22M of taker rebates in the week of 21-27 September ($174k/day), down from ~$255k/day in the 13-20 September week [9][E2]; 135 wallets earned $1,000 or more in the week, 754 earned $100 or more, the median recipient $10, the top wallet $75,028 (6.1%), the top ten 32.1% [9]. For a solo operator it is a percentage of their own fees: 3-18% at the tiers a $1k-$10k book reaches, which is $80-$1,900 a month (derived below). Trend: tied to fee-paying taker volume (gross fees ran $2.6-4.7M a day in the last ten days [11]); the payout fell 32% per day between the two censuses, which may be volume mix (NFL zero-fee days, a slower crypto week) rather than a programme change (unverified), and the venue has cut every other programme's parameters at least once (sports rebate 25% to 15% on 2026-07-10 [5]; referral 30% gross to 10% net [4][E2]) and the docs say every tier parameter "can change at any time without notice" [1]. Fee-free inventory: 174 geopolitics markets (9.8% of 24-hour volume) plus 35 `zero_fees` markets (9.7%; 33 of them one NFL game and its props) in the census [7]. US promo credits: $50 + $25 x 50 + $2,000 per identity at most, once [16].
- **Pros**: Deterministic and enumerable; every input is a public field or an official page [1][2][6]. Daily pUSD payout, no lock-up [1]. It compounds with every taker file (07, 08, 12, 13, 17, 18, 19, 24) and with file 03's maker side, which is fee-neutral. Fee-free geopolitics gives an honest zero-fee sandbox for taker agents [2][7]. The wV formula favours exactly the trades a disciplined taker makes anyway (cheap upside, high-weight categories) [1]. The US rebate tier can be pre-qualified with proof of international volume [14].
- **Cons / risks**: Execution: consolidation concentrates key and operational risk in one account and makes the strategy mix visible on one profile (tier badges show on profiles and leaderboards [1]). Edge decay: parameters are discretionary; the sports fee rose 67% and its rebate fell 40% in one changelog entry [5]; the US venue re-dated its whole schedule "effective September 25, 2026" and raises table tennis to 0.10 on 30 September [14]. Platform ToS: rebates are forfeitable for wash trading, self-matching or "inauthentic trading"; omnibus wallets are excluded; referral clawback covers "linked accounts" [1][4][E9]. Legal: manufactured volume on the US DCM is wash trading under the CEA with fines up to $500,000 per violation (Rulebook 8.10(b)) and CFTC exposure; extra identities for promo credits are fraud on a KYC'd venue [E9][16]. Capital: none beyond the underlying strategies, but a rebate does not rescue a losing taker: the sixth-largest weekly recipient has paid $2.60M of fees, received $708k back and lost $10.0M realised [12]. Measurement: the Data API `fees_refunded` field (up to $3.85M on old wallets) is undocumented and one wallet's `taker_rebate / fees` ratio is 54.9%, above the 50% maximum, so wallet-level ratios are indicative only [12].
- **Expected results**: The rebate is `tier rate x fees`. Hard data on the recipients: the largest weekly recipient (joined 2026-02-13) has $119.0M of lifetime USDC volume, $2.01M of fees (1.69% of volume), $545k of taker rebates (0.46% of volume, 27% of fees), realised PnL +$250k on the live stats endpoint (+$212k on the daily series' last point) and pre-fee trade PnL +$2.05M, so rebates are 2.2-2.6x its net trading profit [12][13]; the second (joined 2026-08-11) paid $1.10M of fees in seven weeks and got $259k back (23.6%) with +$0.78M realised [12]; a wallet with 813 lifetime trades received $39k in the week [9][12]. For a solo book, derived: at Bronze-Silver (3-8%) on $1-5k of monthly fees the rebate is $30-$400 a month; at Gold on $6-10k of fees, $1.1-1.8k. Routing is worth more than the tier for small books: moving a sports taker from a 0.05 game market to the zero-fee game-day version saves 2.5% of notional at mid, which is larger than the 0.35-1.1% net margin the systematic sports takers keep after fees (file 19's own Data API census). Promo credits: ~$45-$47 cash per $50 credit after a hedged conversion at 3.5% US taker fee plus 2.5-4% on the hedge leg (guess, no practitioner data).
- **Competitive landscape**: Not competitive in the usual sense: the rebate is per account, not a shared pool, so another trader's tier does not reduce yours; only the maker pool (file 01, 03) is contested. What is contested is the underlying flow: the top rebate earners are the crypto and sports takers of files 12, 18 and 19 (130k-153k lifetime trades, $1.1-5.1M of lifetime fees [12]). Incumbents' advantage is scale: an Obsidian account pays half the fee of a newcomer on the same signal. No tier-optimisation or fee-routing write-up was found (unverified; the idea appears only in this survey's discovery lenses).
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for consolidating your own honest flow in one account and reading public fee fields; grey for running the same taker strategy from several own wallets (any self-fill is prohibited without an intent element, and rebates are strippable [1][E9]); prohibited for wash or self-matched volume to climb tiers (ToS §4.2, Rulebook 7.2(k),(p),(q); CFTC wash-trading advisory [E9]), for self-referral or linked-account referral farming [4], and for multiple KYC identities on Polymarket US [16][E9]. Promo credits are one per participant and "may be withheld, cancelled or reversed" for coordinated activity [16]. The US programmes are US-only.
- **How you'd validate it**: Cheap and decisive. (1) Fee ledger: replay your last 90 days of fills through the live `feeSchedule` table; compute fees paid, wV by category and the tier a consolidated account would have held each day; compare with the split-account reality (cost: a day). (2) Routing test: for two weeks snapshot `feeType` hourly for all active markets, diff against the docs table, log flips (game-day NFL, legacy `sports_fees_v2`), and recompute each taker agent's PnL under the routed fee (cost: a cron job). (3) Reconciliation: check that the pUSD received from `0x520BF77D…` in the 00:00-00:45 UTC run equals `tier x yesterday's fees` from your own ledger for five days; pass if within 1% [9][12]. (4) US: one US-resident team member runs the $50 credit through a hedged NFL moneyline and records cash retained; pass if above 85% (guess). Do not run a wash test: it is prohibited and negative-sum.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive for the overlay itself (tiers are computed once a day at midnight UTC [1]), execution-sensitive for routing, because fee flags change by calendar day and the wV of a fill is fixed by the entry price you get.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fee-flag discovery | Gamma `feeSchedule` / `feeType` polling [6][7] | Missing a game-day `zero_fees` flip costs 2.5% of notional at mid (sports at 50c) [2] | poll every 15-60 min; re-check at 00:00 UTC and before kickoffs |
| Tier accounting | 30-day wV ledger, daily update [1] | A late consolidation delays the tier by a day; rebates are never backfilled [1] | ledger updated per fill; tier forecast daily |
| Routing / entry | Which account and which market each taker order goes to | Wrong account: fee up to 2x; wrong market version: 0.05 vs 0.03 vs 0 [7] | router in the order path, <10 ms overhead |
| Cancel / exit | Nothing fee-specific; makers pay no fee [2] | none | n/a |
| Hedge leg (promo conversion) | Cross-venue rule alignment | Rule mismatch (sportsbook voids, Polymarket grades "No", Kalshi settles at the last pre-game price) can lose a leg [21] | hedge only moneylines and totals |
| Settlement / recycling | Daily pUSD payout, $1 minimum [1]; US weekly [14] | none | reconcile daily |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Zero for the tier: a fill at 10:00 and a fill at 23:59 earn the same wV and the same rebate [1]. (2) Large for flags: the census shows one NFL game and its props at 0 while the next games in the census (2-6 October) sit at 0.05 [7]; a router that reads the flag once a day can miss the hours between the flip and kickoff (one game observed, flag on at least 4 h 9 min before kickoff; unverified as a general rule). (3) Entry price sets wV: the same $1,000 of crypto notional earns $1,150 wV at 50c and $2,185 wV at 5c [1], so taker agents that buy cheap upside climb faster. (4) Venue delays and rate limits (150 ms crypto taker delay [5], 1 s sports delay per file 18) do not touch the rebate.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted; a cron job next to your existing bots. Do not pay for Dublin hosting or L2 data for this file. Pay instead for a reliable Polygon RPC with log history (public nodes prune within a day; Tenderly's gateway served all seven runs [9]).

### What you are probably underestimating
1. **The rebate is bigger than the trading profit of the people who earn most of it.** The largest weekly recipient has $545k of lifetime taker rebates against $212-250k of realised PnL (the daily series and the live stats endpoint disagree), i.e. 2.2-2.6x; without the programme the wallet is negative [12][13]. Five of the twelve largest weekly recipients are net losers after fees, one by $10.0M with $708k of rebates in hand [12]. If your taker agents are marginal, the tier decides the sign of the book; if they are losing, it only slows the bleed.
2. **Your existing five-wallet layout is throwing away the tier.** Thresholds are per account and rebates apply "from the moment you reach a tier, going forward" with no backfill [1]. Five bots each paying $2k of fees a month sit at Bronze-Silver (3-8%); one account paying $10k sits at Gold (18%) in sports and well past it in crypto (wV per fee dollar 32.9 vs 20 [1][2]). The cost is the loss of wallet-level anonymity (tier badges on profiles and leaderboards [1]) and one key to protect.
3. **The census is one game, not one category.** On 2026-09-28 at 20:06 UTC the only `zero_fees` game markets among the 2,100 most-traded were Eagles-Bears (kickoff 00:15 UTC) and its player props; every other NFL game line in the census (games of 2-6 October and later; the Sunday 27 September games had already closed) showed 0.05 / 15% [7][8]. Files 18 and 19 call this a game-day switch; the switch is earlier than kickoff and the docs table never mentions it [2]. A fee router that hard-codes "NFL is free" is wrong on every day a given game is not being played.
4. **Legacy fee versions are live and tradable.** 143 sports futures, all created before the 2026-07-10 change (latest 2026-07-08), still carry `sports_fees_v2` at 0.03 with a 25% maker rebate, 9.2% of 24-hour volume in the census (Ballon d'Or created 2025-09-22, MLS Cup 2026-02-06, Champions League 2026-07-02) [7][5]. Taker agents pay 40% less there; maker agents get a 67% larger pool share; nothing in the docs says this, and the CLOB market endpoint does not expose it [8][2].
5. **wV rewards the cheap side, so the formula is a hidden portfolio tilt.** A $50 crypto trade at 5c earns 1.9x the wV of the same dollars at 50c [1]; sniping at 97c earns 3% of notional as wV while an arb leg at 50c earns 50% [E2]. A team that chases tier will drift toward longshot buying, which file 24's evidence says loses 19.3c per dollar below 10c. Tier-build with the trades you want anyway; never let the ledger pick the trade.
6. **Wash-climbing loses at every tier, and the arithmetic is public.** A self-matched round trip pays the full taker fee F; the taker rebate returns 3-50% of F only from the day the tier is reached [1]; the maker leg returns at most `rebateRate` of F and only your share of that market's pool (25% politics, 20% crypto, 15% sports) [3]; self-referral is prohibited and would add 10% of net fees at most [4]. Ceiling 80% of F at Obsidian as the sole maker, 18-57% on the way up; reaching Obsidian in crypto costs ~$304k of fees in 30 days while you sit at 0-44%, and the $25,000 bonus is 8% of that outlay [1] (derived). Then the venue can strip the rebate and tier [1], and on the US venue the same loop is wash trading under the CEA [E9].
7. **Level-up bonuses are marketing, not income.** Relative to the fees you must first pay in the cheapest category (crypto), the bonuses are Bronze 16%, Silver 8%, Gold 4%, Platinum 5%, Diamond 6%, Obsidian 8% of the outlay, once (derived from [1][2]); the brief's "5-17%" holds only for the two bottom tiers.
8. **The programme is discretionary and already shrinking on a weekly view.** $1.22M over the seven runs of 22-28 September (paying 21-27) versus $2.04M over the eight runs of 13-20 September [9][E2], a 32% drop per day that may be mix rather than policy (unverified); the docs reserve the right to change rates, thresholds, weights and bonuses "at any time without notice" and to "adjust rebates and tier status for users" at sole discretion [1]; the sports rebate was cut from 25% to 15% with a one-line changelog entry [5]. Model the rebate as a variable cost reduction with a haircut, not as revenue.
9. **The Polymarket US pieces are one gating decision, not five features.** Liquidity Incentive Program (file 01), volume pools and promo credits (this file), the US best-bid-offer signal (file 08), US RFQ combos (file 06) and the ISV route (file 32) all sit behind the same gate: a US person or entity, KYC, funding only from your own financial accounts (Rulebook 3.9), no trading for others (7.2(x)), pre-trade risk controls and a kill switch for automated traders, and fines up to $500,000 per violation (8.10(b)) [E9]. The volume pool page still lists only the May NBA-playoff pool and says future amounts "will be published here" [15][18]; the overview marks it "Open" while the Market Maker and Referral programmes are application-only with undisclosed terms [17][19]; treat the pool as dormant until one is posted.
10. **Sportsbook bonus conversion with Polymarket US or Kalshi as the lay leg exists as a practice, but its economics on this venue are unverified.** Matched-betting guides already warn that a player-prop hedge across a sportsbook and Polymarket / Kalshi can lose both legs when the book voids and the exchange grades "No" [21]; the affiliate page advertising the $50 credit (re-fetched 2026-09-28) repeats the first-party figures except the referral cap (it says up to $350, i.e. 14 referrals; the docs say 50) and is marketing [20][16]. No practitioner PnL was found. It is bounded per KYC identity to a few hundred dollars once, so it is pocket money for the team's one US resident, not a strategy to automate.
