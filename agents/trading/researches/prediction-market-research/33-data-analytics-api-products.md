### Data, analytics, API and infrastructure products
- **One-line summary**: Sell what the platform does not serve for free: archived order books with on-chain-true trade direction, reconciled wallet records, cross-venue normalisation, compliance feeds and hosted bots. At least nine vendors publish price lists ($9-$360 a month), three were acquired in 2026, none discloses revenue, four of the nine sub-variants have no revenue, user or TVL evidence at all, and the ToS bars "market data distributors" from using Polymarket data without a written agreement.
- **Category**: Service & data business [software and data products only; fee-on-flow apps are file 32, audience and content businesses file 34]
- **Maturity**: Established as a category, unproven as income. Evidence by sub-variant: (1) wallet trackers and analytics SaaS: list prices (PolyTrack, Convexly, Oddpool) and one exit, PolyGun's purchase of Polymarket Analytics (250,000+ users, terms undisclosed, 2026-03-09) [22][21][23][19]; (2) historical datasets: list prices at Telonex, Probalytics and PolymarketData, claimed academic users [7][8][9]; (3) unified APIs: list prices at pmxt and Prediction.com, and two exits: Polymarket bought Dome (2026-02-19) and shut it down in April, and agg.market bought Polynode (2026-07-22), which "customers already pay for" [10][11][8][31][45]; (4) low-latency feeds: Polynode's pitch is decoding Polymarket orders "three to five seconds before they land onchain" (vendor claim) [45]; no third-party news feed has users or revenue, and the only priced news feed is sold by its source (Truth API) [30]; (5) calibrated-probability feeds: none beyond free "AI probability" columns [25]; (6) no-code builders: TurbineFi's price list, no user counts [26]; (7) custom bot development and bot-selling [grey]: 4,013 "polymarket bot" repos of supply, no revenue evidence (the scam fringe is file 34) [13]; (8) surveillance and compliance: real contracts, Kalshi-centred: StarCompliance (June), Comply (2026-08-04, Polymarket covered through ZenLedger) and Nasdaq surveillance at Kalshi (headline) [28][29][31]; (9) vaults, funds and OTC: every independent product checked is empty: PolyFund lists no fund with a value, Gondor is "Coming soon", uma.rocks manages "0 UMA ($0) from 0 delegators", PolyHedg is a demo for "John Doe, CFO", and none of the four appears among DefiLlama's 8,411 tracked protocols [32][46], although the demand story is loud (the top 1% of profitable accounts take 76.5% of profits [38]; a 173-point HN thread [37]). The one real OTC business is Galaxy's institutional desk, a principal counterparty whose launch trade ($10M with Arca) was on Kalshi [44]. Oddpool "is no longer accepting new subscriptions" [23]; the only disclosed builder income is Based's ~$1M annualised, from builder rewards, not data (file 32) [27].
- **Requires AI?**: optional. LLMs are used for market matching across venues (Prediction.com's matching-markets endpoint, truthlayer's FAISS + LLM matcher [E6]), rules extraction and "AI probability" columns (Hashdive) [11][25]; none of the paying products depends on it. The "AI analyst" chat quota is a tier feature at Oddpool, not the product [23].
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Pick the gap. The platform keeps no order-book history: `/v2/prices-history` serves a market's whole life only at 12-hour buckets and caps explicit windows at 15 days [1]; the public WebSocket labels trade direction correctly only ~59% of the time, so on-chain `OrderFilled` truth is the differentiator [17][E1]; Data API v1 pages stop at 10,000 rows and v1 is retired on 2026-10-24 [2].
  2. Record. A recorder subscribes to the CLOB `market` stream (book, price_change, last_trade_price, tick_size_change plus best_bid_ask, new_market and market_resolved events) [41] and to Polygon `OrderFilled` logs (V2 exchanges) through an RPC or HyperSync; Dubach's archive ingested 30 billion events in 52 days with a sub-50 ms median ingestion delay and a multi-second tail [17]; Kacho's per-second recorder captured ~89,000 five-minute markets (26.8M observations, ~725 MB Parquet) in two months [18].
  3. Store and reconcile. ClickHouse or Parquet on object storage; join feed events to on-chain fills; reconcile wallet PnL to `/v2/user-pnl` and `/v2/user-stats` (whose `trades` counts distinct markets, not fills [1]); publish the caveats (Convexly withdrew a 100-wallet audit because 85 rows were truncated at a 2,000-fill cap [20]).
  4. Package. Parquet downloads, a REST or SQL endpoint, a dashboard, alerts, or a compliance export; price per seat ($9-$360 a month) or per contract; personal-use licences at the low tiers [7][8][9][10].
  5. Sell. Quants and academics (PolymarketData shows LSE, Cornell, Yale and Bocconi logos [9]), compliance platforms (Comply integrates venue data for preclearance and case management [28]), media (Eventual raised a seed on a Polymarket data partnership, headline only [31]), or an acquirer (three exits in 2026 [19][31][45]).
- **What creates the edge**: Structural gaps plus correctness, not information. The venue's free surface is wide (twenty v2 routes: activity, positions, trades, user PnL, user stats, holders with position economics, leaderboards, biggest winners, builder boards, open interest, live volume, resolutions, price history, status [1]) but shallow in history, cursor-paginated, rate-limited with `429` and `Retry-After` [1], and lagging at busy hours: file 26 measured `/v2/trades` 105-395 s stale, while idle-hour checks found the newest v2 row 0.8 s old (21:52 UTC, 28 Sep) and 2.9 s old (10:20 UTC, 29 Sep), with v1 175-316 s behind [5]. Whoever recorded the book for months owns something nobody can regenerate, which cuts both ways: PolymarketData (L2 snapshots since August 2025) and Probalytics (full depth since November 2025) are already 10-14 months ahead of anyone starting now, so a new entrant can only differentiate on coverage they lack [8][9]. The counterparty is the researcher or bot builder who would otherwise spend $65-200 a month and a quarter of wall clock building the archive, and the compliance officer who needs venue coverage inside a platform "trusted by more than 5,000 firms" [28]. They keep paying because the platform keeps changing (the V2 cutover broke V1 pipelines: `poly_data`'s old pipeline "no longer returns complete data" after April 2026 [E6]) and because free academic dumps stop at a date: Polymarket-v1 ends 2026-04-28 [15].
- **Capital required**: <$1k for a tracker or alert product on the free APIs; $1k-$10k for a dataset business, because a recorder must run three months before there is anything to sell (c7i.large ~$65, 2-5 TB of storage $30-120, RPC or HyperSync $0-100 a month) [47][35]. No trading capital and no lock-up; the "lock-up" is unpaid recording time.
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: CLOB WebSocket with reconnect and gap logging; Polygon logs via Alchemy (30M free compute units, then $0.525 per million [34]), Envio HyperSync ($0 fair-use, $70, $480 a month [35]) or Goldsky ($100 credit, subgraph ~$37 a month at Scale [33]); Gamma keyset for metadata; `/v2` for reconciliation [1][4]. (2) Storage: ClickHouse or DuckDB plus Parquet on S3 or R2; `nahrek/polyledger` (624 stars, created 2026-09-02) shows the V1 + V2 fills-into-DuckDB pattern with the `builder` field exposed [12]. (3) Serving: REST with API keys and quotas, or SQL access as Probalytics does [8]; Stripe billing; docs. (4) Correctness: published methods, coverage statistics, a withdrawal policy (the Convexly precedent [20]); reconcile against on-chain truth, not the feed [17]. (5) Legal: a written data agreement with Polymarket before charging anyone (see the flag), personal-use licences, no resale to capital-market clients [48][E9]. Build time solo: tracker or alert bot 2-4 weeks; dataset product 4-8 weeks plus the recording quarter; compliance export 6-10 weeks including a pilot customer.
- **Opportunity size**: no vendor discloses revenue, so bounds only. The platform earned $85.0M of fees in the last 30 days ($407.6M all time; DefiLlama "revenue" $14.0M for 30 days) [6]; that money goes to Polymarket, makers and rebate tiers, not to data vendors. The one disclosed builder income is ~$1M annualised (Based, from rewards), and Chainstory's "median builder processed roughly $20,000" in Q1 is a leaderboard figure, which file 32 shows is counted in shares [27]. The largest audience product in the niche (250,000+ users, 2.3M traders tracked, ~120M positions indexed) sold for undisclosed terms to a copy bot [19]. Nine priced vendors with, plausibly, tens to a few hundred paying seats each puts the independent retail-data pool in the low tens of thousands of dollars a month (guess). Growing at the B2B end (compliance contracts, Galaxy's OTC desk, ICE's Polymarket signals product and its $2B investment "about data", the last two headlines only [28][44][31]), saturating at the retail end (three free trackers, a closed vendor, platform encroachment by Data API v2 [3]).
- **Pros**: no market risk and no capital lock-up; the same recorder feeds files 01-05 (executable-price backtests) and 26-27 (wallet audit); exits by acquisition are demonstrated three times in 2026; the venue lists its data partners and dashboards, so distribution channels exist [4]; Show HN launches cost nothing (46 Polymarket posts since 2025) [36].
- **Cons / risks**: Platform encroachment: v2 added wallet PnL series, profile stats, windowed volume, biggest winners, holders with PnL and builder boards in one release [3]; the platform retires APIs (v1 on 2026-10-24) and rewrote the exchange (V2, April 2026), which broke V1 pipelines [2][E6]. Commoditisation: a free 1.20B-trade archive with ground-truth direction (14,419 downloads on Hugging Face, CC-BY-4.0) [14][15], a free millisecond Binance-paired corpus [16], a free CC0 five-minute book set [18], a 36 GiB Polymarket + Kalshi dump with 3,864 stars [12]. Attention: 13,846 GitHub repos match "polymarket", 759 created since 2026-08-29, 4,013 "bot" repos [13]; Show HN posts earn a median of 2 points [36]. Correctness liability: truncated histories and mislabelled direction produce wrong claims about named people (Convexly withdrawal; defamation exposure in E9) [20][E9]. Licence: see the flag; whether the incumbents hold written agreements is not public. Supply chain: a suspected typosquat SDK (malice unverified) outranks the official client by stars [E6]. Vendor churn: Oddpool closed to new subscribers, Dome shut after acquisition, pmxt's open-source repo was last pushed 2026-07-18 while its hosted API is still sold [23][8][12][10].
- **Expected results**: no public data, theoretical only. Reasoned guess for a solo builder: a free tracker with a $9-$49 tier reaches 0-30 paying users in 90 days (-$80 to +$190 a month net); a recorded-book dataset at $29-$99 reaches 0-20 seats after the recording quarter (-$400 to +$1,600 a month net), because three incumbents already sell longer book history; a single B2B pipeline (compliance export, media feed, licensed dataset) is worth $1,000-$8,000 a month if it closes and a Polymarket data agreement is in place, and $0 otherwise. The only hard anchors are that the 250,000-user leader exited for undisclosed terms [19], that Based's $1M is rewards income [27], and that the no-code builder's own landing-page showcase shows Kalshi BTC 15-minute backtests at -$293 to +$162 net PnL [26].
- **Competitive landscape**: Crowded and thin. Datasets: Telonex ($0 trial with 5 downloads, $99 single exchange, $199 Pro, personal-use licence) [7]; Probalytics ($39 rolling 30 days, $129 full history, $249 tick-level books; Polymarket books from Nov 2025, Kalshi from May 2026; ClickHouse SQL; four collectors per venue) [8]; PolymarketData (Trader $60, Pro $120, Ultra $360 a month, a quarter off annually; L2 snapshots from August 2025; S3 exports) [9]. Unified APIs: pmxt hosted ($0 with 25,000 credits, $29.99, $99.99 with 1M credits; "Migrate from Dome") [10]; Prediction.com ($0 at 1 req/s, $49 Dev at 20 req/s, $249 Pro at 100 req/s; nine stream channels, MCP) [11]; Polynode, now inside agg.market [45]. Trackers: PolyTrack ($10 a week, $99.99 a year, or $99.99 once in crypto for lifetime) [22]; Convexly ($0, $9 for 3 wallets, $49 for 10, $499 with move alerts, $19 one-time reconciled read) [21]; PredictFolio (free; tax reports, combo accounting, own indexer) [24]; Hashdive (free) [25]; Polysights ("$5.7B" tracked, no public price) [43]. No-code: TurbineFi ($19 Starter with 10 deploys a week, $99, $199, $4,999 Founder) [26]. Compliance: Comply, StarCompliance, ZenLedger, Nasdaq at the venue level [28][29][31]. Infrastructure incumbents: Goldsky with ClickHouse's CryptoHouse, Dune, Allium, DefiLlama, Token Terminal, The Block, Artemis, Blockworks, all listed by Polymarket itself [4]. Funding went to terminals, wallets and rival venues (Based $11.5M; Pascal $9M to build a venue for professional traders, headline) rather than data [27][31]. To compete: on-chain-true direction, gap-free coverage of something the incumbents do not record, and published correctness.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: grey, close to prohibited without a written agreement. Polymarket ToS §4.2 (effective 2026-08-11) bars a "market data distributor", and any "non-retail, professional entity that engages in capital markets activities", from using Polymarket Data "directly or through an API, or any other means, including on-chain, whether in raw, derived, aggregated, or anonymized form" unless Polymarket agrees in writing; it bans reselling the Data to either group and scraping with "robots, crawlers"; the only carve-out is that Polymarket "reserves the right to grant access to public on-chain infrastructure and the Company's builder program" [48][E9]. A paid data vendor is plausibly a market data distributor. Polymarket US: market data "personal, non-commercial", no redistribution or "commercialization of derived works" [E9]. Selling trade signals or auto-execution is a CTA question and outside CFTC Letter 26-25's passive-software relief [E9]. Naming wallets with wrong statistics is a defamation exposure [E9]. Pooling outside capital (vaults, funds, OTC blocks) is CPO / CTA / swaps territory [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: Two weeks, ~$300, before building a product. (0) Ask Polymarket (builder@ or the data-partner contact on the docs page) in writing whether a paid dataset or API derived from its Data needs an agreement, and on what terms; treat a non-answer as a no for anything sold to firms [48][4]. (1) Ship a free single-purpose tool (one wallet audit, one dataset sample) with a waitlist and a $9-$29 pre-order; measure sign-ups per 1,000 visitors from a Show HN post, two subreddits and the builder Telegram. Pass: 20 pre-orders or 200 waitlist emails in 30 days; fail: fewer, and the tracker segment is audience-only. (2) For a dataset: pick a coverage gap the incumbents lack (check Telonex, Probalytics and PolymarketData schemas first), run the recorder for 30 days ($100-$150) and offer the month to three quants and one academic at $99; pass: two paid. (3) For compliance or media: one discovery call each with a compliance platform, a newsroom and a venue-data partner listed on the docs page [4]; pass: a written pilot scope. Kill condition for any of the three: the same data appears on the venue's own API within the quarter (watch the changelog [3]).

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive: subscriptions, datasets and exports are judged over weeks; the recorder is the one execution-sensitive part (every reconnect gap is a permanent hole), and a news feed would be latency-critical for its buyers but is not a solo product.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Book and trade recording | CLOB `market` WebSocket, reconnect logic, disk throughput [17][41] | gaps and duplicates; a dataset with holes sells at a discount (Kacho sells 99.8% coverage [18]); the 2026-08-31 halt kept market data up, so logged incident windows are sellable [E1] | two independent hosts (Probalytics runs four collectors per venue [8]) |
| On-chain confirmation | Polygon blocks ~2 s; HyperSync or Alchemy logs; V2 exchange addresses [E1] | wrong trade direction (feed labels ~59% right) and mis-assigned makers [17][E1] | reconcile every fill to `OrderFilled` within a minute; publish the match rate |
| Wallet and PnL reconciliation | `/v2/user-pnl`, `/v2/user-stats`; v1 cap 10,000 rows [1][2] | truncated histories become wrong public claims [20] | on-chain ledgers per wallet; disclose caps |
| Serving | REST or SQL endpoint, uptime | buyers judge uptime during venue incidents (nine in 90 days [E1]) | 99.5% uptime, status page |
| News or resolution-source feed | source latency; the source sells its own feed at $60,000-$100,000 a month [30] | a relay of a public feed has nothing to sell against the source | do not build; use for own trading (file 17) |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) The free baseline is fast when idle and slow when busy: 0.8-2.9 s freshness on `/v2/trades` at idle hours versus 105-395 s in file 26's busy-hour window [5]; a vendor sells consistency, not speed. (2) Recording gaps are irreversible: a one-hour outage in a two-month capture loses ~0.07% of rows but every market that resolved inside it (derived). (3) For news the source owns the speed: Truth API sells "fractionally faster" posts to "more than 10" customers, mostly high-frequency firms [30].
- **Where to run it**: anywhere reliable; latency does not decide revenue. A read-only recorder can sit in eu-west-2 (London, where the servers are) or eu-west-1: the UK close-only rule restricts opening positions, not reading data [E1]. Two hosts on different providers; no co-location (it excludes builders and apps) [E1].
