# 52 - Small decision bodies: closed-electorate selection, coalition bargaining and committee-vote simulators
_Band: speculative · Evidence grade: D (no forecasting or trading record against a market price anywhere; A for the market baseline measured here from Gamma, CLOB and Kalshi; B for the adjacent network, bargaining and committee-simulation literature, mostly preprints; C for self-reported claims) · Last verified: 2026-09-29_

### Small decision bodies: selection, coalition and committee-vote simulators
- **One-line summary**: Model the few people who decide (a Nobel committee, the conclave, the Security Council, coalition partners, FOMC voters, pivotal senators) from structure first (who can reach the threshold, who can veto, what the contract rule counts), with an LLM only filling in actor tables or playing electors; sell structurally dead longshots, price coalition ladders through their exact rule, price dissent and vote-count brackets. Unproven: nobody has a record against a price. On 20 resolved events the sub-10c names were roughly calibrated, so an unselected short basket earned about zero at the mid; the two big surprises were priced under 1c, and one was a leak.
- **Category**: Speculative-novel [modelling edge on selection, coalition and committee-vote markets]. An institutional twin, not polling (critic note: of the four entries seeded "simulated stakeholder polling", only file 51 is polling; this file and 65 model institutions).
- **Maturity**: Experimental, unproven on any market. Supporting evidence:
  - The best case for structure is Soda et al.'s conclave network, shared via Corriere della Sera on 7 May 2025, the day before the vote: Prevost 1st on status, 2nd on mediation, 8th on coalition building; Tagle led two of the three metrics; Polymarket had Prevost at 1.15% [1].
  - A rival structural model, a semantic-similarity simulation of the College, picked Parolin; its pre-selected candidate list left Prevost out, and a rerun with him placed him second only on one theme [2]. An agent-based model fits conclave durations, not winners [3].
  - Coalitions: Digital Pantheon fixes the real 2019 Flemish partners and simulates only who shapes the agreement text (retrospective, one case); it never predicts which coalition forms [5]. Coalition detection with LLMs plus game theory works in Diplomacy games, not elections [6].
  - Committees: FedSight scores 93.75% on 2023-24 FOMC meetings with no futures baseline or leakage control [9]; persona MEPs reach weighted F1 0.793 [12]; a congressional twin reports no accuracy [13]; the classical SCOTUS model reaches 70.2%, 2.7 points above a 10-year-memory null [14].
  - The "90% accurate" CIA rating of Bueno de Mesquita's model traces to his own Foreign Policy article [8]. Nobody is found trading these markets with simulations: GitHub has 2012-2017 BDM replications and one FOMC-tone nowcaster with placeholder calibration [17].
- **Requires AI?**: optional. LLMs read manifestos, speeches and red lines into actor tables (position, salience, clout, veto), and may play electors or members in straw polls. The network metrics, feasibility checks, bargaining solver, rule mapping and base rates are deterministic. The structure-only arm needs no LLM and cannot leak.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Targets: selection markets (Nobel, UN Secretary-General, TIME / FT Person of the Year), party-leader and PM ladders, coalition ladders, public-vote brackets (FOMC dissent counts, confirmation votes) and deadline deals [18].
  2. Rule compiler: electorate, threshold, veto players and contract mapping (the Swedish ladders count a party only if it "provides at least one cabinet minister" and resolve to the option that "most completely matches" [23]).
  3. Sub-variant A, structure prior and veto-dead flag: a multiplex network of the electors from public directories gives status, brokerage and coalition metrics [1]; flag every candidate who cannot reach the threshold under any bloc combination consistent with stated red lines or a veto. Flagged names are sold; central underdogs are bought only in token size.
  4. Sub-variant B, coalition bargaining: enumerate connected minimal-winning coalitions from seat scenarios, filter by red lines, order formateurs, simulate portfolio talks (manifesto-grounded agents [5] or a deterministic portfolio model) and map each cabinet to exactly one ladder option.
  5. Sub-variant C, the Bueno de Mesquita (BDM) hybrid: LLM analysts reading different source partitions fill the actor table; their disagreement is the input distribution for a transparent expected-utility solver (open-source replications [17]) run over 1,000 sampled tables.
  6. Sub-variant D, public-vote member twins: one agent per FOMC voter or pivotal senator, grounded in speeches and votes, simulated conditional on the decision the liquid market already prices, then shrunk to the base rate. Side use: simulated readers' disagreement after ECB pressers tracks OIS volatility (r ≈ 0.5, 293 events) [11].
  7. Sub-variant E (critic note), bilateral deadline bargaining (deals, strikes, shutdown ends, ceasefire extensions): the actor table and solver estimate whether a deal lands; when (hazard, deadline clustering) belongs to file 70.
  8. Execution: maker-only NO-side on flagged names (maker fee 0; taker 0.05·p(1−p) culture and economics, 0.04 politics, 0 geopolitics [25]), hard cap per event.
- **What creates the edge**: The mechanism: n=1 selection markets are priced on name recognition, while the structure (who can reach two-thirds, who is vetoed, whom the formateur must include) is public. Selection, not blanket selling, is the point: on Polymarket overall, sub-10c purchases lose 19.3c per dollar pooled and 6.3c per contract, but gain 4.1c per event [4] (file 24), so a blanket seller loses in exactly the events where a longshot hits. On coalition ladders, humans confuse support parties with cabinet members: under the same cabinet-minister rule the Greens (MP) trade at 63.5c in the Swedish party market against 54.7-57.9c implied by the coalition ladder, though the party market's earlier deadline should make it cheaper; at executable prices the gap closes (MP options cost 63.2c at the asks, 52.5c at the bids) [23], and rule-coherence trading is file 10's.

  The strongest counter-argument has four parts:
  - (a) The own baseline shows no blanket longshot bias to select from. On 20 resolved events, the sub-10c names were calibrated in aggregate (2 hits against 2.1-3.0 implied at each horizon), and an unselected short basket earned +0.1% per contract seven days out, at the mid [20].
  - (b) The surprises that mattered were informational, not structural. Machado sat at 0.9c a day before the 2025 Peace Prize, then went from 2.1c to 73c between 21:00 and 00:00 UTC, nine hours before the announcement; the Nobel Institute said it had been "prey to a criminal actor" [16][20]. Such leakers buy from exactly the resting longshot sellers this strategy runs (file 30). TIME's 2024 and 2025 favourites reached 98-99c the evening (UTC) before the reveal day, and a $1.86M "will it leak" market resolved Yes on its own price rule [20][22] (press report or private information: not checked).
  - (c) Structure also misses. Prevost was one of three structurally central names, and the other two (Parolin 25.5%, Tagle 19.65%) lost [1]; a second structural model picked Parolin because its candidate list omitted Prevost [2]. Unlisted names win too: the 2024 Literature ladder resolved "Other" [20]. The published ranking did not move Prevost (1.3c on 7 May, 0.2-2.1c through the vote) [20].
  - (d) Capacity on the public-vote side is tiny. The October dissent ladder has $7.7k of volume against $16.05M on the decision, and brackets 1 and 2 have $0 within 5c [18][19]. LLM team simulations show "farcical harmony" [E8], which biases twins against dissent. Aaru says it cannot simulate Powell [E8].
- **Capital required**: <$1k for the retro-test; $2-10k to trade, capacity-bound. Sub-10c names hold a median of about $150 within 5c of mid (range $0-1,028) [19]. Capital locks for weeks to months (Nobel ladders list months ahead; coalition ladders run until a cabinet takes office).
- **Technical requirements**: Built on file 21's stack (dated retrieval, calibration ledger, trading layer). The agent design sketch:
  1. Rule compiler per market, handling "Other" and composite outcomes (TIME 2025 resolved to "Architects of AI / Other" [20]).
  2. Structure layer, no LLM: rosters, network metrics [1], bloc-feasibility check and "dead" flag, coalition enumerator.
  3. Elicitation: two model families × three source-partitioned analysts fill the actor table with citations (manifesto corpora such as POLCA [7]); ~6 calls × 22k ≈ 130k tokens, $0.20-0.40 per event at file 21's Haiku 4.5 ($1/$5) and Sonnet 5 ($2/$10) prices. No agent sees the price.
  4. Dynamics: the deterministic solver costs next to nothing. Optional straw polls (encourage / discourage / veto ballots), GPT-6 Luna to Haiku 4.5: Security Council 15 × 6 polls × 50 seeds ≈ 4,500 calls, ~18M tokens, $2-25 per event; conclave 133 × 5 × 20 ≈ 13,300 calls, $8-80; FOMC 12 × 3 × 50, ~15M tokens, $2-20; batch halves these. Estimates, not measurements.
  5. Aggregation: pre-registered structure-only and structure + role-play outputs, each blended with the mid in logit space at a frozen weight; a per-candidate ceiling such as P ≤ 0.5%.
  6. Trading: NO-side resting orders on flagged names only, 2-5% of capital per event; cancel all resting longshot sells 24 hours before a scheduled announcement or on a ≥ 3c move in an hour (the leak rule).

  Build time, solo: 1-2 weeks per body for the structure layer; 4-8 weeks for all three families (each needs its own data).
- **Opportunity size**: Mid-size in volume, small in sellable edge.
  - Live selection: Nobel Peace 2026 $25.1M volume, $1.98M liquidity, 71 names (30 below 10c); UNSG $206k; TIME 2026 $89k volume, $555k liquidity (23 of 25 names below 10c) [18].
  - Live leader, coalition and deal markets: Sweden PM $9.5M, Romania PM $4.1M, Swedish coalition $38k (21 options), Swedish parties $206k; US-Iran final deal $19.1M, Israel-Iran ceasefire-continues $31.8M [18].
  - Public votes: named-senator ladders exist ("Which Senators will vote for the Clarity Act?" $210k), but no SEC / FCC / FTC commission-vote or FDA advisory-panel vote market was found, only FDA approval outcomes (critic note) [18].
  - Dissent markets: $2.6M across 10 meetings, falling to $61-82k a meeting since June, against $144-284M on each decision market [21]. Kalshi, for US persons, is larger: 281k-487k contracts per dissent-count event, 93k on "who will dissent", 1.26M on Nobel Peace 2026 and 91k on a confirmation vote count [24].
  - Sellable pool: sub-10c names averaged 1.3c seven days out [20], so perfect selection earns ~1-3.5c per contract per event on a few hundred dollars per name (derived); the 20 resolved events here traded $457M, $188M of it the 2025 Speaker vote [20].
- **Pros**: Recurring calendar (Nobel prizes and TIME / FT every year, eight FOMC meetings, a coalition ladder after each election). Structure data is free, and the structure-only arm is immune to training leakage. Maker execution costs no fee. Resolution is mostly clean: none of the 517 sampled markets shows a dispute [20] (the side market on the TIME leak was disputed twice [22]). The leak rule doubles as a defence for any longshot book (file 30).
- **Cons / risks**: (a)-(d) above. One hit costs ~30 times the premium on a 3c name and arrives through leaks you cannot see [16][22]. Every 2023-2025 winner is in training data, so LLM arms need masked names. Left-leaning personas [12], harmony instead of dissent [E8], refusals to role-play named officials; roster turnover; months of lock-up on coalition ladders.
- **Expected results**: no public data, theoretical only. No structural or committee model has been scored against a price [1][9][10].

  Measured here, with no AI: seven days out, shorting every sub-10c name at the mid returned +0.1% per contract (95% CI −1.2% to +1.6%) and +1.0% per event (−0.2% to +2.2%). The 1-10c band earned +3.6% at 7 days only because both hits (Machado, Prevost) sat below 1c, where the premium is under 1c. At 30 days the same band earned +0.2% (−5.4% to +4.4%) with 2 hits against 2.07 implied [20]. These are mid prices: the average name was 1.3c, so a 0.2c half-spread or an adverse-selected maker fill turns the basket to about −0.1% per contract (derived). Adding the excluded $40.9M "Next Chancellor of Germany" event moves none of these figures by more than 0.07 points (verifier check).

  Guess: a structure filter adds 0 to +1.5c per flagged contract. That is −$50 to +$30 a month on $1-5k before LLM costs and after one leak-driven hit a year.
- **Competitive landscape**: No simulator trader found [17]. Prices come from name-recognition retail, bookmakers on Nobel, pope and TIME (odds not checked here), late leakers (file 30), political traders on coalition ladders and Kalshi makers on dissents [24]. Nobody publishes a masked retro-test ledger.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: grey. Trading your own model from one identity is allowed [E9]. The live risk is non-public information: committee decisions taken days early (the Nobel committee had decided "days before" [16]), embargoed TIME covers, blackout-period Fed leaks and staff whip counts are material non-public information; ToS §4.2 has three insider prongs, and the CFTC settled a Kalshi mention-market case with a teleprompter operator [E9] (file 30). Never solicit committee members, electors or staff. Election and government contracts are restricted in 30+ jurisdictions and US persons are excluded from the international venue [E9] (Kalshi lists most of these families for US persons [24]). Short-longshot books need hard per-event caps; directory-site scraping terms were not checked.
- **How you'd validate it**:
  - **Phase 0 (done, $0):** the baseline above [20][21].
  - **Phase 1, masked retro-test, 3-4 weeks, ~$300-1,000 of LLM spend.**
    - Universe: the 20 resolved Polymarket events here, Kalshi's resolved Nobel (all categories), TIME, FT, pope and coalition events as extra scoring targets [24], and pre-2023 conclaves and coalitions for the structure-only arm.
    - Leakage control: names replaced by IDs; structural data dated before the event; a recall probe that drops any event whose masked winner a model can name. The structure-only arm is unaffected. The baseline above anchored horizons on the first hour the winner held ≥ 90c, an outcome-defined clock; Phase 1 anchors on the scheduled announcement or a fixed calendar time instead, and scores "Other" / unlisted outcomes as live candidates.
    - Five pre-registered arms: (1) structure-only flag; (2) structure + LLM role-play; (3) market mid at the same timestamp (CLOB `/prices-history`, only if the market was open then); (4) file 21's plain forecaster; (5) the unselected sub-10c short basket.
    - Metrics: flagged vs unflagged hit rate (Fisher exact); per-event basket return with event-clustered bootstrap; winner log loss against the mid.
    - Power: sub-10c names averaged 1.3c at 7 days [20], so showing a flagged set hits less often than priced needs ~230 flagged names with zero hits (0.987^230 < 0.05), ~40 events if half of each event's ~11 longshots are flagged. The per-event SD of the short basket (2.4 points at 7 days; 9.6 in the 1-10c band at 30 days; both understated with 2 hits) gives 45-80 events for a 1-3 point per-event difference (derived; general bar ~350 binary predictions for a 0.02 edge [15]). Polymarket supplies ~8 such events a year, so Phase 1 can kill the idea but not confirm it.
    - Kill (the pre-registered null): "structure-flagged basket = unselected FLB basket". Keep it if the flagged names hit at under a third of the unflagged rate with p < 0.05.
  - **Phase 2, forward, 12 months, $50-150 a month:** frozen pipeline, paper trades at recorded books on Nobel Peace 2026 (30 sub-10c names today), Nobel Literature, UNSG straw polls, TIME and FT 2026 and the Swedish, Romanian and Israeli coalition ladders [18]; live money only if the flagged basket beats the unselected one after the leak rule and a hit.
  - **FOMC twins:** the market already beats a leave-future-out base rate by 0.20-0.27 multi-bracket Brier (per-meeting SD ~0.5) [21]. Detecting a further 0.10 needs ~200 meetings (25 years, derived). Pool Kalshi's "who will dissent" names (12 per meeting) with legislative vote-count brackets, or drop it.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive (hours to days) for the signal: structure changes slowly and a straw-poll batch takes an hour. The resting book is execution-sensitive in the last 24 hours before a scheduled announcement, when leakers lift longshot offers.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Gamma events; ladders list weeks to months ahead [18] | little lost: prices of dead names barely move | weekly scan |
| Fair value | structure build, elicitation, solver | a stale roster or red line flags the wrong name | refresh on roster or seat news |
| Entry | sub-10c spreads 0.1-5c; ~$150 median within 5c [19] | crossing costs 0.5-2.5c against a 1-3.5c ceiling | maker NO bids only |
| Leak defence | minute-level price watch | Machado went 3.7c → 39c in one hour, ten hours early [20] | cancel on a ≥ 3c hourly move and 24 hours before announcements |
| Settlement | announcement, UMA proposal | coalition ladders lock capital for months | hold to resolution |

- **How speed changes the result**:
  1. The informed move comes hours before announcements: 9 hours early for Machado, the evening (UTC) before the reveal day for TIME 2024 and 2025 [20]. Resting sells left in place pay it. The 24-hour pre-announcement cancel would have avoided the Machado move; a ≥ 3c-per-hour trigger fires only after the 2-4c asks are lifted (2.1c → 3.7c → 39c on the hourly path), so a relative trigger (price doubling) is the safer second rule (derived).
  2. The market converges only at the end. The Fed-chair confirmation vote-count bracket that won was 0.6c at 30 days and 41c at 7 days [20], so structure is worth most early, when depth is thinnest.
  3. Rate limits and uptime do not matter at a few orders a day.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted (not the US, UK or France [E9]). A small VPS and a price watcher are enough, with no co-location.

### Results by investment size
Assumptions: maker NO-side on flagged 1-10c names in 8-15 events a year, $30-300 per name near the touch [19]; average premium 3c; zero maker fee [25]; lock-up 1-3 months; captured edge −1c to +1.5c per contract (guess); one leak-driven hit a year; straw polls $10-100 per event and FOMC twins $2-20 a meeting (derived); VPS $10-65. Every net figure is a guess.

| Capital | Workable? | How it is deployed | Gross / month | Running costs / month | Net / month | Net / capital / month | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1k | as a forward test | 15-30 flagged names × $30-60 | −$10 to +$15 | $10-40 | −$50 to +$5 | −5% to +0.5% | one hit wipes months |
| $5k | marginal | 30-60 names, 4-6 events, $80-150 each | −$50 to +$75 | $30-150 | −$200 to +$45 | −4% to +1% | depth per name, leaks |

- **Minimum sensible capital**: $0 until Phase 1 passes (paper only), then ~$2-3k so that per-event caps still allow a name per event.
- **Capacity ceiling**: ~$5-10k deployed (guess): about 30 sub-10c Nobel names × ~$150-300 within 5c, plus small UNSG, TIME and coalition books [19].
