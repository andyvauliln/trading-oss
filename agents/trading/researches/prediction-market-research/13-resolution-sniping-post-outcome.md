# 13 - Resolution sniping: outcome known, oracle not yet settled (post-outcome bonding and certainty harvesting)
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: A hard data (on-chain papers, official APIs, own live queries) for mechanics, timing and the size of the prize; C self-reported for per-bot economics · Last verified: 2026-09-28_

### Resolution sniping: outcome known, oracle not yet settled
- **One-line summary**: In the window between a knowable real-world result and on-chain settlement, rest bids at 0.99-0.999 (or lift the last stale asks) on the winning outcome, redeem or resell at par, and recycle the capital; the whole business is queue position, breadth and avoiding the rare flip, because the gross edge is 10-100 basis points per cycle.
- **Category**: Arbitrage [carry / tail-risk underwriting: the break-even flip probability equals the discount you buy at, so it is a short volatility position, not a locked spread]
- **Maturity**: Established, thin and shrinking. It is demonstrably run: the paper that defines "bonding trades" (bids for the winning claim at 0.999 with no asks below par after the outcome is known) finds the top-100 providers earn a median 6.22% APY, the single best address made $23,616 over 34,994 trades and the top ten together $113,309 [4]; a 1,000-profile study calls "Certainty Harvester" the most prevalent pattern among profitable wallets [23]; an HN observer found "the most consistent Polymarket winner I saw was placing 95%+ odds many, many times a day" [25]. Live on 2026-09-28 the 0.999 bid on a football match that had just finished grew from 2,649 shares to 25,049 in six minutes and 87,978 in twelve, while no proposal had been posted [11][9]; the top holders of the winning token had bought at average prices of 0.92-0.98 before the result [14]. Decayed where resolution is automatic: crypto up/down markets settle on a Chainlink TWAP with no proposal window (32 up/down windows closed this weekend resolved a median 0.9 minutes after their end time [9]), TradFi price markets settle on Pyth, and Polymarket US settles by exchange determination [E3]. The crypto daily and weekly price-threshold ladders are the exception: they settle on a Binance print but still pass through a UMA proposal with a $250 bond, and at 16:04 UTC a daily BTC market whose 12:00 ET fixing had passed showed `proposed` with 175,543 shares bid at 0.999 and no asks [9][11]. What remains is sports, weather, culture, politics, geopolitics and those crypto ladders; the political and geopolitical markets carry the dispute risk.
- **Requires AI?**: no. Optional for (a) reading resolution text to classify markets by "decidable from a primary feed" vs "needs interpretation" (postponement clauses, source hierarchies, snapshot times), and (b) parsing unstructured sources (court dockets, agency PDFs) into a resolved / not-resolved flag. Queue management, sizing and redemption are deterministic.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Enumerate markets whose event has ended or is about to: Gamma `endDate` and `gameStartTime` (note `endDate` equals kickoff for NFL game markets but kickoff + 6 h for esports, so it is never the event end [10]), sports WebSocket game state, the `market_resolved` WS event, and the Data API `GET /v2/resolutions?condition=…` state machine, whose `status` runs initialized → posed → proposed → challenged → reproposed → disputed → resolved with `was_disputed`, `extended_review`, `resolved_at` and `payouts` fields [9][16].
  2. Confirm the outcome from a primary feed faster than the crowd (final score, station reading, official publication), then verify the market's rules turn that fact into the payout (a postponed game "will remain open until the game has been completed"; a cancelled game pays 50-50 [10]).
  3. Rest bids on the winning token at 0.999 (tick is 0.001 above 0.96 [E1]) and deeper at 0.99-0.995; takers who want cash now sell into them. Makers pay no fee and earn the rebate share of the seller's tiny fee [19]. Lifting a resting ask at 0.99-0.999 instead costs the taker fee `feeRate × p × (1-p)`: 0.025c per share at 0.995 in a 0.05 category, i.e. ~5% of a 0.5c edge; zero on geopolitics markets and on NFL game markets on the day their game is played, when Polymarket switches them to `zero_fees` (the flag is set hours before kickoff: file 28 saw it at least 4 h before a 00:15 UTC start) (the 412 `zero_fees` markets in the 27 Sep sample were that Sunday's games; NFL games days out and CFB lines showed 0.05: files 18 and 19) [19][10].
  4. Wait for the oracle: a whitelisted proposer posts the answer with a $750 (sports and crypto-price markets $250, high-value $25,000) pUSD bond, the 2-hour challenge window runs (+1 h on neg-risk markets between `reportPayouts` and `resolveQuestion`), the adapter resolves, and you redeem [1][2][20][10]. Median time from protocol finality to the first redemption on a condition is 182 s (200 s for positive payouts) across 92,158 redeemed conditions [5].
  5. Or do not wait: resell at 0.999 to the next bidder to free capital immediately, forgoing the last 0.1c. Dump the losing side at 0.001-0.005 to clear dust [23].
  6. If `status` becomes `challenged` or `disputed`, stop bidding: capital is now locked for 4-6 days and the price becomes a bet on the UMA vote, which is file 15's subject [1][2]. Note that `challenged` also persists for months on markets reopened after a too-early proposal (every active "disputed" market on 28 Sep was such a reopening, none in a live vote: file 15), so the same rule, do not bid 0.999 there, applies for a different reason.
- **What creates the edge**: Time preference and operational friction, not information. Winners sell certainty at 0.999 because their capital is worth more than 0.1c per share in the next 2-4 hours; retail sells at 0.99-0.997 to be done; the buyer supplies patience and a redemption pipeline. The paper's settlement-wedge frontier runs from 3.06% annualised at the minimum to 6.89% at the 0.5th percentile, and the discount correlates with Aave lending rates (r ≈ 0.365), so it is a priced lock-up cost; at a two-hour horizon that wedge is a fraction of a basis point, which is why the observed bid sits at the 0.999 tick floor rather than at a computed discount [4]. On the other side of your fill are also informed sellers: in contested markets the 0.998 seller may know how the vote will go [E3], and in the pre-TWAP five-minute crypto era "a bet the market treated as near-certain was overturned one time in three" in manipulated windows [26][27]. Across 184,148 oracle requests, 1,604 (0.87%) were disputed and 1,478 reset, and 83% of clarifications arrive after the request exists but before the first proposal, i.e. inside your holding window [6]. The counterparty keeps paying because 0.1c on a $50 clip is beneath their attention: taker-sell clips at ≥ 0.99 on nine markets that ended this weekend had a median size of 13 shares [12].
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k to run; $10k+ to matter. Each fill locks ~$1 per share for the proposal-plus-liveness window (measured this weekend: soccer markets resolved a median 243 min after kickoff, NFL 259 min, esports 197 min, i.e. roughly 1-2 h after the final whistle [9]) or for 4-6 days if disputed. Bids are two-sided only in the sense that you want breadth: 2,100+ markets closed with a 27 September end date, $78M of volume, 412 of them NFL game markets worth $60.6M [10]; on a weekday the set is a fraction of that. Minimum order 5 shares and $1 notional [E1]; a bot below ~$3k cannot hold queue position in more than a handful of markets at once.
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: Gamma keyset pagination for ending markets, sports WS for game state, `/v2/resolutions` poller (batch up to 20 conditions per call), CLOB market WS (`book`, `tick_size_change`, `market_resolved`), user WS for fills, an outcome feed per category (sports score API, weather station pages, official publication watchers), Polygon logs on the adapters and the ManagedOptimisticOracleV2 for proposal and dispute events if you want them seconds before the Data API. (2) Decision logic: a rules classifier (decidable now? postponement? source hierarchy? snapshot time?), a per-category flip-probability table that sets the maximum bid (0.999 needs a flip rate under 0.1%), queue-share estimator from displayed depth at each tick, capital allocator across simultaneous windows. (3) Execution: post-only GTC bids at 0.999/0.995/0.99, cancel-replace on `tick_size_change`, FAK for lifting stale asks in the first seconds after a print (sports markets carry a 1-second `secondsDelay` on marketable orders during which they cannot be cancelled [10][18]), redemption through the gasless relayer (Builder credentials) or own POL on an EOA, batched per condition because redeem burns the whole balance [E1]. (4) Risk: pull all bids on `challenged`, cap exposure per event complex (Iran-type chains dispute together [E3]), reconcile MATCHED vs CONFIRMED because matched trades can still `FAILED` on-chain [18]. (5) Test: replay `/trades` and `/v2/resolutions` for 30 days of resolved markets. Build: 2-3 weeks for a team that already has a CLOB stack; 6-8 weeks from scratch.
- **Opportunity size**: Small, wide and measurable. Breadth: 2,100+ markets with a 27 September end date closed (a Sunday; Gamma's offset cap stopped the count at 2,100), 1,484 of them `sports_fees_v3`, 412 `zero_fees` NFL, 187 `sports_fees_v2` futures, 7 politics, 7 tech [10]. Depth of the supply: across nine markets that ended in the last two days (weather brackets and one football match), takers sold 9,473 shares into bids at ≥ 0.99 in 173 clips from 102 wallets over 12-58 h windows, i.e. ~$9.5k of notional and ~$10-50 of gross edge in total at 0.1-0.5c per share [12]. Depth of the demand: 25,049 shares bid at 0.999 plus 79,910 at 0.99-0.995 on one just-finished match; 18,457 at 0.999 on a Hong Kong temperature bracket; 10,311 at 0.999 on an O/U line [11]. Historical prize: top-100 bonding providers median 6.22% APY (mean 29.08%), median hold 1-2 h, best address $23,616 lifetime, top ten $113,309 [4]; purchases at ≥ 0.90 across 588M trades earned +0.83c per dollar, and habitual favourite buyers earned less than others [7]. Direction: shrinking. Crypto up/down and TradFi markets have no window (Chainlink TWAP since 2026-08-07; up/down windows resolved within a minute this weekend [17][9]), and the crypto threshold ladders that keep a UMA window carry the deepest 0.999 queue of all (175,543 shares on one daily BTC market [11]); whitelisting cut incorrect proposals 59% and disputes 68% (UMA's own figures) so fewer "too early" reopenings, but also faster, more professional proposals [3]; the 0.999 queue is where everyone now sits.
- **Pros**: Fee-light (makers pay nothing; takers 4-7% of the edge at the extremes [19][E3]); short holding periods (hours) and same-day capital recycling; a public, machine-readable state machine (`/v2/resolutions`) and on-chain ground truth for every competitor's fills; stackable with the maker rebate and with an existing LP or sports stack; no directional model needed; scale-free breadth across thousands of small markets.
- **Cons / risks**: Execution: you are last in a 25,000-share queue at 0.999 unless you were early, and the supply is a few hundred to a few thousand shares per market [11][12]; matched trades can revert (1.8M reverted transactions touching Polymarket contracts in nine months to May 2026 [28]); redeem is all-or-nothing per condition and costs gas outside the relayer. Platform: `endDate` semantics differ by sport [10]; the docs still say "Anyone can propose" although only whitelisted addresses have proposed since UMIP-189 [1][3][E3]; the adapter admin can `flag` and resolve manually after a 1-hour safety period [20][E3]. Capital: a dispute locks the position 4-6 days (docs) and geopolitics p99 is 14 days (commercial study) [1][8]; a "too early" verdict reopens the market and your 0.999 fill is now a live position [1]. Edge decay: the window is gone in crypto, TradFi and on the US venue [E3]; the queue at 0.999 compresses the residual to 0.1c. Tail: at 0.99 a 1% flip breaks even; category dispute rates run 0.2% (NBA) to 4.8% (geopolitics) [8], and 43% of disputes come from the wording of the resolution string [8]. Regulatory: none for trading public information; the E9 table marks resolution sniping "Allowed" on both venues [E9].
- **Expected results**: Hard data, whole population: top-100 bonding providers median 6.22% APY, mean 29.08%; best address $23,616 over 34,994 trades (~$0.67 per trade); top ten $113,309 combined, over data to 2025-12-31 [4]. Purchases at ≥ 0.90 return +0.83c per dollar pooled; habitual favourite buyers (top decile, 15.1% of high-price purchases) earn less [7]. Own samples: on nine markets that ended over the weekend the four most frequent buyers at ≥ 0.99 were small accounts (7,289 lifetime trades with realised PnL -$1,660; 7,350 trades, +$754; 4,555 trades, +$61 on $31k of volume; 1,051 trades, +$5,950), but on the Wuhan temperature ladder of 28 September they were professional-scale: 22,896 to 75,499 lifetime trades with realised PnL of +$25.8k, +$48.2k, +$103.1k and +$490.8k [13]. Self-reported: a 0.88-average-entry "hits 0.98" bot showed +$84 on its tracker and +$11 on-chain after 2.8-3.4% slippage in thin books and ~$0.15 gas on a $4.80 redemption; 91% paper win rate became 84% live [22]; last-60-second entries at 0.80-0.99 in 15-minute crypto markets lost 37.81% [24] (pre-outcome, documented loser, file 12). Guess for a solo bot restricted to sports and weather, bidding 0.99-0.999 post-whistle with 30-60% of capital deployed on average and earning the paper's median-to-mean band (6.22-29.08% APY) on the deployed part: 0.15-1.5% per month on capital, i.e. $15-145 gross per month at $10k, before one dispute; a newcomer behind the queue should expect the bottom of that band.
- **Competitive landscape**: Crowded at the touch: the 0.999 bid on a finished match held 25,049 shares within six minutes of the end [11]; bursts of identical-size buys at 0.9989999 from five to seven different wallets within 20 seconds (Wuhan, Singapore temperature brackets) show copy-trading products or wallet clusters hunting the same 0.1c, alongside accounts with 75,000 lifetime trades and $490k of realised profit [12][13]. Whitelisted proposers (177 addresses by November 2025 [3], 339 on the default whitelist contract on 28 Sep 2026 per file 16's on-chain read; ≥ 5 proposals at ≥ 95% accuracy over six months [3]) see the answer before they post it; the initial 37 already made 96% of all proposals before the whitelist existed [E3]. Sports makers pull quotes at the whistle, so the residual asks at 0.95-0.99 disappear within seconds (file 18). Competitive means: breadth (hundreds of markets per weekend), being in the queue before the print, a rules classifier that keeps you out of the 4.8%-dispute categories, and a redemption pipeline that recycles capital in minutes.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none. Trading public information is Allowed on both venues (E9 traffic-light table: "Resolution sniping on public information: Allowed / Allowed") [E9]. Grey: proposing, disputing or voting on markets where you hold the position (E9: "UMA voting while holding a position: Grey"); disputing correct proposals to delay settlement is treated as manipulation and is visible on-chain [E3][E9]. Data-licence clause: an incorporated proprietary trading firm using the API or on-chain data arguably needs a written data agreement [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: Two weekends read-only. Record L2 books on every market whose game state hits "final" (sports WS) or whose station reading is published, from the print until `resolved_at`; join `/trades` (taker side) and `/v2/resolutions`. Compute, per market, the taker-sell volume at each tick ≥ 0.99, the queue ahead of a bid placed at print + 5 s, and the resulting fill share; multiply by the tick edge; subtract a flip charge from `was_disputed` rates by category over the last 10,000 resolved conditions. Metric: net edge per deployed dollar-day. Pass: ≥ 0.02% per day (~7% annualised, the paper's median) on ≥ 200 simulated fills with no category above a 0.5% dispute rate. Fail: fill share at 0.999 under 5% in the markets that carry the volume. Cost: ~20 engineering hours; all data is free; no capital at risk.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: execution-sensitive. Queue position at 0.999 and breadth decide PnL; the oracle takes hours. The one latency-sensitive moment is the second after the outcome prints, when stale asks at 0.95-0.99 still rest and every sports bot is lifting them; that race is file 18 (in-play feed latency) and file 22 (station readings), not this file.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outcome detection | sports WS game state, score APIs, station pages, official publications | you bid after the queue has formed; the 0.999 bid grew 2,649 → 25,049 shares in six minutes [11] | sub-minute on sports and weather via free feeds |
| Lifting stale asks at the print | FAK orders; 1 s `secondsDelay` on live sports markets, non-cancellable [10][18] | asks at 0.95-0.99 are gone within seconds (file 18); a second late means paying 0.999 | do not compete unless you already run file 18's feed |
| Queue position at 0.999 | post-only bid placed before the crowd, cancel-replace on `tick_size_change` | fill share falls with your position in a 25k queue against a few hundred shares of flow [11][12] | be in the queue at print + 5 s; accept 0.995 for size |
| Proposal and dispute detection | `/v2/resolutions` polling (20 conditions per call) or adapter logs | bidding into a `challenged` market locks capital 4-6 days [1] | poll every 30-60 s; cancel-all on `challenged` |
| Settlement and redemption | `resolved_at`, redeem tx, relayer 25 req/min [E1] | median 182 s to first redemption [5]; late redeemers just idle capital | batch redeem per condition within minutes |
| Capital recycling | resell at 0.999 vs wait | reselling forgoes 0.1c; waiting costs 1-2 h post-whistle (soccer median 243 min from kickoff) [9] | resell only when a new window needs the cash |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Queue formation is minutes, not milliseconds: six minutes after the match ended, ~105,000 shares of bids sat at 0.99-0.999 [11]; being an hour late costs nothing further, being ten minutes late costs most of the fill share. (2) The oracle path is hours: soccer markets resolved a median 243 min after kickoff (IQR 236-247), NFL 259 min (221-316), esports 197 min (146-266) [9]; crypto 1 min [9]. Latency inside those hours is irrelevant. (3) The only sub-second race is against file 18's bots in the seconds after a print; a 500 ms feed lost that race in earnings markets [E1]. (4) Venue mechanics: sports marketable orders wait 1 s and cannot be cancelled [10][18]; standard tier allows 40 orders/s, enough for hundreds of resting bids [E1].
- **Where to run it**: any unrestricted small VPS for the bonding queue and redemption loop; eu-west-1 only if you also chase the print second. Not worth paying for co-location.

### What you are probably underestimating
1. **The competition is the queue, not the outcome.** Six minutes after a football match ended, the 0.999 bid held 25,049 shares (2,649 at the final whistle) with 30,000 at 0.995, 20,000 at 0.993 and 29,910 at 0.99; six minutes later the deeper bids had been pulled up to the touch and 87,978 shares sat at 0.999 [11]; the taker-sell flow into bids at ≥ 0.99 across nine markets that ended this weekend was 9,473 shares in 173 clips, median clip 13 shares, from 102 wallets [12]. A bot that arrives at print + 10 min is behind ~$100k of bids waiting for ~$1k of sellers. Price-time priority is not documented for the international CLOB [E1], so measure your own fill share rather than assume it.
2. **The window has been removed where the volume was, and it survives where the queue is deepest.** Crypto up/down markets settle on a Chainlink TWAP (5-minute: 60 s window since 2026-08-14) [17]; the 32 up/down windows in this weekend's closed sample resolved a median 0.9 minutes after their end time [9]. TradFi settles on Pyth and Polymarket US by exchange determination [E3]. The daily and weekly crypto price-threshold ladders still go through UMA (bond $250, reward $0.6) after a public Binance fixing, so the outcome is knowable to the cent two hours before settlement; that is why a daily BTC market showed 175,543 shares bid at 0.999 four minutes after its 12:00 ET fixing [9][11]. What is left for a small bot is sports, weather, culture, politics and geopolitics: 1,484 + 412 + 187 sports markets against 7 politics and 7 tech markets closed on 27 September [10].
3. **The oracle path is measurable per category and it is not "2 hours".** From kickoff to `resolved_at`: soccer median 243 min (IQR 236-247, n = 44), NFL 259 min (221-316, n = 107), esports 197 min (146-266, n = 32), other sports 246 min [9]. NFL's gap after a ~3 h 10 min game is roughly an hour, soccer's after ~115 min is two, which suggests a shorter liveness or a faster path on NFL requests (unverified; the sports oracle README does not state its liveness [21]). Neg-risk markets add a fixed hour between `reportPayouts` and `resolveQuestion` [20]; a whitelisted proposal that runs past its expiry shows `extended_review: true` [16]. Weather brackets ending at 12:00 UTC were still `posed` (no proposal) at 15:48 UTC [9]; the resolution source publishes the daily extract later, so the window there is set by the station, not the oracle.
4. **Use the state machine, not `endDate`.** `/v2/resolutions` (Data API v2, 2026-09-04) exposes initialized / posed / proposed / challenged / reproposed / disputed / resolved plus `was_disputed`, `was_arbitrated`, `extended_review`, `reporter` (UMA_OO, CHAINLINK, EOA), `payouts` in micro-USDC and `resolved_at` [16][17]; the sentinel `69` means "no proposal yet" [16]. Gamma's `endDate` is kickoff for NFL, kickoff + 6 h for esports, and a nominal noon for weather [10]; `umaResolutionStatus` on Gamma was null on every sports and weather market checked but read `proposed` on a UMA-routed daily crypto market [10]. Gamma's `resolvedBy` shows two undocumented adapter addresses (`0x65070BE9…` on binary markets, `0x69c47De9…` only on `negRisk: true` markets in 200 closed markets), neither of which is on the resolution docs page, which still lists UmaCtfAdapter v3.0 `0x157Ce2d6…` [10][29].
5. **Break-even flip probability equals your discount, and 0.999 is below every category's dispute rate.** At 0.999 you can afford a 0.1% flip; measured dispute rates are 0.2% (NBA), 0.3% (soccer), 0.6% (crypto), 0.9% (weather), 1.6% (politics horse-race), 3.4% (policy), 4.8% (geopolitics) (commercial study, 18,427 markets, use for ranking) [8]; "too early" and reset outcomes reopen the market rather than pay [1]. Disputes are 43% wording, 22% source conflict, 14% late reversal [8]. The only defensible 0.999 bids are on outcomes whose flip probability given a verified primary feed is far below the category dispute rate (a 4-0 football score, a published station reading); everything else needs 0.99 or lower, which the queue rarely leaves for you.
6. **Cancelled games pay 50c and nobody hurries to say so.** Orioles at Yankees on 27 September was cancelled (ESPN scoreboard) [15]; 23 hours later the spread market was still `posed` with 4,431 shares bid at 0.49 and asks at 0.51 [9][11]. The rules pay 50-50 only if the game is "canceled entirely, with no make-up game" and keep a postponed game's market open [10], so the 2% to par is a bet on the schedule and on a proposer bothering; a Reddit-era claim that Polymarket clarifications are never overturned was disproved in March 2025 [E3]. Treat it as a trap unless the league schedule confirms no make-up date exists; at 16:09 UTC the same books still showed 0.49 bid / 0.51 ask [11].
7. **Matched is not filled, and filled is not redeemed.** A trade goes MATCHED → MINED → CONFIRMED or RETRYING → FAILED [18]; 1.8M reverted transactions touched Polymarket contracts between 2025-08-12 and 2026-05-22 as adversaries invalidated unfavourable fills after seeing outcomes [28]. Redeem burns the whole balance per condition and, on an EOA, costs ~$0.15 of gas that was 3.1% of a $4.80 position in one bot's reconciliation [22][E1]; the median first redemption arrives 182 s after finality, so late redeemers are not earning, they are idling [5].
8. **Your paper PnL is not your chain PnL.** The one published reconciliation found +$84 on the tracker versus +$11 on-chain: unrealised gains counted early, fees compounding, 2.8-3.4% live slippage against a 1.5% assumption, and a win rate that fell from 91% to 84% because fast-resolving markets were over-counted [22]. Reconcile against `/activity` REDEEM rows (per outcome since 2026-08-10 [17]) and `/v2/user-pnl`, not against your order log.
9. **The wallets you are racing range from dust clusters to professional books.** On nine weekend markets the four most frequent ≥ 0.99 buyers had 1,051-7,350 lifetime trades and realised PnL between -$1,660 and +$5,950, but on the 28 September Wuhan ladder they had 22,896-75,499 trades and +$25.8k to +$490.8k of realised profit [13]; seven wallets bought 29 shares each at 0.9989999 within 20 seconds on one bracket and five bought 8 shares each within 12 seconds on another [12]. Copy-trading products and multi-wallet clusters compete for the same 0.1c and add to the queue without adding supply, and the accounts with six-figure realised PnL are the ones already at the front of it.
10. **Fees are near zero at the extremes but the category matters.** Taker fee as a share of the gross edge is `feeRate × p`: 5% in sports, weather and culture, 4% in politics, 7% in crypto, 0% in geopolitics and on game-day `zero_fees` NFL markets, which also pay no maker rebate [19][10][E3]. Geopolitics is simultaneously fee-free and the worst tail (4.8% disputes, p99 14 days) [8]. Makers pay nothing, so the whole style belongs to resting bids, and the rebate on a 0.999 fill is a rounding error.
