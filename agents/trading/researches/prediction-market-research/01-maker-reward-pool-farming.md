# 01 - Maker-side reward-pool farming (liquidity rewards, sponsored pools, event programmes, Polymarket US LIP)
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: A hard data (on-chain payouts, official docs, public APIs) for size and distribution; C self-reported for per-bot economics · Last verified: 2026-09-22 (speed, investment-size and services sections added 2026-09-24)_

### Maker-side reward-pool farming
- **One-line summary**: Rest two-sided quotes inside each market's reward band so that the subsidy (Polymarket's liquidity-rewards pools, user-sponsored pools, time-boxed event programmes, and the Polymarket US Liquidity Incentive Program) is the primary income, sized by your achievable share of a pool's score rather than by pool size.
- **Category**: Incentive farming [market-making mechanics, subsidy economics]
- **Maturity**: Established for a handful of automated wallets; decayed for the 2024-style newcomer; Emerging on the US venue (no realised payout data). Proof: the top three lifetime recipients hold $1.23M, $1.13M and $0.63M of `reward_income` [13] (PolyScalping's on-chain tally runs 2-9% higher [15], consistent with the Data API lag in point 4 below); two of them earned $229k and $281k of rewards in the last 90 days against 2026 year-to-date trading PnL of -$40k and +$75k, so the subsidy is the business [13]. Against that: daily payouts are $108k-$170k across ~2,500 wallets, the median recipient gets ~$5 [17], and the 2024-era "$700-800/day on $10k" [10] has no verifiable 2026 analogue.
- **Requires AI?**: no. Optional for two sub-tasks: (a) classifying markets by jump risk before a quoting decision (news-sensitivity from titles and rules), (b) reading changelog and programme pages to re-parameterise the bot when rules change. Scoring, share estimation and quoting are deterministic.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Enumerate rewarded markets. `GET clob.polymarket.com/rewards/markets/current` (public, 500 per page) lists every condition with `rewards_min_size` (smallest order that scores), `rewards_max_spread` (the band in cents around the midpoint inside which orders score), `native_daily_rate` (Polymarket's pool) and `total_daily_rate` (native plus user-sponsored) [4]; `/rewards/markets/multi` adds `market_competitiveness` and sorts by it [5][6].
  2. Understand the score. Each order scores `S = ((v - s)/v)^2 * b` (`v` max spread, `s` distance from the size-cutoff-adjusted midpoint, `b` an undefined "in-game multiplier"); distance dominates size: with v = 3c, 100 shares at 1c score 44.4, 200 shares at 2c score 22.2 [1]. Sides combine as `Q_min = max(min(Q_bid, Q_ask), max(Q_bid, Q_ask)/c)`, `c = 3.0`, while the midpoint is within [0.10, 0.90]; outside it only `min(Q_bid, Q_ask)` counts, so one-sided quotes score zero [1]. Your share is your `Q_min` over everyone's, sampled once per minute at a random offset (up to 1,440 samples per UTC day) and paid at ~midnight UTC; a wallet's day pays only if its total reaches $1, no roll-over [1][2].
  3. Rank by share, not by pool: reconstruct the in-band competitor score from the public book and estimate the share your size and distance would capture.
  4. Quote both sides at minimum qualifying size, as close to mid as your fill tolerance allows, post-only, heartbeat armed [E1]; widen or pull on volatility, scheduled news and when the midpoint approaches.
  5. Stack maker rebates on fills (25% of taker fees, 20% crypto, 15% sports) and holding rewards (3.25% annualised on eligible positions), which accrue independently [31][32].
  6. Reconcile daily against on-chain pUSD transfers from the rewards distributor `0x2c2795EA…a709`, the ground truth for what you and every competitor earned [17].
  7. Polymarket US variant: the KYC'd CFTC venue snapshots the book at a random instant every second; an order scores `discountFactor^(ticks from best) * size`, each side is normalised to 1.0 per snapshot, and a side pays only when aggregate resting size reaches the programme's `targetSize` [19]. Pools are per market and per period (early, day-of, live), public at `api.prod.polymarketexchange.com/v1/incentives` [23], paid 5 + 2 business days after period end [19]; fills also earn the US maker rebate (theta 0.0125) [25].
- **What creates the edge**: A pure subsidy: Polymarket, the US exchange and sponsors pay for displayed depth whether or not it fills [1][7]. The exploitable inefficiency is allocation: pools are published per market while maker count per market is not equilibrated. Of today's top 200 pools ($32.3k/day), 42-46 have competitiveness zero and hold $5.7k-$6.3k/day (18-20%) with nobody scoring; the Fed October markets pay $1,000/day each against competitiveness 9-14, while "U.S. invades Iran before 2027" pays $400 against 321-426 and Le Pen 2027 $262 against ~480 [5] (two queries 30 minutes apart; the ranking is stable, the numbers move by the minute). The payer is Polymarket's treasury (depth as a growth expense) and sponsors; the counterparty of your fills is the taker flow. Polymarket winners are limit-order providers whose orders "resolve favorably" [26], but the average Kalshi maker still loses ~10% [E4, snippet-only: SSRN 5502658 was not fetched by any pack]. The farmer collects the subsidy and pays adverse selection; only the net matters, and nobody outside the top wallets has published it.
- **Capital required**: $10k+ to be meaningful internationally; $1k-$10k works on sub-$50/day pools if the wallet clears the $1 daily total [2]. Minimum size is 20 shares in 12,516 of 16,335 configs, 100-200 shares in the $200-$1,000/day pools, 1,000 per side on NFL game markets [4][E2]; 30 markets at 200 shares two-sided is ~$6k-$12k resting before fills. Resting capital is intraday, but fills create positions held to resolution: ArmageddonRewardsBilly's largest positions are 250,000 NO and 250,000 YES on Vance 2028 (a complete set, ~$250k locked until 2028), then Fed October, 2026 inflation brackets and a US-Iran nuclear-deal market [13]. The US venue needs a KYC'd own-name account; the NFL live moneyline target is 150,000 contracts per side in aggregate [23], so a side pays nobody until that much rests, and holding a fifth of it means ~30,000 contracts at the touch, roughly $15k per side at 50c, all exposed in play.
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: rewards-config poller (`/current`, `/multi`), L2 book WebSocket per candidate, Gamma metadata (end dates, `secondsDelay`, fee type), changelog and status watcher; US: `/v1/incentives` plus the public gateway book. (2) Decision logic: in-band competitor score reconstruction, share estimator, jump-risk classifier (time to resolution, news calendar, one-day price change), allocator maximising reward per unit of markout risk. (3) Execution: post-only GTC, cancel-replace on midpoint moves (no modify), 15-order batches, 40 orders/s standard-tier budgeting, heartbeat, post-restart 2-minute post-only and HTTP 425 handling [E1]. (4) Risk: inventory caps, one-sided-fill handling (re-quote, merge, or exit), emergency flatten and the kill switch the official guidance requires [30]. (5) Test mode: book replay computing the per-minute score share you would have had; `poly-maker` (MIT, 1,500 stars, CLOB V2, `--paper`) is a reference for the regime machine (QUIET = farm in-band, EVENT = pull on sweeps) [11]; its inventory and toxicity logic is file 02's subject. Build: 2-4 weeks for share calculator and allocator on an existing LP stack, 1-2 weeks more for the US venue; 2-3 months from scratch.
- **Opportunity size**: International, hard data. Configured rates on Tuesday 22 Sep 08:30 UTC: 16,335 markets summing to $140,047/day; median $3/day; 82.6% pay under $10/day and 4,898 pay exactly $1; two markets pay ≥ $1,000/day; the top 10 pools are 3.7% of the total, the top 500 35.5% [4]. On Sunday 20 Sep the configured sum was $585k across 16,719 markets because NFL game-day templates ($15,771/day per moneyline, $11,657 per spread and total) were live [E2]; the ~$445k gap is the weekend sports layer (derived). Realised payouts from the distributor's on-chain transfers: $108k-$156k per day (13-20 Sep) to 2,250-2,510 wallets; over eight days 4,832 wallets were paid, the top 10 took 24.0%, top 100 60.4%, top 500 88.4%, median ~$5; the 22 Sep run paid $170.2k to 2,501 wallets (largest $9,330, 100th $355, 500th $31) [17]. The realised international pool is ~$45-60M/year, a quarter of it to ten wallets (derived). Sponsored pools are small: 32 configs adding $247/day [4], down from $7.5k/day two days earlier [E2]; the largest documented sponsorship was ~$70k in February [8]. Time-boxed programmes are the swings: March Madness "$2M+" with $60k/day on live moneylines and a 3.5-second minimum rest [3]; the $1M August crypto TWAP programme "has ended" [1]. Direction: fee-funded taker rebates ($255k/day) and maker rebates ($187k/day), drawn from $32.7M of 30-day taker fees [18], already exceed treasury-funded liquidity rewards ($126k/day) [E2], and the treasury line is the one Polymarket cuts at will. Saturating overall, uneven per market.
- **Pros**: Income independent of fills and direction; enumerable parameters and public on-chain ground truth for competitors' earnings; stacks with rebates and holding rewards on the same orders; two-sided scoring is self-hedging by construction; the US venue has a KYC-restricted participant set and per-side scoring that suits skewed inventory.
- **Cons / risks**: Execution: you are the liquidity; a one-sided fill in a thin market leaves a directional position 5-10% under water (theflowp_'s worst case -$296 on $4k [9]); match-then-fail, cancel-only recovery modes and 99.13% API uptime leave quotes stuck at times [E1]. Platform: every parameter is discretionary (sports rebate 25% → 15%; August programme ended; US pools cut by a third to two-thirds in July) [3][1][22], and benefits are forfeitable "at the Company's sole discretion", judged "retroactively" [29][E9]. ToS: flicker and cancel-on-match quoting are what the 3.5-second rule and the deposit-wallet upgrade were built against [3][12][E9]. Regulatory: on Polymarket US, layering to win snapshots is a Commodity Exchange Act violation, fines up to $500,000 per violation [35]. Capital: fills in long-dated markets lock capital to resolution, and farmer inventory is where informed flow arrives ($143M anomalous profit, 1,950 suspected insider accounts [E4]). Edge decay: makers doubled within a day (302 → 639) and top-5 share fell from 56.5% to 36.9% when a $70k pool appeared [8]; the same happens to any uncontested pool you find.
- **Expected results**: Wallet-level hard data exists via the Data API `v2/user-pnl` endpoint, which separates `reward_income`, `maker_rebate`, `yield_income` and `trade_pnl` [13][14]. The three largest lifetime liquidity-reward recipients [15][16]: **ArmageddonRewardsBilly** (`0xc8ab…6418`): lifetime rewards $1,126,077, rebates $77,437, `trade_pnl` +$373k on $374M of volume and 1.38M trades; 2026 YTD rewards +$809k against trade PnL -$40k; last 90 days rewards +$228.5k (~$2.5k/day) [13]. **Haradwaith** (`0x21ff…0d71`): lifetime rewards $625,183, rebates $49,747, `trade_pnl` +$16.5k on 2.03M trades; 2026 YTD rewards +$511k against trade PnL +$75k; last 90 days +$281k (~$3.1k/day) [13]. **ImJustKen** (`0x9d84…1344`): lifetime rewards $1,229,012 but `trade_pnl` +$2.26M, and in 2026 only $66k of rewards against +$397k of trading PnL: an informed maker who used to farm [13]. On the 22 Sep run the three received $6,459, $1,360 and $403 [17]. `trade_pnl` is mark-to-market (`position_pnl - realized_lp_pnl + fees_charged - fees_refunded` [14]), so 90-day figures swing with open inventory; the YTD numbers are the honest ones. Portfolio values are $830k, $320k and $806k, so the two active farmers earn roughly 0.3-1.0% of position value per day in rewards (derived upper bound; idle cash excluded). Everyone else: median $5/day, 49% under $10 [17]. Self-reported: $10k → $200/day rising to $700-800/day in 2024 with "one or two other bots" [10, pre-fee regime]; simulated 404% APR on a $53/day pool at 83.5% share vs 1% on an $801/day pool, no realised PnL [9]. Marketing: Bravado's "200 to 300 USDC per day on roughly 10,000 USDC" is past tense and unverified [34]; TradoxVPS declines to claim profitability [33]. Net of markouts below the top three: no public data. Guess for a competent solo bot with $20k-$50k internationally: $30-$150/day gross rewards (the 100th-largest recipient got $355 and the 500th $31 on 22 Sep [17]); net, a bot without a fair-value model keeps perhaps half of that in quiet weeks and gives back a month of it on one jump or one programme cut (guess).
- **Competitive landscape**: Crowded at the top (median 32 effective makers per market, ~3 at the extreme [27]), thin in the tail. Ten wallets take a quarter of daily payouts [17]; the two visible pure farmers run 40k-210k trades per 30 days [13], i.e. hundreds of markets under automated cancel-replace. Desks (Jump with an equity-for-liquidity deal on the US venue, Wintermute, SIG, DRW, Galaxy, Flow Traders) compete for sports and crypto depth and for the application-only US Market Maker Program (institutional@qcex.com, undisclosed terms) [21][E4]; QCX also filed a Liquidity Provider stipend programme with redacted terms [28]. `poly-maker` (1,500 stars, 490 forks) lowers the entry bar for everyone [11]. Competitive means sub-second cancels, per-market book reconstruction, an allocator that models competitiveness and dilution, a jump-risk model, and on the US venue a real in-play fair-value model, because live pools are large precisely because in-play quoting is toxic.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for honest two-sided quoting from one identity (E9 traffic light: Allowed on both venues); grey for quoting the same markets from several own wallets (any self-fill breaches the "no change in beneficial ownership" clause, which has no intent element); prohibited for the gaming variants (flicker, spoof, cancel-on-match, multi-wallet Sybil) [E9]. International ToS (effective 2026-08-11) bans spoofing ("with the intent to cancel before execution"), fictitious transactions, wash trading and orders that do "not result in a change in beneficial ownership"; programme benefits "may be canceled or forfeited at the Company's sole discretion" and compliance may be evaluated "retroactively" [29] (§4.2 / §6 per E9's mapping; the exported text carries no numbering). No explicit multi-wallet clause, but rebate and referral terms cite "linked accounts" and "inauthentic trading" [E9]. Cancel-on-match harvesting ("Ghosts of Polymarket", $13.4k to 1,567 addresses) was an exploit largely closed by the 2026-05-04 Deposit Wallet upgrade (daily ghost-fill rate ~8% → 0.3%), though the authors say ghost fills "keep surfacing" [12]; cases and defences are file 31's subject. Polymarket US rulebook (14 Sep 2026): 7.2(p) wash trades, 7.2(q) self-match across "commonly controlled account(s)", 7.2(x) no trading for others, mandatory "order throttles, price collars, and kill switches" for automated traders, fines "up to $500,000 for each violation" (8.10(b)), incentive-programme changes via CFTC Reg 40.6(a) filing with 10 business days' notice (3.15) [35]; 7.2(e) spoofing and 3.9 own-account funding per E9 [E9]. The LIP sits under "Institutional Programs" but is marked "Open" (no application), unlike the Market Maker Program [20]; KYC'd US-person eligibility applies, so a non-US team needs a US entity.
- **How you'd validate it**: Two weeks read-only: poll `/rewards/markets/multi` hourly, record the in-band book for the top 300 pools by `rate_per_day / (competitiveness + 1)`, compute per minute the `Q_min` share a two-sided `rewards_min_size` quote at 1 tick and at half the max spread would have earned, multiply by pool, subtract a markout estimate (midpoint at 1 h and at resolution for minutes your quote would have been crossed). Data: CLOB book WebSocket, `/prices-history`, Gamma outcomes. Metric: net $/day per $1k resting and its dispersion. Pass: ≥ $1.5 net per $1k per day on 20+ markets over 20 days; fail if uncontested pools become contested within 3 days on average (the Telonex pattern). Then a $500-$2,000, 14-day pilot in the top 5 markets, reconciling `/rewards/user/percentages` × pool against distributor transfers to your wallet, logging fills with 24-hour markouts. Cost: a VPS in eu-west-1 (nearest unrestricted region to the CLOB's eu-west-2 [E1]) and ~40 engineering hours; no capital at risk in phase one.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: execution-sensitive. The reward itself is not a speed race, but losses are: scoring samples the book once a minute at a random offset [1], so being 100 ms faster earns no extra share. What costs money is a quote left in place when a market jumps. The US venue is closer to latency-sensitive, because it snapshots every second and scores distance from the best price in ticks [19].

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pool discovery (`/current`, `/multi`) | polling cadence | you miss new sponsored pools and game-day templates; sponsored pools are a speed game against bots watching `/current` [8] | poll every 5-15 min; `new_market` WS events for listings |
| Share estimation | book reconstruction from the L2 WebSocket | a wrong share estimate puts capital in contested pools; latency here barely matters | recompute every 1-5 min |
| Keeping quotes in band | cancel-replace when the midpoint moves (no in-place modify) [E1] | a quote pushed out of the band scores zero until it is replaced | re-quote within ~10-30 s of a mid move |
| Pulling quotes before jumps | news, scheduled events, one-day price change | your resting bid is the one filled at the old price; one 10c jump on 200 shares costs ~$20, several days of a $3-10/day pool (derived) | sub-second cancel on flagged markets; pull minutes before scheduled events |
| Order throughput | per-signer rate limits: 40 orders/s, 80 cancels/s standard; 600/s at Elite ($10M+ 30-day maker volume) [E1] | at 500 markets × 2 sides re-quoted every 30 s you need ~33 orders/s plus the same in cancels (derived), right at the standard cap; the rate limit, not latency, caps breadth | 50-150 markets on the standard tier |
| Dead-man switch and outages | heartbeat every 5 s, cancel-all after 10 s; 99.13% trading uptime, cancel-only recovery modes [E1] | quotes stranded during incidents are the ones picked off | heartbeat armed; flatten script tested |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Reward share: speed-neutral. Minute sampling rewards distance to mid and time in band, not reaction time [1]. (2) Adverse selection: speed-dependent where prices jump. In BTC 15-minute markets, Polymarket quotes took a median 347 ms to respond to large Binance moves [37], so a farmer slower than a few hundred ms is the stale quote in crypto-linked pools. On news markets a ~500 ms newswire was already too slow in a practitioner test [E1]. (3) Minimum rest rules neutralise speed on purpose: March Madness required 3.5 s of resting before an order scored [3], so flicker quoting earns nothing there and is a ToS risk anyway. (4) Venue delays protect makers only in some markets: taker orders wait 150 ms in crypto markets since 2026-09-04 (it was 50 ms from 2026-08-17) and 1 s in configured sports markets, and a pending taker cannot cancel [36][E1]. A resting quote can still be cancelled inside that window, which helps a fast farmer.
- **Where to run it**: for long-dated, low-news pools any small VPS in a non-restricted jurisdiction works, because the sampling is per minute. For crypto-linked, sports or short-dated pools, run in AWS eu-west-1 (Dublin), the nearest unrestricted region to the eu-west-2 matching engine. Do not host in the UK, which is close-only, or in the US [E1][E9]. Co-location in eu-west-2 needs KYC/KYB and excludes Builder Program participants [E1]. It is not worth paying for until a replay shows that fills from missed cancels, not pool share, are what separates you from the top recipients.

### What you are probably underestimating
1. **The configured rate lies on game days.** `rate_per_day` summed to $585k on Sunday and $140k on Tuesday while realised payouts ran $108k-$170k every day; NFL Sunday 13 Sep paid $128.5k against ~$400k of configured NFL rates [4][17][E2]. Sports templates exist only on game days and pay only if someone rests 1,000 shares per side inside 2.5c during the window. An allocator ranking by `rate_per_day` overweights NFL and underweights the ~1,500 weekday markets that actually pay; on weekdays configured and realised are close.
2. **The docs changed the epoch.** The liquidity-rewards page now reads "once per minute at a random offset, so an epoch contains up to 1,440 samples" (re-fetched for this check) [1]; E2's 20 Sep reading had 10,080 samples per 7-day epoch. Share is settled daily, so a bad day cannot be averaged away by a good week, and a market that resolves mid-day pays only for the minutes it was live.
3. **The top farmers quote the whole book.** Haradwaith placed 210k trades and ArmageddonRewardsBilly 40k in 30 days for $70k and $44k of rewards [13]: $0.33-$1.10 of reward per trade, viable only with batching, cancel budgets near the 40/s limit and automated inventory recycling. A 30-market bot competes with 500-market bots for the same pools.
4. **Reward income is lagged in the Data API.** For all three wallets `reward_income` last moved at 2026-09-11 23:00 UTC, eleven days before the query, although all three were paid on-chain on 22 Sep [13][17]. Measure yourself and competitors against distributor transfers, not the API.
5. **Competitiveness zero is a warning, not a gift.** The uncontested $5.7k-$6.3k/day in the top 200 sits in MrBeast view brackets, city-temperature brackets, Senate-odds thresholds and model-release dates [5], all resolving within days with discontinuous prices. Reward per unit of competition is highest exactly where markouts are worst.
6. **Stacking is part of the design.** ArmageddonRewardsBilly's $77k of maker rebates, $9.7k of holding yield and 250,000 YES plus 250,000 NO on Vance 2028 at ~0.50 each [13] show reward-band quoting that deliberately accumulates long-dated inventory in holding-reward markets, plus a complete set with no directional exposure on which holding rewards may accrue (E2 infers this from the formula; unconfirmed by Polymarket). That set is ~$250k parked at 3.25% until 2028: measure the farm's return on the whole $830k book, not on resting quotes. ImJustKen shows the other exit: farm to build a book, then trade it ($2.26M trade PnL against $1.23M rewards) [13].
7. **The $1 pools are a cancel-budget sink, not a floor problem.** The $1 minimum applies to a wallet's daily total ("your earnings for that day"), not per market [2]; the per-market reading in affiliate guides is unconfirmed [E2]. Diversification is therefore not penalised, but with 4,898 markets at $1/day and a median of $3 [4], even 100% share of a $1 pool returns $1 for two resting orders and a day of cancel-replace traffic against a 40 orders/s budget [E1]. Below roughly $10/day a pool cannot pay for the rate-limit slots it consumes.
8. **Sponsored pools are tiny and reactive.** $247/day across 32 configs today [4]; the $70k February sponsorship drew 337 new makers within a day and cut the top-5 share by a third [8]. Sniping them is a speed game against bots watching `/current`, and the sponsor may be an informed trader buying your liquidity [7].
9. **On the US venue the target size is the moat.** NFL moneyline day-of pools carry `targetSize` 500,000 and early pools 240,000 for $550 and $400 of reward; the live pool needs 150,000 for $32,000 [23][24]. Below target a side pays nobody, so small quoters cannot unlock a pool alone and depend on approved market makers showing up. Poll the incentives API (it carries `programId` and status) and expect changes on 10 business days' notice under Rule 3.15 [35].
10. **Programme changes are the main PnL driver.** In 2026: sports rebate 25% → 15% (10 Jul), the $1M August programme opened and closed, US pools cut across politics, tennis, MLB, PGA, UFC, WNBA and esports (effective 13 Jul), a 3.5-second rest rule for March Madness [3][1][22]. A changelog watcher that re-parameterises the allocator within hours beats a better quoting model.
