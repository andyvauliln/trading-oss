# 15 - Rule-ambiguity, clarification and dispute-phase trading (betting on the adjudicator)
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: B (hard data for mechanics, frequencies and price paths: on-chain lifecycle census, own CLOB price queries, two papers, official docs; D for wallet-level PnL of anyone trading disputes, none public) · Last verified: 2026-09-28_

### Rule-ambiguity, clarification and dispute-phase trading
- **One-line summary**: Trade the gap between what a market's rule text plus UMA precedent implies and what the price says, in the three windows where that gap is widest: before resolution when the crowd prices the headline, in the hours after a Polymarket clarification, and during the 2-hour challenge and 4-6 day dispute window when the contract keeps trading and the price becomes a forecast of the UMA token vote rather than of the event.
- **Category**: Information edge [adjudication forecasting; the dispute window is also a capital-lock carry trade]
- **Maturity**: Emerging, with the mechanism proven and the edge unproven. Proven with hard data: the lifecycle and its timing [1][2][5]; the frequency (1,604 disputes on 159,447 proposals, ~1.0%, to Nov 2025 [5]; more than 1,150 markets sent to arbitration in 2026 by mid-May, already above all of 2025 [15]); the concentration in big markets (149 of the top 2,100 closed markets by volume, 7.1%, carry at least one dispute; $4.89B of their $42.65B of volume, 11.5%, sat in disputed markets; a re-run on the top 1,000 gives 95, 9.5% and 12.8% of volume [18]); the fact that the first proposal is overturned in 16.1% of those disputed markets (24 of 149; 15 of 95 in the re-run) [19]; and that prices move tens of points on clarifications and votes (Strategy: Yes 0.615 to 0.0005 inside four hours [21]). A 2026 Economics Letters paper on 233 disputed markets finds the token vote overturns the audited rule-correct winner in 12.4% of cases and that "the dispute-time price predicts the vote share" [7, snippet-only]. Not proven: any wallet that earns from this systematically. No leaderboard, paper or on-chain study isolates dispute-window PnL; "no public data" applies to returns. Also not proven, and contradicted by the cases below: the idea that a literal reading of the rules is the edge. In the six marquee 2026 disputes re-priced for this file, the literal reading won three and lost three [21].
- **Requires AI?**: optional. Web-enabled LLMs reproduce UMA's final ruling on 88-90% of already-disputed markets (DeepSeek V3.1 89.58%, Qwen Max 89.19%, Claude 4.5 Sonnet 88.03% on 259 resolved disputes, with web access cut at the UMA decision timestamp) [6], so an LLM is a cheap first-pass adjudicator and a rules-diff reader. It is useless for the other half of the problem: predicting which markets will be disputed from rule text scores accuracy 0.57 and F1 0.44, "slightly above the random baseline" [6]. Vote forecasting, sizing and the lifecycle monitor are deterministic.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Ingest the resolution lifecycle. Gamma exposes `umaResolutionStatuses` per market (a JSON string such as `["proposed","disputed","proposed","disputed"]`), a singular `umaResolutionStatus` (`disputed` on every reopened market today), `umaBond`, `umaReward` and the rules in `description` [20][23]; the Data API v2 (2026-09-04) adds `GET /v2/resolutions?condition=…` (up to 20 condition ids) with `status` (initialized, posed, proposed, challenged, reproposed, disputed, resolved, arbitration), `proposed_price`, `reproposed_price`, `price`, `was_disputed`, `new_version_q` (rules edited after posing) and `extended_review` (a managed proposal held past its normal expiry) [28][29]. Whitelisted proposers (37 addresses in Sep 2025, 177 by Nov 2025; at least 5 proposals at 95%+ accuracy over a rolling six months) post the proposal; anyone can dispute [4][E3]. Polymarket's own pages still say "Anyone can propose" [1][3].
  2. Know the clock. Proposal, then a 2-hour challenge window; a first dispute resets the question and a second proposal round follows (median 300-2,909 s from reset to the successor proposal, by route) [1][5]; a second dispute sends it to UMA's DVM: 24-48 h of debate ("votes happen every other day"), 24 h commit, 24 h reveal, "4-6 days total" [1][2][16]. Neg-risk markets add a fixed hour and cannot pay 50/50 [E3]. The challenge window is not always 2 hours: a per-market `customLiveness` of 600-1,800 s sits on 72 of the top 2,100 active markets (monthly crypto strike ladders and the Eagles-Bears game), 7,200 s on one [20], so read the field before you count on two hours to dispute. Settlement is batched: every disputed case market checked here settled between 00:31 and 00:58 UTC on its settlement day [19].
  3. Trade one of four set-ups, each with its own counterparty: (a) pre-resolution rules-text mispricing: the market prices the headline while the rules key on a confirmation, a source hierarchy or a calendar date; (b) the proposal and challenge window: a proposal that contradicts your reading is a 2-hour option to dispute (file 16) or to trade against; (c) the clarification event: Polymarket posts an "additional context" update on-chain via the adapter's bulletin board, which "cannot change the fundamental intent" [1][3] but in practice decides the case (Strategy [8][9]); 83.34% of clarifications land after the request is created and before the first proposal [5]; (d) the DVM window: the contract trades for 4-6 days on the vote alone [10][12].
  4. Model the vote, not the text. Build a precedent corpus of resolved disputes (149 high-volume ones are enumerable from Gamma plus `/v2/resolutions` in an afternoon [18][19]) and classify each by the rule that decided it: timing of confirmation versus timing of the event (Strategy: "Confirmation achieved outside of the market's time frame does not qualify" [9]); both-parties confirmation versus press consensus (US-Iran ceasefire, No [E3]); "spirit of the market" versus literal exclusions (US-Iran "permanent peace deal": a 60-day memorandum resolved Yes with more than 99% of voting power [11][23]); precedent consistency (Zelensky suit [E3]). Run the LLM adjudicator with retrieval cut at the current timestamp as a second opinion.
  5. Compare your vote probability q with the price P of that side. The expected return per dollar is q/P - 1 (derived): at P = 0.85 it is -5.9% if q = 0.80, zero at 0.85, +5.9% at 0.90, +11.8% at 0.95, and the losing branch is -100%. The market is already close to q on average [7], so the trade exists only where you have a precedent the crowd is ignoring or a panic seller who needs cash before the 4-6 day lock ends.
  6. Size for a binary loss and a correlated book: quarter-Kelly, f = (q - P)/(4(1 - P)), caps a q = 0.90 / P = 0.85 trade at ~8% of capital (derived); never hold several rungs of one news complex (the Iran ladder had six markets in dispute at once [23]).
  7. Exit at settlement (redeem) or into the 0.99+ bid once the reveal period makes the tally visible; in fee-free geopolitics a taker exit costs nothing, in politics 0.04 × p(1 - p) [E1].
- **What creates the edge**: Three different inefficiencies, and only the first is reliable. (1) Adjudication is a separate, forecastable process that most holders never model: "Some traders now follow whale behavior instead of relying on fundamentals" [14]; disputes are decided by a concentrated, near-unanimous electorate (nine wallets hold nearly half of UMA voting power, 6,400+ accounts participate, and the nine "often vote together and frequently end up on the winning side" [14]; the Strategy vote was 98.6% No [9], the Iran vote above 99% Yes [11]), so once the lean is visible the outcome is close to certain and the residual price gap is a lock-up discount. (2) Forced and frightened selling: the market stays open during the dispute, so holders who need cash or who read the dispute as a "scam" sell the eventual winner at 0.86-0.95 (Iran, 15 June [21]) or the eventual loser into the 0.99 bid. (3) Rule-text mispricing before any proposal: the ceasefire-extension markets traded Yes at 0.66 while press reported an extension that the rules, which required "clear public confirmation from both" governments, did not accept; Yes went to 0.0015 within three days [21][E3]. The other side is retail pricing headlines, whales with positions, and, in the dispute window, voters who know how they will vote: at least 60% of active UMA voters are linked to Polymarket accounts and roughly one in five disputes has a voter with a stake [15]. That last group does not "keep losing"; it is the informed counterparty to avoid.
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k. There is no minimum beyond the 5-share order minimum (`orderMinSize` 5 on 2,095 of the top 2,100 active markets [20]), but lock-up is the constraint: 2 h for an undisputed proposal, 4-6 days per the docs [1], and 10 days in practice when rounds stack (the US-Iran ceasefire market ended 21 April, went through three dispute rounds and settled on 1 May [19][21]); the Zelensky suit market took five rounds [19][E3]. Disputes cluster in one news complex, so the lock is correlated across positions. Bonds are file 16's subject, but the live distribution tells you who can afford to fight: $500 with a $5 reward on 1,698 of the top 2,100 active markets, $25,000 on 95 and $50,000 on 53 [20].
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: a Gamma poller diffing `umaResolutionStatuses`, `description` and `umaBond` on every market above a volume floor (300 req/10 s on `/markets` [25]); `/v2/resolutions` on the same set (800 req/10 s for v2 [25]); a Polygon log watcher on the adapter's bulletin-board updates and on OptimisticOracle propose and dispute events for sub-minute detection (Gamma lagged the Strategy clarification by an unknown amount; the price moved within the hour [21]); UMA's vote dapp for commit and reveal tallies (a JavaScript app, scraping unverified here); press and X for the "consensus of credible reporting" test. (2) Decision logic: rules parser and diff, precedent classifier (the 149 resolved high-volume disputes plus every future one), LLM adjudicator with time-cut retrieval, vote-probability model, q-versus-P threshold. (3) Execution: post-only bids in the dispute window where depth allows, taker only for clarification jumps. (4) Risk: per-complex exposure cap, lock-aware cash ladder, a rule that treats a P4 "too early" verdict as a reopened market, not a loss. (5) Test mode: replay of proposal, dispute and clarification timestamps against hourly prices. Build: 3-5 weeks for a solo developer with an existing Polymarket stack, most of it in the precedent corpus and the monitor.
- **Opportunity size**: Small and lumpy. Population-wide the dispute rate is ~1% (above; 184 of 18,427 markets in the year to May 2026 [17]) and Polymarket says 0.2% of contracts reach a vote [E3]. In money, disputes are concentrated where you trade: of the 2,100 largest closed markets by volume ($4.7M to $1.53B each), 149 had at least one dispute, 118 went to the DVM (106 with two rounds, 11 with three, one with five), 31 of the 149 were neg-risk, and none paid 50/50 [18][19]. Marquee cases in 2026: Strategy sells Bitcoin by 31 May ($375.8M, two disputes, No), US-Iran ceasefire extended by 22 April ($203.6M, three rounds, No), US-Iran permanent peace deal by 15 June ($177.4M, two disputes, Yes, with six sibling rungs disputed alongside), Zelensky suit ($242.2M, five rounds, first proposal overturned), Ronaldo cries at the World Cup ($89.2M, overturned) [18][19][23][24]. Live on 28 Sep 2026: seven of the top 2,100 active markets carry a dispute, with $4.4M of combined volume, and none is in a live vote: the Data API reports all seven as `challenged` with the second proposal unset and last state changes between 8 March and 17 August [19][20]. They are early proposals (six "Yes" on year-end 2026 questions; one "No" on Max Martin attending Taylor Swift's wedding, $181k volume, $484 of resting bids and 28,000 shares of asks [22]) that were disputed, reset and now simply trade on. So the tradeable set is a handful of marquee disputes a month with real depth, plus a long tail with three-figure books, and on a given day there may be no open vote at all. Direction: growing in count (2026 already above 2025 [15]) while the oracle itself is under political pressure and reform has "largely stalled" [14].
- **Pros**: The lifecycle is fully observable on-chain and through two free APIs; the electorate is small and votes near-unanimously, so the vote is forecastable once the lean shows; clarifications and votes create tens-of-points moves in the most liquid markets on the venue; geopolitics, where most marquee disputes live, is fee-free for takers [E1]; the precedent corpus compounds and feeds files 13, 14 and 16.
- **Cons / risks**: Execution: the price already tracks the vote share [7]; you are paid only for the residual, against a counterparty set that includes voters. Binary loss: the Iran No holders (a self-reported $700k position [12]) and the Strategy Yes buyers of 1 June [8][9] lost 100% within days or hours (paths in Expected results). Platform: Polymarket can clarify at any time and has reversed its own clarification within hours (Ukraine minerals, March 2025 [E3]); the adapter admin can flag and resolve manually after a one-hour safety period (17 pause-or-flag and 10 administrative events across 185,550 questions [5][E3]); refunds happened once, in 2024, and were refused in 2025 [E3]. Capital: 4-10 day locks correlated across a news complex. Regulatory: none for trading on your reading; grey if you also vote or propose (below). Edge decay: LLM triage is public knowledge [6], and the same data is one API call away for everyone.
- **Expected results**: No public data on wallet-level PnL from dispute trading; theoretical only for the systematic version. What the price paths say about single trades, from own hourly CLOB data [21] (six marquee cases chosen for size, so these are the best-known paths, not a sample): US-Iran peace deal, 15 June: Yes bought at 0.86 (03:00 UTC, 15 June) or 0.9535 (16:00) paid $1 on 18 June, +16% or +4.9% in three days, with the vote at 99%+ visible from the 17th [11]; the No side, 0.14 to 0.05, went to zero. Strategy, 31 May: No at 0.385 at 14:00 UTC on 1 June (hourly average; CoinDesk reports Yes at 81% intraday [8]) paid $1 on 4 June (+160%) after the clarification (Yes 0.411 at 17:00, 0.0005 at 18:00 UTC); No bought after the clarification at 0.99-0.9975 returned 0.25-1.0% in three days (30-120% annualised, derived) against a 98.6% vote [9]. US-Iran ceasefire extended by 22 April: No at 0.34-0.52 on 21-22 April (Yes 0.48-0.66) paid $1 on 1 May, +90-190% in nine to ten days, on the both-governments rule [21][E3]; Yes buyers included a trader claiming a $20M+ loss [E3]. Israel-Hezbollah ceasefire extended by 26 April: the literal reading lost; No at 0.82 on 26 April went to zero on 28 April when the second proposal held (Yes 0.18 to 0.9985) [21]. Khamenei out by 31 March: two disputes, Yes never left 0.9985-0.9995, no discount to harvest [21]. Across the census, the first proposal was overturned in 24 of 149 disputed markets, 21 of them in DVM cases; the final matched the second proposal in 111 of 118 DVM cases [19], so "trade with the second proposal" is a ~94% rule and the market knows it. Guess for a disciplined solo operator with $10k: two to four tradeable disputes or clarifications a month, average edge +2-5% per trade on the winners at 5-10% of capital each, one full loss per 8-15 trades: -$500 to +$400 a month, with the sign decided by a single bad vote. Clarification snipes (Strategy-type) are the only large payoffs and they require being positioned before the post.
- **Competitive landscape**: Nobody publishes results, and the competition is structural rather than technical: UMA whales and UMA.rocks-style delegation pools (one member, "Scout", was expelled after admitting to voting while holding positions [15]); full-time discretionary traders who list rule-reading first ("Half the disputes I see on Polymarket come from people who didn't read the rules" [13], self-reported); whale-followers [14]; academic LLM adjudicators [6][7]; and the disputers themselves, who have a 2-hour head start on every fight. What it takes to compete: a precedent corpus, sub-minute detection of proposals and clarifications, and the discipline to skip disputes where the vote lean is not yet visible.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for trading on your own reading of the rules and public information (E9 traffic light: trading on own research Allowed; resolution sniping on public information Allowed) [E9]. Grey: voting UMA while holding a position in the market (E9: "UMA voting while holding a position: Grey", under the ToS §4.2 fraud and influence conduct code) [E9]; UMA's own community treats it as manipulation [15]. Prohibited: disputing correct proposals to delay settlement, proposing on a weekend to front-run a clarification, coordinating votes, or pressuring a resolution source (the ToS conduct code covers fictitious and manipulative trading, cornering and collusion [E9]; in March 2026 holders of large positions on an Iranian-missile-strike market harassed and threatened a Times of Israel journalist to change his reporting, he went to the police, and Polymarket condemned it and banned the accounts [30]). Everything in that prohibited list is file 31's subject at survey level. On Polymarket US there is no dispute window to trade: Rule 10.4 lets named officers determine the outcome at their sole discretion and "Determinations made by the Company are final" [E3].
- **How you'd validate it**: A leakage-controlled replay, no capital. Data: (1) enumerate every closed market with a dispute in `umaResolutionStatuses` (Gamma keyset pagination beyond the 2,100-row offset cap [20]; the top-2,100 slice already yields 149 [18]); (2) `/v2/resolutions` for proposed, reproposed and final prices [19]; (3) hourly or minute prices from `/prices-history` around proposal, dispute, clarification and settlement times [21]; (4) the rule text and its versions (`new_version_q` was true on all 149 disputed markets [19]). Strategies to score, each against the market price: A "buy the side of the second proposal at its dispute-window price"; B "buy the rules-literal side" (classified blind, before seeing the outcome); C the LLM adjudicator with retrieval cut at dispute time [6]. Metric: return per dollar locked, hit rate, worst single loss, share of PnL from the top three trades. Sample: 149 large plus at least 500 smaller disputes. Pass: strategy C (or A) beats a flat "hold the price" baseline by 2+ points of expected return per trade with t > 2 and no trade above 30% of PnL; fail if the edge lives entirely in three marquee cases. Cost: 20-40 engineering hours, $50-200 of LLM calls at Sonnet-class prices [26], free data.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: execution-sensitive. Nothing here is decided in milliseconds, but minutes matter twice: a clarification moved Strategy from 0.615 to 0.0005 within four hours [21], and the reset-to-successor-proposal median is 5-49 minutes [5], so a monitor polling every 10 minutes sees the trade after the move. Vote windows are days.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proposal and dispute detection | Gamma `umaResolutionStatuses` poll; `/v2/resolutions`; adapter and OO events on Polygon | you enter after the crowd has priced the dispute; the 2-hour challenge option expires | on-chain events within 1 min; Gamma diff every 1-2 min for the top 500 markets |
| Clarification detection | bulletin-board events; `description` diffs; `new_version_q` | the whole trade (Strategy: four hours from 0.615 to 0.0005 [21]) | on-chain within 1 min; Gamma diff every 1-2 min |
| Vote-lean reading | UMA commit (hidden) then reveal (visible) tallies [16] | you pay the full dispute discount when the tally already shows 99% [11] | scrape the reveal period hourly |
| Entry | CLOB taker in thin books; post-only where depth allows | thin books outside marquee cases ($484 of bids on the largest reopened dispute today [22]) | post-only bids; taker only on clarifications |
| Exit and capital recycling | settlement batch ~00:30 UTC [19]; 0.99+ bids in the reveal period | one extra day of lock per position | sell into 0.995+ once the reveal is lopsided; redeem otherwise |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) The clarification trade is all speed: the four-hour Strategy collapse contained the only three-figure return in the sample [21], and Gamma's copy of the rules lags the chain by an unmeasured interval. (2) The dispute-window trade is not: Iran Yes drifted from 0.86 to 0.9985 over 60 hours while the tally was public [11][21]; being a day late cost 5-10 points of return, not the trade. (3) Rate limits are irrelevant at this cadence (Gamma 300 req/10 s, Data API 800 req/10 s [25]). (4) The 2-hour challenge window is the one hard deadline; missing it turns a dispute you could have filed into a trade you can only take at the reset price.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted; a small VPS is enough. A Polygon RPC with log subscriptions matters more than proximity to the CLOB. Not the UK or the US [E9].

### What you are probably underestimating
1. **The dispute-window price is already a vote forecast.** Wang finds the dispute-time price predicts the vote share on 233 disputed markets [7, snippet-only]; the paths here agree: Iran Yes was 0.9535 at 16:00 on 15 June and the reveal showed more than 99% Yes two days later [11][21]. The 5% you can earn is the lock-up discount plus the residual risk of the 12-16% of cases that flip [7][19]; it is carry, not alpha, unless your precedent read beats the crowd's.
2. **Literal rule reading is not the edge, precedent is.** Three of six marquee 2026 disputes went against the literal text: a 60-day memorandum resolved a "permanent peace deal" Yes [11][21]; a ceasefire extension with "literal-criteria objections" resolved Yes on the second proposal [21][E3]; and Khamenei paid Yes on Polymarket while Kalshi settled at last price [21][E3]. The three that went with the text (Strategy, US-Iran ceasefire, Israel-Hezbollah peace) turned on a confirmation-timing or both-parties clause, which is a recurring precedent class, not a reading skill [8][9][21]. Classify by precedent class before you trade.
3. **In the markets you snipe, the dispute rate is 7%, not 1%.** The population rate is ~1.0% [5][17]; among the 2,100 largest closed markets by volume it is 7.1% (8.4% for 2025 end dates, 7.5% for 2026), 9.5% among the largest 1,000, and 11.5% of their volume sat in disputed markets [18]. That census is by volume, which is what a liquidity-weighted sniping book looks like, not a random sample. A resolution-sniping book weighted by liquidity carries seven times the dispute exposure the headline rate suggests, and geopolitics, where your snipe fee is zero, has the worst tail (4.8% dispute rate, p99 settlement 14 days [17]).
4. **"Proposed" is 84% reliable in big disputed markets, and the vote rejected even the second proposal seven times.** The first proposal was overturned in 24 of 149 (16.1%, 21 of 118 DVM cases; $635.5M of volume, 13.0% of disputed volume); the final differed from the second proposal in 7 of 118 DVM cases [19]. Whitelisted proposers are 99.7% accurate on the population [E3]; the markets you care about are the exceptions.
5. **The clarification is the trade and it arrives without warning.** Strategy: 8-K at 14:00 UTC, Yes 0.615, clarification inside four hours, Yes 0.0005 by 18:00 [21]; 83% of clarifications land before the first proposal [5]; `new_version_q` was true on every one of the 149 disputed markets, so every disputed market had its rules edited after posing [19]. A rules-diff watcher on the bulletin board is the highest-value component, ahead of any adjudicator model.
6. **Locks are longer than "4-6 days" and land at 00:30 UTC.** The ceasefire market ended 21 April and settled 1 May after three rounds; Zelensky took five rounds; all five case markets settled between 00:31 and 00:58 UTC, one batch a day [19][21]. Plan for 10-day locks and lose the settlement day whatever the vote time.
7. **P4 "too early" reopens the market, and it is what every live "dispute" is today.** All seven active markets with a dispute are early proposals (six Yes on year-end 2026 questions: Hamas disarms, Venezuela and Lebanon recognise Israel, Iran ends enrichment, US strikes 14 countries, China GDP at 9%; one No on Max Martin) that were disputed, four of them twice, and now trade between 0.0015 and 0.165 as if nothing happened; `/v2/resolutions` shows all seven as `challenged` with an unset second proposal and last state changes dated 8 March to 17 August, and Gamma's singular `umaResolutionStatus` reads `disputed` on each [19][20]. A bot that buys 0.99 on "proposed" is buying into a P4 in those cases; the disputer keeps half of a $500 bond and the market goes on. Treat `challenged` on a market whose end date is months away as a reopening, not a settlement signal, and price it as an ordinary market again.
8. **Capacity is three figures outside marquee cases.** The largest reopened dispute today has $484 of bids and $1,638 of Gamma liquidity [20][22]; the 14-26 live proposals seen in two scans of the top 2,100 active markets are almost all sports, tweet-count and BTC-strike markets at 0.9995 with nothing to trade [20]. The strategy is two to five real windows a month, not a book.
9. **Your counterparty in the reveal period may be a voter.** At least 60% of active voters trade on Polymarket, one in five disputes has a conflicted voter, and more than half of voting power sits with ten wallets in most disputes [15]; the commit period hides votes for 24 hours and the reveal shows them for the next 24 [16]. The last day of a dispute is an information race you are not in; the first day, before commits, is where a precedent read can still be worth something.
10. **No 50/50 in the data, and neg-risk is a fifth of disputes.** None of the 149 disputed high-volume markets paid 0.5 [18]; 31 were neg-risk, where 50/50 is impossible and an extra hour is added [18][E3]. Do not reserve probability for P3 on binaries beyond a rounding error; do reserve an hour and a converter check on neg-risk rungs.
