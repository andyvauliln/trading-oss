# 04 - Sportsbook-anchored sports and esports market making (devigged fair value, pair lock, outside hedge)
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: C self-reported for operator economics (two detailed first-person accounts, one with a public wallet); A for fees, delays and live cross-venue prices · Last verified: 2026-09-24_

### Sportsbook-anchored sports and esports market making
- **One-line summary**: Rest tiered maker bids on Polymarket sports and esports markets around a fair price taken from sharp bookmakers with the bookmaker margin removed. Keep each game's inventory capped, lock a pair under $1 when both sides fill, or hedge the residual at an outside book.
- **Category**: Market-making [anchored to an external fair value]
- **Maturity**: Two different states. The large Pinnacle-anchored operator is Established but pre-fee and self-reported. A full-time sports maker described $300k in play on NFL Sundays, +$60-65k in a month, +$80k on a Super Bowl and -$100k in ten days on hockey [1], interviewed in October 2025, before sports fees existed. The small long-tail esports pair-lock bot is Decayed. The one documented run made +$4,973 net on $95,830 of volume from January to April 2026. Its fill rate collapsed from 37.4% to 1.0% as faster bots outbid it by a cent and fees arrived [2][3]. The two largest sports PnL wallets (swisstony +$18.4M on $1.85B, RN1 +$12.7M on $1.26B) are mostly directional buyers, not makers [E4].
- **Requires AI?**: no. Removing the margin, freshness checks and inventory control are deterministic. Optional: an LLM or scraper-parser that maps team, player and market names across books and Polymarket. Name mapping caused real losses: one documented bot shipped swapped-team and reused-odds bugs [3].
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Pull odds for the same game from sharp books (Pinnacle, Betfair exchange) or a consensus feed.
  2. Remove the margin ("devig") to get a fair probability. Method matters: power or additive methods vs Shin's method, which assumes insider bettors and shifts probability toward favourites. In one bot, switching the method cut modelled favourite ROI from 11.7% to 1.5% [3].
  3. Rest post-only bids on both outcomes below fair value, in tiers: small size near fair, more size at wider discounts. The documented operator showed $5-10k a side at a 0.5c edge pre-game, unlimited size at 1.5%+ edge, and $2-5k a side in play [1].
  4. If both sides fill, you own a complete pair for under $1 (a locked arbitrage). If one side fills, you hold a +EV directional position, but only if the odds were fresh.
  5. Hedge or close: sell the residual on Polymarket when it crosses fair, or lay it off at an outside book or on Kalshi.
  6. Risk: per-game caps ($25k NFL, $5k college football for the interviewed operator [1]), kill switches around injury news and lineups, and no quoting through game start unless you re-price in play.
- **What creates the edge**: An external anchor. Sportsbooks limit or ban winning bettors and Polymarket does not, so sharp information reaches the Polymarket book late in thin markets. The counterparty is the retail sports taker who crosses the spread. In the World Cup winner market, 66.7% of 194,422 addresses lost and 58k winners averaged $4.85 [E4]. Esports and minor leagues had 20-30c spreads with real volume in early 2026 [3]. What it does *not* create: settlement carry. Sports shows no favourite-longshot bias (longshots +2.4% equal-weighted, favourites -0.23%) [E4], so unlike file 02 there is no rent for holding the favourite; every cent comes from buying below the anchor.
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k for long-tail esports (per-match stakes of $50-500). $10k+ for major leagues, where the operator had $300k in play on NFL Sundays [1]. NFL game-day reward templates require 1,000 shares a side inside 2.5c [E2]. Lock-up: hours to days for game markets, months for futures.
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: an odds feed (see the services table); Gamma sports metadata (`secondsDelay`, `feeSchedule`, game start); CLOB WebSockets; the Polymarket sports WebSocket for live state ("may be delayed" [E1]); injury and lineup news. (2) Decision logic: event and market matching across sources; a devig model per sport; freshness gates (no quote if the odds are older than N minutes, where N depends on sport and volatility); per-game, per-sport and daily caps; pair-completion logic. (3) Execution: post-only tiers, cancel on odds moves, the sports delay (1 s on most live sports markets today [4]; possibly auto-cancel of resting orders at game start, from a possibly stale official repo [E1]). (4) Risk: a kill switch per sport; a rules-mismatch table (DNP, void, overtime, postponement); a cancelled-match policy (cancelled games resolve 50-50 [5]). (5) Test: paper-quote with timestamped odds and fills. Build: 3-6 weeks solo for one sport family; each extra sport adds 1-2 weeks of mapping and rules work.
- **Opportunity size**: Sports is ~39-40% of Polymarket volume [E1]. On 24 Sep 2026 the top 1,100 events included $4.4M/day of NFL, $2.7M MLB, $2.6M Dota 2, $1.1M college football, $0.8M CS2 and $0.4-0.5M each LoL, ATP and WTA [4]. Maker subsidies differ by league. NFL and college-football game markets are fee-free (`zero_fees`, no rebate) [4] but carry the largest reward templates: $15,771/day per NFL moneyline on game days and $32,000 per live NFL moneyline on the US venue's LIP [E2]. Other sports pay a 15% rebate on 0.05 taker fees, 0.19c/share at p = 0.50 (derived). The mispricing a sportsbook anchor can catch is shrinking where it matters most: pre-game NFL moneylines were within one tick across the international book (0.27/0.28), Polymarket US (0.265/0.270) and Kalshi (0.27/0.28) on the evening of 24 Sep [4][6][7]. The long tail still has room, but in dollars it is small: the documented esports bot peaked at ~$2.5k/month [2].
- **Pros**: An objective external fair value; hours-to-days lock-up; paired fills are true arbitrage; sports is the largest retail category; NFL and CFB markets pay large reward pools on top (file 01); the code carries over to Kalshi and Polymarket US.
- **Cons / risks**: Stale odds are the dominant loss. Esports lines moved 5+ pp during a hold in 28-60% of matches, and the median move was 10.9 pp in CoD [3]. Injury "fast clicking" moves NBA prices 40-50c [1]. Competition collapses fill rates within months [3]. Rule mismatches break hedges: Polymarket grades a player prop No/Under if the player does not play, Kalshi settles to the last pre-game price, books void it [8]. Hedge accounts at books get limited to $2-14 stakes within days to months [E5]. Pinnacle closed its public API on 23 Jul 2025 [9]. Regulatory: state actions target sports contracts on the US venue [E9]. Fees: sports taker fees rose from 0.03 to 0.05 and the maker rebate fell from 25% to 15% on 10 Jul 2026 [10].
- **Expected results**: Self-reported only. The large operator: $300k in play, +$60-65k in a good month, -$100k in ten days on hockey, and "If you're thinking about doing what I do, I would say don't" [1] (pre-fee era). The small esports bot: 3,858 bets over 2,785 markets, $95,830 volume, +$4,973 net. That breaks down as +$8,293 from locked pairs, -$3,185 from unhedged legs and -$134 from 236 cancelled matches. CS2 made +$2,380 on $30,123 (7.9%). Monthly net went $1,898, $2,506, $390, $180 from January to April 2026, with a public wallet [2][3]. It is now being re-tested with 10-share orders on a ~$300 balance after a Rust rewrite [3]. Guess for a new solo entrant in late 2026: long-tail esports and minor leagues with 5-minute odds, $0-$1,000/month on $5k-$10k. Major-league pre-game main lines are near zero, because three venues agree within a tick.
- **Competitive landscape**: Major leagues: desks (Jump, SIG, DRW, Akuna sports quants, Kalshi Trading and Flutter on Kalshi) and application-only market-maker programmes on Polymarket US [E4][E5]. Reward farmers sit at one tick in NFL and CFB. Long tail: several bots that outbid by a cent within seconds [3]. Being competitive takes odds latency in seconds, not minutes; clean event mapping; and a per-sport kill switch driven by news.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for quoting on Polymarket from one identity (E9: Allowed). Grey: scraping sportsbook odds (breaches most books' terms; use licensed feeds); multi-wallet setups (self-fill risk); US-venue sports under state challenges [E9]. The interviewed operator's practice of regenerating wallets after being banned from sportsbooks [1] concerns the books' terms, not Polymarket's. Prohibited: trading on non-public injury or lineup information from team insiders (insider prongs of ToS §4.2 and Polymarket US Rule 7.2(g)-(i)) [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: 1) For one family (CS2 + LoL, or MLB), log the devigged anchor every 60 s and the Polymarket book for 3 weeks. 2) Simulate resting bids at fair minus 3/5/7%. Count fills, fill rate lost to 1c overbids, and markouts at +5 min and at settlement; check fills against injury and lineup timestamps. 3) Pass: fill rate above 5% of markets bid, positive settlement markout on single-leg fills, and net > 2% of filled notional after the 0.05 taker fee on any exit. Fail: single-leg fills show negative post-fill line moves on average (you are the stale quote). Cost: an odds feed ($30-120/month) plus ~30 engineering hours; no capital.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: latency-sensitive, and latency-critical in play. Pre-game, odds freshness in seconds to minutes decides PnL: minutes-stale odds turned a winning bot into one that bought exactly the moved lines [3]. In play, the 1 s sports delay and live feeds make it a sub-second contest (file 18).

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor odds | feed type (REST polling vs push stream) | 10-30 min stale odds meant fills landed on the wrong side of 5-10 pp moves [3] | pre-game: <=60 s; near kickoff and in play: push feed <1 s |
| Injury / lineup news | news stream, X, team reports | NBA prices swing 40-50c on injury news [1]; resting bids get hit first | pull all quotes in the game on any news flag within 1 s |
| Queue position | reaction to competitors' 1c overbids | fill rate fell 37.4% to 1.0% in four months [3] | re-quote in <1 s on book change |
| Pair completion | second-leg quoting after the first fills | orphan legs are directional bets | bid the other leg within 1 s of a fill |
| Game-start transition | sports delay, possible auto-cancel at start [E1] | stale pre-game orders or none at all in play | explicit state machine for pre-game, start and live |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Freshness, not model sophistication, drove the documented bot's outcome. Moving from up to 30-minute-stale odds to 5-minute refreshes cost ~$3/day in data [3]. (2) The 1 s sports delay [4] is a maker's cancel window. A pending taker order cannot be cancelled [11], so a maker with a sub-second score or odds feed sees the event before the delayed taker order lands. (3) Pre-game NFL main lines are already arbitraged across venues to within a tick [4][6][7], so speed there buys queue position, not edge.
- **Where to run it**: AWS eu-west-1 for in-play quoting. Pre-game long-tail esports quoting works from any non-restricted VPS, because odds freshness, not network latency, is the constraint.

### What you are probably underestimating
1. **Sports has no favourite-longshot bias to carry.** Sports longshots returned +2.4% equal-weighted and favourites -0.23% [E4], so the settlement-carry rent that pays event-market makers (file 02) is absent. A sports maker without a fresh anchor is simply selling options to people with better information.
2. **NFL and college-football game markets pay no maker rebate.** On 24 Sep 2026 they were `zero_fees` with a 1 s delay, while MLB, esports, tennis and UFC were 0.05 with a 15% rebate [4]. NFL maker income is the game-day reward template (1,000 shares a side inside 2.5c [E2]) plus spread.
3. **Pre-game major-league lines are already arbitraged across venues.** Jets-Lions: international 0.27/0.28, Polymarket US 0.265/0.270, Kalshi 0.27/0.28. Seahawks-Commanders, Bengals-Steelers and Chiefs-Dolphins matched to within one tick too [4][6][7]. The sportsbook anchor pays only where no desk and no Kalshi book compete.
4. **Pinnacle's public API no longer exists.** It closed on 23 Jul 2025 [9]. Your "sharp anchor" is now a reseller feed, a consensus from an aggregator, or a scraper (a terms breach). Budget for it and check its latency yourself; vendor latency claims are marketing.
5. **Competition shows up as fill rate before it shows up as PnL.** The documented bot's fill rate fell 37.4%, 15%, 5%, 1.0% while locked-arb profit went $2,865, $4,158, $1,254, $17 [3]. Track fill rate per sport weekly and kill a sport when it halves twice.
6. **The devig method moves more PnL than the model.** Shin's method cut modelled favourite ROI from 11.7% to 1.5% [3]. Test devig choices against settlement outcomes before tuning anything else.
7. **Rule mismatches turn hedges into bets.** A DNP player prop is graded No on Polymarket, settles to the last pre-game price on Kalshi and is void at books [8]. Games cancelled without a make-up resolve 50-50 [5]. The documented bot had 236 cancelled matches in four months [2].
8. **Hedge capacity is consumable.** Books cut winning accounts to $2-14 stakes within days to months [E5]. The interviewed operator says he regenerates wallets after sportsbook bans [1], which shows that the outside hedge is a wasting asset.
9. **Top sports PnL is directional and volatile, not market making.** 14 of 18 top sports wallets reconstructed were directional buy-and-hold [E4], and kch123 lost $479k in 30 days in Q1 2026 [E4]. Do not benchmark a maker against the sports leaderboard.
10. **The biggest sports subsidies sit on the US venue.** The $32,000 live NFL moneyline LIP pool [E2] needs a KYC'd US account and in-play quoting, and state actions against sports contracts (Ninth Circuit, Connecticut, Missouri) can switch it off [E9].
