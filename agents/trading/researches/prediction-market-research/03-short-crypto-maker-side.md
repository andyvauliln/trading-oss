# 03 - Short-duration crypto up/down: maker / supply side (split-sell, merge, rebates, toxicity filter)
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: B (one peer-style paper and one dataset paper on the maker/retail split; A for fees, delays and live volume; C for per-wallet economics) · Last verified: 2026-09-24_

### Short-duration crypto up/down: maker / supply side
- **One-line summary**: Be the resting liquidity in the 5-minute, 15-minute, 1-hour and 4-hour "Up or Down" crypto markets. Mint Up+Down pairs for $1 and sell both legs above $1, or quote both sides around a spot-driven fair value. Merge leftovers back to pUSD within minutes, collect the 20% crypto maker rebate, and pull quotes in the windows where takers are informed or manipulative.
- **Category**: Market-making [high-turnover inventory business on recurring digital options]
- **Maturity**: Established for a small group of automated wallets, contested and thinning for newcomers. Hard evidence: in one day of BTC 5-minute trading (738,393 trades, $10.38M), makers finished +$45.3K and retail -$66.7K, with takers paying 1.8% [1]. Every taker price band lost money over 4,600 resolved 5-minute windows, and the profitable wallets were running split/merge maker books [5] (self-reported analysis). Against it: no liquidity-reward programme is live on any up/down market today (own check, 24 Sep 2026 [10]); the $1M August programme "has ended" [11]; the taker delay has been changed four times since June [4]; and a dataset paper built to trade these markets from Binance order flow produced a null result, -0.116 payoff units per attempted trade [2].
- **Requires AI?**: no. Fair value is a closed-form digital option on spot plus a partial-TWAP adjustment. Toxicity filters are statistical. AI adds nothing here that a volatility model does not.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Markets: every 5 minutes (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, BNB, DOGE, HYPE, ZEC), every 15 minutes, hourly and 4-hourly, a new binary asks whether the asset closes "Up" or "Down" versus its opening reference. Both the opening reference ("price to beat") and the settlement are Chainlink time-weighted averages (TWAP): 60 s windows for 5-minute markets since 14 Aug 2026, and for 15-minute and 4-hour markets since 7 Aug [4].
  2. Fair value: P(Up) = N(d), where d depends on (current spot or partial TWAP - reference) / (volatility x sqrt(time left)). Inside the final TWAP window, part of the average is already fixed, which shrinks the remaining variance.
  3. Supply side ("split-sell"): split $1 pUSD into 1 Up + 1 Down, rest asks on both legs whose sum exceeds $1 (for example 0.52 + 0.50), and merge any unsold pairs back to $1 before expiry [5][E1].
  4. Two-sided quoting: post-only bids and asks on each leg around fair value, cancelled whenever Binance moves by more than your threshold or the window nears settlement while at the money.
  5. Income: spread capture (usually a single tick, 1c or 0.1c [2]) plus the maker rebate, 20% of the taker fees in that market. Takers pay 0.07 x p x (1-p) per share, so the rebate is 0.35c/share at p = 0.50 [3][12]. It is paid daily in pUSD, pro rata by fee-equivalent within each market, with a $1 minimum.
  6. Recycle: capital turns every 5-15 minutes, so the same $1k can be quoted in dozens of windows a day.
- **What creates the edge**: Contract design produces a stream of short-horizon gambling flow, and the fee regime moved the edge from takers to makers. In January 2026 Polymarket introduced dynamic taker fees explicitly to curb latency arbitrage [E4]. Retail takers lose here faster than anywhere else: the share of profitable wallets falls from 60% in monthly contracts to 25% in 5-minute contracts, and $100 lasts 1.6 days in 5-minute markets against 404 days in monthly ones [6] (abstract via the brief). The maker's rent is realised by holding the favoured side into settlement, not by round-trip spread: 91.4% of persistent makers' profit in the BTC 5-minute sample arrived at settlement, almost all of it from buying the side priced 0.50-1.00 [1]. On the other side: retail up/down gamblers, and bots running latency strategies that no longer clear fees (file 12). They keep paying because the product is entertainment with a 5-minute feedback loop.
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k. Capital recycles every window, so returns scale with turnover and queue position rather than with balance. It is locked only for minutes. Per-window depth caps useful size: BTC 5-minute markets averaged ~$50k of volume per window over the 24 hours to 24 Sep 18:00 UTC (derived from [10]); alt-coin windows are 20-500x thinner.
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: Binance (or Coinbase) spot trades and book over WebSocket; Polymarket's PolyBolt WebSocket (live since 15 Sep 2026: licensed crypto prices and the Chainlink TWAP reference, CLOB API credentials required) [4]; CLOB market and user WebSockets; Gamma discovery of the next window (`5M`, `15M`, `1H`, `4H` tags). (2) Decision logic: a digital-option fair value with partial-TWAP handling; a short-horizon volatility estimator; toxicity rules (a threshold on spot moves within your reaction time, at-the-money seconds-to-expiry buckets, imbalance of recent taker flow); and inventory pairing logic that keeps orphan legs small. (3) Execution: post-only GTC, batch cancel-replace, split and merge through the gasless relayer (25 req/min on `/submit` [E1]), heartbeat, and order-rate budgeting (40 orders/s standard [E1]). (4) Risk: a per-window loss cap, a per-asset daily cap, a kill switch on feed divergence (Binance vs Chainlink), and no quoting across TWAP windows you cannot price. (5) Test: replay on 1-second L2 plus Binance data; the OpenMarket archive has 727M rows, Feb-May 2026 [2]. Build: 3-5 weeks for a solo developer with an existing CLOB client; the fair-value and toxicity work dominates.
- **Opportunity size**: Live volume over the 24 hours to 24 Sep 2026 18:00 UTC: 5-minute markets $15.4M (BTC $14.4M, ETH $0.60M, SOL $0.18M, XRP $0.09M, BNB $0.03M, DOGE $0.02M, HYPE $4k); 15-minute $3.48M; 1-hour $1.04M; 4-hour $0.16M [10]. That is ~$20M a day, well below the spring peak, when 5-minute markets did $2.3B in ~7 weeks and crypto fees were $23.7M in 83 days (~$286k/day) [E1]. At the spring fee level the crypto rebate pool was ~$57k/day (20%, derived); today's per-category pool is not published. The maker trading rent comes on top: makers +$45.3K on one day of BTC 5-minute flow (0.44% of notional, derived) [1]. Direction: shrinking since spring, and without reward programmes since August.
- **Pros**: Minutes-long capital lock-up and very high turnover; a deterministic rebate on every fill; the maker is protected by the taker delay (takers cannot cancel while pending, makers can) [12]; fair value is well defined; outcomes resolve automatically (no UMA or dispute risk).
- **Cons / risks**: Adverse selection: your first leg fills exactly when it is becoming less likely. One practitioner's pair bot completed 60 of 69 pairs, and the 9 orphan legs ate all the profit [9]. Speed: a median 347 ms quote response to large Binance moves marks the stale-quote line [2]. Settlement abuse: when 5-minute markets settled on a single snapshot, manipulators profited and retail bore ~93% of losses; 15-minute contracts showed much less [7]. Parameter churn: taker delay 250 ms, then 50 ms, then 150 ms within three months; TWAP windows changed twice in one week; the reward programme ended [4][11]. Competition: bots are 55-62% of fast-market volume, 6,189 bot addresses made 56M trades at $6-7 each [E1], and top-of-book spreads are already one tick in 91.9% of snapshots (as reported in the brief) [2].
- **Expected results**: Class-level: makers +$45.3K vs retail -$66.7K in 24 hours of BTC 5-minute trading [1]. Self-reported per wallet: a tracked supply-side wallet made ~$143K/month across ~39,000 predictions while showing pages of individual $3,800-$6,100 losing legs [5]. A maker-side bot on 5m/15m markets reported EV of $0.028-0.031 per trade at 78 trades/day, about $2.3/day [8]. The one fully documented research attempt (the taker side, using Binance order flow) failed out of sample [2]. For a new solo maker: no public data. Guess: with $5k-$10k on BTC 5m/15m, a net of -$20 to +$60/day in the first months, positive only if the toxicity filter keeps orphan-leg losses below spread + rebate.
- **Competitive landscape**: Crowded and automated. A handful of split/merge whales (SirMartingale and others) run thousands of windows a day [5]. Top-of-book is one tick, and quotes react in a few hundred milliseconds [2]. Polymarket offers KYC/KYB co-location in eu-west-2 (not for builders) [E1], so the fastest makers can sit next to the matching engine. BTC is contested. ETH, SOL and XRP are thinner but also have little flow. HYPE, BNB, DOGE and ZEC are nearly empty (under $35k/day each in 5-minute markets [10]). To compete you need sub-200 ms cancels from Dublin or co-location, a TWAP-aware fair value, and pairing discipline.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for two-sided quoting and split/merge from one identity. Prohibited: trading the underlying (Binance) to move the settlement TWAP. That is "manipulative trading" under the ToS and Rule 7.2(o) on Polymarket US [E9]. Hedging on Binance inside the settlement window can look identical to it, so do not hedge in the window. Grey: quoting from several own wallets (self-fill risk) [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: 1) Paper-quote BTC 15-minute and 5-minute markets for 7 days on the live WebSocket with your toxicity rules. Record would-be fills when the book trades through your price, pairing rate, orphan-leg PnL at settlement, and the rebate implied by your fills' fee-equivalent. 2) Replay the same rules on the OpenMarket 1-second archive (Feb-May 2026, 727M rows) [2]. Adjust for today's 150 ms delay and 60 s TWAP. 3) Pass: simulated net > 0.2% of filled notional after orphan losses across 2,000+ fills and at least 5 days, with no single day below -1% of capital. Fail: orphan-leg losses exceed spread + rebate on BTC, or fill rates under 5% because of one-tick queues. Cost: ~30 engineering hours; no capital in steps 1-2; a $1k live pilot for 14 days after.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: latency-critical. The median quote response to large Binance moves is 347 ms [2]. The venue's taker delay (150 ms since 4 Sep 2026 [4]) is the maker's protection window only if you can cancel inside it. Market makers who are slower than the move are the flow the fast takers trade against.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spot signal | Binance / Coinbase WebSocket, PolyBolt TWAP stream | you price the digital on a stale spot and get picked off | <20 ms feed-to-model from Dublin |
| Cancel on spot move | cancel round trip to the CLOB | fills land inside the 150 ms delay window against you; pending takers cannot cancel, but you can only if your cancel arrives first [12] | cancel round trip <100 ms from eu-west-1 |
| Near-expiry at-the-money windows | partial-TWAP maths, seconds-to-expiry rules | the classic manipulation and informed window; being slow means holding the losing side into settlement [7] | pull at-the-money quotes in the last 60-90 s unless the fair value is TWAP-aware |
| Queue position | one-tick books, 15-order batches | at one tick you fill only after faster makers, i.e. mostly when the price is about to move | re-quote within 1-2 book events |
| Split / merge recycling | relayer throughput, 25 req/min | capital sits in unsold pairs across windows | batch merges each window |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Delay regime: at 250 ms a 100 ms canceller escaped most spot-driven fills; at 50 ms only co-located makers did; at 150 ms a Dublin bot is borderline (derived from [2][4]). (2) The 347 ms median response means the typical maker is slower than the fast taker; the edge belongs to the tail of fast makers. (3) Rate limits: quoting 8 assets x 2 legs x 2 sides across 5m and 15m windows with sub-second refresh can exceed the standard 40 orders/s [E1]. Restrict to BTC and ETH, or earn the higher tier.
- **Where to run it**: AWS eu-west-1 at minimum. KYC/KYB co-location in eu-west-2 is available to non-builder traders [E1]. It is the only venue-sanctioned way to close the gap to the fastest makers, and it is worth it only after a paper run shows positive markouts at Dublin latency.

### Results by investment size
Assumptions: BTC and ETH 5m/15m only; fill notional 3-10x capital per day; maker margin of 0.0-0.4% of filled notional after orphan losses (the class-level 0.44% in [1] is an average over all makers, with no rebate then); no liquidity rewards (none live [10]). All figures are guesses.

| Capital | Workable? | How it is deployed | Gross / month | Running costs / month | Net / month | Net / capital / month | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1k | yes, as a live test | BTC 15m only, 20-100 shares per side, split-sell pairs | $0-$300 (spread + rebate - orphans) | $70-100 (Dublin VPS) | -$100 to +$200 | -10% to +20% | fixed cost is 7-10% of capital a month; one bad orphan day wipes a week |
| $5k | yes | BTC + ETH, 5m and 15m, 100-500 shares | $0-$1,050 | $70-150 | -$300 to +$900 | -6% to +18% | queue position at one tick; cancel latency |
| $10k | yes | BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP, all horizons, 200-1,000 shares | $0-$2,000 | $100-300 (bigger instance, possible co-location) | -$500 to +$1,800 | -5% to +18% | BTC window depth (~$50k volume per 5-minute window) and toxicity near expiry |

- **Minimum sensible capital**: ~$2k. Enough to quote meaningful size in BTC 15-minute windows while fixed costs stay under 5% of capital a month.
- **Capacity ceiling**: ~$50k-$100k for a solo operator. Past that you are a large share of each BTC window's volume and your own fills move the book. Alt books are too thin to absorb more.

### What you are probably underestimating
1. **There is no reward programme any more.** The $1M August crypto TWAP programme ($550k for 5-minute, $350k for 15-minute, $100k for 4-hour markets) "has ended" [11]. On 24 Sep 2026 none of the live 5m, 15m, 1h or 4h up/down markets returned a reward config [10]. Income is spread plus the 20% rebate, so any business case built in August is out of date.
2. **The taker delay is a moving target.** It was a 500 ms bump until ~February, 250 ms from 5 June, 50 ms from 17 August and 150 ms from 4 September [E1][4]. Your whole toxicity model depends on it. Read it per market (`itode` on `/clob-markets`) and replay every rule under each regime.
3. **The TWAP changes the fair value in the last minute.** Both the price to beat and the settlement are 60 s Chainlink averages [4]. Inside the final window part of the average is known, so the naive N(d) on spot misprices the digital exactly when fills are most toxic (file 82 prices this in detail).
4. **BTC is the market; alt books are nearly empty.** Over the day to 24 Sep, BTC was 94% of 5-minute volume. HYPE did $4k, and ZEC 5-minute markets did $19 in 24 hours [10]. "Less contested alt books" mostly means "no takers".
5. **The 91.4% figure says you get paid at settlement.** Holding the favoured side into settlement is where makers made money in the BTC 5-minute sample [1]. That is also the window where manipulators operated under the old snapshot settlement [7], and where informed takers pick off stale quotes. The profit and the danger sit in the same seconds.
6. **Orphan legs are the P&L, not a rounding error.** A pair bot completing 60 of 69 pairs lost everything on the 9 orphans [9]. Budget orphan losses explicitly per window and stop quoting a leg when its twin cannot fill at your price.
7. **Most of the other side is bots.** Bots are 55-62% of fast-market volume [E1], and the most famous "latency" wallet turned out to be 94% symmetric YES+NO buys, a paired supply-side structure [E4]. Retail bleed funds the pool, but your fills increasingly come from other algorithms.
8. **Hedging on Binance can look like manipulation.** 821 accounts made $8.2M by moving Binance prices into settlement snapshots [E9], and Polymarket US Rule 7.2(o) bans settlement-window manipulation outright [E9]. Keep any hedge trading outside the TWAP window and log why you traded.
9. **Rebates are per market and paid only above $1 a day.** In thin alt windows your share can be 100% of a pool worth cents. Rebates below $1 a day do not pay out [3], so spread quoting across many tiny markets can earn nothing.
