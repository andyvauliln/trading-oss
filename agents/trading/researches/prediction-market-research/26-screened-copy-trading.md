# 26 - Copy trading with forensic and statistical wallet screening
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: B (A for the skill-distribution papers and for the own live measurements of feed latency in two windows, leader tapes and post-fill repricing; C for the three self-reported follower results; D for vendor claims) · Last verified: 2026-09-28_

### Copy trading with forensic and statistical wallet screening
- **One-line summary**: Mirror a handful of slow-conviction wallets whose skill survives a sign-randomisation null and an out-of-sample re-test, detect their fills from on-chain `OrderFilled` events in about 2 s (the Data API feeds ingest in batches and stall for minutes), copy only their taker entries through a gate stack, and mirror their exits. The measured uplift is small (+1.62 pp per market versus the follower's own trades [2]), the three public follower results are one $500 account up $256.85 [1], one paper account up $171 on $10,000 [9] and one $150 account down 8% [12], and the copy-bot product category has lost 88% of its volume since April [23]. Lead with those numbers, not with the build.
- **Category**: Information edge [flow; the "information" is another wallet's judgment, read off a public ledger]
- **Maturity**: Emerging, and shrinking as a market. Hard data exists for the premise (a small skilled minority exists and partly persists: 3.14% of accounts with 10+ events are skilled winners, 44% of those keep the label on held-out data [3][4]; top 5% by rolling accuracy persist out of sample [6]) and for the aggregate return to imitation (+1.62 pp, abstract only [2]). Nothing above self-reported grade exists for a follower's realised PnL: +$256.85 on $500 in five weeks with seven tracked wallets and $391 of notional [1]; +$171 on a $10,000 paper bankroll over six weeks, after a filter that turned -$1,937 on 191 trades into +$2,108 on 81, although the author's own counterfactual shows the 141 skipped entries would have made +$1,485 too (Welch t = 0.69), so the turnaround is the leader's regime, not the filter [9]; $150 to $138 copying a latency wallet one second late (34 wins, 51 losses) [12]. The demand side collapsed: the five named copy bots routed $125.2M in April 2026 and $15.3M in September to date; PolyCop's monthly active users went from 3,502 to 1,071 [23]. The academic authors themselves did not test following [3].
- **Requires AI?**: no. Selection is statistics (randomisation nulls, Wilson intervals, walk-forward re-tests); detection and execution are deterministic. An LLM is optional for reading a leader's market rules before mirroring a position in a category you do not understand (file 64), never for deciding whom to copy.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Universe: every wallet with 30+ resolved positions, from the Data API leaderboard (`/v1/leaderboard`, PnL after fees and before rebates; its `vol` is share volume, ~1.8-2.1x USDC volume) [18], from `/closed-positions?user=` paged 50 at a time (it is sorted by realised PnL descending, so the first page of any wallet is all winners) [21], or from the public per-user dataset (daily PnL, ~87 behavioural features, 2022-11-11 to 2026-03-29) [14].
  2. Skill test: hold every position fixed in market, size, price and time, flip only the direction 10,000 times, and keep wallets whose realised PnL sits in the top tail of that null [3][4]. Done on the two all-time leaders: Theo4's 22 closed positions (14 markets, 9 events) give p = 0.006, Fredi9999's 66 positions (45 markets, 30 events) p = 0.02 (two runs of 10,000 draws each), but one event carries 65% and 56% of their absolute PnL [21]. Add Wilson lower bounds on event-level win rate, a cap on the top event's share of |PnL| (say 25%), and a held-out re-test on the most recent quarter; expect 44-56% of the survivors to fail it [3][4].
  3. Copyability filter (the step most builds skip): drop wallets whose fills are mostly maker rows (RN1: 96-97% of fills in the overlap window are maker fills across two reads; Theo4 96%, Fredi9999 99%), whose taker tickets are tiny and frequent (e46m3: 1,027 taker fills a day, $12 median, 664 markets in a day), or whose edge is latency (swisstony's in-play soccer, file 18) [19][12]. State the flag: `/trades?user=` defaults to `takerOnly=true`; the maker rows appear only with `takerOnly=false`, and neither the v1 nor the v2 row carries a maker / taker field, so the flag is the only way to tell from the API [19]. On-chain, every match emits one `OrderFilled` per maker order with the taker's address and one for the taker order with the exchange as taker, so the chain says which side the leader was on in the same block [20].
  4. Detection: subscribe to `OrderFilled` on the V2 exchanges (`0xE111…996B`, `0xe222…0F59`) through an RPC WebSocket; Polygon blocks arrive every ~2 s and a good gateway serves them at block time [20]. Fallback: `GET /v2/trades?user=…`, with the caveat that it ingests in batches: in one window the same 45 fills appeared there 4.4-16.5 s after the block (median 6.4 s, `timestamp` equal to the block time); 55 minutes later its newest row aged from 105 to 195 s over 90 s of polling, none of 77 on-chain fills from three blocks showed up within 90 s, and over the next seven minutes the global feed refreshed exactly once (about five minutes between batches) while `?user=` queries for RN1, who fills every ~5 s, stayed frozen for six minutes [20]. Do not use the v1 `/trades` feed for detection: its newest row was 35-395 s old in eleven samples at 20:07-20:10 UTC and 495-545 s old at 21:09 UTC on 2026-09-28 [20].
  5. Gate stack: price band around the leader's fill (repricing of the leaders' last 25 fills within 20 minutes was two-sided and noisy: medians +1.0 to +1.2c in one window, -2c to 0c in another, tails -14c to +12c, most rows being maker fills in moving in-play books) [22], slippage cap, per-leader and per-topic caps, minimum resting depth, a fee gate (taker fee = C x rate x p(1-p), 1.25c per share at p = 0.5 in sports, zero in geopolitics) [E1], a rules gate for the market, and a kill switch on venue incidents [E1].
  6. Execution: FAK taker order sized as a fraction of the leader's fill; never chase beyond the band (the $500 bot's 3c gate rejected 69 signals in 48 hours, then a loosened gate paid $18 for a $9 target) [1].
  7. Exit engine: mirror the leader's sells, watch for `PositionsMerge` (a leader can exit by merging YES+NO without a sale) [10][E4], add time decay and a stop, redeem resolved positions.
  8. Ledger: reconcile every fill on-chain; since 2026-07-17 FAK/FOK responses return `tradeIDs`, not `transactionHashes`, and `/v2/user-pnl` is intermittent (file 14) [11].
- **What creates the edge**: Free-riding on a small persistent minority on a transparent ledger [3][4][6], plus the crowd's inability to tell them from lucky one-shots: only 12% of top winners beat the randomised benchmark and ~60% of lucky winners turn into losers out of sample [4]. The gains are concentrated (the top 1% of users capture 76.5% of all trading gains, and monthly performance is only weakly persistent [5]), so the screen is looking for a few hundred wallets, not a class. The counterparty is whoever rests the quote the leader hits and, seconds later, the quote you hit; they lose to the leader's information and lose again to you only if the price has not moved yet. That is why the whole return is a race against drift: within minutes the leaders' recent fills reprice by cents in either direction (medians -2c to +1.2c across two windows, tails -14c to +12c), and a taker copy pays the spread on top [22]. The +1.62 pp differential is the entire budget for fees, spread and drift [2].
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k. Nothing forces size (5-share, $1 minimum orders [E1]); capacity does. The best leaders' taker fills are $250 (RN1) to $2,000-2,500 (Theo4, Fredi9999 in 2024) at the median [19], and thin books move 3-8c before a follower arrives [1]. Capital is locked as long as the leader holds, weeks for election and geopolitics wallets; the copied position cannot be exited faster than the market allows.
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: RPC WebSocket for `OrderFilled` (the V2 event has four topics and 224 bytes of data, two words more than V1; E6 says the builder code is among them) [20][E6], `/v2/trades` as fallback, CLOB market WebSocket `last_trade_price` messages (price, size, side, timestamp, tx hash; no latency statement in the docs) [26], `/closed-positions`, `/v2/user-stats` [25], and a backfill source (HyperSync via `poly_data`, or the Hugging Face dataset) [E6][14]. (2) Screening: nulls, Wilson bounds, walk-forward, copyability classifier (maker share, ticket size, frequency, category). (3) Execution: FAK with `maxSpend`, cancel-replace, rate-limit budget (40 orders/s standard) [E1]. (4) Risk: per-leader cap, per-event cap, drawdown halt, venue-incident halt. (5) Paper mode that records the fillable price at +3 s and +10 s and the leader's later marks. Build time for a solo developer: 4-6 weeks (the Reddit author took five [1]; the vendor review budgets 60-120 hours just to harden an open-source clone [13]). The owner already runs this; the marginal work is the screening and the copyability filter, one to two weeks.
- **Opportunity size**: Small and falling. The addressable pool is the taker edge of copyable leaders, not the leaderboard: e46m3's month is $3.79M of PnL, but it is 47,889 trades since April with $751k realised and $1.19M unrealised [25], a stream of $12 tickets no follower can ride [19]. Aggregate copy-bot flow, the best proxy for how much money tries, was $125.2M in April, $28.5M in August and $15.3M in September to date, while all builder-routed volume fell 24% over the same span [23]. The measured differential (+1.62 pp per market) on a $10k book turning over twice a month is ~$320 a month before costs [2] (derived). Trend: shrinking, and each rule change (V2, pUSD, Data API v2) breaks a cohort of bots [11].
- **Pros**: The premise is true and quantified (skilled minority, partial persistence) [3][4][6]; the tape is public and free, and every fill is enumerable by exchange event, by wallet, and by routing app (`/builder/trades?builder_code=` needs no auth and shows PolyCop's followers paying 0.5% per fill on ~$2 tickets) [24]; the gate stack and exit engine transfer directly to files 27 and 81; paper mode is cheap and decisive; no model to maintain.
- **Cons / risks**: Edge: +1.62 pp is the average over all imitators, most of whom copy the wrong wallets; a self-selected 67% of followers underperform their leader (vendor figure, method unstated) [7]. Execution: repricing of up to 12-14c within minutes, in either direction, on the leaders' own recent fills [22]; taker fee on every entry and exit (1.25c per share at mid in sports, 1.75c in crypto) [E1]; thin-book impact from the leader's own order [1]. Selection: 44% persistence means half your screened list is noise [3], and for one in five losing wallets the cost of taking liquidity alone flips the sign, which is the follower's cost structure exactly [5]; one-event wallets pass the null and never repeat [21]. Adverse selection: leaders that know they are copied can sell into the cascade (hazard paragraph below). Platform: V2 cutover wiped every resting order, changed the order struct and the collateral; the v1 Data API is frozen and lagging [11][20]. Legal: copying public fills is allowed; selling auto-executing copy software is a CTA registration question, and leaders who talk their book create manipulation exposure for their followers' counterparties (E9, Santos) [E9]. Custody: many third-party copy bots hold keys in a Telegram session or are drainers [15][E6].
- **Expected results**: no public data of audit grade. Self-reported: +$256.85 on $500 in ~5 weeks (7 wallets, $391 traded) [1]; +$171 on $10,000 paper in 6 weeks after a filter change (-$1,937 before it; the skipped trades would have made +$1,485 as well, so the filter is unproven by the author's own test), with 5.6-minute median copy delay and paper fills the author calls a lower bound on cost [9]; -8% in 85 copies of a latency leader [12]; manual copying from Telegram alerts captures "roughly 22% of the leader's realised edge" (vendor) [13]. Reasoned range for a screened, on-chain-detected book of 3-5 leaders (guess): -5% to +4% a month with fat tails in both directions, the tail on the upside being one leader's one-shot event and the tail on the downside being the same event going the other way.
- **Competitive landscape**: Crowded on tools, empty on evidence. 43 of the 119 bots in one directory are copy bots, ROI author-published and unverified [16]; PolyCop (0.5% per fill, non-custodial key inside Telegram) [15], Polygun (1% flat per fill, gas sponsored, $1 minimum deposit, per an affiliate review site) [27], Kreo, PolyBot and Stand.Trade route the flow [23]; Stand tracks 1,500 wallets and 5,000 strategies [10]; ~12 open-source clones poll every 30-90 s and lose their maintainer within six weeks [13]. What it takes to be competitive: a screening pipeline that the vendors do not have (PolyCop tells users to look at Top Holders and X for wallets [15]), on-chain detection, and a copyability filter; nobody publishes follower-realised PnL.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for copying public fills from one account [E9]. Grey for: selling or running auto-executing copies for others (CTA registration; Letter 26-25 excludes signals and discretion) [E9]; coordinating with a leader ("acting in concert" to move a price) [E9]; a leader or service trading ahead of its own followers' orders (Rule 7.2(j) front-running) [E9]. Do not follow wallets whose edge is non-public information: that flow is file 30's subject and its counterparties are your losses.
- **How you'd validate it**: Walk-forward on the public per-user dataset [14]: classify on 2024-01 to 2025-06, freeze, paper-copy 2025-07 to 2026-03 with the fill price set to the first trade after the leader's plus 10 s in the same market (own recorder or Jon Becker's parquet [E5]) plus the taker fee and 1c of drift; metric: follower PnL per dollar copied versus copying the raw PnL top 50; sample: 30+ leaders, 500+ copies; pass bar: positive after costs in both halves of the test period and better than the leaderboard baseline by 1 pp per market; cost: two days and $0. Then a 30-day live shadow at +3 s on the on-chain feed with $50 tickets ($1-2k at risk): pass if realised PnL is within 1c per share of the shadow.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: execution-sensitive. On the leaders this strategy is allowed to copy, the price moves 1c at the median over the first 1-14 minutes [22] and the return is decided by whom you copy and how you size; on latency leaders the trade is dead at one second [12], which is why the copyability filter exists.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery / screening | `/closed-positions`, leaderboard, HF dataset; nulls and re-tests | none from speed; a stale screen copies last quarter's lucky wallets | weekly re-screen, monthly re-test |
| Detection | RPC WebSocket on `OrderFilled`; `/v2/trades` fallback | on-chain ~2 s; v2 feed 4-16 s when flowing but batch stalls of 2-3+ min; v1 feed 35-545 s; OSS polling 30-90 s [20][13] | ~3 s on-chain; the v2 feed only as a reconciliation source |
| Entry | FAK at the ask within a band of the leader's price | repricing -14c to +12c within 20 min, medians -2c to +1.2c [22]; each cent is 1 pp of the +1.62 pp budget [2] | fill within 10 s or skip |
| Sizing | leader's ticket, resting depth | oversizing moves thin books 3-8c [1] | 10-25% of the leader's fill, capped per event |
| Exit | leader sells, merges, or resolution | a missed merge exit leaves you long alone [10] | watch `PositionsMerge` and sells; time stop |
| Reconciliation | `tradeIDs`, on-chain logs, `/v2/user-pnl` | ghost positions and mis-marked PnL [1][11] | nightly on-chain rebuild |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Measured, own, two windows: at 20:05-20:13 UTC 45 of 45 on-chain fills from three consecutive blocks appeared in `/v2/trades` 4.4-16.5 s after the block (median 6.4 s) while the v1 `/trades` feed's newest row was 35-395 s old; at 21:07-21:10 UTC the v2 feed's newest row aged from 105 to 195 s over 90 s of polling, none of 77 on-chain fills from three blocks appeared within 90 s, and v1 was 495-545 s behind [20]. At 21:10-21:15 UTC the global v2 feed refreshed once in five minutes and the per-user v1 and v2 queries for RN1 did not refresh at all (297-353 s old) [20]. Both Data API feeds ingest in batches of minutes at busy hours; only the chain streams (blocks served 0-1 s after their timestamp). A copier on either feed or on 30-90 s polling [13] is the "spread business" counterparty. (2) Measured, own, two windows: on the leaders' 25 most recent fills, buying the same size at the current book cost 1.0c (RN1, 1-8 min old) and 1.2c (e46m3, 0.5-14 min old) more at the median at 20:20 UTC, but 2.0c less (RN1, 0.2-11 min, range -14c to +6c) and 0c (e46m3, 3-20 min, range -1c to +12c) at 21:09 UTC [22]; the rows are mostly maker fills in moving in-play books, so this is repricing, not follower slippage; a leader's $2,890 buy at 0.59 was still 0.59 offered three minutes later, while a $10,946 buy at 0.51 was offered at 0.53 seven hours later [22]. (3) Self-reported: one second late on swisstony turned a $156 average profit per trade into a $0.15 loss [12]; a 5-minute polling bot still made +$171 on paper after filtering to pre-game entries [9]. (4) Venue: the 150 ms crypto taker delay (file 12) does not matter here; rate limits (40 orders/s standard) [E1] and the 08-31 and 09-19 incidents that left cancel-only windows [E1] do.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted with a low-latency RPC; the CLOB's location matters less than the block feed. Pay for two RPC providers before paying for Dublin hosting; do not pay for L2 history for this file.

### What you are probably underestimating
1. **Your feed choice is worth minutes, not milliseconds, and the v2 feed is not the fix.** On 2026-09-28 the v1 `/trades` feed's newest row was 35-395 s old at 20:07-20:10 UTC and 495-545 s old at 21:09 UTC; `/v2/trades` showed the same on-chain fills 4.4-16.5 s after the block in the first window, then stalled in the second: its newest row aged from 105 to 195 s over 90 s of polling none of 77 fills from three blocks appeared within 90 s, the global feed then refreshed once in five minutes and RN1's per-user query not at all for six, while the block itself was served 0-1 s after its timestamp by the Tenderly gateway [20]. A bot "detecting in 2-3 s" on-chain [1] and a bot on either Data API feed are minutes apart, which is the whole drift budget several times over [22].
2. **The leaderboard is a maker list.** In the overlap window between the taker-only and all-fills tapes, RN1's fills are 96-97% maker rows, Theo4's 96%, Fredi9999's 99% [19]. You cannot copy a resting bid; you can only copy the taker subset, and for RN1 that subset is ~1,060-1,090 fills a day at ~$250 [19]. `takerOnly=true`, the default, is for once the right filter, but it lives on the feed that lags minutes: no v1 or v2 row says maker or taker, so the only channel that tells you within seconds whether the leader took or was hit is the on-chain `OrderFilled` pair (one record per maker order naming the taker, one for the taker order naming the exchange) [19][20].
3. **Half your screened list is noise by construction.** 44% of skilled-classified accounts keep the label out of sample [3]; 60% of lucky winners become losers when re-checked [4]; the top-50 monthly board shares 10 wallets with the all-time top 50 and the weekly board shares 5 [18]. Size each leader as if it has a coin-flip chance of being real.
4. **A significant null does not mean repeatable.** Theo4 passes the sign-randomisation test at p ≈ 0.006 on 9 events and Fredi9999 at p ≈ 0.02 on 30 (two runs of 10,000 draws), yet one event is 65% and 56% of their absolute PnL and neither has closed a position since January 2025 [21]. Require a top-event share below 25% and activity in the last 90 days, or you are copying 2024.
5. **Frequency and ticket size kill the copy before drift does.** e46m3, second on the September board with $3.79M, made 47,889 trades since April [25] and hits 664 markets in a day at $12 a fill [19]; at 1.25c per share of taker fee and 1.2c of median drift [22][E1] the copy is a fee machine. The $500 bot's best leader was a lottery-ticket buyer at 1-3c [1]: copyable, but only as a longshot book (file 24).
6. **The drift you measure minutes later is noise until you measure it at +3 s on taker fills only.** On the leaders' last 25 fills the live book was 1.0-1.2c worse at the median in one window (5c on a $47 tennis fill after 8 minutes, 7c on a $14 weather fill after 7 minutes) and 0-2c better at the median fifty minutes later, with tails from -14c to +12c, because RN1's rows are maker fills in in-play books that move both ways [22]; a $10,946 NFL total bought at 0.51 was 0.53 offered that evening [22]. The measured differential is +1.62 pp per market [2]; there is no room for a chase, and a repricing statistic that mixes maker rows in tells you nothing about your own slippage.
7. **The product category collapsed, and that changes the tape.** Five copy bots went from $125.2M in April to $15.3M in September to date; Polycule from $13.6M in December to $13.6k; PolyCop's users from 3,502 to 1,071 [23]. Fewer copy cascades means less follower flow to fade (file 27) and fewer bait targets, but also fewer people making the leaders' quotes stale for you.
8. **Leaders can see you and can exit without selling.** Every V2 fill carries the routing app's builder code and PolyCop's fills are public without auth [24][E6]; the Oracle's interview lists activity spread across several wallets, iceberg accumulation and merge exits as standard concealment [10]; the HN thread's summary of why copying fails is a leader who buys, waits for the followers, and sells into them [8]. Selling into copy-followers and thin-book front-running of a leader's market orders are the follower-flow trades of file 27 and are flagged there; here they are your hazard: treat a leader whose fills cluster right before large opposite-side flow from wallets tagged with copy-bot builder codes as bait, and a leader who talks their book in public as a Santos-type manipulation risk for the counterparties you are about to join [E9].
9. **Your ledger endpoints lie by omission.** `/closed-positions` returns 50 rows per page sorted by realised PnL descending, so every first-page "win rate" is 100% [21]; `/v2/user-pnl` returned empty objects for stretches on 2026-09-28 (file 14; file 25's nine calls the same evening all returned data); `/v2/user-stats` moved e46m3's realised PnL from $751k to $681k and its unrealised from $1.19M to $1.26M between two reads 50 minutes apart [25]; since 2026-07-17 FAK/FOK responses carry `tradeIDs` instead of transaction hashes [11]; the bot in [1] carried ghost positions through the pUSD migration. Rebuild positions from `OrderFilled`, `PositionsMerge` and `PayoutRedemption` nightly.
10. **The academic uplift is not a strategy result.** +1.62 pp is the average difference between a follower's copied and non-copied trades in the same market, from an abstract nobody in this survey could open past the abstract [2]; the persistence papers did not test following [3]; the "public fills do not identify the quote lifecycle" paper says you cannot tell a market maker from a taker from the tape alone, and its sample is 2.3 days of the legacy exchange [17]. Your own shadow book is the only evidence that will ever exist for your leaders.
