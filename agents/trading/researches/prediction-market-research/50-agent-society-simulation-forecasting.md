# 50 - Agent-society and wargame simulation forecasting for geopolitics date ladders
_Band: speculative · Evidence grade: D (no backtest, scored record or PnL for the strategy anywhere; A for the market baseline measured here from Gamma and CLOB; B for the adjacent wargaming literature, mostly preprints; C for bridge-repository self-reports) · Last verified: 2026-09-29_

### Agent-society and wargame simulation forecasting
- **One-line summary**: Run many multi-agent crisis rollouts (actors plus an umpire, or a MiroFish / OASIS persona swarm), turn outcome frequencies into a curve over the ladder's deadlines, shrink it toward a reference-class hazard and trade the residual on fee-free geopolitics "by <date>" ladders. Unproven: no scored record exists, the one self-scored pilot lost to the market on every scored event, and on 160 resolved rungs the price beats a plain base rate at every horizon.
- **Category**: Speculative-novel [directional trading on fee-free geopolitics ladders; secondary use as a scenario generator for files 17, 25 and 70]
- **Maturity**: Experimental and unproven on any market, not the brief's "Emerging" (its critic notes are right). Polymarket attempts are hobby repositories: ten match "mirofish polymarket" on GitHub, the largest has 130 stars, and none publishes a backtest, accuracy or PnL [3][7]; PolFish paper-trades without results [6]; the only self-scored pilot, AXRZCE/Prophet, reports "Market forecasts beat simulation forecasts on all scored Track C events" (0 of 16, on short-deadline hard-data, culture and price markets its author calls stress tests) [5]; a public 200-agent run gave 47.9% on "Strait of Hormuz traffic returns to normal by end of April?" against a 31% price, and the market resolved No [8][10]. The adjacent field is negative: off-the-shelf LLMs escalate in wargames [16]; against 214 national-security experts they play more aggressively, keep a "farcical harmony" in simulated dialogue and ignore player traits [15]; they diverge from human play over rounds while justifying moves in a normative-cooperative frame [17]; coercive rhetoric moves -0.86 to +0.80 with the language of play, in opposite directions by model [20]; only 20 of 223 wargame studies let the model both act and adjudicate, as this strategy needs [19]; role collapse and escalation-through-adjudication are named failure modes [18]. Positive adjacent results beat other LLMs, never a market [21][22]. OASIS-class simulators are validated only on propagation scale (~30% normalised RMSE), unused here [2][E8]. Keep it as the owner's seed; treat it as weak.
- **Requires AI?**: yes. LLM agents play the actors and the umpire; one call compiles the scenario (actors, red lines, resolution event, tripwires) from the rules and dated news. Base rate, shrinkage, market blend, sizing and risk limits are deterministic and fitted on your own resolved log.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Universe: open geopolitics events from Gamma (`/events?tag_slug=geopolitics`, 100 per page). A ladder is one question with several deadline rungs; a "by" ladder is cumulative (later rungs ≥ earlier), a "continues through" ladder a survival curve (later ≤ earlier) [10].
  2. Scenario compile: one frontier call turns rules and dated news into 4-6 actors (objectives, red lines), a chance-event table, the exact resolution event and tripwires (observable precursors of each branch).
  3. Rollouts (sub-variant B): 50-200 stochastic playthroughs of 8-12 turns, one simulated week per turn so turns map onto rung dates; opposing actors on different model families; a separate umpire adjudicates moves and draws chance events from the table. Sub-variant A swaps the actors for a MiroFish / OASIS swarm (the bridge's examples: 48-60 persona agents, 28-40 rounds [3]).
  4. Curve: record the first simulated week in which the resolution event occurs; the curve prices every rung at once, coherent by construction.
  5. Shrink: pool in log-odds with a reference-class hazard (the constant hazard below, or file 70's procedural clock), weights fitted on your own resolved ledger; pre-register the null "shrunk sim = base rate only".
  6. Anchor: blend with the mid (file 21's best blend put 0.67 on the market); trade only where the gap exceeds the half-spread plus your measured calibration error (no fee).
  7. Tripwires (sub-variant C): watch news and OSINT for the precursors and reprice the whole ladder when one fires (the race itself is file 17's).
  8. Playbook only (sub-variant D): skip steps 5-6 and hand the branches to a discretionary trader (file 25).
- **What creates the edge**: If anything, structure rather than level accuracy: (a) one curve for the whole ladder where retail prices rungs one at a time; (b) conditional playbooks for faster, consistent repricing when a tripwire hits; (c) no fee, so the hurdle is the spread, a median 1.4c across 652 open rungs (48% at 1c or less) [10][13]. The payer would be the retail YES buyer of dramatic outcomes: rungs priced 15-35c resolved Yes 16.7% of the time at a 23.7% average price [11], the "nothing ever happens" pattern a 986-star meme bot trades blindly [23] and file 24 harvests without simulation. The strongest counter-argument is measured here: on 160 resolved rungs from 152 events (2025-2026, ≥ $10k volume), market Brier is 0.153 one day after listing, 0.075 thirty days before the deadline, 0.045 at seven days and 0.018 at one day, against 0.198 / 0.119 / 0.101 / 0.088 for a leave-future-out Yes-share base rate (every event-clustered 95% interval excludes zero); a time-aware constant hazard does worse at listing and 30 days (0.274, 0.105) and only ties the price at 7 and 1 days (0.060, 0.026), where the sample holds just 7 and 3 Yes outcomes [11]. A simulation shrunk toward that base rate starts 0.045 Brier behind at listing and must recover it from rollouts whose bias changes sign across models, prompts and languages [15][17][20]; ten same-family agents ≈ 1.4 independent forecasters [E8]; ladders are already coherent (one mid-price inversion in 189 adjacent "by" pairs, none executable) [10]; and the other side includes insiders (152 military-market wallets won 97.2% of the time, file 30).
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k, capped by depth, not capital. On 36 sampled open rungs priced 0.10-0.90 the median book held $89 of asks within 2c of mid and $147 within 5c (75th percentile $159 / $402), although Gamma lists a median $11.3k of liquidity per rung [12]. Lock-up is long: open rungs have a median 94 days to the deadline, sampled markets a median 34 days left one day after listing [10][11]. Positions: $50-300 per rung across 20-60 rungs.
- **Technical requirements**: Built on file 21's stack (Gamma and CLOB feeds, date-filtered retrieval, calibration ledger, deterministic trading layer). Agent design sketch: (1) Scenario compiler, one frontier call per ladder (step 2), 5-15 tripwires. (2) Actors, 4-6 per scenario (Iran ladder: US executive, Iranian leadership, Israel, a mediator, a domestic spoiler): each sees public moves to date and its own private brief, never the price; opposing actors on different families (cross-family mixing cuts error correlation 0.68 → 0.40 [E8]). (3) Umpire from a third family: sees every move, applies the chance table and rules each turn, in the contract's own words, whether the event has happened; free-form world invention by the umpire is the "escalation-through-adjudication" failure [18]. (4) Aggregation: rollout curve → rung probabilities → `logit p = a + b·logit p_sim + c·logit p_base` fitted on your ledger only → market blend; null b = 0 pre-registered. (5) Cost, a design estimate, not measured: ~130k input and 16k output tokens per rollout (5 actors × 8 turns × 2.5k / 0.3k plus the umpire 8 × 4k / 0.5k) = $0.02 on GPT-6 Luna, $0.21 on Haiku 4.5, $0.42 on Sonnet 5 at file 21's list prices; 100 rollouts = $2-42 per ladder (half on batch), $1-21 per rung at the median two open rungs, versus $0.36-1.46 per forecast for file 21's plain forecaster. Self-reported swarm costs agree ($0.00-0.54 per prediction [6], ~$2.50 per run [5], $3-5 per 200-agent run [8]). (6) A leak-controlled replay harness, most of the work. Build: 4-8 weeks solo on top of file 21. MiroFish is AGPL-3.0, OASIS Apache-2.0 [1][2]: relevant only if you resell output.
- **Opportunity size**: 439 live geopolitics events hold $36.1M of Gamma liquidity; 248 are date ladders with 652 open rungs, $15.7M of liquidity and $2.84M of Gamma 24-hour volume (both legs of each fill, file 29), but the median ladder traded $93 that day and only 113 rungs did ≥ $1k; about one open ladder rung in ten under the tag carries `politics_fees`, so check `feesEnabled` per market [10]. 260 open rungs sit at 0.10-0.90, where a forecaster can matter: ~$40k of executable size per pass at ~$150 of near-touch depth each (derived). Flow concentrates in a few complexes: "US announces end of Iranian blockade by …?" $626k in the day; "US-Iran ceasefire continues through …?" $4.67M lifetime, ~$0.4M liquidity (brief, 20 September: $1.45M / $377k) [10]. 42-134 by-date events with a ≥ $10k rung closed per month in 2026 [10]. A real 2-point edge on the whole mid-priced set would be worth low thousands of dollars a month (derived); the pool rises and falls with wars.
- **Pros**: Fee-free, 1-2c spreads [13]; ladders resolve often enough to score; scenarios and tripwires help a discretionary trader (file 25) or news bot (file 17) even if the probabilities fail; reuses file 21's pipeline; cheap kill test.
- **Cons / risks**: Edge: the price beats the reference class [11], and a simulation must beat the price; the bias flips direction (escalatory [15][16], cooperative-framed [17], language-dependent [20]), so no fixed correction exists, and cosmetic prompt changes move simulated behaviour by up to 76 points [E8]. Evaluation: contamination is the default inside a training window (frontier-versus-small gap 35.8% → 8.9% post-cutoff [E8]); a 2-point gain takes ~690 questions to prove (below). Counterparty: informed flow ($143M of anomalous profit platform-wide; the largest cases, Iran strike and Maduro, are geopolitics [E4]; file 30). Resolution: "clear confirmation" wording and stacked disputes (US-Iran ceasefire: three rounds, ~10 days, file 15). Capital: rungs in one news complex lose together. Platform: no taker-rebate weight [14]. Cost: rollout spend can exceed gross PnL at small size.
- **Expected results**: no public data, theoretical only. Everything public is null or negative: 0 of 16 scored events won against the market [5]; a single LLM tied or beat a crude swarm on n = 5 with rank correlations flipping sign between runs (+0.71 ↔ -0.71) [4]; the Hormuz run erred the wrong way (Brier 0.229 on that question against 0.096 for the price, derived from [8][10]); no bridge publishes PnL [3][6][7]; viral MiroFish profit posts found by search had no wallet or primary data (unverified). Guess: the shrunk simulation ≈ base rate, and blending it into the price moves Brier by ±0.005 (crude hazard blends moved it from 0.153 to 0.155-0.167 at listing and from 0.045 to 0.044 at seven days, in sample [11]); trading PnL -2% to +1% of deployed capital a month before LLM costs, negative after them below $10k.
- **Competitive landscape**: The tooling is free and crowded: MiroFish 75,256 stars and 11,584 forks [1], OASIS 5,207 stars [2], ten MiroFish-Polymarket bridges with no scored record [3][7], and commercial wrappers selling simulated "stakeholder perspectives" at $2.99-29.99 a month that state "MiroFish has no published calibration record" [9]. The price is set by discretionary geopolitics specialists (file 25), news-latency bots (file 17), NO-side longshot sellers (file 24) and insiders (file 30), not by other simulators. Being competitive would mean a published ledger of several hundred leak-free ladder forecasts; nobody has one.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: grey. Trading your own model from one identity is allowed [E9]. War and violence contracts carry ethical and reputational risk: a March 2026 Democratic bill targets war-related markets and Israel has indicted air-force personnel over Iran-strike bets (Wikipedia-sourced in E9); more than 30 jurisdictions restrict Polymarket, with the US and UK close-only and France ISP-blocked since July 2026 [E9]. Geopolitics hosts one of the two CFTC event-contract insider complaints (a US soldier's $404k+ on "Maduro out by January 31, 2026?", file 30): keep timestamped forecast logs in case your trades precede news. Selling the output as signals raises the CTA and Letter 26-25 questions (file 21, [E9]). Frontier models may refuse military role-play (brief; not measured here).
- **How you'd validate it**: Phase 0 (done here, $0): baseline, base-rate arms, power [11]. Phase 1, retrospective kill test, 2-3 weeks, ~$300-2,000 of LLM spend (50-100 rollouts per event at batch prices). Universe: by-date ladders created and resolved after the training cutoff of every model in the panel (279 by-date events closed July-September 2026 [10]; the post-cutoff share depends on the panel); one rung per event, the one nearest 0.5 at forecast time. Forecast time: listing + 1 day, where LLM forecasters are most competitive [E8] and the price is weakest (Brier 0.153 [11]). Leakage control: retrieval hard-limited to documents dated before the forecast time; manual leak audit of 50 bundles. Six pre-registered arms: raw sim; shrunk sim (weights fitted on the first 40% of events in time order, then frozen); base rate only (leave-future-out share and constant hazard, as here); market mid at the same timestamp (CLOB `/prices-history`, ≤ 15-day windows); file 21's plain forecaster on the same bundle; a 0.67 market / 0.33 shrunk-sim blend. Metrics: Brier and log loss, Brier skill against the market (event-clustered paired bootstrap), proper-betting PnL `2(p − q)` at mid ± the 1-2c half-spread, zero fee. Kill if the shrunk sim does not beat base rate only with a 95% interval excluding zero, or if the blend's point estimate does not beat the market. ~279 events detect only gross effects (≥ 0.03 Brier), so passing earns Phase 2, not capital. Phase 2, prospective, 6-12 months, $1-3k: freeze the pipeline, forecast every new ladder within 24 hours of listing, paper-trade against recorded books. The base-rate-minus-price Brier difference has a standard deviation of 0.187 per question at listing, so a forecaster that departs from the price that much needs ~690 independent questions for a 2-point gain at 80% power (two-sided 5%) and ~2,760 for 1 point (derived from [11]): most of a year of flow (a blend that moves the price less has smaller differences but a smaller gain too). Kill after 200 events if the blend's skill against the market is ≤ 0. Tripwire test (sub-variant C), 4 weeks, $0-65: record 20 ladders' WebSocket and drop the sub-variant if the median time from tripwire headline to a ≥ 5c move is under two minutes. Live-money bar: the blend beats the market by ≥ 0.005 Brier with the interval above zero AND the shrunk sim beats the plain forecaster; otherwise keep only the playbook use.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive (hours to days). A rollout batch takes 30-60 minutes per ladder, and the only window with room for model skill is the first day after listing; the tripwire sub-variant is latency-sensitive but is file 17's race.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery of new ladders | Gamma events feed, `new_market` events | a day late, the listing spread (median 10c at first trade, 2c by hour 24 [E1]) is gone and the price is already better | forecast within 6-24 hours |
| Fair value (rollouts) | 50-200 rollouts (100 ≈ $2-42 per ladder) | minutes do not matter; stale news bundles are a leak risk, not a speed risk | one batch at listing, rerun on a tripwire |
| Entry | ~$150 within 5c per side [12]; zero fee [13] | crossing a 1-2c spread costs 0.5-1c | maker orders at fair value |
| Tripwire repricing | headline latency | news bots and insiders move first | skip unless the tripwire test passes |
| Exit / settlement | UMA proposal and dispute rounds | disputed ladders lock capital for up to ~10 days (file 15) | hold to resolution |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Lifecycle, not latency: market Brier falls from 0.153 at listing + 1 day to 0.018 one day before the deadline [11], so a late forecast competes with a much better price. (2) The spread is the only trading cost: a taker crossing the 1.4c median spread pays ~0.7c, which leaves a 1-point edge almost nothing; a maker keeps it (derived from [10][13]). (3) `/prices-history` allows 1,000 requests per 10 seconds [E1] and 15-day windows [11]; irrelevant to PnL.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted (not the US, UK or France [E9]); LLM calls dominate latency; nothing here justifies co-location.
