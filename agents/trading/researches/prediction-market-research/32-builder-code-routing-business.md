### Builder-code order-routing businesses: terminals, bots, scanners, extensions, SDKs, white-label, US ISV
- **One-line summary**: Stamp your builder code on other people's orders for a flat toll of up to 100 bps taker / 50 bps maker (two wallet apps charge 400 bps) plus discretionary weekly rewards and grants, or declare vendor fees as a Polymarket US ISV; the money is in distribution and a few pro terminals, and the leaderboard everybody quotes counts shares, not dollars (1.6-7x too high).
- **Category**: Service & data business [fee-on-flow; the only survey strategy whose revenue is a function of other people's volume]
- **Maturity**: Established as a programme and as a business for distribution owners and pro terminals: native `builder` attribution has been in the signed V2 order since the 2026-04-28 cutover [10], ~550 codes have routed volume since October 2025 [6], and fee-charging apps with users are observable fill by fill (MetaMask and RedotPay 400 bps on every fill, Bagel, POTS, Polygun and Bitget Wallet 100 bps taker, polymtrade 50/25, PolyCop 50/50, traderline 10/5, SpreadCore 5/0) [7]. Unproven for a new solo entrant without an audience (see Expected results). Decayed for Telegram copy bots: the five named bots routed 125.2M shares in April and 15.3M in September (-88%, from 21% of builder volume in February to 2.5%); Polycule, hacked for $230k in January 2026, is at zero [6][16]. Programme volume peaked in June (1,493M shares), summed active users in July (87,217) [6]. The US ISV / IB route is Emerging: documented but "BETA - SUBJECT TO CHANGE", report delivery "TBD" [12][13].
- **Requires AI?**: no for the routing business. Optional inside the product (LLM market context, screeners, copy-target scoring) as features, not as the toll.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Register a builder profile (Settings > Builders), set a taker and a maker rate in 1 bp steps (defaults 0, maxima 100 / 50 bps), copy the `bytes32` code [1]. The Unverified tier needs no approval and gets 100 gas-free relayer transactions a day and order attribution; its column of the tier table also ticks builder fees, although the Verified section lists "Monetize with Builder fees" as an unlock (the docs are inconsistent) [2].
  2. Attach the code to every order your app signs: it is a field of the V2 order struct and is emitted in every `OrderFilled` event, so attribution is on-chain, not an off-chain label [1][10]. Bridge deposits and withdrawals accept an `X-Builder-Code` header since 2026-06-25 [10].
  3. At match time the Fees Service computes the platform fee (taker-only, `C x feeRate x p x (1-p)`) and the builder fee (`notional x bps / 10,000`, flat, stacked on top); the Builders Service indexes the events and pays the profile wallet. The maker and taker sides of one trade can carry different codes and rates [1].
  4. Apply by email for Verified (10,000 relayer tx/day, leaderboard visibility, private Telegram, "Weekly USDC rewards based on volume (subject to approval)", "Grants (subject to approval)") or Partner (unlimited relayer, highest API limits, priority access) [2]. Reward epochs run Sunday 00:00 to Saturday 23:59 UTC since 2 November 2025; the rate is not published [4].
  5. Choose the segment (named players under Competitive landscape): pro terminal, wallet or exchange integration, Telegram / Discord copy bot, scanner SaaS with in-terminal execution, browser extension, OSS framework with an embedded code, a Combos front-end through the Builder Gateway (custodial omnibus deposit wallet or non-custodial per-user signing; quote competition over 2-50 legs) [11], or the Polymarket US ISV / IB route, where your firm is a "message facilitator" that never holds funds, declares a fixed USD `vendor_fee` per order and collects it at most once a day per participant through a separate funding entity [12][13].
  6. Read the tape: `GET clob.polymarket.com/builder/trades?builder_code=...` is unauthenticated (`security: []`), pages 300 fills by base64 `next_cursor`, and returns `tradeType`, `size`, `sizeUsdc`, `builderFee`, `feeUsdc`, `owner`, `maker` and `transactionHash` for your fills and every competitor's [7][9].
- **What creates the edge**: A subsidy and a distribution asset, not a trading edge. The venue pays gas, provisions accounts and pays weekly rewards to attract order flow; whoever already owns users (a wallet, an exchange, a Discord of sports bettors) monetises them at zero marginal cost. The payer is the end user: 4% on a $5 MetaMask buy or sell, 50 bps on a $2 PolyCop copy fill, on top of a platform fee of 0.04-0.05 x p(1-p) [7][E1], for the convenience of one tap inside an app they already use. Pro users pay almost nothing: betmoar, Gate, Kreo, standtrade, NautilusTrader, NoArb and PolyHelper charge 0 bps, traderline 10/5, SpreadCore 5/0 and Sharktool 5/2, so those businesses live on subscriptions and on rewards and grants nobody discloses [7][16][17]. Concentration is the second edge: in September the top builder took 19.1% of builder volume, the top five 52.6%, the top sixteen 79.4%; 198 of 301 active codes routed under 100k shares and 118 under 10k; the median routed 24,073 shares, about $10k of notional [6][7]. Those are Verified codes only: every row of the public leaderboard and volume series carries `verified: true`, so the Unverified tier where a new builder starts is not even visible [5][6]. A solo developer without an audience is, at best, the median builder.
- **Capital required**: none for the code (registration is free, relayer gas is subsidised); <$1k for a bot, extension or SDK; $1k-$10k for a scanner or terminal with a marketing budget; $10k+ only if you custody (a Combos omnibus wallet holds every user position) [11] or build the US partner stack, which needs a separate funding entity that pre-positions a pooled account and carries accrued vendor fees as unsecured credit exposure to each participant [12][13]. No trading capital is locked; builder fees come out of users' pUSD at match [1].
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Order path: V2 SDK (`@polymarket/clob-client-v2` / `py-clob-client-v2`) with `builderCode` on every order; a relayer key for gasless deposit wallets and CTF operations (25 `POST /submit` per minute; the hash comes from polling `GET /transaction`) [10][E1]; Bridge calls with `X-Builder-Code` [10]. (2) Product: a copy bot needs the on-chain `OrderFilled` detector of file 26 (the Data API feeds lag 100-500 s); a scanner needs sportsbook odds and a devigger (file 04); a terminal needs books, batch cancel and PnL; an extension injects UI into polymarket.com with orders signed in the user's wallet. (3) Custody: non-custodial keeps you outside money-transmission and hack liability and inside the CFTC's passive-software relief; custodial omnibus accounts lose taker rebates and referral rewards [11][E9]. (4) Accounting: pull your own `builder/trades` daily and reconcile `builderFee` against payouts [3][7]. (5) Compliance: the revocation clause, the rate-change notice, geo-blocking of restricted persons [1][E9]. Build time: 1-2 weeks solo for an SDK or extension on an existing stack, 4-8 weeks for a copy bot or scanner with billing, 3-6 months plus legal spend for a US ISV.
- **Opportunity size**: Builder-attributed volume by month, in shares: 32.1M (Oct 2025, 11 codes), 612.0M (Mar 2026, 253), 795.8M (Apr, 287), 1,493.1M (Jun, 300), 1,391.8M (Jul, 325), 648.9M (Aug, 305), 610.1M for 1-28 Sep (301) [6]. The unit is shares: the docs' `BuilderStanding` type says "Builder-attributed volume in shares" [8], and the sum of `size` over one UTC day reproduces the DAY series to the cent for five builders on 27 Sep and four on 28 Sep (Sharkbetting 1,113,710 shares against $411,501 of USDC; Deepcoin 54,408.65 against $19,648) [7]. Chainstory's "~2x" caveat blames maker-plus-taker double counting [16]; the per-builder series are single-sided fills, and the inflation is the share unit. Share/USDC ratios in the last 300 fills run 1.59 (betmoar), 1.96 (SpreadCore), 2.52 (traderline), 2.80 (MetaMask), 5.25 (polymtrade), 7.22 (POTS) and 23.3 (PolyHelper, sub-5c tickets) [7]. So the brief's "top-50 ~$440M/month", the builders page's "$" labels and E6's figures are share counts; September's 610M shares is roughly $250-300M of notional (derived at a 2.0-2.5 mix ratio; guess). Fee pool at sampled rates on September volume, converted to notional: MetaMask ~$10.1M x 400 bps = ~$400k; RedotPay ~$2.7M x 400 bps = ~$107k; Bagel ~$3.6M x ~90 bps blended = ~$33k; PolyCop ~$5.3M x 50 bps = ~$26k; polymtrade ~$5.2M x ~48 bps = ~$25k; traderline ~$27M x ~6.5 bps blended = ~$17k; POTS ~$1.0M x 100 bps = ~$10k; SpreadCore ~$21M with 3% taker at 5 bps = under $1k [6][7] (sampled rates applied to a month: indicative). Trend: shrinking since June, shifting from copy bots to wallet and exchange distribution and pro terminals; entrants keep arriving (Sharktool 0 -> 17.1M shares in two months) [6].
- **Pros**: Free to start, no trading capital, no inventory risk; every competitor's rate, ticket size, taker share and user count is readable without a key [1][7][9]; fees accrue in pUSD at match [1]; the venue subsidises gas and account provisioning, and Verified adds promotion from official accounts [2][3]; the same code labels your users' flow for the analytics of files 27 and 26.
- **Cons / risks**: Platform: fee rights are revocable "in its sole discretion, for any reason or no reason", and orders carrying a disabled code are rejected [1]; rates are public and funded terminals undercut to zero [7]; the venue encroaches on the best products (Combos, first-party copy and context features), and in April 2026 it audited Builders Program startups after Kreo ("find insiders before everyone else") and Polycool marketed mirror tools that "drove hundreds of millions of dollars in additional trading volume" [15]. Distribution partners turn into competitors: Gate, which says it was the first exchange to integrate Polymarket Builder, launched its own white-label prediction-market builder in August 2026 to "compete with its former partner", while still routing 69.7M shares a month through its code [22][5]. Omnibus wallets are ineligible for taker rebates and referral rewards, so custodial products cost users the 3-50% rebate tiers of file 28 [E2][E9]. Legal: see the flag below (custody, signals and discretion fall outside CFTC Letter 26-25; copy or signal services with a US nexus raise a CTA question) [14][E9]. Commercial: the median code earns pocket money; copy-bot demand fell 88% in five months as the fee regime and CLOB V2 arrived and as leaders learned to hide [6][15]; sportsbook-arb edges decay fast (one self-reported esports pair-lock bot made +$2,506 in February, +$390 in March and +$180 in April 2026 on $95.8k of total volume: "more competition for the market making and fees got introduced") [18]. Token policy: builders "must inform the team ahead of any potential token launch" [4].
- **Expected results**: no public data for any builder's realised revenue; everything below is derived from public volumes and sampled rates [6][7]. Distribution integrations: MetaMask's 400 bps on 100% taker flow ($5.00 median ticket, ~2,860 monthly users) is the only top-ten model that plausibly clears six figures a month (~$400k derived); RedotPay ~$107k [5][6][7]. Copy bots: PolyCop ~$26k and Polygun ~$5.5k a month (1.55M shares / 2.81 = ~$0.55M notional x 100 bps), down from E6's peak-month estimates (PolyCop ~$385k in April, Polygun ~$350k in March), which were computed on share counts [6][7][E6]. Pro terminals at 0-10 bps earn near-zero fees and live on subscriptions (SpreadCore $160 / 14 days, $299 / 30 days, $799 / 90 days with 92 active users on its code: at most ~$27k a month if all paid monthly; vendor page) [17] and on rewards and grants (unverified: Chainstory reports PolyTrack's back-calculated "0.5% to 1% of attributed volume" and Based's reported "$1 million" of annualised builder-reward revenue when ranked ninth; Based routed 0.1M shares in September) [16][6]. Solo guess: the median verified code (24k shares, ~$10k notional) at 50-100 bps taker on a 70-100% taker mix earns $35-$100 a month; the 50th code (0.94M shares, ~$400k notional) earns $1.4k-$4k at the same rates; rewards are upside nobody discloses.
- **Competitive landscape**: 301 active codes in September, ~550 lifetime, 45 above 1M shares [6]. Terminals: betmoar (116.5M shares, 803 users, 28% taker, 0 bps; its site claims "$2.65B Trading Volume" and ~$71M of "User Profits", marketing) [25][7], traderline (67.0M, 436), SpreadCore (40.1M, 92), Sharktool (17.1M, 103), standtrade (8.2M, 161). Exchange and wallet flow: Gate (69.2M shares from 19 users, 92% taker, 0 bps), MetaMask (28.1M, ~2,860), polymtrade (27.0M, 4,219), RedotPay (7.9M, 4,814), Bitget Wallet, Jupiter (12.8M from one account) [5][7]. Single-account "builders" (MagicMarkets 28.3M shares, 1 user, 0 bps; Sharkbetting 16.1M, 2 users) are the self-attribution pattern of file 29, tolerable only at 0 bps under the "self-referred" clause [1][5][7]. Copy bots: PolyCop (10.2M, 1,073 users, 50/50 bps), Kreo (2.9M, 646, 0 bps), Polygun (1.5M, 656, 100/50 bps), PolyBot [5][7]. Extension: PolyHelper (19.6M, 86 users, 0 bps; promises "up to 50% of the Polymarket drop that PolyHelper receives" to its top 1,000 users, "All drop mentions are just assumptions") [21][7]. OSS: NautilusTrader (6.2M, 179 users; ~29,500 GitHub stars; its adapter "carries the hard-coded Nautilus builder code. Its builder fee rate is fixed at zero") [24]; pmxt (2,173 stars) [26]. Institutional tooling: River Markets ($8.5M seed led by Haun Ventures, announced 2026-08-09; launched "with trading teams on May 1st, including several of the top 10 traders by volume on Kalshi and Polymarket"; 1.49M shares in September) [23][6]; Kairos ($2.5M from a16z crypto; "$0 platform fee" per E6) [27][E6]; ParlayX ($1.25M) [27]; Paradigm and Kalshi building their own terminals (headlines) [27]; 5(c) Capital, raising a $35M first fund reportedly backed by Coplan, Mansour, Andreessen and Malka [20]; PolyGun bought Polymarket Analytics (250,000+ users) in March 2026 [19]; Chainstory counted 114 verifiable projects, 16 platforms taking 90% of Q1 volume, ~$20M of disclosed native venture funding [16]. White-label competition: Gate's Prediction Builder (August 2026) with a Gate-funded $3M grant programme (Fortune; Dealroom's body says US$1.94M) [22]. To compete: own an audience or a niche the incumbents ignore (a sport, a language, a wallet), charge 0-10 bps to pros and 50-100 bps only to retail, stay non-custodial, and treat rewards as upside you cannot underwrite.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: grey. Allowed: a disclosed builder fee, non-custodial routing, referral links; the Data licence bars proprietary trading firms from Polymarket Data without a written agreement but carves out the builder programme [E9]. Prohibited: routing for restricted persons (US persons on the international venue, UK positions, sanctioned jurisdictions), self-referred or wash volume to earn fees or rewards (revocation; file 29), trading ahead of your own users (ToS §4.2; Polymarket US Rule 7.2(j)) [1][E9]. US: an ISV is "not a broker" and carries no registration; an IB needs CFTC registration, NFA membership, Rule 1.17 resources, NFA 2-9 supervisory policies and BC/DR plans; the partner never holds participant funds (a separate funding entity does) [13]. CFTC Letter 26-25 (Market Participants Division, 2026-09-17) extends to all passive software providers the IB-registration no-action position given to Phantom in Letter 26-09: covered activity is front-end software through which users send orders to CFTC registrants, "including event contracts, perpetual contracts", and the provider may charge users "a transaction-based fee" and share registrant revenue; the conditions exclude custody, express buy or sell signals and discretion over orders, and require risk disclosures, NFA Rule 2-29 marketing compliance and a liability undertaking [14][E9]. It concerns CFTC-registered venues (Polymarket US), not the international venue. Auto-executing copy software and paid signal groups sit outside it [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: Two weeks, no capital. (1) Register a code at 0 bps and route your own bots' flow for a week to learn relayer, attribution and payout mechanics; confirm your fills appear in `builder/trades` (the leaderboard lists Verified builders only) [2][7]. (2) Market test: pull `builder/trades` for the ten codes in your target segment, compute taker share, median ticket, users per day and fee bps, and size the pool as notional x rate, not shares x rate [7]. (3) Demand test: a landing page or Telegram bot with a waitlist; the pass bar for a copy bot or extension is 100 users routing $50k of notional a month within 60 days (at 50 bps, $250 a month: the VPS plus your time); for a scanner, 20 paying users at $99+ a month. (4) Ask builder@polymarket.com for Verified status and, in writing, for the weekly reward rate; treat a non-answer as zero. (5) For a US ISV, have counsel review the ISV Connectivity Agreement and the funding-entity structure before writing code; vendor-fee report delivery is still "TBD" [12][13]. Kill if after 90 days routed notional is under $100k a month and no reward has been paid.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive: the fee accrues per fill whatever your latency, payouts are batched and the leaderboard lags up to 24 h; the product inside is execution-sensitive (a copy bot must see fills on-chain in ~2 s, a scanner must hedge before the book moves), and that is where users judge you.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attribution and fees | code on every order | untagged orders earn nothing | 100% tagged, checked daily [7] |
| Account provisioning | relayer quota (100/day Unverified) | new users cannot deploy wallets [2] | Verified past ~50 signups/day |
| Copy-bot detection | on-chain `OrderFilled` (file 26) | users copy minutes late and churn | log subscription, ~2 s |
| Scanner execution | odds latency, FAK at the touch (file 04) | hedges miss; directional residue [18] | sub-second odds diff |
| Rate changes | 7-day cooldown, 3-day notice [1] | cannot answer a rival's cut for a week | price once, low |
| Payout reconciliation | Builders Service indexing [3] | fees you cannot audit | daily pull of own fills [7] |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) The fee layer does not reward speed: `builder_fee = notional x bps` settles per fill [1]. (2) Product quality does: copy bots lost 88% of volume in five months while pro terminals held or grew [6]. (3) Relayer limits are the first wall a growing app hits: 100 transactions a day covers a few dozen new deposit wallets [2]. (4) A disabled code rejects every order at once [1].
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted; eu-west-1 only if the product needs fast fills. Builders and apps trading for end users are excluded from eu-west-2 co-location [E1]. Do not serve UK, US (international venue) or sanctioned-jurisdiction users [E9].
