# 57 - Cross-lingual source-silo tilt on non-Anglophone and geopolitics markets
_Band: speculative · Evidence grade: D (no forecasting or trading record for the strategy anywhere; A for the market baseline measured here from Gamma, CLOB and Data API; B for the one anchor paper, a preprint that scores LLM forecasts against the price, not outcomes; C for hobby repositories) · Last verified: 2026-09-29_

### Cross-lingual source-silo tilt
- **One-line summary**: Hold the model fixed, forecast each non-Anglophone war or political market twice (English-wire context vs local-language primary sources), and apply the difference as a small logit tilt to the price in fee-free geopolitics markets. Unproven: the anchor paper scored LLM beliefs against the price, not outcomes or PnL; the price does over-predict Russian gains by 5-9 points on 334 resolved territorial markets, but comparable geopolitics rungs are over-priced about as much, the gap is shrinking and fills do not confirm it.
- **Category**: Speculative-novel [information edge on fee-free war markets; secondary use as a disagreement feature for files 21 and 58]
- **Maturity**: Experimental and unproven on any market. The anchor result (Khandoga et al., 111 Polymarket Ukraine markets, January 2025 - January 2026, ~93,000 predictions, three clean models plus one contaminated control) predicts the market PRICE 6 hours to 7 days ahead, not the outcome: when English context pushed a forecast toward capture, the later price moved that way only 28-36% of the time (the "wrong 64-72%", scored against the price path); a contaminated model that knew outcomes erred at the same rate; adding Ukrainian military sources cut the pro-capture bias for all clean models, but the accuracy gain is not significant at the market level (p = 0.10-0.37) [1]. The paper itself reports that the price over-estimated Russian capture by +3.5 points against outcomes, on point-level data with only 3 of 65 territorial markets resolving Yes [1]. E7 found no study of a multilingual edge [E7], and nobody is found trading it (Competitive landscape).
- **Requires AI?**: yes. The same LLM reads each silo's dated documents in the original language and returns a probability; a cross-family panel is optional (sub-variant B). Calibration, the tilt weight λ, sizing and risk limits are statistics fitted on your own resolved ledger.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Universe: open markets whose ground truth sits in a non-English information space (Gamma tags `ukraine-map`, `geopolitics`, non-Anglophone `elections`). Parse the rules for the arbiter (ISW map in 333 of 334 sampled territorial markets, DeepState as fallback in 124, "consensus of credible reporting" in 50 [10]) and take the deadline from the question text: Gamma `endDate` disagrees with it by more than two days on 62 of the 334 [10].
  2. Silos: per theatre, build dated corpora that never mix: English wire and mainstream (the paper's GDELT slice: 16,457 articles, 89% Western, zero Ukrainian-language analytical sources [1]); local official and military-analytical (General Staff reports, Militarnyi, Defense Express, frontline Telegram channels); adversary media (Russian MoD, milbloggers, read as claims); OSINT (geolocated footage, fire and flight data).
  3. Sub-variant A, two-pipeline tilt: one model, two forecasts, English-only and local-only, both given the rules but never the price. Signal τ = logit(p_local) − logit(p_English).
  4. Sub-variant B, K-silo crowd: one forecast per silo, each corrected by its own historical bias on resolved markets, then reliability-weighted.
  5. Tilt: p* = ilogit(logit(q) + λ·τ) around the market price q, λ fitted on earlier resolutions only (null λ = 0).
  6. Trade maker-first where |p* − q| exceeds the half-spread plus calibration error; war markets are fee-free, non-Anglophone elections pay the politics taker fee [9][14].
  7. Sub-variant C, speed leg: watch local primary publications that settle markets. For territorial markets the settling publication is the ISW map, whose watcher is file 13's race; official results pages are file 17's, where the first channel varies by sample (Bluesky, Facebook and YouTube win 24-32% of races [7]).
- **What creates the edge**: Mechanism: the source text, not the model, carries a directional distortion (Russia gets 2.3-3.7x more offensive verbs in model reasoning; ISW-citing predictions hit 50.9% directional accuracy against 61.8% without; chart-only context beat full English context for every clean model) [1]; if the marginal trader and bot read the same English coverage, the price inherits it, and fee-free war markets let a 1-2 point tilt trade [1][9]. The strongest counter-argument is measured here. On 334 resolved Russia-Ukraine territorial markets (130 events, 2024-2026, $65.8M, 114 Yes) the midpoint over-predicts Russian gains by +6.7 points one day after listing [event-clustered 95% interval +0.9, +11.8], +9.4 [+4.2, +14.4] 30 days before the deadline, +5.4 [+2.6, +8.2] at 7 days and +1.4 [+0.2, +2.1] at 1 day [11], the paper's direction (town clustering gives the same intervals; forecast-month clustering widens the listing one to [−2.3, +13.9]). But (a) it is not shown to be source-specific: other geopolitics "by date" rungs (file 50's sample, Russia and Ukraine removed) are over-priced by +6.6 / +7.8 / +2.7 / −0.6 points, and the Ukraine excess, +0.1 / +1.6 / +2.7 / +1.9, straddles zero at every horizon [11]. The control is not price-matched: below 35c, where both have data, Ukraine shows +4.6 [+1.4, +7.4] against +3.3 [−1.1, +6.8]; the 22% of Ukraine rung-horizons at 35c or above carry the largest errors (+14 to +23 points) and the control has 8 such rungs, so a Ukraine-specific component there is untested, not refuted; file 50's own Russia-Ukraine rungs (ceasefire, strike, summit) show none (−8.1 / +2.5 / −1.9 points, wide) [11]. The comparable part is the generic over-pricing of dramatic Yes outcomes that file 24 sells without a model. (b) It is shrinking: +21.3 points on 2024 deadlines (9 events), +9.0 [+4.0, +14.2] in 2025, +5.0 [−0.8, +9.8] in 2026 [11]. (c) A constant no-AI shrink toward "no capture" fitted on the first half of rows in time order hurt the second half at 30 days (+0.012 Brier [−0.017, +0.047]) and helped by 0.003 [−0.008, +0.003] at 7 days; fitted with hindsight on everything it is worth 0.013 and 0.003 [11]. (d) On executed fills 3-14 days out (58 events, ~4.0M shares matched) the share-weighted signed error is +2.1 points per market [−3.9, +7.3], and −9.0 [−22.1, +7.4] on the 12 markets trading at 35c or above; the 7-day midpoint on the same markets gives +2.0 [−6.3, +10.1]: too small to confirm or refute [12]. (e) Arbiters, not territory, settle, and the one Ukrainian-gain signal is 11 markets in 6 events (priced 37% at listing, 64% resolved Yes) [11][16]. Who would pay: English-reading retail buying dramatic Yes; who is already there: OSINT readers, insiders (file 30), NO-side longshot sellers (file 24).
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k, capped by depth. Open territorial markets: 48 of 108 priced 0.10-0.90; a one-market-per-event sample of 12 mid-band Russia-Ukraine books showed a 2c median spread and $262 of bids and $203 of asks within 5c [14]. Lock-up: rungs live 1-3 months; 113 of the 114 Yes markets settled before their deadline [10].
- **Technical requirements**: Built on file 21's stack (feeds, dated retrieval, ledger, trading layer) and file 50's ladder handling. Agent design sketch: (1) Rules compiler: arbiter, fallback source, scheduled deadline, "subsequent loss of control does not count" clauses [16]. (2) Silo retrievers, one per ecosystem, each with a hard publication-date filter and its own timestamped archive (Telegram message dates, the General Staff report archive, GDELT for English); nothing retrieved after the forecast time. (3) Forecasters: the same model for sub-variant A (the paper's design: the difference isolates the text [1]); for B, each silo on a different family where possible, because five families' errors still correlate ~0.70 (file 55). Each agent sees its silo's bundle and the rules, never the price or other silos. (4) Calibration: per silo and horizon, a logit intercept and slope on resolved markets (leave-future-out). (5) Aggregation: A, the tilt above; B, a bias-corrected, inverse-error-weighted logit pool, then blended with the price. (6) Sizing: proper betting, s = 2(p* − q) [E7]. (7) Hygiene: adversary media and Telegram text are untrusted input (prompt injection, propaganda). Cost, an estimate, not measured: per silo ~25k input tokens (15-20 documents; Ukrainian and Russian text tokenises roughly 1.5-2x English, a guess) and 1.5k output; sub-variant A ≈ 55k / 3k tokens = $0.007 on GPT-6 Luna, $0.07 on Haiku 4.5, $0.14 on Sonnet 5 at file 21's list prices, plus 6-12 searches at ~$0.01; sub-variant B with four silos ≈ 2x. Roughly $0.02-0.40 per market-forecast. Build: 3-5 weeks solo on top of file 21, most of it the per-theatre archives and the leak-free replay.
- **Opportunity size**: Small. Open Russia-Ukraine territorial markets: 60 events, 108 markets, $0.63M of Gamma liquidity, $135k of 24-hour volume, a median $19 per market that day, all fee-free [14]. The wider Russia-Ukraine set: 318 markets (311 fee-free), $7.0M liquidity, $643k a day; Middle East $18.8M liquidity, $4.1M a day, concentrated in a few complexes, 6c median spread, ~$40 within 5c; Latin America, Africa, Asia and non-English Europe add $2.6-30.6M of liquidity each, nearly all elections carrying `politics_fees` (1,883 of 1,916 in Latin America) [14]. The brief's Russian parliamentary election closed on 20 September at $52.1M [15]; African and Latin American coverage follows settlement legibility, with conflict lowest [4]. A real 2-point edge on the ~$15-35k of near-touch depth across mid-priced war markets is worth hundreds of dollars a month at most (derived).
- **Pros**: Fee-free war markets and 2c spreads [9][14]; the paper's dataset (MIT licence, ~660,000 predictions from five models) gives a free pilot [2]; specialist sources have beaten thin Polymarket markets in one niche, disease counts [8]; the tilt is a clean, cheap feature for a stacker (file 58) even if it never trades alone; per-silo disagreement flags markets where the English narrative runs ahead of evidence.
- **Cons / risks**: Edge: where a control exists the measured over-pricing is generic, not Ukraine- or language-specific, and it is shrinking [11]; local sources carry their own propaganda, and choosing whom to trust is the whole model. Evaluation: one theatre supplies ~50-90 events a year (53 in 2025, 68 in January-September 2026 [10]), too few for power (below); single-theatre overfit is the default failure. Arbiter: ISW settles; an unauthorised ISW edit on the night of 15-16 November 2025 resolved "Russia captures Myrnohrad by November 15?" Yes while Ukraine was still fighting for the town, and the rule ignores later reversals [16][17][20] (404 Media's "before 10:48 UTC on November 15" conflicts with the tape [18]). Counterparty: insiders in geopolitics (file 30; 332 wallets traded both main Iran-cluster markets [6]); correlated LLM traders become a shared-misinformation liability [5]. Data: DeepState objected to betting use of its map [19]. Contamination: the anchor dataset sits inside newer models' training windows.
- **Expected results**: no public data, theoretical only. The anchor paper reports bias reduction, not profit, and uses the price as its reference [1]; Hindcast shows retrieval hurting where the archive held only speculation [3]. Guess: the tilt adds 0 to 0.003 Brier over the price at 7-30 days, most of it inside what the generic no-AI shrink already captures; PnL −2% to +1% of deployed capital a month before LLM costs, negative after them below ~$5k.
- **Competitive landscape**: No silo-tilt trader found: GitHub and HN return a multilingual question-authoring project and the tools below, none with a scored record or PnL [23][24]. Adjacent tools: a Java bot polling the ISW ArcGIS layer every minute with point-in-polygon alerts (0 stars) [21]; an ADS-B / AIS lead-lag pilot on a Ukraine ceasefire rung (n = 9 days, "suggestive, not significant") [22]; Numinous offers agents an OSINT "Indicia" feed [25]; PrediHermes and other OSINT-simulation bridges have no record (file 50). The price is set by discretionary specialists (file 25), NO-side longshot sellers (file 24), arbiter watchers (file 13) and insiders (file 30); whether Ukrainian- and Russian-reading traders dominate these books is unverified. Being competitive means a pre-registered ledger across several theatres; nobody has one.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: grey. Trading your own model from one identity is allowed [E9]. War and casualty markets carry reputational and, in some countries, legal risk: the anchor paper's authors call them "commodification of human catastrophe" [1], and a March 2026 bill targets war markets (file 50, [E9]). Users in Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk are excluded as sanctioned territories [E9]; screen any paid Russian data source against sanctions lists before paying. Never contact, pay or pressure an arbiter or source: the Myrnohrad edit is the case (ISW removed a researcher's name on 18 November) [17]; do not use DeepState data for betting without permission [19]. Insider enforcement is rising in geopolitics (file 30); keep timestamped forecast logs. Selling the tilt as a signal raises the CTA question [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: Phase 0 (done, $0): the no-AI calibration test, the control against other geopolitics rungs, the fill check and power [11][12]. Phase 1, retrospective kill test, 3-4 weeks, ~$300-1,500: markets created and resolved after every panel model's training cutoff, in at least three theatres (Russia-Ukraine territorial and ceasefire, Middle East, non-Anglophone elections), target 300-650 markets from 100+ independent events, one rung per event per horizon; horizons listing + 1 day, 30 and 7 days before the question-text deadline, counted only if the market was open then; silo archives snapshotted with publication timestamps, manual leak audit of 50 bundles. Seven pre-registered arms: market mid; English-only agent; local-only agent; tilt (λ fitted on the first 40% of events in time order, then frozen); K-silo crowd; file 21's plain forecaster; and the no-AI generic Yes shrink measured here, because it explains the comparable part of the measured bias; report the 35c-and-above rungs separately, since the control cannot price them. Metrics: Brier skill against the price (event-clustered paired bootstrap), log loss, proper-betting PnL on recorded books and Data API fills, fee-free for war markets, politics fee for elections. Pass: λ > 0 with an interval excluding zero, the tilt beats the price by ≥ 0.005 AND beats the generic shrink, in at least two theatres. Kill: the pre-registered null "tilt = zero", or a tilt that does not beat the generic shrink. Power (80%, two-sided 5%, n = (2.8·SD/δ)², derived from [11]): the paired Brier-difference SD grows with the size of the tilt, so it is taken at realistic tilts. At 30 days a 0.2-0.3-logit shift has SD 0.031-0.046: the 0.006-0.009 such a shift gained even with hindsight needs ~180-210 independent questions, and a 0.005 increment over the generic shrink ~300-650. At 7 days the best constant shift gains at most 0.003 with hindsight (SD 0.023-0.044), which needs ~700-1,600 questions, so the 0.005 bar there is reachable only by a market-specific tilt. Ukraine alone yields ~50-90 qualifying events a year, so Phase 1 must pool theatres and can only reject large effects. Phase 2, forward, 6 months, $500-2,000: freeze the pipeline, forecast every open war market weekly, paper-trade against recorded books; add the brief's check that where |local − English| ≥ 15 points the price moves toward the local view within 7 days ≥ 60% of the time; live money only if Phase 1's bar holds forward.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive (hours to days). The tilt is a slow view refreshed daily or weekly; the fast leg (arbiter-map and official-page watching) is latency-sensitive but belongs to files 13 and 17.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Gamma `ukraine-map` / `geopolitics` feeds; rules parse | late listing forecasts meet a sharper price (Brier 0.198 at listing, 0.047 at 7 days [11]) | forecast within 24 h of listing |
| Silo forecasts | dated archives per theatre | stale bundles are a leak risk, not a speed risk | daily run, rerun on a front-line change |
| Entry | ~$200-260 within 5c per side, 2c spread [14]; zero fee [9] | crossing costs ~1c, most of any plausible tilt | maker orders at p* |
| Arbiter watch (sub-variant C) | ISW map refresh | the Myrnohrad rung went from 0.9c to 75c in 13 minutes after the shading [16] | leave to file 13 |
| Settlement | UMA proposal; no dispute rounds in this family | early Yes settles in days; No waits for the deadline | hold to resolution |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Lifecycle, not latency: the price's Brier falls from 0.198 at listing to 0.005 a day out and the midpoint bias from +9.4 to +1.4 points [11], so any tilt earns early or not at all. (2) The spread is the only cost on fee-free war markets, and the Ukraine-specific excess (≤ 2.7 points, not significant) is about one spread. (3) Data API v1 `/trades` retires 2026-10-24 (file 33); fill-based replays should move to v2.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted (not the US, UK or France [E9]); LLM calls dominate latency; nothing justifies co-location.
