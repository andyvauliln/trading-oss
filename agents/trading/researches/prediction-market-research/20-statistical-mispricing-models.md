# 20 - Statistical / quantitative mispricing models (polls, ratings, base rates, nowcasts, recalibration)
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: B (large-sample calibration and public-baseline papers on Polymarket and Kalshi data; no verified Polymarket trading PnL; one small live Kalshi run with a venue-affiliated co-author) · Last verified: 2026-09-28_

### Statistical / quantitative mispricing models
- **One-line summary**: Build probability models from public data (forecast ensembles, nowcasts, ratings, base rates and the market's own documented miscalibration by domain and horizon), convert the model-vs-price gap into positions with a proper-scoring-rule sizing rule, and express it as resting orders. The gaps are documented; the tradable size on Polymarket is small; no Polymarket PnL has been published for any variant.
- **Category**: Directional trading [systematic, model-vs-price, horizon-locked capital]
- **Maturity**: Emerging, by sub-variant. (a) Domain and horizon recalibration: documented on 353M trades (political prices compress toward 50%; Polymarket slope 1.45), never tested net of costs (the paper cites Kalshi's mean fee of ~1.2-1.3c per contract only to rule it out as an explanation), and "roughly half of the raw slope variation" is noise [2]. (b) Public-baseline disease models: the one clean paper finds the FluSight ensemble strictly dominates Polymarket's flu ladders and simple baselines beat its measles ladders [1]; capacity is tens to a few thousand dollars per rung [16][17]. (c) Macro nowcasting: no edge on the mean. Kalshi's Fed markets beat fed funds futures on the eve of every FOMC meeting from 2022 to June 2025 and its inflation and unemployment forecasts match the Bloomberg consensus [9][10], Polymarket's Fed and CPI ladders sat within 1-3 pp of Kalshi's on 28 Sep [16][18], and LLM nowcasters that matched Bloomberg in aggregate lost on CPI [8]. (d) Election and poll models: the 2024 evidence runs the other way (Polymarket beat polls in swing states [12]; only 67% of its markets beat chance in the last five weeks, against 93% on PredictIt [11]). (e) The forecast-to-bet layer: the only positive live result in this family, +80% on $200 in 26 days on Kalshi, with a Kalshi Research co-author [4]. (f) Base-rate and reference-class models: untested on Polymarket; the one published hint is that the reference class flips the sign of the longshot return (-6.3c per dollar equal-weighted, +4.1c grouped by parent event [3]); file 70 has the procedural version. Hurricane markets exist (Category-4 US landfall before 2027: $347k volume, $1.4k liquidity) with no published test of NOAA outlooks [16]. Nothing here is Established on Polymarket.
- **Requires AI?**: no. The models are regressions, ensembles and recalibration maps. Optional: an LLM to map each market's rules to the right series, table and revision convention (which CDC table, seasonally adjusted or not, rounding at bracket edges). LLM nowcasting itself is not an edge: web-search agents tied the Bloomberg consensus in aggregate and lost on CPI, retail sales and PCE (87% of their shortfall) [8].
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Pick a market family with a public model that has a track record: CDC FluSight ensemble quantiles for the weekly influenza-hospitalisation ladders [1][22]; the CDC measles counter plus a pace baseline for the annual case ladder [1][20]; the Cleveland Fed daily nowcast and the BLS calendar for CPI ladders [19][21]; the calibration slopes of [2] for politics; Elo-type ratings for match-style markets.
  2. Convert the model to bracket probabilities under the exact resolution rule (bracket edges, "higher bracket on ties", cumulative vs weekly, revision window), and check the ladder itself: [1] found Polymarket putting mass on impossible outcomes (decreasing cumulative counts).
  3. For recalibration, map the price through the published slope, p* = sigmoid(b x logit(p)): b = 1.45 on Polymarket politics, 1.83 in Kalshi's one-week cell, so a 0.70 political price becomes 0.78-0.83 [2]. Subtract the settlement discount of the horizon (3-7% annualised, which explains 48-88% of the apparent long-horizon miscalibration) [E3].
  4. Compare with the bid and ask, not the mid; require gap > fee + half spread + a noise haircut. Fee is `rate x p(1-p)` per share: at p = 0.70, 0.84c in politics (rate 0.04), 1.05c in economics (0.05), zero in geopolitics [E1]; a 1c spread adds 0.5c (derived).
  5. Size with the proper-betting rule for the Brier score, position proportional to 2(p - q), capped per market and per event cluster; not Kelly, which turned the same forecasts into -42.7% to -99.9% [4].
  6. Rest orders at the model price (maker rebate 25% of the taker-fee pool in politics and economics [E1]); take only when the gap exceeds ~2x the hurdle. Hold to resolution, redeem, and log every forecast against the price for the tests below.
- **What creates the edge**: Four candidate sources, in descending order of evidence. (1) Domain miscalibration: political prices are underconfident at every horizon on both venues (Kalshi slopes 1.34 at 0-1 h to 1.73 at one month or more; Polymarket 1.45; politics ECE 0.117 against 0.008 for sports and 0.007 for crypto) [2]; the counterparty is the retail political trader who hedges, roots or buys longshots (sub-10c purchases lose 19.3c per dollar; politics longshots -16.3% equal-weighted) [3][E4]. (2) Public expertise the crowd ignores: the flu and measles books are hobby-sized and priced without the domain's standard forecast, so the optimal combination "puts zero weight on the markets" [1]. (3) Distributional information in macro ladders: the market mean matches consensus, but tails carry information (a +0.1 pp lagged CPI surprise raises P(month-on-month above 0.3%) by ~4.7 pp after controlling for consensus [7]); the edge, if any, is in the shape. (4) Execution and sizing: accurate forecasters lose on order books through sizing and execution, and the belief-to-trade layer alone turned a -10.7% flat-stake replay into +15.9% [5]. Against all four: calibration barely predicts wallet profit on Polymarket (Spearman +0.148 across 8,656 wallets; the median wallet gets 66% of its PnL from one event) [15]. A calibrated model without concentration and capacity is not a business.
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k. The constraint is depth and lock-up, not the model. Political markets one to six weeks out carry $60k-$460k of Gamma liquidity at 1c spreads [16], so $10k deploys; every macro ladder except the Fed decision does not: on 28 Sep the September CPI rungs had $24-$216 resting within 2c of mid, the jobs 50-100k rung $58 / $77, the unemployment 4.1% rung nothing within 2c and a 13c spread, core CPI 0.2% a 14c spread [17]. Capital is locked to resolution: 1-17 days for macro prints, 1-6 weeks for the political cells where the slope is largest, at a priced discount of 3-7% annualised [E3]. Minimum order 5 shares; tick 0.01, 0.001 below 0.04 and above 0.96 [16].
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: Gamma, the CLOB WebSocket, `/prices-history` and Data API v2 history [E1]; model feeds: FluSight hub (weekly in season [22]), CDC measles counter (Thursdays [20]), Cleveland Fed nowcast (daily [19]), BLS calendar [21], Kalshi REST for a deeper second price [18]. (2) Models: rule-aware bucketisation; recalibration slopes re-estimated on your own resolved sample with event-clustered errors; a settlement-discount curve. (3) Sizing: proper betting, 5-10% of capital per market, a cap per event cluster (sibling rungs of one election are one event), horizon-matched cash. (4) Execution: post-only resting at model price, cancel-all before scheduled prints (the release second is file 17's territory), cancel-replace rather than modify [E1]. (5) Test mode: a forecast log with price, bid, ask, depth and outcome per market; Brier and paper PnL against the price baseline. Build: 2-3 weeks for a solo developer on an existing LP stack (the new parts are bucketisation, recalibration and sizing); 6-8 weeks from scratch. Do not build a macro nowcast: the Cleveland Fed publishes one daily for free [19].
- **Opportunity size**: Small and uneven: the deep markets are efficient and the mispriced ones are shallow. Fed decisions: the October 2026 event had $15.5M of volume, $2.05M of liquidity and $16.5k of bids / $25.6k of asks within 2c of mid on the hike rung (28 Sep 15:52 UTC) [16][17], priced 0.70/0.71 against Kalshi's 0.69/0.70 with 60,038 contracts bid, no change 0.28/0.29 against 0.27/0.28 [18]: a professionally arbitraged pair. September CPI: Polymarket's ladder implied P(>= 0.6%) ~0.64 and P(>= 0.5%) ~0.92 against Kalshi's 0.66 and 0.89 (derived from mids) [16][18], on $44k of event volume and $11k of liquidity; the Cleveland Fed nowcast that day was 0.50% headline and 0.20% core [19], so the nowcast's point sits in the 0.5% bucket (0.27/0.29) while both crowds lean 0.6%. Asterisk counts 1,647 "interpreting news" markets ($1.25B, 85% US interest rates) with median volume falling from $49k to $13k in 2025 [14]. Disease: 23 weekly flu-hospitalisation ladders in 2026 (weeks 12-34) with $225k of total volume, $261-$39k per week [16]; the 2026 measles ladder has $8.07M of lifetime volume but $26.5k of liquidity, $2.7k of bids within 5c on the 6k rung [16][17]. Politics one to six weeks out (the largest cells in [2]): Brazil's first round, Bulgaria's presidency, Senate control and the balance of power resolve 4 Oct-15 Nov with $60k-$460k of liquidity per market at 1c spreads [16]. Direction: politics depth growing; macro ladders stagnant on Polymarket while Kalshi holds the depth [14][18]; disease seasonal.
- **Pros**: Public, free, well-documented inputs; the recalibration pattern replicated across two venues and 429k contracts [2]; edges that move over hours to weeks, so no latency race; maker expression earns the rebate instead of paying the fee; the sizing layer is a reusable module for every other directional file (21, 24, 25 and the speculative band).
- **Cons / risks**: Execution: capacity of tens of dollars per rung on macro and flu ladders [17]; the release second sweeps stale quotes (file 17). Model: a calibration slope is an in-sample average over 2021-2025 with half its variation noise [2]; political events cluster (one election night is one draw), so the ~350 independent resolutions needed to confirm a 2 pp edge [6] can take a year or more; election-poll models have no 2024 support [11][12]; fabricated polls now target markets (an 11-point-lead poll in the $106M LA mayor race was "later exposed as fake") [26]; 538's averages were shut on 2025-03-05 and the archive removed in May 2026 [24]. Capital: lock-up to resolution at a 3-7% annualised priced discount [E3]; correlated losses across sibling rungs. Platform: taker fee 0.04-0.05 x p(1-p) and 1-14c spreads on thin ladders [16][E1]. Reputation and data: health markets have attracted neither traders nor institutional users [14]; CDC counts are "preliminary and subject to change" [20].
- **Expected results**: No public Polymarket PnL for any variant. Elsewhere: the Kalshi live run, Gemini 3 with proper betting, $200, 26 days (Apr/May 2026), 1,605 forecasts, 236 fills, +80.33% ROI, Sharpe 3.35, win rate 50.9%, fees $26.31, co-authored by Kalshi Research [4]; Raven-Agent's 44-decision Polymarket replay: +15.9% (95% CI +3.8% to +30.0%) with risk controls and quarter-Kelly, against -10.7% flat stake [5]. Calibration studies report Brier improvements, not money: the slopes explain 71.5% of out-of-sample calibration variance on Kalshi [2], and no paper tests the tilt after spread, fee and lock-up. Guess for a solo book (own simulation: 12 uncorrelated markets a month at 0.60-0.85, 8% of capital each, one-month lock-up, 12-month averages): with a true net edge of 2 pp, $1k earns ~$27/month (10th-90th percentile of the yearly average -$37 to +$91), $5k ~$133 (-$188 to +$446), $10k ~$270 (-$360 to +$905); with zero edge the same book swings +/- $65, $320 and $650 a month. A 2 pp edge is indistinguishable from luck inside a year at any of these sizes.
- **Competitive landscape**: The Fed and CPI centres are arbitraged against Kalshi within 1-3 pp by cross-venue bots (file 07) and desks (E4 key fact 14); a League of Legends paired-comparison model only tied Polymarket's log-loss on 928 maps [13], which is what happens where professionals already price a ratings model. Ratings can disagree with a liquid price without being right: the 2026 World Chess Championship market prices Sindarov at 0.71/0.73 on $718k of liquidity [16] while the September Elo gap (2778 vs 2703 [25]) implies 0.86-0.92 over 14 games (derived; draw rate 45-65% a guess); the market knows things the rating does not. The mispriced niches (flu, measles, thin macro rungs) are uncontested because they are too small for anyone with payroll, and they stay small. Political recalibration has no visible incumbent: it needs patient, horizon-matched capital and event-clustered bookkeeping, which turnover bots do not do (E4, underestimate point 7). Being competitive means owning the sizing and bookkeeping layer, not a better model.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for trading public data on your own models (E9 traffic light: Allowed on both venues). Grey if you incorporate: the data-licence clause bars "proprietary trading firms" from using Polymarket data, including on-chain, without a written agreement [E9]. Kalshi as a hedge or reference is lawful only where you are eligible (KYC, 140+ countries; a US person cannot pair it with Polymarket international) [E5][E9]. Never trade a market whose input you can influence (Rule 7.2(i) on the US venue) [E9]; health-outcome markets carry reputational, not legal, exposure.
- **How you'd validate it**: Three leakage-controlled tests, then a pilot. (1) Recalibration, out of sample: every Polymarket politics market resolved since 2026-01-01 (Gamma `closed=true`, `/prices-history` at T-7 d and T-30 d); apply the published slopes (1.45 and 1.83, no refitting); compute Brier of raw vs recalibrated prices and paper PnL of proper betting at the historical bid/ask with fee 0.04 x p(1-p) and the horizon's settlement discount; event-clustered bootstrap. Pass: lower recalibrated Brier with the clustered CI excluding zero and positive paper PnL over >= 350 markets [6]; fail if the gain vanishes once sibling rungs are clustered. Cost ~20 hours, free data. (2) Macro: log score of the Cleveland Fed nowcast (bucketised with a ~0.1 pp normal error, a guess to be replaced by its published error) against Polymarket and Kalshi mids at T-24 h for the next six CPI and six jobs releases [19][21]; resolved history for a longer backtest exists (27 Fed, 29 jobs, 38 unemployment, 7 monthly CPI ladders [16]). Pass: the nowcast beats the market log score in >= 8 of 12 and positive paper PnL at the ask; given [8][9], expect failure. (3) Disease: replay FluSight ensemble quantiles against the 23 weekly 2026 flu ladders at T-1 day [1][22], and a pace baseline against the measles rungs (3,659 cases on 24 Sep; the 5k rung at 0.885 needs the year-to-date pace of 13.7 a day to continue, the 6k rung at 0.405 needs 1.74x that) [16][20]; pass: positive paper PnL after walking the book depth. (4) Pilot: $500-$1,000, maker orders only, proper betting, three months, comparing fills to the paper book.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive. Calibration slopes are stable for months, FluSight updates weekly and nowcasts daily; a model that must be first is a latency strategy, not this file. The one fast moment is the scheduled release, and the right response there is to be absent.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model refresh | weekly and daily feeds [19][20][22] | nothing measurable inside a day | daily repricing |
| Entry | resting orders at model price; 25% maker rebate pool [E1] | taking costs 0.8-1.3c per share at p = 0.7 plus half the spread (derived) | post-only |
| Scheduled releases | BLS 08:30 ET calendar [21]; books are swept within hundreds of ms (file 17) | your resting quote is the stale one | cancel-all 1-5 min before every release |
| Exit | hold to resolution and redeem | none; selling early pays a 1-14c spread twice | never sell into a thin book |
| Reference leg | Kalshi REST, no auth for prices [18] | none; a 1-3 pp cross-venue gap is file 07's business | poll every few minutes |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Recalibration: a price sampled an hour later has the same slope; [2] measures horizon cells, not minutes. (2) Macro ladders: the only speed-dependent PnL is the release second, where a quote left resting pays the whole print. (3) Disease: the CDC counter updates weekly, and a rung with $69 of bids within 2c cannot absorb a fast trader anyway [17]. (4) The 250 ms taker delay is documented for crypto and finance up/down markets only, and the CLOB market records for the Fed and core-CPI markets carry no delay flag (`sd: 1` appears only on live sports markets; own check 28 Sep 16:13 UTC) [E1][17]; 99.13% uptime and cancel-only modes matter only around releases [E1].
- **Where to run it**: any small VPS in a non-restricted jurisdiction; eu-west-1 is not worth paying for unless you also quote around releases. Not the UK or US [E1][E9].

### Results by investment size
Assumptions: maker expression; net edge 0-4 pp per position after fee, spread and discount (guess); 12 positions a month, limited by independent political events and by $24-$2,700 of resting depth on macro and disease rungs [17]; PnL from the simulation in Expected results (12-month averages).

| Capital | Workable? | How it is deployed | Gross / month | Running costs / month | Net / month | Net / capital / month | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1k | marginal | 8-12 political and macro positions of $50-$100 | $0-$55 (guess) | $5-15 | -$15 to +$40 | -1.5% to +4% | fixed costs and one clustered election loss |
| $5k | yes | 12 positions of $300-$400; politics plus one or two macro ladders; flu and measles in season | $0-$270 (guess) | $15-70 | -$70 to +$200 | -1.4% to +4% | thin rungs cap disease and macro at a few hundred dollars each |
| $10k | yes | 12-15 positions of $600-$800, mostly politics | $0-$525 (guess) | $70-150 | -$150 to +$400 | -1.5% to +4% | ~10-15 independent events a month; yearly-average 10th percentile -$360 even at a 2 pp edge (derived) |

- **Minimum sensible capital**: ~$2k, so that 10 concurrent $150-$200 positions leave room for a cluster loss; below it running costs exceed the expected edge.
- **Capacity ceiling**: ~$50k-$100k for the political tilt (guess; sampled markets hold $60k-$460k each [16] but a 5 pp tilt moves a 1c-spread book); ~$1k-$5k per season for flu and a few thousand for measles [17]; zero incremental capacity on Fed and CPI centres, where Kalshi sets the price [18].
