### Concentrated discretionary research bets and purchased proprietary data
- **One-line summary**: A human reads what the crowd does not (poll crosstabs and sampling rules, the procedural calendar behind a headline, local-language sources, the Fed's signalling) and puts a few large positions on where the price is wrong, holding to settlement; at the top end, buying private data the market lacks. It produced the largest fortunes on the venue and it is the least repeatable strategy in this survey.
- **Category**: Directional trading [discretionary, human-in-the-loop; the systematic versions are files 20 and 21]
- **Maturity**: Established as a practice, unproven as a repeatable income. Hard data: the all-time politics leaderboard is Theo4 +$22.05M, Fredi9999 +$16.62M (now +$16.66M in the politics cut), zxgngl +$11.45M politics, Len9311238 +$8.71M, RepTrump +$7.53M, PrincessCaro +$6.08M, walletmobile +$5.94M [1]; every one of those seven wallets booked its whole lifetime PnL in November-December 2024 and has made zero fills since 2025-01-01 [2]. Theo4's 16,002 fills sit between 14 Oct and 13 Nov 2024; walletmobile's 5,025 fills all fall on 5-6 November [2]. The mid-tier is alive: gopfan2 has realised +$1.58M since August 2024 on $37.4M of USDC volume (4.2%) and +$135k in September 2026; RememberAmalek (interviewed below) +$849k realised on $17.3M; SlotinCap +$538k on $839k of volume in 398 fills since April 2026 [2][3]. The style persists at 1-5% of turnover with extreme concentration; its hall of fame is one election.
- **Requires AI?**: optional. The decisions are human. An LLM is useful as an analyst-assist layer (rule-text parsing, crosstab extraction from pollster PDFs, base-rate packs, local-language summaries, a pre-registration ledger); sizing and entry stay with the person. The fully automated version is file 21, which loses to the price on liquid markets.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Pick a small universe you can out-read: one election cycle, one central bank, one region's politics. RetardBoyBilly wins "almost each month" on Fed-funds markets because "people like Steve Liesman know for certain what the Fed decision will be 2-3 weeks before" [11]; RememberAmalek runs "fewer, bigger, highly researched bets" [9].
  2. Sub-variant A, polling and crosstab forensics: check sample composition (party ID, recalled vote, likely-voter screen), the field dates and the pollster's house effect; RememberAmalek's South Korea trade rested on the rule that exit polling is illegal there except on election day, so the published exit poll used "incomplete data from just the final day": the market traded 51% and the result was 49.4% [9]. The Mamdani entry at 8% came from a Marist poll he judged "mathematically wrong" [9].
  3. Sub-variant B, post-event "reversing stupidity": after a salient event, find the folk belief the price now embeds and sell it. Semi's examples: the top three papal candidates summed to 75% among 150 cardinals, so buy NO on all three; "tax on tips" priced as if a bill could pass inside a recess; inaugural TV-ratings and Rick Scott-for-leader markets above 50% [10]. Do not react in the first seconds ("Most people panic and make emotional decisions in 5 seconds" [9]); those belong to file 17.
  4. Sub-variant C, local-language and political-culture reads: Prophet bought Nawrocki when "everyone in Poland was saying it was 50-50, but Polymarket had it at 80-20 for Trzaskowski", allocated ~20% of bankroll after round one and ended "around 2.5x my bankroll" [12]; he blames Romanian traders expecting a repeat of their annulled election [12].
  5. Sub-variant D, commissioned private data: identify a systematic error in public information, buy an instrument that corrects it, size. Theo commissioned YouGov "neighbour" polls (who will your neighbours vote for) in Pennsylvania, Michigan and Wisconsin on 15 October 2024 to surface shy Trump voters, staked about $80M across 11 accounts and made about $85M [13][14][15]; Polymarket confirmed four of the accounts as one French trader on 2024-10-24 [16]. Three of his named wallets are still #1, #3 and #17 all-time, $44.8M between them [1][4].
  6. Enter with resting limit orders where possible (politics taker fee 0.04 x p(1-p), makers pay nothing and share a 25% rebate pool [22]); build over days or weeks (SlotinCap: 82% of notional in one US-Iran peace-deal event, one $328k fill; gopfan2: 74% of its last 1,000 fills in the Russian-parliament event [3]).
  7. Hold to settlement; sell only when the thesis is priced (RetardBoyBilly: "You can always sell at around 90%" [11]). Capital is locked for weeks to months, softened on the flagged long-dated markets by the 3.25% holding reward (50 of the 3,310 midterm markets carry it, including House, Senate and Balance of Power) [5].
  8. Log the probability and the thesis before each entry and measure the price a week later and at settlement (validation below).
- **What creates the edge**: Information the crowd has not processed, sized when the reading is confident. The counterparties: (1) headline readers and post-event panic, the retail base whose politics-first users make a median 13 trades at $6 [E4]; (2) the political underconfidence of the price itself, slope 1.45 on Polymarket and 1.83 on Kalshi at one week to one month, so a 0.70 political price a week out maps to ~0.83 [18][E4]: favourites backed by strong private data are structurally cheap, which is exactly Theo's 2024 trade; (3) longshot buyers, who lose 16.3% equal-weighted and 46.2% pooled in Politics [19][E4]; (4) foreign herding, as in the Romanian read of Poland [12]. Sizing, not accuracy, is where the money is: across 8,656 wallets calibration explains 2.2% of profit variance (Spearman +0.148), the top-100 hold a median of 18 resolved positions, the median wallet's largest event drives 66.2% of its PnL, and the worst-calibrated quartile earned 8.2x the median PnL of the best ($14,437 vs $1,766); the authors call every profit figure provisional (37.8% of histories cut at 2,000 fills, a bug dropped 40% of fills) [17].
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k to run it honestly, $10k+ before research time pays. Positions lock until the event (weeks for a Fed meeting, months for a midterm race); turnover is 1-2x a quarter, so expected profit is edge x turnover, not edge x volume. The private-data variant is a $100k+ game: a serious state poll is a five-figure item (guess; YouGov publishes no prices) and pays only where books are deep: Democrats-control-the-House shows $783k of bids and $820k of asks within 5c at 0.92 / 0.93; the Senate 0.62 / 0.63 with $464k / $96k; Michigan Senate $58k / $68k; Maine $7k / $73k; Texas Senate $44k / $56k; the state-level midterm races have a median lifetime volume of ~$12k and median liquidity of $65-80k (250 events on a loose selection, 77 on a strict state-name match; medians $11.5k / $65k and $12.9k / $81k) [5][8].
- **Technical requirements**: Almost none for the human version: a Polymarket account, the Betmoar terminal (0 bps builder, a "custom page" RetardBoyBilly uses instead of bots [11][E6]), pollster PDFs, a spreadsheet. The realistic automation target is an analyst-assist agent with human sizing: (1) Gamma new-market watcher with rule-text and resolution-source extraction; (2) a base-rate pack per market family (reference classes, domain calibration slopes [18], the FLB tables [19]); (3) poll ingestion: crosstab tables out of PDFs, sample-composition checks, house-effect adjustment against the aggregate; (4) local-language source digests (the speculative full version is file 57); (5) a pre-registration ledger that stores your probability and thesis, then computes closing-line value (CLV) and Brier against the market. Reuse file 21's stack. Build: 3-6 weeks solo; LLM spend $20-150 a month at file 21's usage. The agent must never size or enter; sold as a product, auto-execution or signals are a CTA registration question [E9].
- **Opportunity size**: Concentrated in a few deep events per year. The 2026 midterms are the next depth window: at least 300 events under the `midterms` tag, ~3,300 markets, $52.4M lifetime volume, $28.0M of Gamma liquidity, $6.1M traded in the last week; House control alone $12.5M of volume and $952k of liquidity; ~960-980 markets carry liquidity-reward pools and all but eight charge the 0.04 politics fee [5]. Fed-path markets: the October 2026 decision event holds $15.65M of volume and $2.2M of liquidity (economics fee 0.05 / 25% rebate); the January and December 2025-26 events closed with $659M and $394M [7]. Politics as a whole: the 300 most active events carry $8.5-8.8M of 24-hour volume and $297M of liquidity; ~4,550-4,590 markets at 0.04, ~540 without a fee schedule (largely fee-free geopolitics), 429 with holding rewards [6][22]. Profit pool: the top-10 politics wallets held $94M of lifetime profit in May 2026 [20], but the 2024 cohort is gone and September's politics leaderboard is topped by multi-category and bot accounts (ndb1 $1.35M, whose fills are NFL and World Cup; balthazar, 887k lifetime fills) [1][2]. Trend: volume growing into the midterms, edge per dollar unknown; the structural biases are replicated and slow, which is why they survive.
- **Pros**: No infrastructure, no latency race, no fee on maker entries; the two largest documented biases in the venue's price (political underconfidence, non-sports longshot overpricing) are slow enough for a human; deep books on the marquee races; holding rewards on the long-dated flagged markets; the research compounds into files 15, 20 and 21.
- **Cons / risks**: Survivorship: the leaderboard shows the winners of one coin flip; zxgngl made $11.45M on the 2024 election and then lost $3.64M holding 11.08M Tyson shares at 0.329 into Paul-Tyson, a position it still holds at $0 [2][3]; RememberAmalek lost $25k against Biden dropping out [9]; Semi was "totally crushed" on Tulsi Gabbard NO and lost on Harris [10]. Concentration is the strategy and the risk: 69.9% of scored wallets take most of their PnL from one event [17]. Self-reports do not reconcile: the interview credits RememberAmalek with ~$300k on Mamdani and ~$500k on South Korea [9], but the named wallet shows +$117k realised for all of 2025, a $25,000 South Korea redemption and $4,125 on the Cuomo market; other wallets are possible, but the on-chain figures are 5-20x smaller [2][3][25]. Capital lock at zero yield outside the holding-reward markets. Resolution risk on the markets where rules matter (file 15). Information risk: one interviewee cites "contacts in governments around the world from my family's business" [9]; that is prohibited insider conduct under ToS §4.2 and the CFTC has charged non-US residents for Polymarket trades [E9]. Multi-account size masking is monitored ("multi-account coordination") and a rule violation on the US venue [E9]. France, Theo's country, opened a gambling-law inquiry after his win [15] and has been ISP-blocked since July 2026 [E9]; US persons cannot use polymarket.com at all [E9].

- **Competitive landscape**: Uncrowded in the sense that matters: nobody can out-size you into a thin state race, and the 2024 whales have left. Crowded in the sense that the marquee markets are priced by the same public polls everyone reads and by desks (Jump, Wintermute, SIG, DRW) that quote them [E4]. Discretionary makers such as Domer ("it's all manual ... most of my trades are my orders being matched", ~$300M volume, 5,000+ markets in 2024) sit on the other side of your taker entries [21][pre-2025]. Informed political takers exist and are concentrated: 0.26% of winning taker wallets took ~50% of politics taker gains in Dec 2025-Feb 2026 [E4]. Competitive means a domain you read better than the aggregate, patience to enter over days and the nerve to size; no verified 2025-26 repeat of Theo's playbook was found (unverified either way).
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for trading your own research from one identity outside restricted jurisdictions (E9 traffic light: own research, models: Allowed) [E9]. Grey: splitting size across accounts (the international ToS has no multiple-account clause but "multi-account coordination" is monitored conduct; on Polymarket US every account must be disclosed and self-matching is prohibited, rulebook 3.6(f) and 7.2(q)); selling the research or an auto-executing assistant (CTA; Letter 26-25 excludes signals); managing others' money from one account [E9]. Prohibited: any use of tipped, employer or government information (ToS §4.2 three insider prongs; CFTC cases Van Dyke and Spagnuolo, one a Swiss resident) [E9]; the "family contacts in governments" source in [9] is on the wrong side of that line. Commissioning polls is lawful; publishing them while positioned is a market-manipulation question on the US venue. France: ISP block since July 2026 [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: Three months, no capital. (1) Pre-registration: for one domain (a midterm map or the Fed path), write your probability and one-paragraph thesis before every would-be entry into a ledger, with the price and depth at the time [5][8]; paper-size at 5-10% of a notional $10k. (2) Metrics: CLV in cents one week later and at settlement, Brier versus the market, paper PnL after the 0.04 fee and the quoted spread; 60-100 pre-registered calls detect a 5-point average edge (rough power at outcome variance 0.25). Pass: mean CLV > +2c at n >= 60 and paper PnL > 0 after costs. Fail: edge confined to markets that later moved on news you did not anticipate. (3) Private-data pilot: one $2-4k DIY panel with state and party quotas (Pollfish $0.95 per response plus quota fees; Prolific $8-12 an hour plus a 42.8% fee [23][24]) on one contested primary; pass if its error against the result beats the public average and the market price at field time. (4) Only then a $2-5k live book, reconciling fills, fees and rebates through the Data API [2]. Cost: $0-50 of tooling, $2-4k if the poll pilot runs.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive. The edge lives in reading and sizing over days; the interviewees' explicit rule is to wait after news [9]. The only fast parts, election-night returns and scheduled prints, are file 17's business.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Gamma new-market feed, poll release calendars | you miss the first-day mispricing that file 05 quotes into | daily scan; alerts on new markets in your domain |
| Fair value | crosstab reading, base rates, local sources | none from speed; everything from depth | hours per position; pre-registered before entry |
| Entry | resting bids under the ask; depth $7k-$820k within 5c on midterm books [8] | taker fee 0.04 x p(1-p) (1c at 50c) plus 1-2c spread per turn [22] | GTC bids built over days; lift only when the thesis is time-critical |
| Exit | selling into the crowd once priced (~90%) [11] | bid depth thins in the last days; you hold to settlement | pre-set exit levels; accept settlement lock |
| Settlement / capital recycling | event date; UMA proposal and 2-hour liveness; disputes (file 15) | capital locked weeks to months at 0% except holding-reward markets [5] | horizon-matched capital; 3.25% holding reward where flagged |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Nothing between 100 ms and a day: the 2024 whales accumulated over four weeks (Theo4 14 Oct-13 Nov) and SlotinCap over months [2][3]. (2) The first minutes after a poll or a court ruling are negative for you: the wallets that win those are file 17's takers and the rest is panic you should fade, not join [9][10]. (3) Rate limits and uptime are irrelevant at tens of orders a week; the 99.13% trading-API uptime matters only if you need to exit on election night [E1].
- **Where to run it**: a laptop; nothing is worth paying for in hosting. Location is a legal, not a latency, question: not the US, the UK, France or any close-only jurisdiction [E9].
