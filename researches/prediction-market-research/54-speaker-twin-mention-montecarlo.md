# 54 - Speaker-twin Monte Carlo for mention markets (scripted speakers, maker expression)
_Band: speculative · Evidence grade: D (no synthetic-transcript forecaster has been scored against a price anywhere; A for the own Gamma / CLOB / Kalshi / Fed-transcript baseline measured here; B for the one paper, which asks an LLM for a probability rather than simulating speech; C for the two self-reported bot records) · Last verified: 2026-09-29_

### Speaker-twin Monte Carlo for mention markets
- **One-line summary**: Simulate a scripted speaker's next earnings call, Fed press conference or White House briefing a few hundred times, count strike words under the contract's rule, blend with a transcript base rate and the price, and rest maker quotes where the blend disagrees. Unproven: no synthetic-transcript forecaster has been scored against a price, the one LLM result loses to the market alone, and on 2,597 resolved scripted strikes the price is far ahead of any base rate.
- **Category**: Speculative-novel [information edge on scripted-speaker mention strikes, expressed as maker quotes]. The NO-side ladder that sells over-priced 10-50c strikes is a behavioural maker strategy and lives in file 23 (critic note).
- **Maturity**: Experimental for the twin, and no record exists for the Monte Carlo design itself. The only paper (Kim et al.: 856 Kalshi earnings-call contracts, 70 calls April-December 2025, GPT-5.1 seven days out) asks for a probability given the prior-quarter transcript, up to 100 news items and the price, and never generates transcripts: market Brier 0.1402, model 0.1470, a 0.7 / 0.3 mix 0.1392, α chosen on a held-out split, no interval [1]. The one public live record, a Kalshi speaker-mention detector (337 transcripts, Beta-Binomial posteriors from 12,490 resolved outcomes), lost $16.40 over 1,115 small bets; its LLM scorer had Brier 0.352 against the mid's 0.238, its pure Beta-Binomial scorer went 43-89 for −$13.37 (self-reported) [2]. The only profitable record, $5,515.94 net on $342,506 of cost over 2,717 Kalshi mention markets, came from an always-NO ladder whose author's LLM "thought every word ... was higher chance to hit than the market prices implied" (self-reported) [3]: file 23's strategy, not this one. About twenty 2026 GitHub mention-market repositories publish no scored twin [22]. Adjacent work simulates speech for style, not counts: role-play is scored on judged fidelity [17][18], synthetic earnings-call transcripts are a counterfactual analysis tool [21], and LLM agents reading ECB transcripts predict the market's reaction, not the wording [19].
- **Requires AI?**: yes for the twin: an LLM writes synthetic prepared remarks and, for earnings calls, analyst-question twins; everything else (rule parser, base rate, blend, sizing) is statistics. The same pipeline without the twin is file 23's base-rate model.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Targets: Polymarket's `earnings-call-mentions` series (131 of 135 resolved events resolve if the term is "mentioned by anyone during this event", analysts included), Fed-chair press conferences ("if Warsh says the listed term", reporters excluded) and White House briefings ("the acting White House Press Secretary") [11]; improvisers (Trump rallies, podcasts, sports announcers) are excluded because nobody can simulate them [13].
  2. Corpus: the speaker's last 8-20 transcripts with speaker labels (federalreserve.gov publishes every press conference [10]; earnings transcripts need a licensed source); the week's news digest dated before the forecast time.
  3. Twin: 200-500 rollouts of the whole event (prepared remarks plus Q&A, with analyst or reporter twins built from each questioner's past questions) at non-zero temperature.
  4. Parser: apply the contract rule to every rollout (plurals and possessives count, other forms not; compounds; "N+ times"; who counts as the speaker; acronyms) and take the share meeting it.
  5. Base rate: the share of the speaker's previous K transcripts meeting the same rule, Beta(1,1) (a hierarchical Beta-Binomial across speakers for sparse words).
  6. Blend: logit or linear mix of twin, base rate and mid, weights fitted on earlier events only; act only where the blend differs from the mid by more than half the spread.
  7. Express as small resting maker orders (fee 0; takers pay 0.04·p(1−p), 1c a share at 50c [12]); cancel before the event starts, because script holders are the tail risk [3][E9].
- **What creates the edge**: Mechanism: a frequency base rate cannot see the agenda (a new product, a tariff shock, an analyst who always asks about China); a generator conditioned on this week's context and the questioner roster could, and on Polymarket earnings calls analysts' words count [11]. The payers would be file 23's retail YES buyers. Strongest counter-argument, in four parts. (a) The evidence is negative: LLM-alone loses to the price and the best blend gains 0.001 Brier with no interval [1]; LLM speech overuses nouns, possessives and "we" [15], so simulated agenda-noun counts run high, as the NO-ladder author's LLM did [3]; individual twins correlate r = 0.20 with their people [16]; a $1B simulation vendor says it cannot model Powell [13]. (b) The price already knows most of it [7]: on 2,597 resolved scripted strikes in 163 events (December 2024 - September 2026) the mid's per-strike Brier is 0.152 at T-24h (144 events, 1,220 Yes of 2,249), 0.154 at T-6h, 0.156 at T-1h (from the scheduled start; strikes counted only if listed by then); a leave-future-out hierarchical Beta-Binomial on Polymarket's own history scores 0.246, worse by 0.094 [0.084, 0.105], and a blend fitted on earlier events puts 0.90-1.00 on the price. On the 15 Fed-chair pressers a Beta(1,1) base rate over the chair's previous eight transcripts scores 0.204 against the mid's 0.179 at T-24h (302 strikes, 139 Yes); blended with weights fitted on earlier events (0.75-1.0 on the price) it gains 0.0018 [0.0000, 0.0040] on 225 strikes in 10 events and nothing at T-1h [10]. That sliver, not the market, is the bar a twin must clear; the price does not sharpen before the event because the information arrives during it (file 23's live leg, insiders). (c) The mispricing that exists is structural and file 23's: at T-24h strikes whose mid is 20-30c resolve Yes 11.3% (221 strikes) and 30-50c 30.4% (441), and NO on every 20-50c strike at the mid makes +11.6c a share [8.2, 15.0] (662 strikes, 138 events; file 23: +14.6c). But `prices-history` is the raw book midpoint (equal to CLOB `/midpoint` on 24 of 24 live strikes, e.g. 0.605 on a 0.26 / 0.95 book [8]), not a tradable price. At executed prices (Data API v1 `/trades`, `takerOnly=true`, 54,606 fills before T-1h [7]) a resting NO seller filled by 20-50c YES buyers earned +8.9c a share averaged per strike [5.1, 12.4] (678 strikes, 129 events): +9c on taker orders under 100 shares, −4c on 100-1,000-share orders that carry 59% of filled shares, so −0.4c size-weighted [−7.6, +11.1] and +4.7c [−2.1, +13.6] capped at 20 shares a fill; taker NO buyers made +4.9c per strike [1.3, 8.6] after the fee. The rent sits in small tickets, and no twin is needed to collect it. (d) Capacity: NO-side maker capital filled at 20-50c before T-1h was a median $363 per event (p75 $1,488) [7], and volume is shrinking (below).
- **Capital required**: <$1k to test, a few thousand to trade; capacity-bound. Pre-event books on the two live calls (Micron, Nike) show a median 35c spread, a median $64 of YES bids and $16 to buy NO within 5c of the touch per strike [8]; strikes settle within hours of the event, so capital recycles weekly.
- **Technical requirements**: Agent design sketch. (1) Rule compiler: the event's rules text and the Mentions PDF into a machine rule per strike (speaker set, forms, compounds, thresholds); on the 320 Fed-chair strikes a plural / possessive regex over the chair's turns reproduces 316 resolutions [10], so ~1% of strikes sit where parser and resolver disagree. (2) Corpus store with speaker-labelled turns and dates. (3) Twin agents: one speaker agent (sees the corpus, the prepared-remarks structure, the dated news digest, never the price); one questioner agent per expected analyst or reporter (sees that person's past questions); a moderator agent that orders the Q&A. (4) Counter: the compiled rule over each rollout. (5) Aggregator: share of rollouts, shrunk toward the transcript base rate (weights fitted leave-future-out), then mixed with the mid. (6) Maker execution with per-event caps and a pre-start cancel. Cost per event, estimated not measured: 300 rollouts × ~12k output tokens (a Fed transcript runs 4,500-9,500 words, the chair's share 3,200-7,500 [10]; an earnings call is similar, estimate) = ~3.6M output plus ~15M mostly cached input tokens: ~$3 on GPT-6 Luna, ~$25-35 on Haiku 4.5, ~$40-65 on Sonnet 5 at file 21's list prices, half on batch; $0.10-2 per strike. Kim et al.'s single market-conditioned call costs cents per contract [1]. Build: 3-5 weeks solo for Fed and briefings, plus a transcript licence and 2-3 weeks for earnings calls.
- **Opportunity size**: Small and shrinking on Polymarket [6]. Earnings calls: median $39-42k per event in late 2025 and early 2026 (Netflix January 2026 $6.6M the outlier), $11.5k in July-September 2026 ($861k, $235k and $145k over 24, 20 and 13 events); median strike $1,721 in 2025, $531 now. Fed-chair pressers: $0.6-5.5M per event under Powell in 2025, $143-311k for Warsh's three; median strike $25.7k in 2025, $4.0k for Warsh. Briefings: up to $273k per event (January 2026), median strike $381 since July. Kalshi (US persons only) is 10-30 times larger in contracts, roughly half that in dollars (derived): ~100 earnings events in July-September 2026 at a median ~90k contracts (Costco 263,853 against Polymarket's $15.2k, Palantir 229,315 against $16.0k); its July and September Fed pressers traded 1,070,747 and 754,609 contracts against $311k and $143k [9]. Kalshi counts only "any [company] representative (including the operator of the call)" [9], Polymarket anyone, so analyst-spoken words separate the venues (the announcer analogue: "Mahomes" 68.5% vs 79.5% at Super Bowl LX [4]).
- **Pros**: Scripted speakers repeat themselves, and Fed transcripts are free and speaker-labelled [10]; the parser, corpus and base rate are reusable by file 23; strikes resolve within hours, so feedback is fast; maker fills pay no fee and earn a share of the 25% rebate pool and $1-25 a day of liquidity rewards per strike [8][12].
- **Cons / risks**: Edge: (a)-(d) above. Leakage: every model has read past transcripts; Gao et al.'s look-ahead propensity is positive in-sample and collapses only after the training cutoff [20]. Resolution: ~1% parser-resolver disagreement [10]. Timing: Gamma's `startTime` is an hour early on 6 of 15 Fed pressers and wrong-dated on one [6][10]; DraftKings' November 2025 rule text gave the wrong time, leaving 10 of 13 strikes decided at "T-6h" [7]; "next briefing" events list before the briefing is scheduled, so a briefing's T-24h is a hindsight anchor. Counterparties: script holders (file 30) and speakers who read the list aloud (Coinbase Q3 2025: ~$84k riding, ~$4k of it on Polymarket [4][5]). Venue: per NPR the CFTC is "taking a hard look" at mention contracts as "potentially very easy to manipulate", and Kalshi pulled sports mentions on 2026-08-14 [5]. Cost: LLM spend per event is of the order of a strike's near-touch depth.
- **Expected results**: no public data, theoretical only. Adjacent: Kim et al.'s blend beats the price by 0.001 Brier with no interval [1]; the live detector lost $16.40 [2]; the NO ladder made 1.6% on cost on Kalshi (self-reported) [3]. A no-AI Fed hypothesis for file 23: taking the transcript base rate's side where it differs from the mid by more than 20c a day out made +13.9c a share at the mid and +8.2c [0.1, 16.1] after a 5c half-spread and the fee (70 strikes, 15 events, one of several cells, gone by T-1h), and is negative at half of today's 35c median spread [8][10]. Guess: a twin-plus-base-rate blend adds 0 to 0.003 Brier over the price; at $16-64 of near-touch depth and ~25 events a month that is −$100 to +$100 a month gross before $40-700 of LLM spend: negative net unless file 23's small-ticket NO rent carries it.
- **Competitive landscape**: No twin trader found [22]. Prices on Polymarket's scripted strikes are set by retail YES buyers, file 23's NO sellers, a maker wall of 0.95-0.98 asks on most live earnings strikes [8], and live-leg snipers [4]. On Kalshi volume is 10-30 times larger in contracts and the published bots (the detector [2], the NO ladder [3], an earnings scanner with 17,127 transcripts that never executes [22]) work from base rates. Being competitive would mean a leak-free, pre-registered ledger nobody has.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: grey. Own models on public transcripts: Allowed [E9]. Prohibited: trading on advance text, a script or a teleprompter (ToS §4.2 insider prongs; CFTC 9289-26 against the White House teleprompter operator Gabriel Perez: $107,539.02 disgorgement, $65,000 penalty, three-year ban, Kalshi mention markets [E9], file 30); for earnings calls an insider's script is also securities MNPI. Never influence a speaker, questioner or audience (file 31). Never publish synthetic transcripts of real people: LLM impersonations of public figures are judged more authentic than the originals [14]; keep outputs as internal counts. Earnings-call transcripts are copyrighted [2]; federalreserve.gov transcripts are public domain. Mentions list on the international venue, not the US app (NPR, August 2026), and are under CFTC review [5].
- **How you'd validate it**: Phase 0 (done, $0): market baseline, base-rate arms, executed-price check, parser audit and power [7][10]. Phase 1, retrospective kill test, 2-4 weeks, ~$300-2,000 of LLM spend: Fed-chair pressers, briefings and earnings calls on Polymarket and Kalshi (Kalshi as scoring target only) listed and resolved after every model's training cutoff; one forecast per strike at T-24h before the scheduled start taken from the rule text or the Fed calendar (briefings only once scheduled), strikes counted only if listed by then. Leakage control: retrieval limited to documents dated before T-24h; Gao et al.'s look-ahead test per (speaker, date) [20]; audit of 30 rollout bundles for verbatim transcript recall. Six pre-registered arms: transcript base rate (previous eight transcripts, same parser); twin only; twin shrunk to the base rate (weights fitted on the first 40% of events, then frozen); market mid; file 21's plain forecaster in Kim et al.'s market-conditioned form [1]; mid + shrunk twin. Metrics: per-strike Brier and log loss against the mid, event-clustered paired bootstrap; maker PnL at recorded books after rebates, per strike and size-weighted. Power [7][10]: an arm that disagrees with the mid as much as a base rate does (per-event summed Brier-difference SD 1.11 over ~16 strikes) needs ~390 events for 0.01 per strike (~120 Fed pressers: SD 0.79 over ~20 strikes); the pass test compares two blends near the mid and is cheaper: the Fed no-twin blend minus mid has SD 0.068 per event over ~22 strikes, so 0.002 per strike needs ~18 pressers and 0.001 ~72 (rough: 10 events). Polymarket listed ~110 scripted events in January-September 2026; Kalshi's copies add strikes, not independent events. Pass: mid + shrunk twin beats mid + base rate (worth 0.0018 on Fed pressers [10]) with the interval above zero AND the twin beats the base rate on log loss. Kill (the pre-registered null): "twin + base rate = base rate + market", i.e. a frozen twin weight of zero or a gain over the no-twin blend ≤ 0 after 200 events. Phase 2, forward, 3 months, $100-500: frozen pipeline on every scripted Polymarket event, paper maker orders at recorded books; live money only if Phase 2 repeats Phase 1's sign.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive (hours to days). The rollout batch runs once, a day before the event; the price barely moves from T-24h to T-1h [7]. The live leg (transcribe and lift stale quotes when a word is said) is latency-sensitive and is file 23's race.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Gamma `earnings-call-mentions`, `warsh-mentions`, `leavitt-next-briefing` series; events list about a week ahead (BlackBerry: seven days) [6] | nothing lost: early prices are no worse than late ones [7] | daily scan |
| Fair value | 200-500 rollouts, 30-90 minutes on batch | none; stale news bundles are a leak risk, not a speed risk | one batch at T-24h to T-30h |
| Entry | maker orders inside 35c median spreads [8] | crossing costs half the spread plus 0.04·p(1−p) | rest inside the reward band, ≥ min size |
| Pre-start cancel | script leaks and live-leg snipers | a resting YES ask is lifted the second the word is said | cancel all at T-5 min |
| Settlement | UMA proposal a few hours after the event; 13 of 3,037 scripted strikes show a dispute round in Gamma's `umaResolutionStatuses`, 230 a second proposal [6] | capital held a day or two longer | hold to resolution |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Market Brier is 0.152 at T-24h and 0.156 at T-1h [7]: forecasting earlier costs nothing, and there is no pre-event drift to front-run. (2) The live leg reprices strikes from 0.24 to 0.94 in a second and splits $0.4-0.5k among 6-13 wallets (file 23); it is not this file's edge. (3) Rate limits and uptime are irrelevant at ~16 orders an event.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted (not the US, UK or France [E9]); LLM batch jobs dominate; no co-location.
