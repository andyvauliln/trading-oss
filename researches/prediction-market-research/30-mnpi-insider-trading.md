# 30 - Trading on material non-public information (flagged: illegal, survey only)
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: B (A for the CFTC releases and the on-chain reconstruction of the charged wallets, re-run here from the Data API; B for the academic screens, which have unknown precision; C for platform self-reports on surveillance and referrals; D for any "follow the insider" PnL, none public) · Last verified: 2026-09-28_

### Trading on material non-public information (flagged: illegal, survey only)
- **One-line summary**: People who hold classified, corporate or production-side knowledge of an outcome buy the outcome on Polymarket before it is public; two of them were charged by the CFTC and the Southern District of New York in 2026, a third (a White House teleprompter operator trading Kalshi's mention markets) settled for 1.6x his profit, two Israelis were indicted for espionage-class offences [22][26] and an OpenAI employee was fired [24][25]. This file exists because those trades are the counterparty every maker, longshot seller and near-certain-bond buyer in geopolitics, mentions, corporate and awards markets is filled by, and because the cost can be measured from the public tape. It is not a strategy to build.
- **Category**: Information edge (prohibited) [flagged: illegal and ToS-violating; survey level only]
- **Maturity**: Established practice (illegal). Evidence that it happens at scale is hard: the CFTC's first two event-contract insider complaints both concern polymarket.com trades, one by a US soldier ($404,000+ on "Maduro out by January 31, 2026?") and one by a Swiss-resident Google engineer (~$1.2M on 23 Year in Search contracts) [1][2]; a screen of 210,718 wallet-market pairs over February 2024 to February 2026 finds a 69.9% win rate and ~$143M of anomalous profit, a stated lower bound [6][7][36]; a separate lifecycle heuristic flags 1,950 accounts with mean realised profit $15,012.92 and median $2,758.29 [8][11]; the Anti-Corruption Data Collective found 152 military-market wallets with a 97.2% win rate and $8M of profit through 2026-05-05 [13]. Own checks below reproduce the Van Dyke wallet to the dollar and the Spagnuolo lane to within 1% [15][29][30]. What is not established is that anyone lawfully earns from it: the follower and alert-service variants belong to file 27, and the only practitioner on record who tried to detect and ride insiders "did not succeed" [17], and the academic leakage scores that would quantify per-market front-running are computable for only 0.7% of markets and find zero 30-minute and 2-hour pre-event leakage on the largest Iran contract [9][10]. Enforcement is rising, so the practice is shrinking at the margin while remaining a permanent feature of the tape.
- **Requires AI?**: no. The insiders in the documented cases used a browser or a bot: seven fills in the Van Dyke case, 33 on the Iran rung for Magamyman, a few thousand mostly resting orders over two months for Spagnuolo [29][30][32]. For the honest builder the AI question is one of detection and belongs to file 27; here the only tooling is a ledger that measures what informed flow costs you.
- **How it works (simple flow)**: documented pattern at survey level; each step is what the charging documents and the public tape show, not a recipe:
  1. Someone with a duty of confidence learns the outcome: an Army master sergeant "involved in the planning and execution" of the Maduro operation [1][21]; a Google software engineer with access to the Year in Search rankings [2]; a White House teleprompter operator who saw speeches before delivery and traded Kalshi's mention markets [3]; an Israeli Air Force reservist major who attended a closed briefing two days before the June 2025 strike [22].
  2. Money goes into a wallet. The wallets are not always fresh: "Burdensome-Mix" had four markets and 15 fills in its whole life [29], "AlphaRaccoon" had traded since May 2024 across 92 markets [15][30], and "Magamyman", the largest pre-strike buyer on the Iran market, had 99 markets and 721 fills since October 2024 [32]. Fresh wallets are a minority of the large pre-news buyers in every tape checked here: 10 of the 45 largest in the Maduro window ($22k of $116k), 10 of the 40 largest in the Iran window ($39k of $263k, all ten created between 00:50 and 05:56 UTC on the day of the strike) and 10 of the 45 largest before the OpenAI browser launch ($65,838 of $286k, against Unusual Whales' "13 brand-new wallets, $309,486", whose window and definition are not published) [24][31][32][33]. The clearest fresh-wallet case is the second Maduro-cluster account in Nechepurenko's Table 3: created at 03:42 UTC on 3 January, 3 h 18 min before the move, $141,619.92 of realised PnL, and the second-largest pre-news buyer in the own recount [8][31].
  3. They buy the outcome at longshot prices over hours to days, as takers or as resting bids. Van Dyke's seven Maduro fills were all taker buys at 0.066-0.10 between 2025-12-31 00:20 and 2026-01-03 02:58 UTC, four hours before the price moved [29][31]; Spagnuolo's Year in Search lane was 2,709 fills in 23 markets of which only 122 were taker fills, so 42% of the lane's notional (54% of the wallet's) was resting orders that other people hit [30]. Magamyman's tape is a reversal rather than a clean entry: long Yes since 16-18 February and hedged with No from 18 February, the wallet sold its Yes at 0.14-0.16 on the afternoon of 27 February, then bought $85,515 of Yes at 0.170-0.223 in three fills from 05:15:53 UTC on 28 February, 58 minutes before the price jumped, sold 190,257 of those shares back within 90 seconds and unwound its No from 05:39; net new Yes exposure in that hour was about 250,000 shares for $53,000 [32].
  4. The event becomes public and the market goes to 1.00 within one to four hours: Maduro hourly mean 0.399 at 06:00 UTC on 3 January, 0.81 at 09:00, 0.997 at 10:00 [31]; Iran 0.165 at noon on 27 February, 0.435 at 06:13 the next morning, 0.85 two minutes later [32].
  5. They redeem. Every step is on Polygon with a wallet address, a pseudonym and a transaction hash, which is why a blog reconstructed the Google wallet from the DOJ figures alone and why the Data API returns the indicted profit to the cent [15][29].
  6. Polymarket's surveillance or an outside analyst flags the wallet, the platform refers it (100+ referrals, 315+ wallet details provided, "2 arrests, referred by Polymarket"), and the CFTC files a civil complaint alongside a criminal complaint or indictment [1][2][18][19].
- **What creates the edge**: misappropriated information, nothing else: the insider does not need speed, a model or capital. Who pays: (a) makers resting asks at longshot prices and the near-certain-bond buyers holding the NO side (files 02, 14, 24), the population that otherwise wins on Polymarket because its limit orders resolve favourably [12]; (b) takers who sell the outcome into the insider's resting bids, as in the Year in Search case where the insider was mostly the maker [30]; (c) copycats who arrive after the move (file 27). The bill is visible on the tape. In the 96 hours before the Maduro news, 171 wallets took 1.28M Yes shares below 0.35 for $119k; at settlement the resting sellers of those shares had lost $1.16M, 10.6% of the market's Gamma-reported $10.97M volume (which counts both sides of every fill) and 25% of the $4.54M of one-sided taker notional in the tape [28][31]. In the six hours before the Iran strike, fills of at least $1,000 alone show 40 wallets taking 1.40M Yes shares at or below 0.35 for $263k, a $1.14M loss to the sellers, and the last hour's $181k of such buying was three to twenty times the hourly rate of the preceding day [32]. Insider trades also move the price seven to twelve times more per dollar than skilled trades [11], so a maker's inventory is marked against them before they can cancel. Why the losers keep paying: geopolitics and the old Year in Search markets carried no taker fee and no rebate pool, so the resting side earns nothing for the risk [27][28]; the crowd cannot distinguish informed longshot buying from ordinary retail longshot buying until the move; and most of the 1,950 flagged accounts trade one event once, which no leaderboard filter catches [8].
- **Capital required**: for the conduct, whatever the insider has: $32,538 bought 436,760 Yes shares in the Maduro case [29]; the Google case staked ~$2.75M for ~$1.2M [15]. For the honest builder there is no capital line, only a cost line: a reserve against informed flow in the families named below, sized by the "How you'd validate it" ledger.
- **Technical requirements**: none for the conduct. For the builder, an exposure ledger: (1) data in: your own fills with counterparty wallet, market, price and time (Data API `/trades?user=`, which defaults to `takerOnly=true`; pass `takerOnly=false` to see your maker fills, since the two views differ by an order of magnitude on an insider wallet [30]); (2) per market, the terminal price move and its timestamp from `/prices-history`, and the resolution record from `/v2/resolutions` [34]; (3) a rule that tags every fill that landed within N hours before a terminal move of more than 40 points as "pre-news"; (4) a monthly report of pre-news losses by category and by counterparty wallet age. Two to four days on an existing stack; the detectors that act on it in real time are file 27.
- **Opportunity size**: for insiders, large and shrinking: ~$143M of screened anomalous profit over two years is the academic lower bound, so roughly $70M a year across the platform [6]; 1,950 heuristic-flagged accounts at a mean of $15k is ~$29M [8][11]; the 152 military Orcas took $8M over about a year [13]; the individual cases run from $107,539 (Perez) through $404,000 (Van Dyke) to ~$1.2M (Spagnuolo) [1][2][3]. The ACDC data end on 2026-05-05 and the Mitts-Ofir window ends in February 2026, before the April and May charges, the May House Oversight investigation and the referral of "dozens" of military accounts to the DOJ [14][21], so the current rate is unknown and probably lower. For the honest builder the relevant size is the cost, which concentrates in geopolitics (fee-free, the two largest cases), mentions (Perez), corporate and tech (Google, OpenAI) and awards or culture ladders (Year in Search); own tape: 10.6% of the Maduro market's both-sides volume (25% of one-sided taker notional) was lost by pre-news sellers [31].
- **Pros**: none for a lawful actor. The three things worth taking from the record: the tape is complete, so the cost is measurable per family rather than guessed; the platform's own enforcement now removes some of the flow (referrals, freezes, the April and May cases) [18][19]; and the same pattern that identifies insiders after the fact is a legitimate toxicity feature for quoting, which file 27 covers.
- **Cons / risks**: for the insider: criminal exposure in the SDNY (wire fraud plus CEA counts; Van Dyke and Spagnuolo both face parallel criminal cases) [1][2]; CFTC settlements at roughly 1.6-2x profit plus a three-year ban (Perez $107,539.02 disgorgement plus $65,000; Santos $17,569.98 plus $17,500) [3][4]; in Israel, charges of transferring secret information, bribery, obstruction and, for the civilian, aggravated espionage, with the friend changing his username and cancelling wagers after press reports, a second major arrested in August 2026 and a crew member quoted that "the entire squadron is on Polymarket" [22][23][26]; employment termination (OpenAI) [24]; the ledger never expires, so a 2025 trade can be charged in 2026 [15]; and non-US residence is no shield (Spagnuolo lives in Switzerland) [2]. For the honest builder: adverse selection concentrated in exactly the markets with no fee and no rebate; a maker who widens on every fresh wallet also loses the ordinary flow; and anyone who markets "find insiders" tooling gets audited by the platform (Polycool and Kreo, April 2026) [16].
- **Expected results**: for the conduct, the documented outcomes are the return series: $404,000+ profit and an indictment [1]; ~$1.2M and a criminal complaint [2]; $107,539 kept for two months, then $172,539 paid back and a three-year ban [3]; $162,663 on one strike plus later bets, then an espionage indictment [22]; a firing [24]. For following or fading this flow, no public data, theoretical only (file 27). For the honest builder the expected result is a cost: derived from the two tapes above, a maker who supplied 1% of the resting depth taken in the pre-news window would have lost ~$11,600 in the Maduro market and ~$11,400 in the Iran market, before any fee income, because those markets paid none [27][31][32].
- **Competitive landscape**: the insider competes with nobody until the news, then with the copycat bots and whales that ACDC documents following Orca trades with $200,000 and $100,000 orders within hours [13], and with the platform's models: Polymarket says trading and wallet activity is scored by custom machine-learning models, Chainalysis, Palantir and investigators with FBI and Coinbase backgrounds, escalating when two independent sources point at one wallet [18][19]. Outside analysts (ACDC, Unusual Whales, Bubblemaps, the Nechepurenko papers, the Mitts-Ofir screen) publish the fingerprints within weeks [6][8][13][24]. Retail alert products (Presage, Kreo, PolyTrack, Polysights) sell the same signal and are the subject of the April 2026 builder audit [16][E6].
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: prohibited. International ToS §4.2 carries three insider prongs (breach of duty, tippee, position of influence) and Polymarket US Rule 7.2(g)-(i) repeats them verbatim [E9]; the integrity page states them in plain language with military, halftime-show and mentions examples [18]; the rules were re-published on 2026-03-23 [20]. US law: CEA §6(c)(1) and Regulation 180.1(a)(1) and (3) (the CFTC's February 2026 advisory names them, citing two Kalshi cases: a candidate fined $2,246.36 with a five-year suspension and a YouTube editor fined $20,397.58 with a two-year suspension; Kalshi counted five enforcement cases and its first lifetime ban by August 2026) [5][35]; the Van Dyke release calls it the first CFTC insider case in event contracts and the first use of the "Eddie Murphy Rule" for government information [1]; Santos was charged under the manipulation prong for posting about an outcome he controlled [4]. Mitts and Ofir argue the law still has gaps (Rule 180.1 is narrower than 10b-5, wire fraud needs commercial value to the source) and that H.R. 7004 covers only federal officials [6]; the House Oversight Committee opened an investigation of both platforms on 2026-05-22 citing "more than 80" suspiciously timed Polymarket bets [21]. Knowingly trading alongside flow you believe is stolen information is a legal grey zone on a CFTC-supervised contract and counsel-worthy [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: the only lawful test is of your own exposure. Data: your fills for the last 90 days with `takerOnly=false`, `/prices-history` at one-minute fidelity for every market you touched, `/v2/resolutions` for terminal prices [34]. Method: for each resolved market, mark the first minute in which the price moved more than 40 points toward the final outcome; tag your fills in the preceding 24 and 96 hours that were on the losing side; sum the loss at resolution and divide by your gross fee and rebate income in the same family. Sample: at least 200 resolved markets per family. Pass or fail: if pre-news losses exceed 25% of gross income in geopolitics, mentions, tech or culture, that family needs the quote-pull trigger of file 27 or narrower size; if they are under 5%, the concern is priced. Cost: two days and no money. A second, free check on any candidate wallet is the one used here: `/v2/user-pnl` returned $409,882.03 for the Maduro wallet against the indictment's $409,881 [8][29].

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: execution-sensitive. The conduct itself is slow (Van Dyke bought over three days, Spagnuolo over two months, Magamyman 57 minutes before the news) [29][30][32]; what is fast is the price once the news lands (Iran: 0.435 to 0.85 in two minutes) [32], so for the honest builder the only latency that matters is the one between an anomalous fill and a cancelled quote, which is file 27's problem.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposure ledger (this file) | Data API fills with `takerOnly=false`, `/prices-history`, `/v2/resolutions` [34] | you misattribute pre-news losses to variance and keep quoting the same families | nightly batch; no latency |
| Recognising informed flow | wallet age, single-market concentration, size at longshot prices, funding graph [8][13][15] | you are the resting ask that gets lifted at 0.07-0.22 [29][32] | file 27: sub-second on the fill stream |
| Quote pull or widen | cancel latency on the CLOB after a flagged fill | Maduro: four hours from the last insider fill to the move [29][31]; Iran: 57 minutes [32]; both are enough if you act on the fill, not the price | 1-5 s |
| Hedge | none available: the information is not in any other market until it is in all of them | cross-venue books move together (files 07, 08) | do not plan one |
| Settlement and capital recycling | resolution batch; geopolitics p99 settlement 14 days [E3] | your losing NO inventory is locked while the winner redeems | irrelevant to the loss |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Nothing here is decided in milliseconds; the insider trades land minutes to days early and the CLOB's 150 ms crypto taker delay does not apply to these families [E1]. (2) The observable warning is a burst in longshot taking: Iran's last pre-news hour carried $181k of Yes buys of at least $1,000 against $8-54k in each of the preceding 23 hours; Maduro's last hour carried $99.9k against $4-19k [31][32]. A maker who reacts to the burst within a minute keeps most of the inventory; one who reacts to the price keeps none. (3) Rate limits are not binding at this cadence: Gamma allows 4,000 requests per 10 s (300 on `/markets`) [E1]; the Data API has no published limit but answered 429 after roughly 80 rapid calls in this session, so a ledger should pace itself at one or two calls a second [31]. (4) The ledger is a nightly job.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted; the ledger is a batch job on a small VPS. The real-time detector is file 27's and does not need co-location either.

### Results by investment size
Assumptions: this file has no return series, so the rows show (a) the documented outcome for the people who did it and (b) the adverse-selection cost an honest maker in the affected families should budget, derived from the two tapes above: pre-news sellers lost 10.6% of the Maduro market's Gamma volume (25% of one-sided taker notional) and at least 1.3% of the Iran market's $89.65M Gamma volume (fills of $1,000 or more only) [28][31][32]; a maker who is 1% of the resting depth in such a market absorbs 1% of that loss. Both tapes are the two largest publicly charged cases, so these loss rates are a ceiling selected on the outcome, not an average over geopolitics markets; the monthly rows assume at most one such event in your books per month.
