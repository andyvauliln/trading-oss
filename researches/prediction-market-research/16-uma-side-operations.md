# 16 - UMA-side operations: whitelisted proposing, dispute bounties, staking and DVM voting
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: A (own on-chain census of the managed oracle over seven days, two independent passes, plus the DVM contract and the Gamma / Data APIs, for size, concentration and parameters; B for whitelist history and voter concentration; C for per-proposer practice, which is affiliate / self-reported) · Last verified: 2026-09-28_

### UMA-side operations
- **One-line summary**: Earn from the oracle layer instead of the order book: post the answer to a Polymarket question as a whitelisted proposer for a $0.6-$5 reward per undisputed answer; dispute wrong or premature answers for half the proposer's bond; or stake UMA and vote on the disputes that reach the Data Verification Mechanism (DVM) for a ~21% emission yield paid in UMA. The proposer pool is larger than the evidence packs assumed (~$29k/day over the week to 28 Sep, ~$10M/year, derived from on-chain settlements), but two addresses take about half of it, and the durable value for a trading team is the feed: a proposal is the earliest public, machine-readable signal that a market is decided.
- **Category**: Incentive farming [oracle-layer operations; in practice an information feed for files 13, 14 and 15]
- **Maturity**: Established as a mechanism and as an income for about ten addresses; Emerging at best for a newcomer. Over the seven days to 16:45 UTC on 28 Sep 2026 (own on-chain census, two passes [19]): ~205,000 proposals (~29,000/day) from 94 addresses; ~206,000 undisputed settlements paid ~$202,500 of rewards (~$29k/day, ~$10.5M/year, derived), $19-25k on weekdays and $42-48k on Saturday and Sunday when sports settle; the top address took ~35% (~$10k/day), the second ~17%, ranks 3-5 earned $1.4k-$1.6k/day, ranks 6-10 $0.5k-$0.9k/day, ranks 11-20 $150-$400/day and the median of ~95 earners ~$20-30/day [19]. The critic's arithmetic ($0.6-$5 against $500-$750 posted) is right per proposal; volume turns it into a business for ten addresses, and the question for a newcomer is share, not size. The two leaders look like venue-run resolution bots (no positions or portfolio value in the Data API, first on sports, absorbing a third of the week's disputes between them), but ownership is unverified [19]. The live whitelist holds 339 addresses, up from 177 in November 2025 [19][5]. Disputing is an Established mechanism with negligible income (~96 disputes in the week, 0.05% of proposals; all-time upper bound ~$0.6M, derived from [14]). Staking is Established, ~21% a year in UMA (derived from on-chain emission and stake) and dominated by UMA price risk [21][22].
- **Requires AI?**: optional. Sports and price questions are deterministic feed matching against the rules. An LLM helps on the long tail: reading rule text and "additional context" for non-sports questions, classifying candidate disputes as too-early / source-conflict / wording, and drafting voter evidence. Web-enabled LLMs reproduce the DVM's verdict 89.6% of the time once a dispute exists but cannot predict which markets get disputed [E3].
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Every market not settled by Chainlink, Pyth or the US exchange is a `YES_OR_NO_QUERY` question on UMA's ManagedOptimisticOracleV2 (MOOV2, Polygon `0x2C0367a9DB231dDeBd88a94b4f6461a6e47C58B1`) [7]; the sports-score oracle [25] made no requests in either sampled window. The adapter creates the request at market initialisation: ~245,000 `RequestPrice` events in the week (~35,000/day), all in USDC.e (`0x2791…4174`, not pUSD as the docs say) with a $250 UMA final fee, a bond of $250 (97%) or $500 (3%; ten requests at $2,500), and rewards of $0.8 (83%), $0.6 (8%), $1.5 (5%), $5 (2%) or $3.5 (1%), the $3.5-$5 tier being the neg-risk politics questions [19][1]. Gamma shows the pair as `umaBond` / `umaReward` [23].
  2. Proposing. A whitelisted address calls `proposePrice` with 0, 0.5 or 1 and posts bond plus final fee ($500 or $750 in USDC.e); other senders revert with "Proposer not whitelisted" [7]. Liveness is not always two hours: about half of all requests carry a custom liveness, mostly 900 s (57% of those), 1,800 s (18%) or 7,200 s (15%), with 600 s and 3,600 s for the rest; the other half use the 2-hour default [19][1]. Undisputed, the proposer is paid bond + fee + reward at settlement; payouts cluster at $500.8, $500.6, $501.5, $755 and $753.5 [19]. Gas is ~$0.005-$0.008 per proposal [22].
  3. Whitelist entry. Risk Labs' default list requires ≥ 5 Polymarket or Predict.fun proposals at > 95% accuracy over the trailing 6 months, snapshotted on the 2nd of each month; newcomers qualify on requests that are not whitelisted; front-running another proposer's mempool transaction gets you removed [6][17]. Polymarket decides per market whether the whitelist applies; UMA enforces it [5].
  4. Disputing stays permissionless [4][5]. Match the bond inside the liveness window; the first dispute resets the question for a second proposal, the second sends it to the DVM (24 h commit + 24 h reveal, rounds every other day, 4-6 days total). The winner gets their bond back plus half the loser's; "too early" and 50/50 verdicts pay the disputer [3][1]. In the week: ~96 disputes (0.05% of proposals), 30 of them against the second-largest proposer, and 71 disputed requests settled at $625 or $1,000 [19].
  5. Staking and voting. Stake UMA in VotingV2 (Ethereum `0x0043…34ac`): emission 0.13 UMA/s, 19,475,387 UMA staked, 7-day cooldown, GAT 5M UMA, SPAT 65%, up to 1,000 requests per round, 39 pending `YES_OR_NO_QUERY` requests in round 10,362 at query time [21]. Each missed or wrong vote costs 0.1% of stake, redistributed to correct voters; gas rebates need ≥ 1,000 UMA [10][8].
  6. Feed the rest of the stack. `ProposePrice` and `DisputePrice` logs, Gamma's `umaResolutionStatus` (18 of the top 1,000 markets were "proposed" at query time) and the Data API `/v2/resolutions` (status, `extended_review`, `was_disputed`, `new_version_q`, prices, payouts) give a market's state 10 minutes to 2 hours before tokens pay out [23][24]: file 13's window.
- **What creates the edge**: A subsidy and a race. Polymarket funds the reward on every request, so it goes to whoever posts the first correct answer after the event: on sports (900 s liveness, $0.8) a latency race against score feeds, on neg-risk politics questions ($3.5-$5, $750 posted) a rules-reading race with a longer window; binary questions carry ~74% of the week's rewards at ~$1.00 per settlement, neg-risk questions ~26% at ~$2.64 [19]. Nobody is "on the other side": the payer is the venue, which can cut or reassign the reward at will, and the loser is the proposer who is second or wrong. Disputing pays only if the DVM agrees. Staking is protocol inflation paid for reliable participation; the DVM is a Schelling game in which nine wallets hold nearly half of the voting power among 6,400 accounts and "often vote together" [18], so the correct vote is the majority's, not the truth's [E3].
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k for proposing, and capital is not the binding constraint: each open proposal locks $500 or $750 in USDC.e for 10 minutes to 2 hours, so 500 proposals a day at a 30-minute average lock need ~$5k-$8k rolling, at a 2-hour lock ~$21k-$31k (derived). A $1k proposer can hold two $500 sports proposals at once and no $750 neg-risk one. One lost dispute costs the whole posted amount. Disputing locks $500-$750 for 4-6 days per dispute. Staking: any size; 1,000 UMA (~$400) unlocks gas rebates; 7-day cooldown [10][21].
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: Polygon logs from MOOV2 (public RPCs cap, prune or throttle `eth_getLogs` on this contract; a paid RPC is the reliable route [19]); Gamma for rules, `umaBond`, `umaReward`, `resolvedBy`; the Data API `/v2/resolutions` (≤ 20 ids per call) [24]; official score feeds; an Ethereum RPC for `getPendingRequests` [21]. (2) Decision logic: a rules engine per question family, including cancellations that resolve 50/50 (~1% of proposals [19]), a too-early classifier, and per-family accuracy tracking against the 95% bar. (3) Execution: a Polygon signer with a USDC.e float (pUSD must be off-ramped first), nonce management for bursts, no mempool sniping [6]. (4) Risk: cap concurrent bonds per family; never propose a question you hold; halt on `new_version_q` rule changes [24]. (5) Test: replay a month of `ProposePrice` events against your own answers and timestamps. Build: 1-2 weeks on an existing resolution-sniping stack, 4-6 weeks from scratch, plus one to two months of qualifying proposals before the snapshot; a voting bot (commit / reveal automation, Discord evidence scraping, majority-following rule) is 1-2 weeks.
- **Opportunity size**: Proposer rewards ~$29k/day (~$10.5M/year) averaged over the week to 28 Sep, $19-25k on weekdays and $42-48k on the two weekend days; the two leading addresses take ~53%, leaving ~$13-14k/day for ~90 other addresses, most of it to ranks 3-10 (derived from [19]). Gamma's configured rewards on markets ending in the next 24 h summed to only ~$11k across ~7,000 UMA-resolved markets (~3,100 more end without a bond: crypto up/down settles on Chainlink), so the settlement flow, not Gamma's end-date filter, is the right measure [23][19]. History: 184,148 requests, 159,447 proposals, 1,604 disputes to block 79,721,080 [14]; ~1% disputes then, 0.05% now; UMA reported disputes -68% after whitelisting [5] while marquee-market disputes rose in 2026 (> 1,150 by mid-May) [E3][18]. Staking: 4.1M UMA/year of emissions (~$1.66M at $0.404) over 19.5M staked, 21.05%/year (derived) [21][22]; a June 2026 governance proposal would cut emission to 0.095 UMA/s and the APY to ~15%, outcome not stated and the on-chain rate unchanged [12][21]. Direction: proposing grows with market counts (~35k new questions a day) and thins per proposer as the whitelist grows (37 → 177 → 339) [5][19]; staking yield is being cut by design [11][12].
- **Pros**: Income paid by the venue rather than by a counterparty; rewards and bonds are on-chain ground truth; no order-book adverse selection; the proposer bot reuses a resolution-sniping team's feeds and rules engine; the whitelist is a moat once inside; the `ProposePrice` and `DisputePrice` streams are free, exact and 10 min-2 h ahead of settlement; staking income is uncorrelated with market outcomes.
- **Cons / risks**: Race economics: two addresses win ~53% and the top five ~69% of rewards; mempool timing is a removal offence [6][19]. Break-even accuracy is brutal: $0.8 against $500 posted breaks even at 99.84%, $5 against $750 at 99.34% (derived); the affiliate's 99.6% is for $2 on $500 [17]; two proposers once lost $32,250 combined on premature proposals [16][15]. Whitelist bootstrapping is thin: the legacy OOv2 carries ~90 requests a day from one unidentified requester, in native USDC with rewards of $0.000005 or nothing, on which two addresses made 29 proposals in the 24 hours to 16:50 UTC, and 4 correct out of 5 fails the bar [20][17]. Disputes lock capital 4-6 days and are decided in the DVM's eyes; being right on the facts lost the March 2025 Ukraine-minerals vote [E3]. Staking: UMA's $37.5M market cap sits against single disputed markets of $60M-$345M [22][E3]; the token price dominates the yield; slashing is 0.1% per vote, so an unattended round with 39 pending requests could cost several percent of stake (docs say "per vote", read here as per request [10]); Polymarket has said it is building its own "truth" layer [E3]. Regime risk: the Data API's `extended_review` state ("a managed proposal sits past its normal expiry" [24]) means Polymarket can hold a proposal beyond liveness, and it controls which markets are whitelisted [5].
- **Expected results**: Hard data, seven days [19]: leaders ~$10k and ~$5k/day; ranks 3-5 $1.4k-$1.6k/day; ranks 6-10 $0.5k-$0.9k/day; ranks 11-20 $150-$400/day on 500-1,750 settlements a week; the 40th earner ~$35/day; median of ~95 earners ~$20-30/day; $0.6-$0.8 per binary answer, $3.5-$5 per neg-risk answer; ~96 bonds ($48k-$72k) put at risk in disputes against ~$202k of rewards, the DVM deciding who keeps what. Affiliate practitioner: ~50 proposals, zero disputes, "a civic contribution to markets I already trade, not a way to make money" [17]. Disputer income: no public data; guess $0-$100/month for a bot filing only clear too-early cases, with a -$500 to -$750 tail per wrong call. Staking: ~21%/year in UMA at today's emission and stake (derived [21]); UMA's own FAQ quoted 28-32% on 18-20M staked [pre-2025, possibly stale] [9]; ~$175/month gross on $10k before slashing, gas and price moves, ~$125 if the 0.095 UMA/s cut passes [12]. Guess for a newcomer: $0 for the first one to two months (qualifying proposals, then the monthly snapshot); then $10-$60/day, the rank-30-to-60 tier, at 15-75 wins a day; $100+/day only on reaching the rank-20 tier, which today means ~1,000 first-correct settlements a week; one $500-$750 loss every few thousand proposals.
- **Competitive landscape**: 339 whitelisted addresses, 94 active proposers and ~95 reward earners in the week; reward shares top-1 ~35%, top-2 ~53%, top-5 ~69%, top-10 ~80%, top-20 ~90% [19]. A third of the week's disputes hit the second-largest bot, the price of proposing fastest [19]; whitelisted proposers already made 96% of proposals at 99.7% accuracy before the whitelist existed [4]. Disputers: about a dozen addresses a week, two of them also large proposers [19]. Voters: ~6,400 accounts, nine wallets with nearly half the power, ≥ 60% of active voters linked to Polymarket accounts, a top voter staking 2.07M UMA [18][E3][13]. Competitive means sub-second feeds, per-family accuracy above 99.8%, a rolling bond float, and for voting an evidence pipeline that never misses a reveal.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for proposing or disputing truthfully from an address that holds no position (E9 traffic light: Allowed); grey for voting while holding a position (no ToS clause; the fraud and "position of influence" prongs are the hooks; ≥ 60% of active voters trade on Polymarket and ~20% of disputes had a conflicted voter) [E9][E3]. Prohibited and fully visible on-chain: disputing a correct proposal to delay settlement or spook holders (manipulative trading, ToS §4.2), proposing on a question you hold, coordinated whale voting; those cases, governance capture and proposals timed against clarifications are file 31's subject. Polymarket's docs still say "Anyone can propose a resolution" with a "$750 pUSD" bond [1][2]; on-chain the proposer set is whitelisted, the bond is $250 or $500 plus a $250 final fee, and the currency is USDC.e [7][19]. The ToS disclaims UMA outcomes and caps liability at $100 [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: Proposer: (1) one month of read-only replay: index `RequestPrice`, `ProposePrice` and `Settle` on MOOV2 via a paid RPC; for every request compute the answer your rules engine would have posted and when, from your own feeds; metric: first-and-correct share by family and would-be accuracy; pass: ≥ 99.8% accuracy on ≥ 2,000 questions and a first-correct share worth ≥ $100/day at the observed reward mix; ~30 engineering hours, $0-$50 of RPC. (2) Qualification pilot: 5-10 proposals on non-whitelisted requests at $500-$750 each, only with a primary source in hand; fail if any is disputed. Disputer: shadow-score every proposal for 60 days and count the disputes you would have filed and their DVM outcome; proceed only if the too-early subset wins ≥ 90% (no capital). Staker: read `emissionRate` and `cumulativeStake` daily and run 1,000-5,000 UMA through five rounds with an automated majority vote; pass if realised yield matches the derived 21% and no round is missed; the cost is UMA price risk.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: latency-sensitive. The first correct proposal in a Polygon block (~1.5 s blocks) takes the reward, so seconds decide on sports; mempool front-running is banned, which removes the sub-block race [6]. Disputing needs minutes inside liveness; voting is speed-insensitive (48-hour rounds).

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Event detection | official feeds (shared with files 13 and 18) | the leaders post within the first blocks; you win what they skip | 1-3 s source-to-tx on sports; minutes on politics |
| Rules verification | per-family rules engine incl. 50/50 cancellations | a wrong fast proposal loses $500-$750 and the 95% bar | ≥ 99.8% per family before enabling it |
| Proposal transaction | gas ~$0.005-$0.008 [22], nonces, RPC latency | a stuck nonce forfeits a burst of rewards | dedicated RPC, gas bumps, 2-3 signers |
| Bond recycling | liveness 600-7,200 s; anyone can settle | idle bonds cap concurrency | settle your own requests on expiry |
| Dispute filing | checking proposals against source inside liveness | miss the window and the wrong answer settles | alert on every `ProposePrice` in covered families |
| Commit / reveal voting | 24 h + 24 h windows; 39 pending requests [21] | a missed round costs 0.1% per request | cron with a majority fallback |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) The two fastest addresses took ~53% of the week's rewards; ranks 6-20 still earned $150-$900/day on what they skipped [19]. (2) Liveness of 10-30 minutes on ~40% of requests turns sports bonds over 4-12 times a day, so $5k of float supports hundreds of proposals; 2-hour politics questions tie capital longer for $3.5-$5 [19]. (3) Speed does not help against disputes: a third of them hit the second-fastest high-volume proposer [19]. (4) Polygon stalls (2026-09-19 [E1]) delay proposals and settlements alike.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere near a Polygon RPC and your score feeds; eu-west-1 buys nothing. Keep proposer and voter keys away from wallets holding positions in the questions covered.
