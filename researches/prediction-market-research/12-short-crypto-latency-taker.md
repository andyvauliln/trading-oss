# 12 - Short-duration crypto up/down: spot-lead / oracle-aware latency taking
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: B (one open millisecond-level dataset paper and one pre-registered study, both null; official fee, delay and settlement docs; live API checks) for the mechanics and the null results; C for every profit claim, which is self-reported or second-hand · Last verified: 2026-09-28_

### Short-duration crypto up/down: spot-lead / oracle-aware latency taking
- **One-line summary**: Stream Binance (or the Chainlink feed that actually settles the market), detect a move the 5-minute / 15-minute / hourly "Up or Down" book has not yet priced, and lift the stale ask before the maker cancels. The most-copied Polymarket bot idea of late 2025; in 2026 it pays the peak of the fee curve, waits out a 150 ms non-cancellable delay, settles on a 60-second TWAP, and every public measurement of it is negative for anyone not co-located.
- **Category**: Directional trading [latency taking: fee-paying, adverse-selection-bearing; not arbitrage, nothing is locked]
- **Maturity**: Established historically, decayed: no evidence of current profitability for a non-HFT taker. The lag is real (Polymarket quotes respond to large Binance moves after a median 347 ms [1]), but both careful public studies fail to monetise it: OpenMarket's 43-feature walk-forward model does not beat the probability implied by Polymarket's own mid (AUC 0.8377 vs 0.8405) and "simulated positive-EV trading nets -0.116 normalized payoff units per trade" at 1% fee and 0.5% slippage [1]; a pre-registered study on 9,225 windows concludes "No edge for taker after realistic costs. The market is efficient" [2]. A retail test of 5,826 entries found the momentum side resolved 74.8% of the time while the ask charged 75.3%: "Zero fillable lag" [3]. The poster-child wallet ($313 to $414k in a month, Dec 2025-Jan 2026 [10][11]) has been "Inactive since March" [14] and was reconstructed as a maker, not a sniper: 94% symmetric YES+NO buys, median position under $6 [13]. The January fee, "redistributed daily ... to liquidity providers" [12], was read by the trade press as aiming "to neutralise latency-based arbitrage strategies"; it is "highest when odds are closest to 50%" [11].
- **Requires AI?**: no. The signal is a spot move against a reference price; fair value is a digital option with a TWAP adjustment. The machine-learning variant is what OpenMarket tried, and it underperformed the mid [1].
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Markets. Every 5 minutes (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, BNB, DOGE, HYPE, ZEC), 15 minutes, hour and 4 hours a binary asks whether the Chainlink 60-second TWAP at the end of the window is at or above the TWAP at the start; the resolution source is the `btc-usd-twap-60s-streams` stream, "not ... other sources or spot markets" [8]. Fee schedule `rate 0.07, takerOnly, rebateRate 0.2`; tick 0.01 (0.001 near the extremes); minimum order 5 shares; no liquidity-reward config; windows are created ~24 hours ahead [8].
  2. Signal. Subscribe to Binance BTCUSDT trades. Track the move since the window's price-to-beat; OpenMarket's "large move" is ">= 5 bps within 1 s" [1]. Compute P(Up) = N(d), d = (spot - reference) / (sigma x sqrt(time left)); inside the final 60 s replace spot with the partly-fixed TWAP estimate (file 82).
  3. Compare with the CLOB ask, never the Gamma mid: a 36.7% paper win rate on this exact strategy was "a fantasy created by mismatched price feeds" [17].
  4. Take. Send a FAK/FOK buy of the favoured token. It is held `pending` for 150 ms (since 2026-09-04 14:00 UTC; 50 ms from 17 Aug, 250 ms from 5 Jun), "cannot be canceled", balance reserved, re-validated at expiry [4][5]. The delay is a per-market flag, not a category rule: `GET /clob-markets/{condition_id}` returned `itode: true` on live 5-minute, 15-minute and hourly up/down windows and no such key on the 4-hour and daily up/down markets at 16:25 UTC on 28 Sep (own check), so the 4-hour and daily books are undelayed [8]. Resting makers can cancel during those 150 ms. If the ask survives you pay `0.07 x p x (1-p)` per share: 1.75c at 0.50, 1.47c at 0.70, 0.63c at 0.90 [6].
  5. Hold to settlement; selling back costs the spread and a second fee. Break-even true probability when buying at p is p + 0.07p(1-p): 0.5175 at 0.50, 0.7147 at 0.70 (derived from [6]).
  6. Oracle-aware variant: price off the Chainlink TWAP, not Binance. ETH carries a ~0.12% structural Binance-to-Chainlink offset that turned one "edge" into a signal that always fired Up [3]; a self-reported bot claims 0.3-0.8% divergences for 10-30 s and a win rate of 52% to 57-58% after switching feeds, no PnL [19]. PolyBolt streams the TWAP with CLOB credentials since 15 Sep [4][28].
  7. Late-window variant (documented loser): buying 0.80-0.99 in the last 60 s lost 37.81%; "a market trading at $0.95 YES is priced correctly" [17]. Decided windows are file 14's subject.
- **What creates the edge**: Speed against makers who have not yet repriced. Of 15,148 large Binance moves in 54 days, 4,272 produced a directionally consistent best-bid change within 2 s, at a median 347 ms, against a 16 ms median venue lead-lag [1]. What is briefly mispriced is large: realised 1-second BTC volatility on 28 Sep was 0.57-0.65 bp across two 1,000-second samples (95th percentile 1-second move 1.2-1.4 bp, maximum 7.4 bp) [9]; at 0.65 bp a 5-minute window's one-sigma is ~11 bp, so a 5 bp move at the open shifts fair P(Up) from 0.50 to ~0.67, and with 60 s left to ~0.84 (derived). So the edge exists for ~350 ms and dwarfs the fee; the question is who captures it. Three things decide it against a solo taker. The delay: in 150 ms BTC's expected absolute move is ~0.2 bp, so the delay does not change fair value, it gives the maker 150 ms to cancel [4][5]. The fee: takers paid 1.8% of notional in a one-day BTC 5-minute sample and finished -$66.7K while makers finished +$45.3K, 91.4% of persistent makers' profit arriving at settlement from holding the side they were sold [20]; 20% of every taker fee is rebated to the makers of that market [6]. Competition: bots are 55-62% of fast-market volume and 6,189 bot addresses made 56M trades at $6-7 each by March [15]; the first taker to reach the stale ask takes all of it. On the other side of a filled stale ask: a maker slower than you, or a retail limit order (median trade ~$5-10 on live windows [29]); the makers keep paying only while they are slower than 150 ms plus your path, and the fastest are co-located in eu-west-2 [E1].
- **Capital required**: <$1k to $10k, and capital is not the constraint. Positions last 5-15 minutes and settle automatically. What caps size is the stale quantity at the touch: on a live BTC 5-minute book at 15:47 UTC the top five ask levels held 200-1,300 shares each (0.76 x 498, 0.77 x 1,328, 0.78 x 454), about $1-3k per level [8]. A $10k bot deploys $1-3k into the few events a day where the ask is stale, if it arrives first.
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: Binance WebSocket `aggTrade` / `bookTicker` (the `data-api.binance.vision` mirror when `api.binance.com` returns HTTP 451 [9]); PolyBolt for the Chainlink TWAP [28]; CLOB market WebSocket; Gamma for the next window's token IDs [8]. (2) Decision: TWAP-aware digital fair value, short-horizon volatility, a stale-ask detector with a fill-probability model conditioned on the 150 ms delay, a fee-adjusted edge threshold. (3) Execution: pre-signed EIP-712 FAK orders, one HTTPS round trip to eu-west-2, `tradeIDs` polling [4]; match timestamps are second-granular and the official SDKs add pre-trade network checks, so build your own timing harness [E1]. (4) Risk: per-window cap, no re-entry after a rejected pending order, daily stop, kill switch on Binance-vs-Chainlink divergence and on changelog events. (5) Test: replay against the OpenMarket archive (1.64B rows, 14 Mar-1 Jul 2026 [27]) with the CLOB ask as the fill price. Build: 2-3 weeks for the taker on an existing CLOB client; 4-6 weeks for the measurement harness, the only part worth building first.
- **Opportunity size**: In the 24 hours to 15:50 UTC on 28 Sep 2026: BTC 5-minute $10.57M (288 windows), ETH 5-minute $0.60M, BTC 15-minute $3.31M, ETH 15-minute $0.36M, 4-hour up/down across all assets ~$0.1M [8]. In late March weekly fast-market volume was $532M (~$76M/day, 77% BTC) and 5-minute markets did $2.3B in 7-8 weeks [15]: fast-market volume is down ~80% from spring (derived). Crypto taker fees were $23.7M in 83 days (~$286k/day) [15]; at today's ~$15M/day and a 1-1.8% taker rate the taker class pays roughly $150-270k/day (guess), the pool this strategy must beat before earning anything. Direction: shrinking; every 2026 change (fees, delay, TWAP, rebates) moved rent from takers to makers.
- **Pros**: Mechanical Chainlink settlement, no UMA or dispute risk; minutes of capital lock-up; the measurement stack is exactly what file 03's maker needs, so the research is reusable; the fee is near zero at the extremes (0.63c at 0.90, 0.33c at 0.95) [6]; a free public dataset exists to kill the idea in a day [27].
- **Cons / risks**: Execution: the fee is 1.75c where the signal fires, larger than a one-tick stale quote [6]; the pending order locks balance and cannot be cancelled while the maker can [5]; live diverges from simulation (shadow +0.8% to +11.9% vs live -27% over 4,000+ trades; take-profit slippage mean -8.8c; stops fired below trigger 86.4% of the time; ghost positions from the positions API lag) [16]. Platform: the delay changed four times in 2026 (500 ms bump removed ~Feb; 250 ms 5 Jun; 50 ms 17 Aug; 150 ms 4 Sep) [4][E1], the TWAP window twice in one week [4], and the Q4 2026 Rust CLOB rewrite (10-20x better p99) speeds up the makers you race [E1]. Edge decay: complete for a Dublin taker unless a new delay cut arrives; the 50 ms window lasted 18 days [4].
- **Expected results**: Hard data, all negative or null: -0.116 normalised payoff units per attempted trade at 1% fee and 0.5% slippage (the real fee at mid is 1.75%) [1]; "No edge for taker after realistic costs" on 9,225 windows [2]; 4,569 favoured-side taker decisions over 4,604 resolved 5-minute windows lost in every ask band, -1.6 to -6.5 pp against break-even [3]; -27% live for a momentum bot on 5- and 15-minute BTC/ETH [16]; -37.81% for last-60-second favourite buying [17]. Self-reported positives, none verifiable: 81% win rate and +$3.48 per $10 on a 29-market single-day sample from a poster who sells bot consulting [18]; 57-58% win rate after switching to Chainlink, no PnL [19]; a VPS vendor's anecdote of $1,000 to $1,869 then a 50% loss in two days [25]. The one wallet-level profit ever attributed to this strategy (0x8dxd, $4-5k clips [10]) was a paired-buy maker book [13] that stopped in March [14]: the profit was on the other side of this trade. For a solo taker today: no public data of profitability. Guess: -0.5c to -1.5c per share of expectancy for a Dublin bot that fires on every large move; about zero for one that fires only on the top decile of moves with a sub-100 ms path; positive only co-located and at a high taker-rebate tier, and then capped by stale size.
- **Competitive landscape**: The most contested niche on the venue. Bots are 55-62% of fast-market volume [15]; 14 of the 20 most profitable wallets were bots by March and only 7-8% of wallets are profitable [26][13]; an HN practitioner building his own book indexer called these markets "way too competitive and HFT for me" [22]. Makers reprice in a median 347 ms with a co-located fast tail [1][E1]; a how-to written the day of the 50 ms cut says "pure latency arb between Binance and Polymarket is dead - the fee alone usually exceeds the edge" and demands execution "ideally <30ms" [23]; after the 150 ms change practitioners say "the first look is less valuable" [24]. Open-source latency bots are plentiful (one repo found for this check had already been removed, HTTP 404). Taker-rebate tiers make large takers cheaper: 3% at $2k of 30-day weighted volume to 50% at $10M+, crypto weight 2.3, so at p = 0.50 Gold (18%) needs ~$174k of notional a month, Platinum (32%) ~$870k, Obsidian (50%) ~$8.7M, cutting the mid fee to 1.44c, 1.19c and 0.88c and break-even to 0.514-0.509 (derived from [7]; file 28).
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for taking on public price feeds as a non-restricted, non-US person (E9 traffic light: "Trading on own research, models, speed" Allowed) [E9]. Prohibited: trading Binance spot to move the settlement TWAP ("manipulative trading" in the international ToS; Rule 7.2(o) on Polymarket US) [E9]; a hedge inside the 60-second window looks identical on-chain, and a study of ~821 accounts earning ~$8.2M from settlement-window manipulation was reported alongside the TWAP change [21]. Grey: the UK is close-only, Ireland's API is unrestricted [E1]; `api.binance.com` returns 451 from restricted regions [9]; the data licence bars "proprietary trading firms" from Polymarket data without a written agreement, which bites once you incorporate [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: Do not build the bot first. (1) Replay, one week: load the OpenMarket archive [27]; detect Binance moves >= 5 bp within 1 s; for each, take the CLOB ask (not the mid) 50, 150 and 350 ms later in the size actually resting; subtract `0.07 x p x (1-p)` and one tick of slippage; mark to settlement. Metric: mean net markout per share and capturable size per event. Pass: > +0.5c per share at t > 2 over >= 200 events at the 150 ms mark with >= 500 shares capturable; the archive predates the 150 ms delay and 60 s TWAP, so a pass is an upper bound. (2) Measure your own path for a week from eu-west-1: Binance receive timestamp to CLOB acknowledgement, with 5-share orders at prices that will not fill; a median above ~200 ms ends the test. (3) Only then a $500, two-week pilot at <= 100 shares per event, logging pending rejections, fills, and 60-second and settlement markouts. Cost: ~40 engineering hours, ~$65 of VPS, a few dollars of fees.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: latency-critical. The opportunity is the 347 ms median between a large Binance move and the first best-bid response [1]; a taker's path plus a fixed 150 ms non-cancellable delay [4] has to fit inside the slower makers' reaction, and the fastest makers sit in eu-west-2 [E1].

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signal ingest | Binance WebSocket from Dublin; PolyBolt TWAP [28] | you detect the move after the fast makers re-centred | 20-60 ms feed-to-model (vendor claims of 0.8-2 ms Dublin-to-CLOB are unverified [E1]) |
| Fair value | TWAP-aware digital, cached vol | not the bottleneck | in-process |
| Order submit | pre-signed EIP-712, HTTPS to eu-west-2; async commit pipeline since 17 Jul [4] | vendor figures of 80-180 ms tick-to-order predate the pipeline change [E1] | 30-80 ms (guess) |
| Pending delay | fixed 150 ms, non-cancellable, re-validated at expiry [4][5] | a maker cancel inside it turns your fill into a rejection | not compressible |
| Fill and settlement | MATCHED to MINED to CONFIRMED; Chainlink TWAP at window end [E1][8] | a RETRYING/FAILED trade leaves you flat when you thought you were long | poll `tradeIDs` |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) A 200 ms path arrives at 350 ms, when the median maker has already repriced [1]; only sub-100 ms paths see the stale ask more often than not (derived). (2) The delay regime is the PnL switch: at 250 ms a Dublin bot rarely saw a live stale quote, at 50 ms (17 Aug-4 Sep) it had ~100 ms of slack, at 150 ms it has none against makers that cancel inside ~250 ms (derived from [4]). (3) Rate limits do not bind a taker; uptime does: 99.13% trading uptime, cancel-only modes, pending orders rejected at re-validation [E1][5]. (4) The Q4 2026 Rust CLOB (10-20x better p99) makes every maker faster [E1].
- **Where to run it**: eu-west-1 or not at all; KYC/KYB co-location in eu-west-2 is the only sanctioned way to beat co-located makers and excludes Builder Program participants [E1]. Do not pay for it before the replay passes at Dublin latency, which no public study has managed.

### Results by investment size
Assumptions: BTC 5- and 15-minute markets; selective taking on the top decile of moves (10-30 events a day); clips capped by stale size at the touch ($1-3k per event [8]); fee 1.75c at mid [6]; expectancy -0.5c to +0.3c per share (the guess above). Every figure is a guess anchored on the null results.

| Capital | Workable? | How it is deployed | Gross / month | Running costs / month | Net / month | Net / capital / month | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1k | technically (5-share minimum); expectancy negative | $50-200 clips, 10-30 events a day | -$60 to +$60 | $65-100 (Dublin VPS) | -$160 to -$5 | -16% to -1% | fee exceeds the median stale quote; fixed cost 7-10% of capital |
| $5k | no better | $500-2,000 clips | -$250 to +$300 | $70-150 | -$400 to +$230 | -8% to +5% | stale size at the touch idles most of the capital |
| $10k | no better | $1,000-3,000 clips; bigger instance, co-location application | -$400 to +$500 | $100-300 | -$700 to +$400 | -7% to +4% | the 150 ms delay and faster takers; capital does not buy speed |
| Scale reference | the only documented "win" | $4-5k clips, zero-fee era (Dec 2025-Jan 2026) [10][11] | $414k in a month claimed [10] | n/a | reconstructed as a maker book, 94% paired buys [13]; inactive since March [14] | n/a | not reproducible under 2026 fees and delays |

- **Minimum sensible capital**: ~$2k so that a $65-100 VPS does not dominate; but the strategy is latency-limited, not capital-limited, and no capital level fixes a negative expectancy.
- **Capacity ceiling**: the stale quantity at the touch, $1-3k per event and 10-30 events a day, ~$30-90k of daily throughput for the single fastest taker (derived from [8]); a solo bot saturates at $5-10k deployed.
