# 24 - Favourite-longshot harvesting: systematic longshot selling / NO buying, conditioned on category
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: B (A for the bias itself: two papers on 588M Polymarket and 72M Kalshi trades plus own resolved-market replay and live books; D for anyone's strategy PnL: the only public Polymarket attempt is a self-described meme bot with no wallet) · Last verified: 2026-09-28_

### Favourite-longshot harvesting: systematic longshot selling / NO buying, conditioned on category
- **One-line summary**: Be the structural seller of 2-15c YES (equivalently the buyer of 85-98c NO) across many unrelated non-sports events, expressed as resting NO bids in the events that attract heavy longshot spending, sized per event for the rare correlated hit. The bias is real and large in dollar terms (sub-10c purchases lose 19.3c per dollar [1]); the harvest is small, slow and concentrated in exactly the markets where disputes and insiders live.
- **Category**: Directional trading [carry / short event volatility; maker expression; overlaps with 14's bond carry at the 95c+ end]
- **Maturity**: Emerging (the bias is Established on hard data; the harvest is unproven). The bias is documented on hard data: on Polymarket, purchases below 10c lose 19.35% of the dollars paid (95% CI -46.99% to +8.30%, clustered by event) and 6.30% per contract equal-weighted (CI -8.38 to -4.22), while purchases at or above 90c earn +0.83% pooled, Nov 2022 to Mar 2026, fees and rewards excluded [1]; on Kalshi, takers earn -1.12% and makers +1.12% per trade and a 1c YES has a -41% expected value against +23% for a 1c NO [2][3]. What is not documented is anybody harvesting it: no Polymarket wallet with attributable longshot-selling PnL is public, and the one open-source attempt, the "Nothing Ever Happens" always-buy-NO bot (986 stars, last commit 2026-04-13), calls itself "mostly a meme" and published no wallet [8][9][10][11]. Own replay of 6,703 resolved 2026 markets (below) finds the seller's edge real but small and uneven: buying NO at the mid 30 days before the scheduled end on YES priced 2-15c returned +2.6% per position (event-clustered 95% CI +1.3% to +3.8%), +2.8% per event and -1.8% weighted by volume; politics, the largest category, +1.0%; finance and economics negative [21]. The maker-side version is what the winning cohort already does implicitly (winners are limit-order providers whose orders "resolve favorably" [E4]); the taker-side version is priced in.
- **Requires AI?**: no. The screen is a base-rate table by category, price and horizon, an event-clustering rule and a fee-spread gate. Optional: an LLM rules-reader to reject markets whose YES can be triggered by wording rather than by the event (file 64's subject), and a news classifier for the post-hype variant (file 17's feeds). An LLM forecaster (file 21) as the fair-value input is a different strategy.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Universe: every non-sports market with YES between 0.02 and 0.15 and an end date inside 180 days: on 2026-09-28, 7,050 markets in 2,652 events (politics 3,220, finance 893, tech 627, crypto 572, culture 534, weather 501, fee-free geopolitics 392), median liquidity $1,509, median spread 3.7c, 353 trading at least $1k a day [19]. Sub-2c rungs (8,438 markets, $324M resting at 0.999) are file 14's zone [19].
  2. Category and structure filter: exclude sports (longshots earn +2.4% equal-weight, +18.1% pooled, so the bias is absent [1]) and finance-price ladders (the 95-contract Kalshi CPI study finds no bias [5]; Polymarket finance longshots are -2.6% equal-weight with a CI spanning zero, and -72% pooled because a few option-like ladders soak up the dollars [1]). Prefer many-candidate fields: longshot buyers lose 34.2% equal-weight in events with 26+ child markets, 15-16% with 6-25, 2.3% with one, and gain 39.5% in 2-5-child events [1, Table 15]. Own replay adds one tradable cut and one diagnostic: neg-risk rungs returned -0.4% to the NO buyer at 7 days (+0.2% at 30) against +3.7% (+4.5%) for standalone markets; a split by final volume (+0.1% above $100k against +2.5% below, at 7 days) is diagnostic only, because final volume is unknown at entry and accrues when something happens (hit rates rise from 0% in the $500-2k bucket to 5.5% above $100k) [21]; the band is 62% neg-risk [19].
  3. Fair value: a base-rate table by category x price bucket x days-to-end from Gamma closed markets and CLOB `/prices-history` (the replay below is the seed), shrunk toward the price, less the horizon's settlement discount (3.06-6.89% annualised [E3]).
  4. Express as a maker: rest NO bids (equivalently YES asks) one tick inside the model price, post-only, in the markets where YES-taker flow exists. Never cross a 3-4c spread to buy NO: on a 0.92 outlay the half-spread alone is ~2% (derived), more than the +0.83% per dollar the whole favourite side earns [1].
  5. Size per event, not per market: all rungs of one field are one draw. Cap 2-5% of capital per event and 15-25% per category; at a NO price of 0.92 one hit costs 11.5 wins (derived: q/(1-q)).
  6. Hold to resolution, redeem (gasless relayer for Deposit Wallets [E1]), recycle. Watch `umaResolutionStatuses` and Data API `/v2/resolutions` on every held position (files 13, 15): a disputed longshot is a position, not a bond.
  7. Two overlays: post-hype NO entries after a story spikes a YES ("small, but consistent returns", no numbers [9]); and file 23's count ladders, where NO on 10-20% buckets won 90 of 97 [13].
- **What creates the edge**: A behavioural premium plus a risk premium, paid by three groups. (1) Lottery demand: the top decile of past longshot buyers supplies 26.6% of next-month sub-10c purchases and 30.4% of them are still there six months later, yet their return (-21.96%) is no worse than everyone else's (-20.33%): the loss is spread across buyers who do not repeatedly seek it [1]. Live, taker YES buying in the 80 most active longshot markets runs at ~$96k a day with a median ticket of $7.6 [20]. (2) Subsidised churn: the Taker Rebate Program weights volume by (1 - entry price), so a $50 YES buy at 5c earns 19x the weighted volume of a $50 NO buy at 95c (derived from [16]); tier climbers are paid to buy what this strategy sells. (3) A risk premium: a Wang-transform loading of 0.176 fitted on 2,460 resolved Polymarket contracts reproduces the bias mechanically, is concentrated in illiquid long-horizon contracts, vanishes in high-volume markets and decays toward zero as resolution nears [6]. The counterparty you must not fill is the fourth group: 48% of YES-taker dollars arrive in tickets of $500 or more [20], and $143M of anomalous informed profit sits mostly in fee-free geopolitics [E4]. The bias persists because makers collect it as a spread, not a forecast: on Kalshi maker returns are "indistinguishable across position directions" and three-quarters of taker losses are execution within price-direction cells [3][4]; on Polymarket it survives at every experience level (the most experienced quintile loses most, -28.7% pooled), under both order types (posted offers -3.5%, accepted offers -31.2%) and in markets with no fee [1].
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k to run, $10k+ to matter. Capital is the whole product: a NO at 0.92 locks 92c to earn 8c for weeks (1,835 band markets end within a week, 2,863 in 31-90 days, 2,009 in 91-180) [19], at a yield of zero: only 243 of the 17,363 tail markets carry the 3.25% holding reward (complete YES+NO sets earn it, verified on-chain in file 14), and none of the 2-15c band earns liquidity rewards on a one-sided quote, because outside a 0.10-0.90 mid the reward formula requires two-sided depth [17]. Minimum order 5 shares; ticks tighten to 0.001 above 0.96 [E1].
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: Gamma `/markets/keyset` with `after_cursor` (the offset endpoint refuses offsets above ~2,000 and caps `limit` at 100 [19]), `feeType` and `feeSchedule` per market, `negRisk` and event ids for clustering, CLOB `/book` and `/prices-history`, Data API `/trades?market=` (taker-only by default) to measure YES-taker flow per market. (2) Base-rate model: category x price x horizon table with event-clustered errors; monthly refit. (3) Rules and structure screens: reject markets with `umaResolutionStatuses` already non-empty, markets whose wording lets YES trigger without the event, and small fields (step 2). (4) Execution: post-only NO bids (YES asks) via `POST /orders` batches, cancel-all on a volatility or news trigger (a resting YES ask is the first thing an informed taker lifts), heartbeat dead-man switch [E1]. (5) Risk: per-event and per-category caps, a hit-rate monitor against the model, a dispute monitor on `/v2/resolutions`. (6) Test mode: replay on resolved markets (the validation below) and a 90-day paper book. Build: 2-3 weeks solo on an existing LP or bond-carry stack (files 02, 14 share the order manager and the redemption loop); 5-7 weeks from scratch.
- **Opportunity size**: Small and concentrated. The counterparty flow is the cap: ~$96k a day of taker YES buying across the 80 most active longshot markets (10th-90th percentile per market $62 to $4,359 a day; crypto price ladders $51k, fee-free geopolitics $19k, politics $13k, culture $5.5k) [20]. If that flow loses the paper's 6-19c per dollar, the transferable pool is ~$6-18k a day, ~$2-7M a year, split among the makers already resting there (median 32 effective makers per market [E4]). By comparison, taker NO buying in the same markets runs at ~$631k a day [20]: the crowd is on the NO side as takers, paying the spread to the YES bidders. Direction: shrinking per dollar (the risk loading "vanishes in high-volume" markets and decays toward resolution [6]; the paper's Q1 2026 purchase cohort shows negative longshot returns among resolved purchases, still provisional [1, Appendix B]) but growing in breadth as 300k markets a month are listed [E1].
- **Pros**: The best-documented behavioural pattern on the venue (two independent 500M+ trade samples, consistent sign in every non-sports category equal-weighted [1][2]); no latency race; maker expression pays zero fee and near-zero spread; the same book, screens and redemption loop serve files 13, 14 and 23; category-conditioning is a free, evidenced improvement (finance out, sports out, big fields in [1][5]).
- **Cons / risks**: Edge: the pooled loss (-19.35%) has a confidence interval spanning zero and flips to +4.09% when each parent event counts once, because spending concentrates in the low-return events [1]; a seller who diversifies across events is diversifying into the +4% ones, and the replay's per-dollar return is negative [21]. Capital: locked at 0% for weeks; 11.5 wins per loss at 0.92 (derived); "1 loss sets you back 10-20 wins" [9]. Tail: hits are correlated (war, a court ruling, a primary upset) and the dispute rate is liquidity-weighted: 7.1% of the 2,100 largest closed markets, 16.1% of those overturned (file 15), 4.8% of geopolitics markets [E3]; a YES that hits on wording costs the full 92c. Insiders: fee-free geopolitics is where the Iran and Maduro cases sit [E4]; large YES tickets are half the taker dollars [20]. Execution: median band spread 3.7c and liquidity $1.5k [19]; the taker version loses the half-spread (derived). Platform: taker fee `rate x p(1-p)` is 0.2-0.7% of a NO outlay at p = 0.05-0.10 (derived from [14]); fee-free geopolitics pays no rebate [15]; 99.13% uptime with cancel-only modes [E1]; incentive parameters move without notice (sports rebate cut, tier thresholds) [16][18].
- **Expected results**: no public strategy PnL. Public inputs: sub-10c YES buyers lose 6.3c per contract and 19.3c per dollar, posted-offer buyers 3.5c, 90c+ buyers earn 0.83c per dollar [1]; Kalshi makers +1.12% per trade [2]; the always-NO bot's break-even is a 73% NO rate against a 65c ceiling, no wallet, one $100 wet run -$5 in a month [8][9][10][11]; Polymarket's accuracy page now shows 77.8% of resolved markets ending NO (73.3% in April), a base rate that is already the average NO price, not an edge [12]. Own replay (leakage-controlled: entry at the last 12-hour price 1, 7 or 30 days before the scheduled end date, never the resolution time; Gamma closed markets ending 1 March to 20 September 2026, median end 31 March; non-sports, non-up/down, volume >= $500; n = 6,703 markets) [21]: for YES priced 2-15c, buying NO at the mid 30 days out returned +2.58% per position (n = 891 in 474 events; 95% CI +1.32% to +3.75%, bootstrap clustered by event), +2.84% per event and -1.82% weighted by volume, with 4.0% of the longshots hitting against 6.4% implied; at 7 days out +1.98% (n = 838; CI +0.61% to +3.46%); at 1 day out +0.66% (n = 1,036; CI -1.25% to +2.45%, spanning zero). By category at 30 days: politics +0.98% (n = 284), culture +5.2%, tech +5.3%, fee-free geopolitics +5.8% (n = 74), finance -5.0% and economics -7.7% (n = 36 and 26), with the caveat that 424 of the 891 band markets (48%) predate the `feeType` field and are categorised by title keywords; the neg-risk and volume cuts are in step 2. The 15-35c band paid more per position (+13.3% at 30 days, CI +8.6% to +18.9%, but -2.3% per dollar); the naive always-NO rule (YES 35-65c) shows the paper's split in one line, +24.9% per market and -15.4% per dollar; sub-2c rungs return +0.4-0.5% per position (file 14's zone). Caveats: mids not asks, no spread, fee or dispute delay, 2 of the 37 hits disputed, sample March-April heavy; and the replay fills the passive buyer in every market, whereas a resting NO bid is filled preferentially where YES takers are informed, so +2.6% per position is an upper bound on a maker's realised figure. Guess for a solo maker book (own Monte Carlo, re-run in verification: net edge +1.5-2.5% per position at a 4.4-5.5% hit rate on NO at 0.93, 22-40 positions in pairs that share event risk, 80% of capital deployed, 45-day lock so capital turns 0.67x a month): $1k earns about +$0 to +$8 a month net of costs (10th-90th percentile of gross -$64 to +$40), $5k +$25 to +$55 (-$133 to +$195), $10k +$50 to +$115 (-$172 to +$401); one hit costs a month or more of income at every size. Nobody should expect the paper's 19c per dollar: that is what the buyers lose, across all sellers, before spread.
- **Competitive landscape**: Crowded on the wrong side, thin on the right one. The taker NO side is crowded: the always-NO bot has 113 forks [8], a "bond-style" framework repo reports a profitable window "later retired" with no numbers [22], The Oracle's readers pile into the same 10-20% count buckets [13], and NO takers outspend YES takers 6.6 to 1 in the active longshot books [20]. The maker side is the incumbents' business: median 32 effective makers per market, ~3 at the extreme tail, 13-18% quoted spreads in the lowest-price decile [E4][7], plus the desks that now quote the liquid centre [E4]. Being competitive means owning the event-clustered base-rate table and the patience, not speed: the books that pay are the ones nobody with payroll wants ($200-$5k of asks within 2c on most band markets; $9-57k on the House-control, Iran-invasion and Clarity Act markets) [20].
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none. Trading public prices on your own model from one identity is Allowed on both venues [E9]. Grey only if you incorporate: the data licence bars "proprietary trading firms" from Polymarket data "including on-chain" without a written agreement [E9]. The ethical edge case is the counterparty, not you: if a fresh wallet lifts your YES ask in size before a war headline, you were the insider's exit liquidity (file 30), which is a sizing lesson, not a compliance one.
- **How you'd validate it**: (1) Replay, two days, free data: pull every resolved non-sports market since 2026-01-01 (Gamma `closed=true` keyset), take the last CLOB `/prices-history` point 7 and 30 days before the scheduled end, bucket YES 2-5c, 5-10c, 10-15c by category and field size, compute the NO return at the historical ask (not mid) minus `rate x p(1-p)`, cluster the bootstrap by event and report the equal-event and dollar-weighted numbers separately; pass = positive net return in both weightings for at least one category-horizon cell with >= 350 markets (the power needed to see a 2 pp edge [23]) and a hit rate below the implied one; fail = the edge survives only equal-weighted or only in sub-2c rungs. (2) Flow check, one hour: Data API `/trades` on the 100 most active band markets; pass = YES-taker flow above $50k a day, median ticket under $20, under a third of the dollars in $500+ tickets. (3) Paper book, 90 days, then $2k live maker-only in the passing cells; metric = realised NO return versus model and versus the equal-event baseline, fills per day, and time-to-redeem; kill if a single event exceeds 5% of capital or if two hits arrive with a dispute flag. Cost: ~30 hours and one VPS.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: execution-sensitive. Latency does not decide PnL (the edge is a base-rate carry over weeks), but fill quality does: a NO bought through a 3.7c spread has lost the year's edge, and a YES ask left resting through a news shock is the first quote an informed taker lifts.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Universe and base-rate refresh | Gamma keyset, monthly refit [19] | nothing measurable inside a day | daily scan, monthly refit |
| Entry | resting NO bids, post-only [E1]; YES-taker flow of $62-$4,359 a day per market [20] | taking pays 0.2-0.7% fee plus ~2% half-spread on a 0.92 outlay (derived) | never take; queue at the model price |
| Cancel on shock | news, whale YES buys, `price_change` on the market WS [E1] | your ask is lifted at the pre-news price (the -31% "accepted offer" bucket, seen from the other side [1]) | cancel-all within seconds of a volatility or size trigger |
| Hedge leg | none; a partial hedge exists only in file 11's option ladders | n/a | none |
| Settlement and recycling | resolution, dispute window, redeem [E1][E3] | capital idles days longer; a dispute converts a bond into a bet | redeem the day of resolution; monitor `/v2/resolutions` |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Between shocks the book is stale for hours and a quote posted at 09:00 or 15:00 fills the same flow. (2) At a shock, the cost of being slow is the whole position, not a tick: the paper's accepted-offer longshot buyers lose 31% because they buy after the move; your resting ask is what they hit before it. (3) Taker delays are irrelevant here except in crypto price ladders, where the 150 ms crypto taker delay applies to the lifter, not to you [18]; there is no delay flag on politics or geopolitics markets [19]. (4) Uptime: a 4 h halt with resting quotes wiped (CLOB V2 cut-over, incidents [E1]) is a nuisance, not a loss, for a hold-to-resolution book.
- **Where to run it**: any small VPS outside restricted jurisdictions; eu-west-1 buys nothing for a strategy whose fills arrive over days. Not the UK or US on the international venue [E9].

### Results by investment size
Assumptions: maker fills only (no fee, no spread paid); net edge per position +1.5-2.5% (replay equal-weight +2.0-2.6% [21] less a maker adverse-selection haircut, i.e. a 4.4-5.5% hit rate at an average NO price of 0.93); 80% of capital deployed in 22-40 positions held ~45 days, so capital turns 0.67x a month; hits clustered by event; running costs from the services table; capacity per market limited by YES-taker flow ($62-$4,359 a day, median $481 [20]). Figures are the Monte Carlo in Expected results (derived): gross = deployed capital x edge x 0.67.

| Capital | Workable? | How it is deployed | Gross / month | Running costs / month | Net / month | Net / capital / month | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1k | marginal | 22 NO positions of ~$36 across events | ~$8-13 (derived; 10th-90th -$64 to +$40) | $5-10 | +$0 to +$8 (10th pct -$70) | 0% to +0.8% | one hit is 4-5% of capital; fixed costs are the edge |
| $5k | yes | 35 positions of ~$115, 3-4 categories, max 4% per event | ~$40-67 (derived; -$133 to +$195) | $10-20 | +$25 to +$55 (10th pct -$150) | +0.5% to +1.1% | number of independent events with real YES-taker flow |
| $10k | yes | 40 positions of ~$200, plus file 14's bonds in the idle balance | ~$80-133 (derived; -$172 to +$401) | $15-40 | +$50 to +$115 (10th pct -$200) | +0.5% to +1.2% | you become most of the ask-side depth on median band markets ($200-$5k within 2c) |

- **Minimum sensible capital**: ~$3k, so that 30 positions of $100 leave the per-event cap intact and a single hit costs under 4% of capital; below it the VPS and data costs are the edge.
- **Capacity ceiling**: ~$50k-$100k solo (guess): the whole transferable pool is ~$6-18k a day across all makers [20][1], and a book that rests more than ~10% of a market's daily YES-taker flow stops filling.
