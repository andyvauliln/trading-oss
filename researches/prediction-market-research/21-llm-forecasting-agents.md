# 21 - LLM forecasting agents (direct retrieval-and-reason forecasters trading the gap)
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: B (A for accuracy versus the price and for the controlled real-money runs: papers, live leaderboards, fetched benchmark files; C self-reported for vendor books and agent-platform success rates; D for viral PnL claims; no attributable Polymarket PnL) · Last verified: 2026-09-28_

### LLM forecasting agents
- **One-line summary**: Retrieve news for a market, have a frontier LLM (or a small cross-family ensemble) produce a calibrated probability, anchor it to the price and trade the residual with a deterministic sizing rule. It is the baseline files 50-78 must beat, and on liquid markets it still loses to the price.
- **Category**: Directional trading [AI-native; usable as a fair-value input to market-making]
- **Maturity**: Emerging. Accuracy: the best systems are at statistical parity with superforecasters on ForecastBench [2] (rank 1 Google DeepMind entry 69.1 vs superforecaster median 68.8 on the Brier Index, p = 0.62; 21 of 377 entries not significantly below) [1], but entries see the crowd price on market questions [3], the superforecaster baseline is a frozen 2024 snapshot [4], and the price still wins head to head: market Brier 0.1106 vs 0.1258 for the AIA Forecaster on 1,610 liquid questions, the blend beating both [7]; on Prophet Arena today the best entry scores 0.8020 ± 0.0042 (1 - Brier) against the Kalshi market's 0.7995, skill +0.25 ± 0.42, every interval straddling zero [5][6]. Money: controlled real-money deployments lost on both venues (Prediction Arena, $10,000 per model [9]), except a $200, 26-day Kalshi pilot with a proper-scoring sizing rule (+80.33%, trimmed +24.2%) co-authored by Kalshi Research [10]. No Polymarket wallet with audited LLM-driven PnL is public [29].
- **Requires AI?**: yes. The LLM does retrieval planning, source reading, rule interpretation and the probability judgment; everything else must be deterministic: market universe filter, price anchoring, calibration map, sizing (`s = 2(p - q)` under Brier, or quarter-Kelly after an edge filter), position caps, drawdown halt, fee and spread gate, and the evaluation ledger [10][11]. An LLM should never size or override the risk layer.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Universe: open markets from Gamma (keyset pagination, 100 per page [39]), minus sports (files 04, 18, 19) and short crypto (03, 12); keep those with more than 7 days to run and a two-sided book; tag the fee category (politics/finance/tech 0.04, economics/culture/weather 0.05, crypto 0.07, geopolitics 0; taker-only, 15-25% rebated to makers) [39][40].
  2. Retrieval: 4-7 agentic searches per question over dated sources (Claude's optimum; more queries hurt) [8]; discard anything dated after the forecast timestamp; treat Polymarket's own AI "Market Context" as untrusted (in about a third of the annotated markets among 152 audited, AP citations that did not link to real AP articles) [36].
  3. Forecast: one reasoning call per model, probability plus a short rationale; 4-10 members if ensembling [20], from different model families (cross-family diversity cuts error correlation from 0.68 to 0.40; DPO-tuned same-family agents correlate at 0.70 and ten of them are worth ~1.4 independent forecasters) [13]. Fine-tuning on resolved questions helps the model but has not beaten the price (outcome-RL 14B: Brier 0.190 vs market 0.151) [19].
  4. Calibrate and anchor: fit Platt scaling per system on your own resolved log (+0.016 Brier on Metaculus bots; frontier models are overconfident on Kalshi questions, ECE 0.12-0.40, so measure the direction rather than assume it) [45][47]; then blend with the market price (AIA's best weight was 0.33 on the model; market-conditioned prompting beat the price on earnings-call mention markets) [7][35].
  5. Gate: trade only where |p - q| exceeds fee plus half-spread plus your measured calibration error, and only where LLMs beat the price: early after listing and at 0.30-0.70, never near resolution or on strong-consensus markets [8].
  6. Size and execute: `s = 2(p - q)` of a fixed unit, capped per market and per correlated cluster; rest a maker order at fair value where the spread is wide (makers pay no fee and earn the rebate), take only when the gap survives the fee [10][40].
  7. Evaluate: log every forecast with timestamp, price, book snapshot and cost; score Brier against the entry price and PnL at executable prices; stop or refit when the score gap turns negative after 200 resolved questions [14].
- **What creates the edge**: Breadth and cheap attention, not intelligence. The price beats any single model on liquid markets [5][7][12] and moving a prominent market 5 points costs ~$0.7-1.0M [37], so the residual must come from (a) markets nobody has priced yet: at listing and on day one LLMs post positive Brier skill (+0.167 at Open+1 for Claude Opus 4.5; +0.301 on toss-ups) while every model sits below -0.7 at Close-1 [8]; (b) the long tail, where the counterparty is a thin book rather than a professional: on 2026-09-28, 7,460 of 19,760 non-sports markets accepting orders had under $10k of liquidity and more than a week to run, and 877 of those were two-sided toss-ups with a median $494 of liquidity and a 7.5c spread [39]; (c) biases the LLM "loses less" on: a 32-model ensemble on 822 resolved Polymarket questions made +4.0% against -0.5% for the price by fading optimism and longshots, not by being more accurate (secondary source) [34]; (d) numeric and rules questions, where bots beat human pros [20]. Who pays: retail takers in low-attention markets (the top 1% of accounts take 76.5% of profits by resting limit orders that resolve favourably [E4]) and users who defer to a model rather than reason with it [46]. They trade headlines late, when the LLM has no edge either, so the real contest is with makers already quoting off a model.
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k. Capacity, not capital, binds: PolyBench's positive alpha survived $10 lots and died above about $500 per market against the real book [12]; long-tail toss-ups hold $19-$211 within 5c of the touch at $1-2k of Gamma liquidity and $270-$7,300 at $10k [39]. A $5k book spread over 50-100 markets at $50-$100 each is the natural size; lock-up is the market's life (median 36.5 days to resolution for non-sports markets [39]). Running costs bind as well: at $0.40-$1.60 per forecast (derived below) a 300-market weekly re-forecast is $120-$480 a week.
- **Technical requirements**: Reference design (reused by files 50-78): (1) Data in: Gamma keyset feed and `new_market` WebSocket events; CLOB books and `/prices-history`; a search API and fetch layer with a hard date filter; a resolved-market archive with rules text. (2) Forecast module: prompt with rules, deadline and a dated evidence bundle, one call per model; a cheap triage model (Haiku 4.5 or Gemini Flash-Lite class, $0.25-$1 per MTok input [41][43]) ranks thousands of markets so the expensive model sees dozens. (3) Calibration and anchoring: per-system Platt map and market blend, refit weekly from your own ledger only. (4) Trading layer, deterministic: fee and spread gate, `2(p - q)` sizing, per-market and per-cluster caps, daily loss halt, maker-first execution, heartbeat and kill switch [E1]. (5) Evaluation: timestamped forecast ledger, Brier and log score against entry price, fee-adjusted PnL at executable prices, Murphy decomposition to catch market-tracking [14], a leak audit of retrieved documents. (6) Test mode: forward paper trading at official fees (the `agent-next/polymarket-paper-trader` gym, 399 stars [33]) or a cutoff-staggered replay. Cost per forecast: Metaculus winners used ~28 calls and ~$1.40 per question [21]; at 2026 list prices 28 calls of ~8k input and 1k output tokens cost $0.73 on Claude Sonnet 5, $0.36 on Haiku 4.5, $1.46 on Opus 5.5, plus $0.01 per web search (derived from [41][42]). Build: 3-5 weeks for a solo developer with an execution stack; the prompt is a day, the leak-free evaluation harness is most of the work.
- **Opportunity size**: The long tail plus the first day of new markets. Live on 2026-09-28 (first 60,100 order-accepting markets in the keyset listing; the set is larger): 19,760 non-sports markets, 13,894 with zero 24-hour volume, 894 with ≥ $1k/day; 598 non-sports toss-ups with ≥ $10k liquidity and > 7 days hold $18.7M at a median 2c spread; the 877 long-tail toss-ups hold $1.05M [39]. At a 2-4 point average edge on fully turned-over long-tail books the pool is low tens of thousands of dollars a month for everyone combined (derived), and shrinking as prop firms deploy agents [E7]. The liquid pool is where the money is, and there the price wins. Trend: accuracy improving ~0.016 Brier a year [3]; PnL evidence flat to negative; cost per forecast falling (Sonnet 5's $2/$10 introductory price became permanent [41]).
- **Pros**: Cheap breadth (thousands of markets triaged for tens of dollars); measurable with proper scores before any money is risked; the same pipeline supplies fair values to a maker (file 02) and rules readings to files 15 and 64; model upgrades arrive free (scaffolding is worth ~9 months of base-model progress [21]); no oracle, latency or KYC dependence; 861 fee-free geopolitics-type markets remove the taker fee from the hurdle [39].
- **Cons / risks**: Edge: on liquid markets and late in a market's life the price is better [5][7][8][12]. Monoculture: rivals' frontier models reach the same view (identical World Cup pick in 92% of matches [15]), so LLM-visible mispricings are competed away first [E7]. Execution: long-tail spreads of 9-18c [39] exceed any plausible taker edge; fees ate 13% of starting capital in the one positive pilot [10]. Capital: a wrong model is wrong across correlated markets at once; Kelly on miscalibrated p lost 43-100% on forecasts that made +8-22% with proper betting [10]. Evaluation: backtests are contaminated by default (frontier-vs-small gap 35.8% to 8.9% on post-cutoff questions; 1.65% of AIA's search results carried foreknowledge; Hindcast's leak-free replay of 216 Polymarket markets reports retrieval gains over zero-shot baselines for open-weight models, and none beating the price at listing) [16][17][18]. Platform: Polymarket's in-product AI is a poisoned retrieval source [36]. ToS: none for own trading; selling signals or auto-executing for others raises CTA and Letter 26-25 questions; an incorporated team using API data may be a Capital Market Client [E9].
- **Expected results**: Controlled, real money: Prediction Arena, six frontier models, $10,000 each, 2026-01-12 to 03-09: Kalshi -16.0% to -30.8%; Polymarket (from 02-09) 0.00% to -2.68%, mean -1.1%; the arena is now paused [9]. Gu et al., Gemini 3 with proper betting on Kalshi, $200 for 26 days: 1,605 forecasts, 236 fills across 129 pre-filtered markets, $360.67 final, +80.33% ROI, +24.2% trimmed, Sharpe 3.35 (90% CI 0.90-6.12), 50.9% win rate, $26.31 of fees, 15.7% max drawdown [10]. Prophet Arena's live "Return" column (simulated proper betting against the Kalshi ask, no fee, depth or period stated) shows +12% to +67% for entries at Brier parity with the market, where the paper's version of the metric had GPT-5 below break-even [5][6]: an upper bound, not a track record. Vendor, self-reported: FutureSearch's Kalshi real account +5.9% on $100,000 from 2026-06-09 to 08-11 (22 won, 9 lost, 70 open), Kalshi paper +49.8%, Polymarket paper -19.6% [24]; Olas Polystrat agents, which do trade Polymarket's CLOB [27], 4,200+ trades in their first month with 37% then 33% of agents profitable, so two thirds lost [26][28]; a Kalshi mention-market bot netted $5,516 on $342,506 of cost from a structural maker rule after its LLM probabilities "underperformed" [38]. Backtests: PolyBench, 36,165 predictions by seven models over one week, two profitable (+17.6% and +6.2% confidence-weighted return at $10 lots), five losing 9-25% [12]; Raven-Agent on 44 archived Polymarket decisions: flat stake -10.7%, edge-proportional -55.5%, risk module with quarter-Kelly +15.9% (95% CI +3.8% to +30.0%) [11]. Viral: the "$1 into $3.3M Claude bot" is a sports-arbitrage wallet by the same article's description [30]; a practitioner with audited numbers calls such posts "absolute LARPs" [31]; five viral posts checked against primary data in September 2026, none survived [29]; a seven-bot review put an "AI ensemble" bot at -43.3% [32]. No Polymarket wallet with attributable LLM-driven PnL is public. Guess for a solo builder on the long tail with a proper-betting layer: Brier at or slightly above the price on early toss-ups; PnL after fees and spread between -5% and +5% of deployed capital per month, dominated by a few correlated losses; positive expectation is plausible only as a maker.
- **Competitive landscape**: Crowded on the model side, empty on the evidence side. GitHub search returns 191 repositories for "polymarket llm" and 150 for "polymarket ai agent trading"; Polymarket's reference agent is archived (3,804 stars, last push 2024-11-05), its successor `agent-skills` is a static skill set (190 stars, 2026-02-19), CloddsBot (2,898 stars, pushed 2026-09-18) makes no PnL claim [33]; none publishes audited live results. Commercial forecasters: Mantic ($25M seed on 2026-09-18 after its bot finished second, behind the anonymous bot laertes, in a Summer 2026 Metaculus Cup where five bots placed in the top ten of 22 entrants) [22][23], FutureSearch ($0.15-$2 per question) [25], Cassi, Torchcast, Lightning Rod, Preseen (unverified "$35 to $1.94m"); prop firms are deploying agents [E7]. Competitive means a cross-family ensemble, a private data or language advantage, a leak-free ledger of 1,000+ resolved forecasts, and maker execution.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for trading on your own model from one identity (E9 traffic light: "trading on own research, models, speed" is Allowed on both venues). Grey: selling the forecasts or auto-executing for others (CTA definition; CFTC Letter 26-25 relief excludes software that generates express buy or sell signals); an incorporated prop entity using Polymarket data "directly or through an API" needs a written data agreement under the ToS; Polymarket US bars trading for others (Rule 7.2(x)) [E9]. Prohibited: feeding the model non-public information [E9]. The "AI bot" narrative is also the main lure of drainer scams, so never run a third-party agent with wallet access [32].
- **How you'd validate it**: Phase 1, replay, two weeks, ~$150 of API spend: 400 resolved non-sports markets from the last six months, forecast at three cutoffs (listing + 1 day, midpoint, 3 days before close) with retrieval hard-limited to documents dated before each cutoff and a manual leak audit of 50 evidence bundles; baseline = CLOB midpoint at the cutoff; metrics = Brier skill score vs the price and fee-adjusted proper-betting PnL at the book snapshot; fail if BSS ≤ 0 at listing + 1 day on toss-ups. Phase 2, forward, 10-12 weeks, $300-600: pre-register a frozen prompt and rule set; forecast every new non-sports market with a two-sided book within 24 hours of listing plus the 0.30-0.70 long tail; paper-trade `2(p - q)` at executable prices with fees (the open paper-trading gym or your own recorder) [33]; ≥ 350 resolved forecasts are needed to detect a 2-point edge at 80% power [14]; pass = BSS > 0 with a 95% bootstrap CI excluding zero and paper PnL > 0 after fees; stop if the score gap is negative after 200 resolutions. Phase 3: $1-2k live at $20-50 clips, maker-first, for 8 weeks, reconciled against on-chain fills.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: execution-sensitive. The forecast itself is speed-insensitive (edge lives in the first day after listing and decays over days, not milliseconds [8]), but whether you cross a 7-18c long-tail spread or rest inside it, and how you size, decides the sign of the PnL [10][11][39].

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery of new markets | Gamma keyset feed, `new_market` WS events [E1] | you forecast after the makers have quoted; the Open+1 window is where LLM skill is positive [8] | forecast within 1-6 hours of listing |
| Forecast pipeline | 4-7 searches and 5-30 LLM calls per market; batch API is 50% cheaper but asynchronous [41] | minutes matter only on news days; a stale bundle is a leak risk, not a latency risk | 2-10 minutes per market; batch overnight re-forecasts |
| Entry | fee gate, half-spread, book depth; the order-lifecycle page still lists a 250 ms taker delay on finance markets (not re-verified since June) [E1] | taking across a 15c spread on a $500 book costs more than any measured edge | maker orders at fair value; take only when gap > fee + half-spread + calibration error |
| Sizing | proper betting vs Kelly on the same forecasts [10] | Kelly -43% to -100%, edge-proportional -55.5% on identical forecasts [10][11] | `2(p - q)`, per-market and cluster caps |
| Exit / hold | markets resolve at a median 36.5 days out [39]; early exits had negative average PnL in Prediction Arena [9] | churning pays the spread twice | hold to resolution unless the model flips by more than the round-trip cost |
| Re-forecast cadence | cost per forecast $0.4-1.6 [41][42] | too frequent burns budget; too rare misses regime changes | weekly, plus event triggers |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Lifecycle, not latency: four of ten models beat the market at Open+1, all sit below -0.7 BSS at Close-1 [8]; a day late at listing is the whole edge. (2) Spread dominates fee: a politics taker at 0.50 pays 1c per share; the median long-tail toss-up spread is 7.5c, so a taker needs an 8-9 point edge to break even, a maker about 1 point (derived from [39][40]). (3) Size kills alpha before speed: PolyBench's edge vanished above ~$500 per market [12]. (4) Uptime, cancel-only modes and the documented 250 ms finance taker delay change nothing for a strategy that trades hours after listing [E1].
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted; a small VPS is enough, since LLM calls dominate latency. Do not host in the UK or US [E9]. Nothing here justifies eu-west-1 or co-location.

### Results by investment size
Assumptions: maker-first execution in non-sports long-tail and newly listed toss-ups; 50-150 open positions of $20-$100; a captured edge of 1-3 points of notional per resolved position after fees in a good regime and -2 to -4 in a bad one (the spread between the +4.0% ensemble backtest [34], the -1.1% Prediction Arena mean [9] and the +24% trimmed pilot [10]); monthly turnover ~1x (median 36.5 days to resolution [39]); LLM spend $0.5 per forecast at 300-600 forecasts a month; VPS $10-65. Every figure is a guess derived from those inputs.

| Capital | Workable? | How it is deployed | Gross / month | Running costs / month | Net / month | Net / capital / month | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1k | as a forward test only | 20-40 positions of $25-50, cheap-model triage, frontier model on ~150 forecasts | -$40 to +$40 | $85-160 (LLM $75-150, VPS $10) | -$200 to -$50 | -20% to -5% | API spend exceeds any plausible gross; the value is the ledger, not the PnL |
| $5k | yes, marginal | 60-100 positions of $50-80 across politics, culture, economics, tech, fee-free geopolitics | -$200 to +$200 | $160-340 (LLM $150-300, VPS $10-40) | -$500 to +$50 | -10% to +1% | edge must be ≥ 2-3 points net of spread just to pay the API bill |
| $10k | yes | 100-150 positions of $60-100, weekly re-forecasts, maker quotes on wide books, model blend feeding an LP bot | -$400 to +$400 | $250-500 (LLM $200-400, VPS $65) | -$900 to +$150 | -9% to +1.5% | long-tail depth ($19-$211 within 5c at $1-2k of liquidity [39]); correlated losses across a model's blind spot |

- **Minimum sensible capital**: ~$5k, because running costs are fixed at $150-400 a month and the gross edge scales with deployed size; below that the API bill is the strategy's largest position.
- **Capacity ceiling**: ~$15k-$30k solo (guess): the 877 two-sided long-tail toss-ups hold $1.05M of Gamma liquidity in total and alpha decays above ~$500 per market [12][39]; beyond that you are trading liquid markets, where the evidence says the price is better.
