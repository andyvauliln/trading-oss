# 11 - Derivatives-implied relative value (options, futures, merger-arb spreads vs Polymarket)
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: B (two papers with small or old samples, one reproducible reality check, a live comparison); C for practitioner trades · Last verified: 2026-09-24_

### Derivatives-implied relative value
- **One-line summary**: Price Polymarket's "above K on date", "hits K by date", earnings, FOMC and deal-close markets off the listed derivatives that price the same distribution (option surfaces, futures, merger-arbitrage spreads). Sell the Polymarket side that is persistently rich, usually upside longshot YES, and optionally hedge with an option vertical or perps.
- **Category**: Arbitrage [statistical relative value; model-risk-bearing, not locked]
- **Maturity**: Emerging, with the edge mostly explained away. The measured wedge is real in sign. In September 2023, Polymarket BTC threshold YES sat 5.6 pp above Binance-option-implied values (t = 6.46) and 11 pp above Deribit, with a ~4 h half-life, largest at low probabilities and long maturities [1]. A 2026 study finds gold and silver upside overpriced by 8.9 and 5.3 pp against CME options, with half-lives under three days [2]. But the only delta-hedged backtest rests on 16 trades with "marginal statistical precision" [1]. A July 2026 reality check on Polymarket touch markets against Deribit finds that the apparent 5-130x mispricing "evaporates" in a closed-market backtest, explained by the variance risk premium plus the favourite-longshot bias [3]. Live on 24 Sep 2026 the short-tenor wedge on BTC was ~0.5-0.9 pp on the upside tail [4], an order of magnitude below the 2023 figures.
- **Requires AI?**: no. Surface fitting (SVI/SSVI), the Breeden-Litzenberger digital (the negative strike-derivative of the call price), barrier pricing and basis adjustments are quantitative finance. Optional: an LLM to read and normalise each market's resolution source, snapshot time and "hit" vs "close" semantics, which is where most errors come from.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Map each Polymarket market to a derivative-implied probability:
     - terminal "above K at T" = a tight call spread (C(K-a) - C(K+a)) / 2a, which already includes the volatility skew;
     - one-touch "hits K by T" ≈ 2 x the vanilla delta, or the reflection principle [5];
     - FOMC decisions from fed funds futures (CME FedWatch is a model output, not a tradable price) [E5];
     - deal-close markets from the merger-arbitrage spread;
     - earnings "beat" markets from historical beat rates plus the options-implied move [6].
  2. Adjust for basis: the resolution source (Polymarket daily "Bitcoin above" markets settle on the Binance BTC/USDT 1-minute close at 12:00 ET [4]; Deribit settles on its index at 08:00 UTC), the expiry gap, and the "hit" definition.
  3. Compare with the Polymarket bid and ask, not the mid. Trade when the gap exceeds the fee (crypto 0.07, finance and politics 0.04, economics 0.05, times p(1-p)) plus the basis error.
  4. Express mostly by selling rich YES (buying NO at 1 - p), ideally as a maker to earn the 20-25% rebate instead of paying the fee.
  5. Hedge optionally: a Deribit or IBIT vertical for terminal markets, or Polymarket perps (0.040% taker / 0.0125% maker at the base tier [7]) for delta. Unhedged, it is a variance-premium harvest.
  6. Exit on convergence (half-life hours to days) or hold to resolution.
- **What creates the edge**: Two things, only one of them free. The free one: slow transmission between segmented venues. The gap mean-reverts within hours for BTC [1] and days for metals [2]. The priced one: Polymarket buyers pay for lottery-like upside, the favourite-longshot bias, which in crypto costs longshot buyers ~15% [E4]. But risk-neutral option prices already embed a variance risk premium, so part of the "wedge" is fair compensation for selling tails [2][3]. On the other side: retail buyers of cheap upside YES on Polymarket who cannot easily reach options. They keep paying because $0.03 lottery tickets are the product.
- **Capital required**: $1k-$10k unhedged; $10k+ hedged. Buying NO at 0.95-0.99 ties up nearly $1 per share for a ~0.5-1c edge. Deribit options trade in minimum 0.1 BTC (~$8.4k notional at 24 Sep prices) [8], so a vertical hedge needs several thousand dollars of Polymarket exposure to be sized sensibly. Lock-up: days for daily and weekly ladders, months for "by year-end" ladders.
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: Deribit public API (no auth for market data [8]), CME / IBIT option data for commodities and ETFs (paid, unverified), the Binance 1-minute close or PolyBolt licensed prices [9], Polymarket ladders via Gamma and CLOB. (2) Decision logic: surface fitting with arbitrage-free interpolation; call-spread digitals; barrier pricing for touch markets; horizon, index and time-zone basis corrections; a gap-over-cost threshold per strike. (3) Execution: maker quotes skewed to the option-implied value (preferred), or taker NO buys; optional hedge orders on Deribit or perps. (4) Risk: tail limits per strike cluster, a correlated-crash scenario (all "above" rungs lose together), a basis kill switch. (5) Test: daily snapshot job of the full ladder against option digitals, then outcomes. Build: 2-4 weeks for a solo developer with options experience, 6+ weeks without.
- **Opportunity size**: Modest and concentrated in crypto. On 24 Sep 2026 the crypto-price ladders led Polymarket's crypto volume: "what price will Bitcoin hit in September" $0.89M/24 h, "Bitcoin above on Sep 25" $0.50M, "Bitcoin hit before 2027" $0.32M, "Ethereum hit in September" $0.28M [4]. Commodity and equity thresholds are thin: the SPY September hit ladder had 23 markets and ~$140k of total volume on 20 Sep (brief), and daily SPX, WTI, gold and silver up/down markets carried $50-200/day reward configs and $10-40k/day of volume [4][10]. Live wedge for Sunday's "Bitcoin above" ladder against Deribit 27SEP26 call-spread digitals [4][8]: 86k 0.205 vs 0.199; 88k 0.047 vs 0.038; 90k 0.0165 vs 0.009. That is +0.6 to +0.9 pp (1.2-1.9x relative) on the upside tail, before the 8-hour horizon mismatch, which inflates Polymarket's out-of-the-money values slightly. In-the-money rungs were cheaper than the options (84k 0.590 vs 0.624). Direction: small and stable at short tenors; larger but variance-premium-dominated at long tenors.
- **Pros**: An external, liquid, professionally arbitraged reference; the wedge has the same sign in three independent samples [1][2][4]; mean reversion gives exits before resolution; Polymarket perps offer a same-collateral delta hedge [7]; crypto markets resolve automatically, with no UMA dispute risk.
- **Cons / risks**: Short-tail payoff: many small wins, one touch or rally loses 20-100x the premium. Model risk: risk-neutral is not real-world probability, and the variance premium is a legitimate price for tail risk [2][3]. Basis: Binance candle vs Deribit index, 12:00 ET vs 08:00 UTC, "hit" at any time vs "close above". Fees: the crypto taker fee at p = 0.05 costs ~0.33c per share, about half of a 0.6-0.9 pp wedge. Hedge frictions: Deribit's 0.1 BTC minimum and short-dated option spreads of ~30% of price (0.0006/0.0008 BTC bid/ask on the 88k call [8]). Access: Deribit is not open to US persons, and a US person cannot trade Polymarket international [E9].
- **Expected results**: Published: a delta-hedged proxy on 16 trades with net alpha 0.067, t = 2.10, p = 0.053 on 2023 data [1] (the brief's reading of the paper; the abstract says "profitable after transaction costs, though with marginal statistical precision"). The 2026 reality check finds no robust edge once realised touch frequencies replace model prices [3]. Worked practitioner trade: an IBIT March 62/63 call spread against 95 "BTC $110k by 31 March" contracts, with modelled returns of 32-131% if no touch and expiry and reference basis risks spelled out [11] (self-reported, 2025). Guess for a solo maker on BTC and ETH daily and weekly ladders: 0.3-0.8 pp of expected edge on filled notional after fees, a few hundred to a few thousand dollars of fills a day at $10k, with lumpy tail losses. Net $0-400 a month at $10k, and negative months whenever BTC trends.
- **Competitive landscape**: Crypto ladders are quoted by makers who track Binance within hundreds of milliseconds (file 03) and increasingly by option-savvy desks. The finance category showed a maker-taker gap of only 0.17 pp in one microstructure study [12]. Commodity and equity ladders are thin and less contested, but tiny. Open-source reference implementations exist, with no published results [3][13].
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for non-US persons trading Polymarket international and hedging on Deribit or perps (E9: trading on own models is Allowed). Prohibited: trading the underlying to move a settlement reference (Rule 7.2(o) on Polymarket US; manipulative trading in the international ToS) [E9]. Keep hedge activity away from the settlement minute.
- **How you'd validate it**: 1) A daily snapshot job for 30 days: every live crypto and commodity threshold market (bid, ask, depth) against the option-implied digital from call spreads, with the horizon and index basis corrected. 2) Record the gap by moneyness, tenor and category, then realised outcomes and gap convergence. 3) Paper-trade: sell YES (buy NO) when gap > 2x cost; hedge variant vs unhedged. 4) Pass: positive PnL from convergence (not from resolution luck) over 200+ observations with t > 2, and a realised touch frequency below the Polymarket price in the traded buckets. Fail: the edge disappears when the model digital is replaced by the realised frequency, which is the finding of [3]. Cost: ~30 engineering hours; free data (Deribit, Gamma).

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: execution-sensitive. The wedge half-life is hours to days [1][2], so speed matters little for long tenors. For daily crypto ladders the reference moves every second, so stale quotes are picked off by makers tracking Binance (median quote response 347 ms [14]). Fill quality at the bid and ask and correct basis matter more than latency.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surface and digitals | Deribit REST or WebSocket, refit cadence | long tenors: no loss from a 1-minute refit; daily rungs: stale fair values | refit every 10-60 s for dailies, 5-15 min for monthlies |
| Polymarket quoting / taking | CLOB; crypto taker delay 150 ms [9] | taker entries cost 0.07 x p(1-p) and wait 150 ms | maker quotes skewed to the implied value |
| Hedge leg | Deribit 0.1 BTC minimum; wide short-dated spreads [8] | hedge cost exceeds a sub-1 pp edge | hedge only size above ~$5k of payout, longer tenors |
| Settlement minute | Binance 1-minute close at 12:00 ET [4] | none from speed; do not trade the underlying near the fixing [E9] | no hedging activity in the fixing window |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) At 4-hour to 3-day half-lives, arriving minutes late costs little of the convergence [1][2]. (2) On dailies the relevant speed is the maker's, not the arbitrageur's: other makers re-centre in ~350 ms [14]. (3) Execution cost dominates: the fee is ~0.3c per share on a 5c rung against a 0.5-0.9 pp wedge, and the maker rebate (20% of that fee) turns a marginal taker trade into a viable maker trade.
- **Where to run it**: any unrestricted VPS for weekly and monthly ladders; eu-west-1 if you quote dailies.

### Results by investment size
Assumptions: unhedged maker quoting on BTC and ETH daily and weekly "above" and "hit" ladders; captured edge 0.3-0.8 pp of filled notional after fees; fills of 1-5x capital a week; tail losses included as a monthly drawdown. All figures are guesses.

| Capital | Workable? | How it is deployed | Gross / month | Running costs / month | Net / month | Net / capital / month | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1k | yes, unhedged | NO on 2-4 rich out-of-the-money rungs a day, $50-200 per rung | $15-$120 | $10-20 | -$60 to +$80 | -6% to +8% | one BTC trend day hits every short rung at once |
| $5k | yes, unhedged | 5-10 rungs across BTC and ETH, maker quotes | $60-$350 | $10-70 | -$250 to +$250 | -5% to +5% | tail clustering; basis errors |
| $10k | yes; hedge possible for the biggest clusters | adds weekly and monthly ladders; Deribit verticals above ~$5k of payout | $120-$600 | $70-150 | -$400 to +$400 | -4% to +4% | Deribit 0.1 BTC minimum and ~30% relative option spreads on short tenors [8] |

- **Minimum sensible capital**: ~$1k unhedged; ~$15k-$25k if you want to hedge with Deribit verticals.
- **Capacity ceiling**: rung depth. The Sep 25-27 BTC rungs each showed ~$40-50k of Gamma "liquidity" [4]. A solo maker saturates at ~$25k-$50k across crypto ladders (guess). Commodity and equity ladders are much smaller.
