# 55 - Diversity-engineered synthetic crowd: internal LMSR, wagering, meta-prediction and information-partitioned Delphi panels
_Band: speculative · Evidence grade: D (no forecasting or trading record against a market price for any mechanism in this file; A for the own measurements on ForecastBench's published forecasts and on Polymarket's APIs; B for the adjacent literature: preprints on TruthfulQA, maths, medical QA and synthetic tasks) · Last verified: 2026-09-29_

### Synthetic crowd with an internal market
- **One-line summary**: Run a panel of LLM agents that differ by model family and evidence shard, aggregate them through an internal market, wagers or second-order questions, and pass only the gated gap to the Polymarket price to a stacker. Unproven: on 574 resolved Polymarket question-rounds five families' errors correlate 0.70 even within outcome (~1.3 independent forecasters), their average loses to the price by 0.064 Brier, and blending it in adds nothing measurable.
- **Category**: Speculative-novel [aggregation layer for AI forecasters; a feature for file 58's stacker, not a standalone trade]
- **Maturity**: Experimental; no mechanism here has been scored against a market price. The one partitioned-evidence panel tested on Polymarket-derived questions (InfoDelphi, 375 PolyGym questions, one model family) beat single-agent and debate baselines, Brier 0.178 vs 0.216 / 0.202, and reported no price benchmark [5]. Five coordination designs on 100 post-cutoff Polymarket markets scored 0.153-0.181 against the market's 0.152, and no pairwise difference survived Bonferroni [6]. The internal-market evidence comes from TruthfulQA with 7-9B models [1], a synthetic environment with a known Bayes posterior [15] and, for WALLA, a synthetic forecasting set and medical QA [3]; meta-prediction only from human forecast datasets [12] and QA debates [13]. Nobody is found trading Polymarket or Kalshi through an internal agent market [21]; PolyBench's 36,165 per-model Polymarket predictions sit behind a login [22].
- **Requires AI?**: yes. LLM agents supply beliefs, predictions of their peers and of the price, wagers and rationales. Everything else (wealth updates, weights, pooling, the N_eff monitor, the blend with the price, sizing) is deterministic code fitted on the resolved ledger.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Universe and timing as file 21: new non-sports markets with a two-sided book, forecast within 24 hours of listing, mid 0.10-0.90.
  2. Crowd: K = 6-9 agents = 3 model families × 2-3 evidence shards. InfoDelphi routing: rank retrieved documents by BM25 (a keyword relevance score), give the top half to everyone (public core) and deal the rest round-robin into disjoint private shards [5].
  3. Round 1, blind and independent: each agent returns p_own, p_peers (its guess of the other agents' mean), p_mkt_hat (its guess of the Polymarket mid, which it never sees), a wager of internal credits and a short rationale.
  4. Round 2, Delphi variant only: each agent reads the others' rationales, not their numbers, and revises [5].
  5. Aggregate in parallel and log every arm. (a) Internal market: if agents bet Kelly (the stake that maximises long-run log wealth), a clearing market's price is the wealth-weighted average of beliefs and wealth updates exactly as Bayes' rule [10]; use that limit, computed in closed form from the reported beliefs, instead of an LMSR (logarithmic market scoring rule, the automated market maker of [1][15]) traded in LLM rounds, which lost information in both tests [1][15]. (b) WALLA: wager-weighted pool with a leave-one-out payout that makes truthful reporting dominant [3]. (c) Meta-prediction: weight agents by how well they predict their peers (peer prediction) [12] and shift toward the answer whose actual support exceeds its predicted support ("surprisingly popular") [14]. (d) Control: an L2 logistic aggregator on the agents' log-odds, fitted leave-future-out [2].
  6. Knowledgeable-dissent filter: a candidate only when agents predict the price well (|p_mkt_hat − mid| ≤ 3c) yet disagree with it (|p_crowd − mid| ≥ 10c); otherwise defer to the price.
  7. Health: rolling within-outcome error correlation ρ and N_eff = K / (1 + (K − 1)ρ); suspend the crowd when N_eff < 2.
  8. Output: crowd residual, disagreement and dissent flag go to file 58's market-anchored stacker; execution uses file 21's fee-and-spread gate and `2(p − q)` proper-betting sizing, maker-first [28].
- **What creates the edge**: Mechanism: with Kelly bettors a market learns at the optimal rate and has low worst-case log regret to its best participant [10]; market equilibria can implement product- and mixture-of-experts ensembles [11]; a wager exposes per-question confidence that a stated probability hides (the largest bets ~99% correct vs ~74% for the smallest, on 100 maths and logic questions [4]); peer prediction finds experts without track records (14 human forecast datasets [12]) and lets a correct minority win a debate [13], though E8 found no forecasting test of truth-serum aggregation over LLM agents [E8]; partitioned evidence creates the dispersion a human crowd has and an LLM panel lacks [5]; a forecaster that loses to the price alone can still improve it in a blend [17], and hybrid human-plus-bot markets match or beat bot-only ones [18]. The payers would be file 21's thin-book counterparties. The strongest counter-argument has three layers. (a) Monoculture is at least as severe on real market questions as in the lab. On ForecastBench's published zero-shot forecasts (made on the due date from the question alone, no price or news, scored against that day's price), five families on 574 resolved Polymarket question-rounds (472 questions, October 2025 - June 2026) have a pairwise error correlation of 0.91 (range 0.87-0.94 over the ten pairs), N_eff 1.08; within resolved-Yes and within resolved-No questions (shared outcome removed) it is 0.70-0.71, N_eff 1.30; three cross-family pairs (0.60-0.78) are no less correlated than same-family pairs (0.49-0.80) [16]. The lab's cross-model 0.40 came from 7-9B open models on TruthfulQA and correlates binary right / wrong vectors, not probability errors, so the figures are indicative, not like for like [1]. Personas inside one model do nothing: in Nous's released outputs ten wallet-persona agents of one model correlate 0.984 on 42 Polymarket questions (N_eff 1.01; own recomputation) [8]. (b) Smarter aggregation does not rescue it: the five-family mean scored 0.144 against the price's 0.079 (+0.064, 95% CI +0.042 to +0.087); a logistic stacker fitted only on questions resolved before each round was worse than the plain mean (+0.015, +0.005 to +0.026); and a blend of mean and price fitted the same way was no better than the price (+0.002, −0.002 to +0.006) [16]. Douven's 23% stacker gain was on 94 Manifold questions with the LLMs still ~2x worse than the market [2]. (c) Mechanisms can destroy information: in a synthetic environment with a known Bayes posterior an LMSR among LLM agents ended farther from it than every static pool (gap 0.269 vs 0.187 for the median pool), and persistent bankrolls made it slightly worse [15]; deliberation cut accuracy from 83.43% to ~76% on 1,189 Kalshi resolution questions [7]. Caveat: zero-shot forecasts without retrieval bound, not measure, a retrieval-fed crowd on disjoint shards; that is what Phase 1 tests.
- **Capital required**: none until validation; then file 21's sizing, <$1k to $10k: $20-100 per position, capital locked to resolution (median 36.5 days in file 21's census). The binding costs are LLM calls and time, not capital.
- **Technical requirements**: Built on file 21's stack (feeds, dated retrieval, ledger, deterministic trading layer). Agent design sketch: (1) Agents: 3 families at the $2 / $10-12 per MTok tier (Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-6 Sol, Gemini 3.1 Pro; file 21 prices) × 2 shards = 6 agents; admit a further family only if its measured within-outcome correlation with the panel is lower (Grok 4 and a fine-tuned gpt-oss-120b sat furthest from the frontier cluster on the Metaculus AI Benchmark [9]). (2) Information: the dated BM25 public core and private shards of step 2, documents dated before the forecast time only; no agent sees the price; round 2 shares rationales only. (3) Elicitation: one JSON per call (step 3), wager 0-100 credits, rationale ≤ 150 words. (4) Aggregators: step 5's arms plus a confidence-weighted logit pool; the Kelly pool updates wealth on each resolution [10]; WALLA's learned wager head needs a resolved ledger [3]. (5) Health and kill rules: rolling within-outcome ρ and N_eff; wealth concentration (suspend if one agent holds > 50%); cross-agent SD of p_peers (drop the meta-prediction arm below 2 points: no second-order information). (6) Output to file 58: logit(crowd) − logit(mid), disagreement, dissent flag. Cost per question-forecast, estimated: 6 agents × 2 rounds = 12 calls of ~8k input and ~1k output tokens ≈ 110k tokens ≈ $0.31-0.34 at those list prices, plus ~24 searches at $0.01 ≈ $0.24, so ~$0.55; ~$0.10-0.20 on GPT-6 Luna / Haiku 4.5; batch halves the model part (derived from file 21's price table). InfoDelphi's 3-agent, 2-round design on a mini model cost ~$12 for 375 questions [5]. Build: 2-3 weeks on top of file 21's harness; the leak-free replay and the ledger are most of it.
- **Opportunity size**: No pool of its own: at best a fraction of file 21's (long-tail and day-one toss-ups, low tens of thousands of dollars a month for everyone combined). Measured addition of a zero-shot crowd over the price so far: none [16]. The testable flow is large: 21,856 resolved Yes / No markets with ≥ $5k volume were listed and closed in 2026 up to 21 September, in 5,855 events (politics 7,914 the largest group), 1,200-4,000 closing a month, plus 49,073 weather buckets (file 22) [23]; 84% of sampled events sit at 0.10-0.90 one day after listing [24].
- **Pros**: Every arm reuses one set of calls, so the ablations are nearly free; the N_eff metric is a cheap kill switch; disagreement and the dissent flag may help file 58 abstain even if the crowd's mean never beats the price; the closed-form market needs no extra LLM calls [10].
- **Cons / risks**: Edge: (a)-(c) above. Leakage: backtests are contaminated by default (frontier-vs-small gap 35.8% → 8.9% on post-cutoff questions [2]); InfoDelphi cut evidence at the resolution date rather than a forecast date, so its gain may include late news [5]. Wealth dynamics: a lucky agent takes over, and persistent bankrolls hurt in [15]. Sample size: wager heads and peer weights need hundreds of resolutions, and gaps of 0.006-0.011 between configurations 1,500-2,000 more paired questions [6]. Cost: 12 calls per question against 5-30 for file 21's single forecaster. Security: private shards built from web pages can carry prompt injection that debate amplifies. Correlated losses: one shared blind spot hits every position at once, and LLM crowds carry a pool-wide acquiescence bias toward Yes [27].
- **Expected results**: no public data, theoretical only. Adjacent: +12-18% Brier over single-agent and debate baselines on Polymarket-derived questions, no price benchmark [5]; coordination designs 0.001-0.029 behind the price [6]; own measurement: zero-shot five-family crowd 0.064 behind the price, blend with the price 0.002 behind (not significant) [16]. Guess: the market, WALLA and peer-prediction arms land within ±0.003 Brier of the logistic aggregator; the crowd as a feature in file 58 adds 0-0.003 Brier over file 21's single forecaster; standalone PnL −5% to +3% of deployed capital a month before LLM costs, negative after.
- **Competitive landscape**: Research groups and hobby repositories; nobody live with a record. GitHub searches return 84 / 28 / 4 repositories for "polymarket multi-agent" / "polymarket swarm" / "llm prediction market lmsr", none with an audited PnL; PolySwarm (50 personas on Polymarket) reports calibration, not trading results, and PolyGnosis 2.0 finds a "pervasive consensus bias" in every configuration [19][20][21]. Commercial forecasters already ensemble model families (86% of Fall 2025 Metaculus winners aggregate, 4-10 members [E7]), so a cross-family ensemble is table stakes; the untested part is the mechanism layer.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none specific; own-model trading from one identity is allowed [E9]. The international venue's geoblock and close-only lists apply to API trading; an incorporated team using Polymarket data may be a Capital Market Client needing a written agreement [E9]. The OSINT-sensor variant (one agent per public feed) must stay on public data: anything from officials or contractors is MNPI [E9]. Retrieval shards inherit each source's terms.
- **How you'd validate it**: Phase 0 (done, $0): the ForecastBench panel [16], the Polymarket replay pool [23][24] and live depth [25]. Phase 1, replay, 3-4 weeks, ~$1,100-1,700 of LLM spend (~$1.20 per question: two forecast times, the ρ = 1.0 control adds half again, batch prices): 900-1,400 post-cutoff resolved Polymarket binaries at 0.10-0.90, one per event, drawn from the 5,855 events listed and closed in 2026 (about 4,500 once price-threshold ladders and weather are excluded) [23], created after every panel model's training cutoff; the market's own Brier on a sample of such events is 0.179 one day after listing (321 events, 118 Yes; 0.205 on the 271 at 0.10-0.90), 0.110 seven days before the scheduled end (315, 106 Yes) and 0.071 one day before (315, 103 Yes) [24]. Two biases in that sample: it kept markets that stayed open 8 days after listing (a look-ahead; 72 dropped early closers re-pulled score 0.100, so the ex-ante listing figure is nearer 0.17), and `prices-history` is the raw book midpoint (listing + 1 day 0.194 on the lowest-volume third vs 0.169 on the top) [24]. Filter on the scheduled end only; score against recorded books. Forecasts at listing + 1 day and 7 days before the scheduled end, counted only if the market was open then; retrieval hard-limited to documents dated before the forecast time, manual leak audit of 50 bundles. Pre-registered arms: best single agent; equal-weight mean; logit pool; L2 logistic aggregator (leave-future-out); closed-form Kelly-market pool; WALLA; peer prediction + surprisingly popular; Delphi-partitioned panel; ρ = 1.0 control (every agent sees all evidence); independent vote (round 1 only); market mid; file 21's plain forecaster; and each crowd blended with the mid, weight fitted on the first 40% in time order and frozen. Metrics: Brier and log loss, Brier skill vs the mid (question-clustered paired bootstrap), proper-betting PnL at recorded books after fees, N_eff, rank correlation of wager size with correctness. Kill criterion, the pre-registered null: "internal market = plain logistic aggregator" (likewise WALLA and peer prediction); drop any mechanism whose Brier is not below the aggregator's with a 95% interval excluding zero, and drop the crowd if the aggregator-plus-mid blend does not beat the mid. Power, derived from [16] on the paired difference (not the SD of the market's own Brier): the per-question SD of the blend-minus-market Brier difference is 0.036-0.039 on all market questions and 0.049-0.056 in the 0.10-0.90 band, but that fitted blend put only ~5% logit weight on the crowd; at a 20% weight the band SD is 0.068. So a 0.005 gain needs ~415-1,000 questions if the crowd stays a small tilt and ~1,400 at a 20% weight, and 0.01 needs ~100-250 / ~360 (80% power, two-sided 5%); aggregator-vs-aggregator differences have SD ~0.10 (stacker vs mean), so separating two aggregators by 0.005 takes ~3,100 questions and 0.015 ~350: Phase 1 can only kill mechanisms or confirm large effects. Phase 2, forward shadow, 8-12 weeks, $400-1,000: frozen pipeline on every new non-sports market within 24 hours of listing; paper-trade the knowledgeable-dissent subset (pass: ≥ 150 flagged questions with positive alpha against the mid and the complement at or below zero). Live money only if the blend beats the mid by ≥ 0.005 with the interval above zero AND the crowd beats file 21's single forecaster.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive (hours to days). A 12-call crowd takes 5-15 minutes per market, and the only window where LLM forecasters show skill is the first day after listing (file 21).

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Gamma keyset feed, `new_market` events | a day late, the price is already better than any LLM (file 21) | forecast within 1-24 hours of listing |
| Crowd run | 12 calls, ~24 searches | minutes are irrelevant; stale shards are a leak risk, not a speed risk | one run at listing + 1 day, batch re-runs |
| Aggregation and health | closed-form pools, stacker, N_eff | milliseconds; no LLM trading rounds | recompute on each resolution |
| Entry | live mid-band spreads median 2.5c below $10k liquidity, 1.0c above [25]; taker fee 0.04-0.05·p(1−p) [26] | crossing costs about half the spread plus up to 1.25c of fee at 0.50 | maker orders at fair value via file 21's gate |
| Exit / settlement | UMA resolution | early exits pay the spread twice | hold to resolution |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Lifecycle, not latency: LLM Brier skill is positive at listing + 1 day and below −0.7 one checkpoint before close, and two-model ensembles do not beat the market [29] (file 21). (2) The spread is the cost: a crowd residual of 2-3 points survives as a maker order, not as a taker trade across a 2.5c spread plus 1c of politics fee [25][26]. (3) Rate limits, taker delays and uptime do not matter at a few orders a day.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted (not the US, UK or France [E9]); LLM calls dominate latency; nothing justifies co-location.
