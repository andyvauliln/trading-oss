# Terms of service, regulation and legal exposure by strategy type
_As of 2026-09-20. Evidence pack E9 for the Polymarket strategy survey._

Method note: the session's WebSearch budget was already exhausted, so nothing here comes from search snippets. Every source was fetched directly (primary pages, CFTC press room, hub pages and links found on them). Three primary documents were read in full text: the polymarket.com Terms of Use (Google Doc embedded in the /tos page), the Polymarket US Rulebook PDF dated 2026-09-14, and CFTC Staff Letter 26-25. Evidence grades: **[P]** primary/official, **[N]** news, **[A]** affiliate, **[W]** Wikipedia (secondary; its underlying references were not fetched). This is a fact pack, not legal advice; lines marked _inference_ are mine.

## Key facts

1. The international Terms of Use (operator **Adventure One QSS, Inc.**, software licensed from Blockratize; Panama law) are **effective 2026-08-11**. "Restricted Persons" are anyone who resides in, is located in, is incorporated in, or has a registered office or principal place of business in the US, Alberta, Australia, Belgium, British Columbia, France, Germany, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Ontario, Poland, Quebec, Russia, Singapore, Slovakia, Slovenia, Taiwan, Thailand, the Netherlands, New Zealand, the UK, or any jurisdiction on the geoblock list, **plus anyone trading "on behalf of" or "directed, coordinated or controlled by" a Restricted Person**. "There are no exceptions" [1][P].
2. VPN use to circumvent is "strictly prohibited"; the remedy is **close-only mode** at sole discretion. Every trader clicks an attestation: not a U.S. person, not located in a restricted jurisdiction, not using a VPN [1][2][P].
3. ToS §4.2 now contains a full exchange-style conduct code: three insider prongs (breach of duty, tippee, position of influence), spoofing, front-running, fictitious trades, cornering, wash trading, "violating bids or offers", collusion, and any order "that does not result in a change in beneficial ownership" (no intent element) [1][P]. The same three insider prongs appear verbatim in Polymarket US Rule 7.2(g)-(i) [9][P].
4. The ToS has **no clause on multiple accounts or wallets** (zero occurrences of "multiple"), but the Transparency page lists "multi-account coordination" and "circumvention of jurisdictional restrictions" among monitored conduct [1][5][P].
5. Sanctions in the ToS: terminate access, **"prohibit you from participating in any reward or incentive programs or product launches"**, cooperate with law enforcement, sue. Program benefits "may be canceled or forfeited at the Company's sole discretion", with compliance evaluated "retroactively" (§6) [1][P].
6. Data licence trap: a "non-retail, professional entity" in capital markets (the list names market making, **proprietary trading firms**, hedge funds, fintech companies) may not use Polymarket Data "directly or through an API, or any other means, **including on-chain**" without written agreement; reselling Data to such clients or to data distributors is banned; scraping with "robots, crawlers" is banned. Carve-out: the Company "reserves the right to grant access to public on-chain infrastructure and the ... builder program" [1][P].
7. Enforcement numbers published by Polymarket: "100+" referrals to law enforcement and "315+" wallet details provided (Transparency, modified 2026-08-18); the Integrity page shows "90+" referrals and "2 arrests" and names **Chainalysis and Palantir** [4][5][P].
8. The CFTC has charged insider trading in contracts "listed on Polymarket.com", the venue whose own footer says it "is not regulated by the CFTC": Van Dyke, a U.S. soldier, 436,000+ Yes shares, $404,000+ profit, 2026-04-23; and Spagnuolo, "a resident of Switzerland" and Google engineer, 23+ contracts, about $1.2M, 2026-05-27, with a parallel SDNY criminal complaint. The releases cite only "the Commodity Exchange Act and CFTC regulations", not sections [2][22][23][P].
9. Settled CFTC event-contract cases cost roughly 1.6-2.0x the profit plus a 3-year trading ban: Perez (White House teleprompter operator, Kalshi mention markets) $107,539.02 disgorgement + $65,000 penalty; Santos (false social posts about his own SOTU attendance) $17,569.98 + $17,500 [24][25][P].
10. Polymarket US = **QCX LLC d/b/a Polymarket US** (DCM) + Polymarket Clearing (QC Clearing LLC, DCO) + the app provider PM US Tech (an ISV). QCEX was bought for $112M (2025-07-21); the CFTC Amended Order of Designation allowing intermediated access came 2025-11-25 [11][20][21][P].
11. Polymarket US rulebook (version dated **2026-09-14**): fine up to **$500,000 per violation** (8.10(b)); an individual participant "shall not trade for a person or Entity other than itself" (7.2(x)); accounts may be funded only from the participant's own financial accounts (3.9(c)-(d)); automated traders "must implement pre-trade risk controls including order throttles, price collars, and kill switches"; fragmenting beneficial ownership to defeat cross-account surveillance is itself a violation (7.2(m)) [9][P].
12. CFTC Staff Letter 26-25 (2026-09-17) gives no-action relief from introducing-broker registration to "passive software providers" for DCM trading, **event contracts included**, only if the provider never holds user assets, never "generate[s] express 'buy' or 'sell' signals", and exercises no discretion over routing or execution [33][P].
13. CFTC posture under Chairman Selig is openly pro-market: the 2024 event-contract proposal and the sports advisory (Letter 25-36) were withdrawn 2026-02-04; an ANPRM followed 2026-03-12 and NPRMs 2026-06-10 and 2026-06-25; the agency has sued **nine states** (AZ, CT, IL, KY, MN, NM, NY, RI, WI) [28][29][30][31][34][P].
14. Courts are split on sports contracts: Third Circuit (April 2026) for preemption; **Ninth Circuit 2026-08-28: sports event contracts are "likely not swaps"**, so Nevada may regulate them; the Tenth Circuit ruled against Kalshi for Utah; three certiorari petitions are pending [38][40][42][43][N].
15. States are naming Polymarket directly: Connecticut cease-and-desist 2026-09-10 (nine platforms) and Missouri 2026-09-16/17 (six operators, 30 days to comply); a Nevada judge granted a preliminary injunction against Polymarket on 2026-05-29 [38][39][N][54][W].
16. "More than 30 jurisdictions" restrict Polymarket. France moved from a 2024 geoblock to a full ISP block (July 2026), the Dutch KSA rejected Adventure One's appeal (2026-06-23), South Korea blocked access 2026-08-18 **and its police opened an investigation into users**, and ESMA said (2026-09-10) it is "unclear why all EU Member States are not included" in the restricted list [44][45][46][50][N].
17. Tax: the IRS has issued no guidance. Candidate treatments are gambling income, §1256 60/40, swap/financial-product treatment, or binary-option exclusion from §1256. From tax year 2026 only **90% of gambling losses** are deductible (OBBB) [52][N].

## 1. Polymarket international ToS (effective 2026-08-11)

**Who may trade.** The capitalised notice lists the jurisdictions in key fact 1 and then incorporates the geoblock list by URL, "which we may update from time to time without notice" (§4.1) [1]. Sanctioned territories (Iran, Syria, Cuba, North Korea, Crimea, Donetsk, Luhansk) are separately excluded [1][3].

**Sources disagree.** The geoblock page has three tiers: 7 fully blocked OFAC entries (positions cannot even be closed), 36 "close-only on frontend and API" entries (including US, UK, France, Germany, Italy, Belgium, Poland, Singapore, Australia, Brazil, Taiwan, Thailand, four Canadian provinces), and 5 "close-only on the Polymarket frontend; the API itself is not restricted": Ireland, Japan, Malta (sports only), the Netherlands, South Korea [3]. The ToS nevertheless names **Ireland and the Netherlands** as Restricted Persons, and names Hungary and Slovenia, which the geoblock page does not list; Spain, Portugal, Switzerland, Denmark, Bulgaria, Indonesia and Argentina have ISP blocks yet appear on neither list [1][3][45][54]. The UI string says only "The US, Ontario, GB, and OFAC sanctioned territories are restricted jurisdictions" [2]. CoinDesk reports "39 countries" on Polymarket's list [44]. _Inference: an API that accepts your order is not evidence that you are permitted._

**US persons and proxies.** The Restricted Person definition reaches entities incorporated or headquartered in the US and anyone acting for or controlled by one; §4.2 also bans using the site "for or on behalf of any person residing in" a prohibited jurisdiction [1]. An offshore shell controlled by a US founder is inside the definition.

**KYC.** No KYC at onboarding. §4.3 lets the Company demand information "to confirm that you are not a Restricted Person"; failure means termination and exclusion from rewards and product launches [1]. The live UI carries a "Self Certification Required" flow: ID plus recent proof of residence in a non-restricted jurisdiction, "respond within 14 days or your account will be put into close only mode", with separate Individual (KYC) and Entity (KYB) forms [2]. KYC/KYB also gates co-location in `eu-west-2` [3]. France's ANJ cited "insufficient identity verification" when ordering the ISP block [45]. No volume or withdrawal threshold that triggers KYC was found.

**API and bots.** Accepting the ToS happens "by connecting via an API". There is no bot prohibition; limits and rate limits can change without notice (§3.3). What is restricted is data: scraping, and any Data use by Capital Market Clients (key fact 6) [1].

**Resolution and recourse.** Users acknowledge contracts "are resolved by the UMA Optimistic Oracle" and that the Company "is not responsible for any disputes". Liability is capped at **$100**; disputes need a written complaint, a 60-business-day tolling period, then individual arbitration under Panama law; arbitration opt-out within 30 days of acceptance [1].

**Program terms.** Referral: "disqualify referrals and withhold or claw back rewards for ... self-referrals, linked accounts, and inauthentic trading"; omnibus wallets ineligible [6]. Taker rebates: may "adjust or remove rebates and tier status for ... wash trading, self-matching, or other inauthentic trading"; omnibus wallets ineligible [7]. Maker rebates: percentage "at the sole discretion of Polymarket" [8].

**Enforcement examples.** Published: counts in key fact 7 and the Van Dyke case, which Polymarket says "happened because we caught it" [4]. CoinDesk repeats "over 100 cases" referred [48]. The Columbia study (Nov 2025) estimated ~25% of historical volume as wash trading, 14% of 1.26M wallets, and linked it to no KYC, zero fees and airdrop farming; Polymarket did not comment [53]. No public reward clawback or airdrop sybil-filter statement was verified in this session (see E2 for program-level detail).

**What changed in 2025-2026.** Verified: the 2026-08-11 effective date, the conduct code in §4.2, the integrity site (references April 2026 cases) and the transparency page [1][4][5]. A BusinessWire release titled "Polymarket Publishes Enhanced Market Integrity Rules Across Its DeFi Platform and CFTC-Regulated U.S. Exchange" (2026-03-20) could not be fetched (HTTP 403) [59]; no ToS diff was obtainable.

## 2. Polymarket US

- **Timeline.** 2022 CFTC order, $1.4M [54][W]; DOJ and CFTC probes closed July 2025 [54][W]; QCEX acquisition 2025-07-21 [20]; Amended Order 2025-11-25, with surveillance, clearing and Part 16 reporting duties and no launch date given [21]. App launch date and rollout steps: not verified.
- **Structure.** The app is an ISV front end; trading is governed by the DCM and DCO rulebooks [11]. Partner types: ISV (not a broker), IB (same APIs, CFTC/NFA obligations), FCM (VPC peering; FIX, gRPC, REST; segregated customer accounts; must assess "appropriateness of prediction market trading for each customer") [12][13]. NFA performs regulatory services under an RSA [9][10].
- **Who can use it.** 18+ "or the higher age required in your jurisdiction", KYC/AML and sanctions screening; name, DOB, address, sometimes ID plus selfie [11][15]. An affiliate page claims "49 States" and says availability varies by contract [57][A]; the state list is unverified. Finance-industry employees may trade unless their firm deals in event contracts or they hold MNPI [14].
- **Scope.** Sports, politics, economics, crypto, weather, culture, mentions, combos [57][A]; no exchange-set size limits ("You can place any order size") [18]. Fees: Θ × C × p × (1-p), taker Θ 0.0695, maker rebate 0.0125, effective 2026-09-17 [17].
- **API.** Authenticated `api.polymarket.us` and public `gateway.polymarket.us`; keys required; docs do not restrict API use to institutions [16]. App ToS: "use bots or automation except through authorized APIs" is prohibited; market data is "personal, non-commercial", with no scraping, redistribution or "commercialization of derived works" [11].
- **Rules that bite bots.** 7.2(e) spoofing and quote-stuffing; 7.2(f) collusion; 7.2(j) trading on nonpublic knowledge of another's impending order; 7.2(k),(p),(q),(r) wash and self-match "across all accounts under common ownership, control, or direction, whether ... via API"; 7.2(o) settlement-window manipulation; 7.2(y) acting on non-public order information "however acquired"; 3.6(f) multiple intermediary accounts allowed if disclosed and never self-matched; 3.15 incentive-program changes need a Reg 40.6(a) filing and 10 business days [9]. The CFTC warned on 2026-08-12 that event-contract incentive filings are often deficient (Letter 26-23) [32].
- **Differences.** The US venue is custodial, KYC'd, surveilled by NFA, with CFTC-filed rules and AAA arbitration in New York; the international venue is non-custodial, UMA-resolved, Panama-governed, and "not regulated by the CFTC and operates independently" [2][9][11].

## 3. Regulation 2025-2026

**CFTC.** Enforcement advisory 2026-02-25: insider trading reaches event contracts through CEA §6(c)(1) and Reg 180.1(a)(1),(3); wash and pre-arranged trades through §4c(a)(1)-(2)(A) and Reg 1.38(a); disruptive trading through §4c(a)(5). It cites two Kalshi cases: a candidate trading his own race ($246.36 + $2,000, 5-year ban) and a YouTube channel editor ($5,397.58 + $15,000, 2-year ban) [26]. The Van Dyke release calls it the first CFTC insider case in event contracts and the first use of the "Eddie Murphy Rule" [22]. Kalshi later banned Santos for life and fined him $70,000+; it counts five cases [48]. Rulemaking: ANPRM (2026-03-12), NPRM on enumerated activities amending Reg 40.11 (2026-06-10), NPRM on event-contract data reporting under Parts 15-18 (2026-06-25) [28][30][31]. Letter 26-08 tells DCMs to act as "front-line regulators", with sports nuances [27].

**Academic view.** Mitts and Ofir flag 210,718 wallet-market pairs, a 69.9% win rate and ~$143M anomalous profit (Feb 2024 - Feb 2026) and argue the law has gaps: most contracts are not securities, Rule 180.1 is narrower than 10b-5, and trading on lawfully obtained information without deception "stays legal". H.R. 7004 (Torres) covers only federal officials and staff [51]. A March 2026 Democratic bill targets war-related markets [54][W]. Israel has indicted air-force personnel over Iran-strike bets [54][W].

**States.** DeFi Rate's tracker (updated 2026-09-18) counts 133 matters, 120 lawsuits, 28 states. CFTC v. Arizona: preliminary injunction 2026-05-05. CFTC v. Minnesota: PI 2026-07-27, against a state ban effective 2026-08-01 [54][W]. CFTC v. New York: hearing 2026-09-14, Judge Schofield sceptical; earlier TROs denied. Michigan PI against Kalshi sports contracts with a $500,000/day geolocation penalty. Sixth Circuit Michigan appeals include Polymarket [38][41]. New York's AG sought $36B from Kalshi, and the CFTC used emergency authority to keep the exchange operating (2026-08-11) [34]. Connecticut threatens CUTPA civil penalties "and/or criminal penalties" and subpoenaed 15 media organisations and 5 app-store or payment firms [39].

**Abroad.** [54][W] dates: France ANJ 2024-11; Switzerland 2024-11; Poland and Singapore 2025-01; Belgium 2025-02; Bulgaria 2026-02; Portugal 2026-03; Brazil 2026-04; Indonesia and Spain 2026-05; Italy re-block 2026-07; Denmark 2026-08. KSA: penalty payments accrue after a missed 2026-02-17 deadline; "merely a blockchain interface" defence rejected [50]. Korea rejected the P2P defence because the operator "manages market creation and trading rules ... and charges fees" [44]. ESMA's three lenses: national retail binary-option bans, MiCA, gambling law [46]. JPMorgan ended its banking relationship in October 2025 over "regulatory worries" [49].

**US tax.** Under gambling treatment, taxable = winnings − 0.9 × losses, so a flat hedged book shows taxable income equal to 10% of gross losses (_inference from the 90% cap_). §1256 may be unavailable if contracts are binary options or swaps; foreign nationals may face 30% withholding if treated as US-source gambling income. One practitioner: "we are probably headed to the Supreme Court" [52]. Both ToS put tax entirely on the user [1][11]. 1099 practice: not verified.

## 4. Legal exposure by strategy type

- **Wash trading, spoofing, layering.** Prohibited on both venues [1][9][11]. International consequences: close-only mode, rebate removal, reward forfeiture, referral [1][7]. US venue: up to $500,000 per violation plus CFTC exposure [9][26]. The "no change in beneficial ownership" clause has no intent requirement, so an LP engine quoting from several wallets needs its own self-match prevention [1]. Cancel-heavy quoting is fine unless done "with the intent to cancel before execution" [1][9].
- **Sybil and multi-wallet reward farming.** No per-se multi-wallet ban on the international venue, but referral clawback for "linked accounts", "inauthentic trading" language in rebates, retroactive compliance review, and exclusion from "product launches" [1][6][7]. On the US venue it is a rule violation (7.2(m), 3.9) and a KYC misstatement (7.2(t)) [9].
- **Non-public information.** Prohibited: breach of duty, tips from duty-holders, trading where you or your principal can influence the outcome [1][9]. Not prohibited by the text: private research, speed, modelling, public-data scraping [51]. Grey: information from a leak where you "have reason to know" of a breach (tippee prong) [1]. Offshore status and non-US residence did not protect Spagnuolo [23].
- **Copy-trading and front-running.** Copying public on-chain fills uses public information; no clause forbids it. "Front-running" is banned but undefined in the ToS; Rule 7.2(j) defines it as trading on "nonpublic information regarding an impending transaction by another Person" [1][9]. Prohibited: a builder, copy service or signal seller trading ahead of its own users' orders. Santos shows that misleading public statements while holding a position are manipulation under §6(c)(1), which matters for leaders who talk their book [25]. Groups "acting in concert ... for the purpose of moving" a price breach both rule sets [1][9].
- **Oracle, governance and settlement manipulation.** The ToS disclaims responsibility for UMA resolution and has no clause on voting while holding positions [1]. Fraud, "manipulative trading" and the influence prong are the available hooks (_inference_). Documented settlement abuse: 821 accounts made $8.2M by moving Binance prices in the last seconds before 5-minute, 15-minute and 4-hour crypto snapshots, with 93% of losses borne by retail; Polymarket moved to 30-60 second TWAPs via Chainlink Data Streams; no bans or clawbacks were reported [47]. Rule 7.2(o) bans this explicitly on the US venue [9].
- **Operating on both venues.** A US person cannot lawfully touch the international venue, even through an entity it controls [1]. A non-US person can use the international venue; whether a non-US resident can onboard to Polymarket US was not verified [11][15]. The two books are separate legal venues [2]; US-only sports strategies also carry state shut-off risk [38][42].
- **Outside capital (fund or vault).** International: trading for Restricted Persons is banned; omnibus wallets lose rebates and referral rewards; no agency or assignment rights [1][6][7]. US venue: individuals may trade only for themselves, so a fund must onboard as an Entity Participant or sit behind an FCM or broker, with own-name funding [9]. NFA defines a commodity pool as pooled funds trading futures, options "or swaps", with CPO registration required unless Reg 4.5 or 4.13 applies [56]. Whether an on-chain vault trading offshore event contracts is a pool is unresolved (_inference: assume yes if there is any US nexus_).
- **Selling signals or software.** A CTA is anyone who "for compensation or profit, advises others ... as to the value of or the advisability of trading" futures, options or swaps; exemptions include 15 or fewer clients without holding out, and advice "not based upon knowledge of or tailored to" a customer's account [55]. Letter 26-25 protects passive front ends only, and requires risk disclosures, NFA Rule 2-29 marketing compliance, a joint-and-several liability undertaking with the DCM or FCM, and a filed notice consenting to CFTC jurisdiction; signals, discretion or custody fall outside it [33]. Reselling Polymarket data to professional firms needs a licence [1].

## What a builder who already runs LP / cross-venue arb / resolution-sniping / copy-trading bots is likely to UNDERESTIMATE

1. **The offshore venue is inside CFTC and DOJ reach, including for non-US residents.** Two of the first three CFTC event-contract insider cases concern polymarket.com trades, one by a Swiss resident [22][23]. Every trade is permanently on-chain and Polymarket passes wallet details to authorities (315+) using Chainalysis and Palantir [4][5].
2. **Incorporating makes you a "Capital Market Client".** The data clause covers proprietary trading firms and on-chain data; a solo trader who forms a trading company arguably needs a written data agreement [1].
3. **"API not restricted" is not permission** (Netherlands and Ireland), and the London matching engine sits in a close-only country, so hosting location and corporate domicile need separate checks [1][3].
4. **Self-fills are strict-liability style.** Multi-wallet LP, arb and sniping stacks can cross each other; the ToS clause has no intent element and rebates can be stripped for "self-matching" [1][7].
5. **Any ToS breach, not only sybil behaviour, can forfeit rewards and future "product launches"**, judged retroactively [1].
6. **Words are trades.** Santos was sanctioned for misleading posts plus a position; copy-trade leaders, signal channels and group chats create manipulation and collusion exposure [25][1][9].
7. **Settlement-window activity in the underlying is now a studied abuse pattern** (821 accounts, $8.2M); hedging a crypto-market snipe on Binance inside the window can look identical [47].
8. **Polymarket US is not "the same bot with KYC".** Own-name funding, no trading for others, mandatory kill switches and collars, disclosure of every intermediary account, $500k-per-violation fines, non-commercial market data [9][11].
9. **Sports capacity on the US venue is a legal option, not a given.** Ninth Circuit ruling, Connecticut and Missouri orders naming Polymarket, Michigan's $500k/day geolocation penalty [38][39][42].
10. **User-level risk is emerging abroad** (Korean police probing users) and the restricted list grows without notice; ESMA is pushing for all EU states [44][46][1].
11. **Tax can turn a hedged book into income**: the 90% loss cap, and the two legs of one arb may fall under different regimes [52].
12. **No practical recourse**: $100 liability cap, Panama arbitration, 60-business-day pre-dispute wait, UMA outcomes disclaimed [1].
13. **A copy-trading or signal SaaS is a registration question**, not just a product: Letter 26-25 excludes signals and discretion, and the CTA definition covers swaps [33][55].
