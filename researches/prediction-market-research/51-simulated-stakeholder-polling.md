# 51 - Simulated stakeholder polling: synthetic electorates and twin panels
_Band: speculative · Evidence grade: D (no forecasting or trading record against a market price anywhere; A for the market baseline measured here from Gamma, CLOB and the Silver Bulletin series; B for the adjacent silicon-sampling literature, mostly preprints; C for vendor claims) · Last verified: 2026-09-29_

### Simulated stakeholder polling
- **One-line summary**: A synthetic poll (LLM respondents grounded in real people's posts or interviews, reweighted to the population) nowcasts the next approval or sentiment print, or a race nobody polls, to trade Polymarket's poll-print brackets and thin election markets. Unproven: no record against a price exists, silicon respondents reproduce priors rather than change, and on 80 resolved weekly approval events a no-AI random walk on the published number already ties the price.
- **Category**: Speculative-novel [information edge on poll-print brackets and under-polled election markets]. The only file in the band that tests polling simulation (critic note: 52 and 65 are institutional twins; 53 and 76 model other aggregates).
- **Maturity**: Experimental and unproven on any market. One prospective scored hit: PoSSUM (1,054 X users read by GPT-4o, then MrP, fielded 17-26 October 2024) called 50 of 51 states (P(R) = 0.65), with a 2.6-point anti-Trump margin bias and only 61% of results inside its 90% intervals [1]. Dynamo-K called 3 of 3 Korean presidential winners retrospectively (95% CI 29-100%; best case 2.1-point MAE on the 0.73-point 2022 race) after recalibrating agent ideology to Gallup; unmitigated, 97% of moderate agents voted progressive (mitigation cut MAE 5.2x, 36.8 → 7.1 points) [2]; FlockVote "replicates" 2024 after the fact [12]. Aaru ($88M raised, ARR under $10M) claims its NYC mayoral primary simulation landed within 2,000 of ~2M votes and admits it cannot predict Trump or Powell [13]; its September 2024 projection was Harris +4.2 against an actual Trump +1.5 [E8]. Simile ($200M Series B at $2B) sells simulated customers, not polls (marketing) [14]. Nobody is found trading poll-print or election markets with synthetic respondents (nine GitHub and three HN searches; PoSSUM's repository has 13 stars) [15].
- **Requires AI?**: yes. LLMs play respondents: each reads one person's posts, images or interview and answers the resolving source's exact question. Turning answers into a number is statistics fitted on resolved history: MrP, the panel-to-aggregate link, debiasing, the carry-forward baseline and sizing.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Targets: Polymarket's weekly "Trump approval rating on <date>?" (six 0.5-point brackets on Silver Bulletin's trend line, final once the next day's point is published), monthly UMich sentiment brackets [16]; thin election markets, mostly non-US races and ridings [20].
  2. Sub-variant A, trace-grounded MrP (PoSSUM-style): sample ~1,000 users who post about the race through the X API; a multimodal LLM infers each one's vote intention and demographics from recent posts; MrP (multilevel regression and poststratification: a pooled model of vote by demographic and regional cell, reweighted to census and turnout counts) corrects for who posts [1].
  3. Sub-variant B, standing twin panel: 300-500 agents, each grounded in one real person's interview or 500-question battery (the public Twin-2K-500 set of 2,058 US adults is the free start [8]); each day they read a dated news digest and answer the exact question.
  4. Sub-variant C, delta nowcast: never use the panel's level; add only its change since the last real print to a carry-forward of the published aggregate, with a coefficient fitted on history (null: zero).
  5. Sub-variant D, hybrid: when synthetic estimate and price disagree and the book is deep enough, buy a real online micro-poll to calibrate or override (the Theo precedent: commissioned "neighbour" polls, file 25).
  6. Sub-variant E, defensive twin: flag polls from unknown or unrated firms and fade the moves they cause.
  7. Convert the forecast into bracket or win probabilities; post maker orders where the gap exceeds half-spread plus fee (politics taker fee 0.04·p(1−p) per share, economics 0.05, makers pay nothing [26]).
- **What creates the edge**: Mechanism: between real polls the market prices the last print plus narrative, so a panel that registers shifts before pollsters publish, or a cheap poll where none exists, is new information [1]; the payers would be hobbyists in thin weekly brackets. The strongest counter-argument has three layers. (a) Silicon respondents measure the prior, not the change: across 400,000+ participants LLM answers match item means but explain 3.05% of respondent-specific variance against 53.6% human test-retest, and richer personas do not help [4]; across 30 European countries individual recovery is negligible and an average of neighbouring countries, no LLM, beats every model condition [3]; twins built on 500+ answers correlate r = 0.20 with their humans [7]; predictions of 26,000 EU voters' 2024 votes "largely fail" [6]; persona agents exaggerate partisanship and do not diversify ensemble errors [10][11]. Interview grounding helps individuals (86% of test-retest vs 74% for demographics [5]), not week-to-week drift [9]. (b) A no-AI baseline already ties the market: on 80 resolved weekly approval events (481 brackets, March 2025 - September 2026), per-bracket market Brier is 0.122 / 0.126 / 0.100 five, three and one day before the date; a random walk on the latest Silver Bulletin value in the chart version published before each forecast time scores 0.122 / 0.121 / 0.103, every event-clustered 95% interval of the difference straddling zero [18]. The aggregate is a random walk (daily change SD 0.24 points, lag-1 autocorrelation 0.01) [17]; the week's change is set by which pollsters publish and their house effects, a mechanical process (files 20, 71) twins do not see, so a panel must add information the aggregator's own inputs do not carry. (c) Capacity: two snapshots of the live weekly event show 2-8c spreads and $1-93 within 5c per bracket [19]; where books are deep (liquid US races) prices follow professional aggregates [E8].
- **Capital required**: <$1k to low thousands, capacity-bound. Weekly brackets take $20-90 per bracket near mid and recycle weekly [19]; thin races $300-1,000 each, locked until election day. Data is the binding cost: ~$300-500 per PoSSUM-style poll, $1.5-3.2k per real micro-poll (below).
- **Technical requirements**: Built on file 21's stack (feeds, dated retrieval, calibration ledger, trading layer). Agent design sketch: (1) Target compiler: rules into resolving source, exact question, population, rounding and finalisation rule (file 71's print spec). (2) Respondents: A, 1,000 X users, each agent sees only that user's last ~50 posts and images; B, 300-500 twins, each sees its person's interview or battery plus the day's digest of headlines dated before the forecast time. No agent sees the price or the aggregate; two or more model families (mixing cuts error correlation 0.68 → 0.40 [E8]). (3) Aggregation: MrP in Stan / brms to census and turnout margins (A); for B and C, the panel's approval change Δ enters `v_D = v_last + β·Δ + ε`, ε from the empirical k-day volatility, β fitted on the first 40% of events in time order and frozen, null β = 0. Debiasing: changes only, never levels [4]; corrections for progressive skew and third-party collapse [2]. (4) Bracket probabilities with the one-decimal rounding, then a 0.5 / 0.5 stack with the mid. (5) Cost, estimated, not measured: B, 500 twins × ~12k input tokens per daily run ≈ 6M tokens, $0.60-6 a day at file 21's list prices (GPT-6 Luna to Haiku 4.5), $2-45 per weekly event on batch; A, ~5,000 user lookups and ~70,000 post reads on the X API (~$400 [24]) plus ~20M multimodal tokens ≈ $300-500 per poll (Dynamo-K runs 5,000 agents for ~$0.25 on open-weight models, without trace reading [2]); D, Pollfish $0.95 a response plus quotas = $1.5-3.2k for n = 1,000, ±3 points (file 25). Value of information (derived): a $2k poll pays back only if it unlocks ~$2k of edge, e.g. 20,000 shares at 10c, against $1-93 within 5c per weekly bracket and $300-1,000 per thin race, so it pays only in books deep enough that professional polls already exist (Theo's 2024 trade, file 25). (6) A leak-controlled replay harness and a vintage recorder (every published chart version is retrievable with its timestamp [17]). Build: 3-6 weeks solo for B / C on top of file 21; A adds 2-4 weeks per country for poststratification frames.
- **Opportunity size**: Small and shrinking on Polymarket. Weekly approval brackets: Gamma volume $0.22-1.12M a month March 2025 - February 2026, $373k in April and $158k in May 2026, $21-69k a month June-September, $1.9-11k per event (both legs of each fill, file 29); UMich $13-184k a month since June; weekly Up or Down and generic ballot under $1.2k per event [16]. The comparable pool is Kalshi's (US persons only): its weekly RCP approval brackets traded 80k-333k contracts per event in August-September [21]. Elections: 1,754 open events hold $335M of Gamma liquidity but traded $3.2M that day, the median event $10; of 275 non-US events resolving by March 2027, 96 hold $10-250k and traded ≥ $100 that day (median $21), e.g. Quebec ridings and the Abbotsford-Mission by-election [20]. A real 3-point edge on $1-2k a month of near-mid turnover is worth tens of dollars a month (derived).
- **Pros**: Weekly resolution gives fast feedback; the aggregate's history, poll tables and every vintage are free [17]; the carry-forward arm costs nothing; the fake-poll detector protects any election book.
- **Cons / risks**: Edge: (a)-(c) above. Leakage: every 2024 "replication" is in-sample for current models [12][E8], and the approval series sits in training data up to each model's cutoff. Staleness: twins age (Twin-2K-500 dates from May 2025 [8]). Resolution: the provisional latest point moves until the next day's point appears (up to 0.68 points [17]); 4 of the 80 events had a disputed first UMA proposal (three proposed No on the bracket that resolved Yes) while Data API `/v2/resolutions` reports `was_disputed: false` on all four, so read Gamma `umaResolutionStatuses` too [16]. Capacity: $1-93 within 5c [19]; volume down ~90% from its 2025 monthly average [16]. Data: X API caps pay-per-use at 3M post reads a month [24]. Refusals: newer GPT-4o versions refused PoSSUM's inputs at "unacceptable rejection rates" [1]. Legal and reputational: see flag.
- **Expected results**: no public data, theoretical only. Adjacent: PoSSUM's prospective hit with a 2.6-point bias [1] and Aaru's 5.7-point September miss [E8]; no scored record against a market. The no-AI arm measured here: buying whichever side the real-time random walk favours by more than 10c, crossing a 3c half-spread and paying today's politics fee, made +9.9c per share three days out (108 trades, 58 events; 95% interval +2.1c to +17.8c; +8.9c at a 4c half-spread), but +2.5c [−5.4, +10.3] five days out, +0.0c [−10.4, +10.9] one day out and nothing distinguishable from zero at a 5c threshold; over the nine cells tried, the best one's Bonferroni interval includes zero (−1.0c to +20.9c), and 331 of the 481 brackets had no taker fee when they traded [16][18]. A hypothesis for file 20, not a result, worth tens of dollars a month at $1-93 of depth within 5c (derived). Guess: a debiased panel adds 0 to 0.005 Brier over carry-forward; PnL −5% to +5% of $1-1.5k monthly turnover, −$75 to +$75 a month before LLM and data costs, negative after; PoSSUM-style polls on thin races cost more than near-mid depth can return.
- **Competitive landscape**: No synthetic-poll trader found [15]. The price is set by hobbyists reading Silver Bulletin, statistical modellers (file 20), poll-release takers (file 17) and liquidity-reward quoters: the live brackets pay $2-25 a day each, $50-72 a day across the event in two snapshots, to makers within 4.5c [16][19] (file 01). Synthetic-research firms sell to marketers, with no public election scorecard [13][14][E8]. Being competitive means a ledger of pre-registered, leak-free print forecasts; nobody has one.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: grey. Trading your own model from one identity: Allowed [E9]. Collect posts through the paid X API, not scraping [24]. Inferring political opinions from EU users' posts processes a GDPR Article 9 special category; the 9(2)(e) exception covers data "manifestly made public by the data subject", which inference may exceed (legal advice needed) [25]. Twin panels of recruited people need informed consent for this use; Twin-2K-500 is CC-BY-4.0 [8]. Never publish synthetic numbers as a poll: in August 2026 Median Strategies' fabricated polls (LA mayor, Wisconsin Democratic governor primary, Nevada governor) moved Kalshi's Bass contract from about 63c to 65c within 15 minutes, and roughly 20 Polymarket accounts traded thousands of contracts within six minutes of the post; the firm called it a "social experiment" [22][23]. Publishing a poll to move a market is manipulation (file 31). Election contracts: 30+ jurisdictions restrict Polymarket; South Korea blocked it on 2026-08-18 and its police are investigating users, which bears on Korean targets like Dynamo-K's [E9]. Selling the output as signals raises the CTA question [E9].
- **How you'd validate it**: Phase 0 (done, $0): market baseline, real-time carry-forward arm rebuilt from chart version timestamps, power [18]. Phase 1, retrospective kill test for B / C, 2-3 weeks, ~$200-1,000: weekly approval events created after every panel model's training cutoff (39 in January-September 2026 if all cutoffs precede 2026), forecasts at five, three and one days, counted only if the market was open then; twins from Twin-2K-500; digests from date-filtered retrieval with a manual leak audit of 50; carry-forward from the chart version published before the forecast time [17]. Six pre-registered arms: raw panel level; debiased delta (β frozen after the first 40% of events); carry-forward; market mid at the same timestamp (CLOB `/prices-history`); file 21's plain forecaster given the series and digest; a 0.5 / 0.5 delta-market blend. Metrics: per-bracket Brier and winning-bracket log loss against the market and carry-forward (event-clustered paired bootstrap); PnL at recorded books after fees. Kill if β's interval includes zero or the debiased delta does not beat carry-forward. Power: the per-event summed Brier difference between real-time carry-forward and market has SD 0.15 / 0.19 / 0.24 at five / three / one days [18]; with that as the proxy, a 0.01 per-bracket gain (0.06 per event) needs ~48 / 79 / 129 events and a 0.02 gain ~12 / 20 / 32 (80% power, two-sided 5%, derived), so Phase 1 can reject only large effects. Phase 2, forward, 6 months, $300-800: frozen pipeline on Polymarket's weekly and UMich brackets plus Kalshi's RCP brackets as scoring targets only [21]; paper-trade at recorded books; live money only if the blend beats the market by ≥ 0.005 per bracket with the interval above zero AND the delta beats carry-forward (~26 events detect only gains ≥ 0.02, so expect the gate to stay shut unless the effect is large). Sub-variant A, forward only: pre-register 10 of the 96 active non-US races ($3-5k of polls), score vote-share MAE against the final polling average and Brier against the mid seven days out; ten races can kill the idea, never confirm it. Sub-variant E: log polls that move an election price ≥ 3c within 15 minutes and paper-fade unrated pollsters; August 2026 gives three examples [22].

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive (hours to days). The panel runs once a day and the edge, if any, is in the week's drift; repricing when a new poll moves the aggregate is file 17's race.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Gamma `recurring` / `approvals` tags; weekly events list 4-9 days before the date [16] | little is lost: the price is no better than carry-forward until the last day [18] | daily scan |
| Fair value | daily panel run (30-60 min); latest aggregate from the chart | carrying the final value from two days back instead of the latest provisional one costs ~0.01 Brier one day out [18] | refresh after each chart publish (~14 a day, 17 in 2026 [17]) |
| Entry | 2-8c spreads, $1-93 within 5c [19]; taker fee 1c at 50c [26] | crossing costs 2-5c a share, most of any plausible edge | maker orders, ≥ 20 shares, inside the 4.5c reward band [16] |
| Poll-release repricing | new-poll latency | news takers and Kalshi move first [22] | skip |
| Settlement / recycling | next day's point finalises the value; UMA proposal | weekly recycling; election positions lock for months | hold to resolution |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Freshness, not latency: the market beats a random walk on the two-day-old final value one day out (+0.013 Brier), and the value actually on the chart at the forecast time closes the gap [18]. (2) Taking costs a 1-4c half-spread plus up to 1c of fee; the best in-sample cell keeps +9.9c at a 3c half-spread and +8.9c at 4c, the other cells nothing distinguishable from zero [18][26]. (3) Rate limits and uptime do not matter at a few orders a day.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted (not the US, UK or France [E9]); LLM calls dominate latency; nothing justifies co-location.
