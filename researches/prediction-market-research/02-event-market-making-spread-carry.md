# 02 - Event-market making: spread capture vs settlement carry, rebate wedge and taker-delay last look
_Band: established-emerging · Evidence grade: B credible reports and papers for who wins (makers as a class); C self-reported for per-bot economics; A for fees, rebates and delays · Last verified: 2026-09-24_

### Event-market making: spread capture vs settlement carry, rebate wedge and taker-delay last look
- **One-line summary**: Quote both sides across many politics, culture, tech, economics and long-tail event markets as an inventory business. The on-chain evidence says the money comes mostly from which side you end up holding at settlement and from choosing where to quote, helped by the deterministic maker rebate and the taker fee, and only marginally from the quoted spread.
- **Category**: Market-making [inventory and selection business; subsidy-first quoting is file 01]
- **Maturity**: Established as a class, unproven for a new solo entrant. Automated limit-order providers are the documented winners on Polymarket. About 800 "high-frequency, bot-like" accounts each cleared more than $100k, $131M in total, from January 2025 to April 2026 [3]. The top 1% of profitable users take 76.5% of profits, and winners "provide liquidity using limit orders that resolve favorably" [4]. Market makers are under 0.5% of accounts and sit in the group that takes more than 30% of gains [6]. No public net PnL exists for any small event-market maker, and the reference open-source bot warns that it "can lose money" [16]. Institutional desks arrived in 2026 (Wintermute two-sided from 1 June; Jump, SIG, DRW and others) [17][18], so the mid-book of liquid markets is saturating while the long tail is not.
- **Requires AI?**: optional. Deterministic quoting, inventory and rebate logic come first. An LLM helps with (a) tagging markets by jump risk and informed-flow risk from title and rules, (b) reading clarifications and rule edits, which move prices, and (c) deciding which side of a multi-outcome field is the tail-demand side. It never sets the quote.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Universe: pick event markets outside the specialist files (not short crypto up/down, file 03; not sports priced off sportsbooks, file 04). Check each market's `feeSchedule` in Gamma, because rebates exist only where takers pay fees. Politics, finance, tech and mentions pay 0.04, economics, culture, weather and other 0.05, crypto 0.07, geopolitics 0. NFL and college-football game markets were `zero_fees` on 24 Sep 2026 [10][13].
  2. Fair value: the microprice (the size-weighted mid of the best bid and ask) plus slow anchors, namely cross-listed prices (Kalshi, Polymarket US, file 08), base rates and the market's own recent trade flow.
  3. Quote post-only on both sides (post-only rejects any order that would take liquidity). Skew both quotes against inventory and widen them as volatility or measured toxicity rises. Toxicity is the rate at which your fills are followed by adverse price moves. Pull quotes on news and before scheduled events.
  4. Inventory policy, the core decision: either flatten each fill, or keep the side that the evidence says pays at settlement. In the one-day BTC 5-minute sample of the most recent study, makers' profit came almost entirely from buying the side priced 0.50-1.00 and holding it (+$52.4K in 0.50-0.80, +$24.8K in 0.80-1.00, -$0.5K from selling above 0.80) [1].
  5. Exits: merge YES+NO pairs back to $1 pUSD through the gasless relayer rather than crossing the spread. Redeem at resolution.
  6. Income lines per fill: (a) spread; (b) maker rebate = rebate share x taker fee on your own fills, paid daily in pUSD above $1 [9]; (c) liquidity rewards where configured (file 01); (d) 3.25% holding rewards on inventory in flagged markets [23].
  7. Measure every fill against the on-chain `OrderFilled` event, not the public feed, and mark it to resolution, not to 5 minutes.
- **What creates the edge**: Four things. First, *screening rent*. Takers who buy longshots and tail outcomes are overpaying in every non-sports category: longshots return -15% to -26% in politics, crypto, culture and weather [21]. A patient maker ends up holding the underpriced complement and collects at settlement. In the BTC 5-minute sample, 91.4% of persistent makers' profit arrived at settlement and under 9% from trading before it. Makers finished +$45.3K and retail -$66.7K, and the authors say the same split holds across all Polymarket markets in Akey et al.'s full 588M-trade record [1]. Second, a *fee wedge*. The taker pays `feeRate x p x (1-p)` per share and the maker pays nothing [10], so a taker hitting your quote needs information worth more than the fee plus your half-spread. Third, the *rebate*: 25% of that fee in most categories, 20% crypto, 15% sports, computed per market from your own fills' fee-equivalent [9]. At p = 0.50 it is worth 0.25c/share in politics, 0.31c in economics or culture, 0.35c in crypto and 0.19c in sports (derived from [9][10]). Fourth, *venue delays*. On markets with a taker delay, a pending taker order cannot be cancelled while a resting maker order can [11]. On the other side of the trade are late, price-insensitive retail takers. Individual traders "lose money because they trade late and accept worse odds" [3], and on Kalshi takers lose 1.12% per trade while makers gain the same [25]. They keep paying because they pay for immediacy and entertainment.
- **Capital required**: $10k+ to run the business, $1k-$10k to validate. Capital sits in resting quotes, but any carry policy locks it in inventory until resolution, often months for politics. Minimum order size is typically 5 shares [E1]. Quoting 50 markets at $100-200 a side takes $10k-$20k before inventory builds up.
- **Technical requirements**: (1) Data in: CLOB market and user WebSockets, Gamma metadata (`feeSchedule`, `secondsDelay`, end dates, neg-risk flags), on-chain `OrderFilled` via an indexer for true trade direction and builder tags, and a changelog and status watcher. (2) Decision logic: microprice fair value, inventory skew, a toxicity estimator from your own markouts at 1 min, 1 h and resolution, a regime switch (poly-maker's QUIET / TRENDING / EVENT is a reference [16]), and a per-price-band inventory policy (flatten vs carry). (3) Execution: post-only GTC, cancel-replace (no modify), 15-order batches, 40 orders/s and 80 cancels/s standard budgets, heartbeat, and handling of HTTP 425 and the 2-minute post-only mode after restarts [E1][28]. (4) Risk: per-market and per-event inventory caps, jump limits by time to resolution, a kill switch (mandatory on Polymarket US [31]), and self-trade prevention across your own wallets [30]. (5) Test: paper mode on the live book plus replay on recorded L2 data. Build: 4-6 weeks for a solo developer on top of an existing LP stack; 2-3 months from scratch.
- **Opportunity size**: The maker-side pie is large but mostly already owned. The ~800 bot-like accounts cleared ~$131M over 16 months (~$98M/year, derived; this includes non-maker bots) [3]. Maker rebates paid ~$187k/day and liquidity rewards ~$126k/day in mid-September across all categories [E2]. Spreads show where rent is left: across markets in the top 1,100 events by 24-hour volume on 24 Sep 2026, the median full quoted spread was 2.0c in politics, geopolitics and crypto, 3.0c in "other" and 6.7c in finance and economics, for mids between 0.10 and 0.90 [14]. On the top-100 markets the median effective half-spread was ~0 [2], so spread capture in liquid markets is gone. Direction: saturating in liquid markets (institutional desks), stable in the long tail (median ~32 effective makers per market, ~3 at the extreme [2]), and smaller per unit in fee-free categories, which carry no rebate.
- **Pros**: The class is demonstrably profitable; carry rent does not depend on speed; rebates are deterministic per fill; merges recycle inventory without crossing; it stacks with rewards (01) and holding rewards; and it runs on the infrastructure an LP builder already has.
- **Cons / risks**: Execution: binary jumps to 0/1, fills that match and then fail on-chain, cancel-only modes and 99.13% uptime [24]. Adverse selection: 1,950 suspected insider accounts whose trades move prices 7-12x more per dollar [20], and ~$143M of anomalous informed profit concentrated in exactly the markets a carry maker would hold (Iran, Maduro) [19]. Capital: carry locks money to resolution. Platform: rebate percentages are discretionary (sports 25% to 15% on 10 Jul 2026 [12]), and the CLOB V2 cut-over wiped every resting order on 28 Apr 2026 [E1]. Measurement: public-feed trade direction is right only ~59% of the time [2]. Edge decay: institutional desks and a promised Rust CLOB with 10-20x better p99 latency [32].
- **Expected results**: Class-level hard data only: makers ended +$45.3K against retail -$66.7K in one day of BTC 5-minute trading [1]. Top-5% skilled wallets earn $121 per market as makers against $63 as takers, extracting $228M over three years, while aggregate spread transfer is negligible [8] (SSRN abstract via the brief; SSRN returned 403 to this check). One account made ~$3M in 2026 on more than a million trades in Oscars markets [5]. In politics, 0.55% of profitable maker wallets took 50% of maker gains, ~$8M of ~$16M, December 2025 to February 2026 [7]. Per-bot economics: self-reported only. @defiance_cr went from $200/day to $700-800/day on $10k in 2024, pre-fee and reward-driven [29] [pre-2025 conditions, possibly stale]. On Kalshi the average maker still loses ~10% [26] (search snippet only). For a new solo entrant: no public data. Guess: with $20k-$50k across 50-150 long-tail markets, $50-200/day gross (spread + rebate + rewards) and -$50 to +$100/day net after markouts, with a fat left tail. Only a per-band carry policy with measured positive settlement PnL makes it clearly positive.
- **Competitive landscape**: Two-tier. Liquid politics, macro and crypto-linked markets are quoted by desks (Wintermute, Jump with an equity-for-capacity deal, SIG, DRW, Flow Traders, Galaxy) and by reward farmers at one tick [17][18][E4]. Polymarket is also building an in-house desk for RFQ parlays [E4]. The long tail (~300k new markets a month, ~32 effective makers median) is served by bots like poly-maker (1,500 stars) and by discretionary makers such as Domer, "most of my trades are my orders being matched" [E4]. To compete you need a per-market toxicity model, a settlement-carry policy, sub-second cancels on news and breadth under rate limits.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none for honest two-sided quoting from one identity (E9: Allowed on both venues). Grey for quoting from several own wallets, because any self-fill breaches the "no change in beneficial ownership" clause, which has no intent element [30]. Prohibited: spoofing and layering ("with the intent to cancel before execution"), wash trades and cross-account self-matching; on Polymarket US, Rule 7.2 carries fines up to $500,000 per violation, and kill switches and price collars are mandatory for automated traders [30][31].
- **How you'd validate it**: 1) From your existing LP fills (or 2 weeks of paper quoting on 30 long-tail markets), decompose PnL per fill into spread, rebate (rebate share x fee), rewards and mark-to-settlement, split by price band (0-0.2, 0.2-0.5, 0.5-0.8, 0.8-1.0) and by regime (trending vs flat). Use `OrderFilled` for direction. 2) Pass if settlement-marked PnL is positive in at least two bands over 150+ resolved fills and beats rebate + rewards alone. Fail if 1-hour markouts are positive but settlement markouts are negative, which is the longshot pattern [21]. 3) Pilot with $2k-5k in the passing bands only, carry policy on, 30 days. Cost: ~40 engineering hours on an LP stack, no capital in step 1.

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: execution-sensitive. The rent is realised at settlement and through choosing where to quote, so selection and inventory policy beat raw speed. Speed decides losses: a quote left up through a news jump is the fill that costs a month of spread.

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fair value / microprice | L2 WebSocket, cross-venue anchors | quotes centred on a stale mid are picked off by anyone watching Kalshi or the US book | update each book event; anchors every 1-5 s |
| Quote refresh | cancel-replace, 15-order batches | you sit behind one-tick competitors and fill only when wrong (adverse selection by queue) | <500 ms from book change to new quote |
| News and event cancels | news feed, calendar, price jumps | stale quotes filled at pre-news prices; a ~500 ms newswire was too slow in a practitioner test [27] | pre-event pulls by schedule; sub-second on price triggers |
| Taker-delay last look | `secondsDelay`, `itode` flag | the delay is a free cancel window only if your signal arrives inside it; politics and culture markets have no delay [11][13] | only relevant on crypto (150 ms), finance up/down (250 ms), sports (1 s) |
| Inventory / carry decisions | settlement-marked markout statistics | none from speed; errors here are policy errors | daily recalibration |
| Merges and redemption | relayer, 25 req/min on `/submit` [E1] | capital recycled hours later instead of minutes | batch merges every 15-60 min |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) In markets without a taker delay, the fastest canceller keeps the rent. Where crypto spot drives the price, Polymarket quotes took a median 347 ms to respond to large Binance moves [33], which marks the stale-quote line. (2) Taker delays changed four times in 2026: crypto went from 250 ms to 50 ms on 17 Aug and back up to 150 ms on 4 Sep, and sports markets hold marketable orders for 1 s [12][13]. Each change moves the maker's free-option value, so parameters must be read from the API, not hard-coded. (3) The rate limit caps breadth more than latency does: 40 orders/s standard vs 600/s Elite [28].
- **Where to run it**: AWS eu-west-1 (Dublin), the nearest unrestricted region to the eu-west-2 matching engine; London is close-only [E1][E9]. A small VPS anywhere works for the carry book, but not for the quoting engine.

### Results by investment size
Assumptions: 20-100 long-tail event markets, 5-share minimum, full quoted spreads of 2-3c [14], rebate 0.25-0.31c/share at mid [9], daily fill turnover of 20-40% of resting capital, and markouts eating 50-100% of spread + rebate in bad weeks. All figures are guesses derived from these inputs; no small maker has published net PnL.

| Capital | Workable? | How it is deployed | Gross / month | Running costs / month | Net / month | Net / capital / month | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1k | no (learning only) | 5-10 markets at $50-100 a side | $20-60 (spread + rebate) | $10-70 | -$60 to +$30 | -6% to +3% | fixed costs; one jump fill equals a month of spread |
| $5k | marginal | 20-30 markets at $80-120 a side, carry in the passing bands | $150-450 | $70-120 | -$150 to +$250 | -3% to +5% | inventory locked to resolution eats quoting capital within weeks |
| $10k | yes, for a pilot business | 40-60 markets, $100-150 a side, 30-50% of capital reserved for carry inventory | $350-1,000 | $70-250 | -$200 to +$600 | -2% to +6% | capital lock-up in carry positions; toxicity in news-driven markets |

- **Minimum sensible capital**: ~$5k. Below that, quotes are too small to matter in queues and fixed costs dominate.
- **Capacity ceiling**: long-tail depth per market is small, so for a solo operator returns per dollar flatten beyond ~$100k-$250k (guess). Beyond that you are quoting liquid markets against desks.

### What you are probably underestimating
1. **The famous 91.4% is one day of BTC 5-minute trading.** The figure comes from 738,393 trades across 275 markets in about 24 hours, with takers paying 1.8% and no rebate. The claim that it holds platform-wide rests on the authors' reading of Akey et al. [1]. Treat it as a strong hint that settlement carry is where makers earn, not as a measured event-market number. Measure your own.
2. **Short-horizon markouts lie about longshots.** Posted-offer longshot buys look good at 5 minutes (+3.9%) and at 1 hour (+16.9%), then lose 36.3% at resolution [21]. A toxicity model trained on 1-minute markouts will keep buying exactly the inventory that loses at settlement.
3. **Your trade-direction data is wrong ~41% of the time** if it comes from the public WebSocket [2]. Effective spread and adverse-selection estimates can flip sign on 50-67% of top-100 markets depending on the source [E4]. Rebuild direction from `OrderFilled`.
4. **The rebate is small, deterministic and absent where you might expect it.** At p = 0.50 it is 0.19-0.35c/share; at p = 0.90 it falls to 36% of that. It is zero in geopolitics and in the zero-fee NFL and college-football game markets [9][13]. It cannot pay for one bad fill in fifty.
5. **Taker rebates thin the fee wedge exactly against the heaviest takers.** Takers at the Platinum-Obsidian tiers get 32-50% of their fees back [15], so the most active, and plausibly best-informed, flow faces half the wedge you modelled.
6. **Builder codes label the flow for free.** `/builder/trades` shows per-app fills without auth. MetaMask and RedotPay flow is all taker, in $2-5 tickets, paying 4% on buys [22]. Condition quote width on the builder tag of recent fills; you are probably pricing all takers the same.
7. **Multi-outcome fields pay more rent and carry more traps.** The cost of completing an outcome set rises with the number of outcomes (coefficient +0.060 on log n, 96% of it per-leg committed markup) [1], so quoting every leg of a large neg-risk field collects more premium. But incoherent sums are harvested by converter bots within seconds (file 09), and augmented "Other" legs change meaning as placeholders are named [E1].
8. **Fee-free geopolitics is where informed flow lives.** No rebate, no fee wedge, and the largest documented insider cases (Iran strike, Maduro) [19]. Quote it wider or not at all.
9. **Holding rewards pay you to carry.** 3.25% a year on inventory in 228+ flagged markets (presidential, nominee, crypto-price and "out before 2027" families) [23][E2]. In those markets a carry policy has a positive floor that a flatten policy gives up.
10. **Platform events are PnL events.** Order wipes (28 Apr 2026), 2-minute post-only after restarts, HTTP 425, a 4 h 24 min halt on 31 Aug, and a Rust CLOB replacement due in Q4 2026 [E1][32]. Run a state-reconciliation job after every incident.
