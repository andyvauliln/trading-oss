# 56 - Multi-agent adversarial debate applied as a residual on the market price
_Band: speculative · Evidence grade: D (no debate forecaster has been scored against a market price and then traded; A for the own measurements on ForecastBench's published forecasts and on Polymarket's Gamma, CLOB and Data APIs; B for the adjacent debate literature, mostly preprints on non-forecasting tasks) · Last verified: 2026-09-29_

### Multi-agent adversarial debate applied as a residual on the market price
- **One-line summary**: Two advocates from different model vendors argue YES and NO from one dated evidence pack. A third-vendor judge, who sees neither the price nor who argued what, outputs a probability. It is used only as a small logit-space correction to the Polymarket price, and an independent-ensemble control arm can switch the debate off. Unproven: in the one post-cutoff Polymarket test, debate scored worse than an independent ensemble, which scored worse than the price (100 markets, not significant). On 574 resolved Polymarket question-rounds an independent five-vendor ensemble applied as a residual adds nothing measurable (−0.0011 Brier, 95% interval −0.0055 to +0.0028).
- **Category**: Speculative-novel [AI aggregation layer feeding a price residual; a feature for file 58's stacker, not a standalone trade]
- **Maturity**: Experimental; the brief's "Emerging" rests on attempts that report no PnL. The closest test is one model (claude-opus-4-6) on 100 post-cutoff Polymarket binaries with web search disabled. There, peer-critique debate scored Brier 0.170 against 0.159 for an independent ensemble and 0.152 for the market, and cost $0.23 against $0.10 per market [2]. The only forecasting-specific deliberation gain is −0.020 log loss (p = 0.017) on 202 Metaculus questions, for three different vendors sharing information, not for same-model groups, with no crowd or market comparison [1]. An FOMC debate beat plain LLM baselines with no futures baseline [23]. PolySwarm (50 personas mixed with the price, quarter-Kelly) publishes an architecture and no results [13]; no debate repository reports Polymarket or Kalshi PnL [28][29].
- **Requires AI?**: yes. LLMs argue assigned sides from a dated evidence pack, judge, and write red-team pre-mortems. Everything that touches money is deterministic and fitted on the resolved ledger: citation checker, residual weight λ, debate switch, fee-and-spread gate, `2(p − q)` sizing, veto rule.
- **How it works (simple flow)**:
  1. Universe and timing as in file 21: non-sports markets with a two-sided book, mid 0.15-0.85, forecast within 24 hours of listing and optionally 7 days before the scheduled end.
  2. Evidence pack: a fetcher (not the agents) pulls documents dated before the forecast time and numbers them. Scraped text is wrapped as quoted data.
  3. Advocates: YES (vendor A) and NO (vendor B), assigned regardless of belief, each write an opening (≤ 300 words) and a rebuttal (≤ 200 words); every claim cites a document number and a checker deletes uncited claims. The caps matter because verbosity drives persuasion override [8].
  4. Judge (vendor C): reads the rules, the pack and the anonymised transcripts and outputs p_judge, seeing no price and no model names.
  5. Control: the same three vendors forecast independently from the same pack, with no debate; their mean is p_ens.
  6. Rationale-quality variant: weight agents by Explanation-Quality-Marker scores [14], argumentative coherence [16], a rolling Martingale entrenchment score [15] and track record, never by length or confidence.
  7. Residual: p = σ(logit q + λ·(logit p_panel − logit q)), where q is the mid and λ is fitted walk-forward on your own resolved ledger. λ is pooled across categories until a category has a few hundred resolutions (below). The switch: debate is on only while its rolling residual Brier beats the control arm's.
  8. Gate and size (file 21): trade only where |p − q| exceeds the fee plus half-spread plus calibration error, maker-first, at `2(p − q)` units (proper betting, not Kelly [12]).
  9. Veto-only variant: the red team never originates trades. Given candidates from a statistical signal (file 20's recalibration map, file 24's longshot fade), it is told "this position lost; build the most plausible story from the dated pack", and the judge returns pass, half size or veto.
  10. The reference-class proposer/challenger debate for the prior moves to file 70 with file 68's reference-class tournament (critic note).
- **What creates the edge**: Mechanism: adversarial search is meant to surface evidence a single pass misses, and a blind judge is meant to resist anchoring on the crowd. Debate helps judges where one side holds information the judge lacks (76% LLM-judge accuracy vs a 48% naive baseline [9]; debate beats direct answering only on extractive tasks with information asymmetry [10]). A residual keeps the price's information: an agentic forecaster that loses to liquid-market consensus (0.1258 vs 0.1106) still improves it in a blend (0.106) [11] (file 21). The payers would be file 21's thin-book counterparties. The strongest counter-argument has three layers.
  (a) Debate adds little over voting. "Majority Voting alone accounts for most of the performance gains" attributed to debate, which is a martingale over beliefs [4]; with correlated errors standard debate cannot beat majority voting [17]; five debate methods on nine benchmarks "often fail" to beat chain-of-thought or self-consistency [5]. Homogeneous 7-8B teams adopt the majority answer up to 85.5% of the time at 2.1-3.4x the tokens [6]; strict conformity is 29% of flips, 57-77% correct-to-wrong [7]. Persuasive falsehoods override truthful answers with high judge confidence [8], and LLM judges fail against deceptive debaters [31][E8]. On resolution questions, deliberation fell to ~76% against 83.43% for an independent confidence-weighted vote [3].
  (b) The residual has little room. Measured here on ForecastBench's published zero-shot forecasts (no price, no retrieval) for 574 resolved Polymarket question-rounds (472 questions, 141 Yes, median horizon 30 days), the five-vendor mean scores 0.144 against the price's 0.079 [25] (file 55). As a residual with λ fitted walk-forward and pooled across categories, it changes Brier by −0.0011 [−0.0055, +0.0028] on the 395 evaluable rounds; the in-sample pooled λ is only 0.15 (90% interval −0.05 to 0.30). Per-category λ is noise at these sizes (politics −0.90 on 35 Yes, culture +0.40 on 8 Yes) and only raised the variance (−0.0013 [−0.0076, +0.0050]). One vendor's residual does as well as the ensemble's (difference −0.0007 [−0.0034, +0.0020]) [25]. This control arm is a floor, not a ceiling: debaters would read retrieved evidence.
  (c) Costs: a debate run costs 2-4x the control arm ([2]; estimate below). The room sits in a few categories: 7 days before the scheduled end, mid-band markets (0.10-0.90) score 0.175, weakest in economy (0.204), politics (0.198) and science (0.198), not sensitive to volume tercile and ~0.176 once markets that closed within 8 days of listing are put back [26].
- **Capital required**: none until validation; then file 21's sizing, <$1k to $10k: $20-100 per position, locked to resolution (median 36.5 days, file 21). LLM spend binds, not capital.
- **Technical requirements**: Built on file 21's stack (feeds, dated retrieval, ledger, deterministic trading layer). Agent design sketch:
  - (1) Roles and vendors: YES advocate Claude Sonnet 5, NO advocate GPT-6 Sol, judge Gemini 3.1 Pro, rotated weekly. Cross-vendor pairs are no less correlated than same-vendor pairs on Polymarket questions (0.60-0.78 within outcome, file 55), so vendor rotation is hygiene, not diversity.
  - (2) Information: all see the rules and the numbered pack, the judge also the transcripts; nobody sees the price, the book or identities.
  - (3) Guards: a citation checker, word caps, a paraphraser that normalises style before the judge, and advocates with no tools (injection surface = the fetcher). Debate amplifies static prompt injection up to 9.9x on smaller models, and domain-camouflaged payloads evade detectors (Llama Guard 3 caught none) [18].
  - (4) Aggregation: p_judge and p_ens enter the residual (pooled walk-forward λ); the switch runs on the rolling paired Brier; markers scored by Haiku 4.5, entrenchment over each agent's last 100 questions; a veto costs one red-team and one judge call.
  - (5) Cost per forecast, estimated, not measured: 4 advocate calls plus 1 judge call is ~52k input and ~7k output tokens; the control arm adds 3 calls (~24k / ~3k). At $2 / $10 per MTok that is ~$0.25, plus ~6 searches at $0.01 and ~$0.01 of marker scoring: ~$0.30-0.35, of which the control arm is ~$0.08. The cheap tier (Haiku 4.5, GPT-6 Luna, Gemini 3.8 Flash) costs ~$0.08-0.12; batch halves the model part (prices from file 21), in line with [2] and [6].
  - Build: 3-4 weeks on top of file 21's harness; the leak-free replay and the ledger are most of it.
- **Opportunity size**: No pool of its own: at best a slice of file 21's (long-tail and day-one toss-ups, low tens of thousands of dollars a month for everyone combined); measured addition of an independent LLM panel over the price so far: none [25] (file 55). The testable flow is large: 21,856 resolved Yes / No markets with ≥ $5k volume were listed and closed in 2026 in 5,855 events (file 55). In a 1,010-event sample (drawn, like file 55's, from markets that lived ≥ 8 days: a look-ahead touching ~6% of draws), 870 were open 7 days before the scheduled end and 437 traded at 0.10-0.90 [26].
- **Pros**: The control and single-agent arms reuse the debate's pack, so ablation is cheap, and the switch degrades the system to file 21's ensemble; transcripts are auditable. The veto needs loss-spotting, not forecasting skill: an LLM filter on Kalshi economics lead-lag signals raised the win rate from 51.4% to 54.5% and cut the average loss from $649 to $347 [19], and EQMs spot underperformers better than stars [14].
- **Cons / risks**: Edge: (a)-(c) above. Leakage: backtests are contaminated by default; Hindcast's frozen archive closes it [24] (file 21). Persuasion: a fluent wrong advocate moves a confident judge [7][8]. Security: every scraped page is an injection vector [18]. Correlated losses: one shared blind spot hits every position. Veto risk: a pre-mortem always finds a story, so a veto biased toward dramatic narratives removes winners too; no study of a red-team agent improving Brier exists [E8].
- **Expected results**: no public data, theoretical only. Adjacent: debate 0.018 Brier behind the market and 0.011 behind an independent ensemble on 100 Polymarket markets [2]; the independent-ensemble residual measured here is −0.0011 [−0.0055, +0.0028] [25].
  - The veto's target at executed prices: favourites priced 0.55-0.90 seven days before the scheduled end, bought on the 5,120 taker fills a buyer could have matched in the next 24 hours (Data API v1 `/trades`, `takerOnly=true`) across 247 markets, won 78.9% against a mean fill of 0.769: +1.7c per market after the taker fee (95% interval −3.3c to +6.3c) [27]. Counting all 9,147 fills, including sells that hit the bid ~3c lower, flatters this to +2.0c.
  - Politics, the brief's example, made +0.2c [−11.0c, +11.4c] on 58 markets, and its mid-band calibration slope at T-7d is 0.73 [0.33, 1.29] on 79 markets, below the published Polymarket politics mean of 1.45 [20][26]. Only fee-free geopolitics stands out (+11.9c [+2.2c, +19.6c], 26 of 31 favourites on No), and its interval includes zero after adjusting for the eight categories tested (−3.1c to +21.5c): a hypothesis for files 24 and 70, not this file.
  - Guess: the debate residual lands within ±0.003 Brier of the ensemble residual; standalone PnL −5% to +3% of deployed capital a month before LLM costs, negative after.
- **Competitive landscape**: Hobby repositories and papers; nobody live with a record. GitHub returns 13 repositories for "polymarket debate" and 0 for "polymarket red team"; the largest (orallexa, 67 stars) paper-trades equities with prediction-market prices as one input, and PitchProphetAi (soccer, paper trading, Brier-vs-market gate) and whoisk4rthik's bull/bear bot have 0 stars and no results [28]. The equities template TradingAgents (bull/bear researchers, 109,233 stars) has no prediction-market results [22]; HN has one debate app on Polymarket events (1 point, 2025) [29]. Reconciling several forecasts is table stakes (AIA's supervisor ensemble 0.1125 vs single 0.1182, file 21; 86% of Fall 2025 Metaculus bot winners aggregate [E7]); the untested part is adversarial assignment plus a blind judge.
- **Regulatory/ethical flag**: none specific; own-model trading from one identity is allowed [E9]. The international venue excludes US persons and puts 36 jurisdictions in close-only mode [E9]. Scraping is restricted by the ToS, so the rationale-quality variant cannot harvest Polymarket comments at scale; commentary is a weak signal anyway (0.506-0.599 directed accuracy on a commenter's position side [21]). An incorporated team using Polymarket data may be a Capital Market Client needing a written agreement [E9]. Treat all retrieved text as hostile [18].
- **How you'd validate it**:
  - Phase 0 (done, $0): the control arm on ForecastBench [25], the T-7d calibration pool [26], the veto target at executed fills [27].
  - Phase 1, Hindcast-style replay, 3-4 weeks, ~$200-400 at batch prices.
    - Sample: 450 post-cutoff resolved Polymarket binaries, one per event, from the 5,855 events above; created after every panel model's training cutoff; forecasts at listing + 1 day and 7 days before the scheduled end, counted only if open then (filter on the scheduled end, never on how long a market actually stayed open).
    - Leakage control: retrieval hard-limited to documents dated before t, or a frozen archive, where retrieval helps only if the archive discussed the event beforehand [24]; manual leak audit of 50 packs.
    - Pre-registered arms: best single vendor; independent three-vendor ensemble; debate with blind judge; debate judge as residual; ensemble as residual; rationale-weighted judge; market mid (CLOB `/prices-history`, ≤ 15-day windows); file 21's plain forecaster. λ is fitted on the first 40% in time order and frozen.
    - Metrics: Brier, log loss, Brier skill against the mid (question-clustered paired bootstrap), proper-betting PnL at recorded books and buyer-executable fills after fees.
    - Kill criterion (the null): "debate residual = independent-ensemble residual". Drop debate unless the debate-minus-ensemble residual difference has a 95% interval below zero; drop the file if the ensemble residual does not beat the mid.
    - Power (derived from [25], question-level paired SD at a fixed λ in the 0.15-0.85 band the replay uses; 80% power, two-sided 5%): residual minus mid SD 0.048 (λ = 0.15) to 0.064 (λ = 0.20): the brief's 0.003 bar needs ~2,000-3,500 questions, 0.005 ~720-1,270; residual minus residual SD 0.023-0.031: a 0.005 gap needs ~165-295, 0.002 ~1,000-1,800. 450 questions resolve only ~0.006-0.008 against the mid and ~0.003-0.004 between residuals: Phase 1 kills or confirms only large effects, and the rationale-weighting ablation (brief's bar 0.002) cannot be decided on one replay.
  - Phase 2, forward shadow, 8-12 weeks, $150-300: the frozen pipeline on every new non-sports market within 24 hours of listing until 350 resolutions (file 21's bar for a 2-point edge), paper-traded maker-first. Live money only if the debate residual beats the mid by ≥ 0.003 with the interval above zero AND beats the ensemble residual; at 350 mid-band resolutions the interval's half-width is ~0.005 (SD above), so in practice the bar is an observed gain of ~0.005.
  - Veto A/B on file 20 / 24 candidates: pass if the veto cuts average loss or drawdown by ≥ 20% while giving up < 10% of return (brief). With favourites losing ~21-23% of the time [26][27], a veto that flags a quarter of candidates at a 35% loss rate against 17% for the rest needs ~230 candidates, and a weaker 30% vs 19% split ~610 (two-proportion power, derived).

### Speed and execution sensitivity
- **Speed class**: speed-insensitive (hours to days). A debate run takes 5-15 minutes, and the only window where LLM forecasters show skill is the first day after listing (file 21).

| Part of the strategy | What it depends on | If you are slower or execute worse | Realistic target for a solo team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Gamma keyset feed, `new_market` events | a day late, the price is already better than any LLM (file 21) | forecast within 1-24 hours of listing |
| Evidence pack | date-filtered fetcher, 4-7 searches | a stale or leaky pack is a correctness risk, not a speed risk | one pack per forecast time, archived |
| Debate and control | 5 debate calls plus 3 control calls | minutes are irrelevant; batch halves cost but runs asynchronously | same-day run; batch for re-forecasts |
| Entry | live mid-band spreads median 2.5c below $10k liquidity, 1.0c above (file 55); taker fee 0.04-0.05·p(1−p), geopolitics 0 [30] | crossing costs about half the spread plus up to 1.25c of fee at 0.50 | maker orders at the residual fair value via file 21's gate |
| Veto | one red-team plus one judge call per candidate | a late veto after entry costs the round trip | veto before the order is sent |
| Exit / settlement | UMA resolution | early exits pay the spread twice | hold to resolution |

- **How speed changes the result**: (1) Lifecycle, not latency: LLM skill is positive at listing + 1 day and below −0.7 BSS one checkpoint before close (file 21). (2) The residual is small, so the spread decides the sign (derived). With λ = 0.15 [25], a panel at 0.30 against a 0.50 mid moves fair value only to 0.468, a 3.2-point gap. That clears the ~2.25c a taker pays (half of a 2.5c spread plus the 1c politics fee at 0.50) by about 1 point, less than any plausible calibration error: maker-only. (3) Rate limits, taker delays and uptime do not matter at a few orders a day.
- **Where to run it**: anywhere unrestricted (not the US, UK or France [E9]); LLM calls dominate latency; nothing justifies co-location.
